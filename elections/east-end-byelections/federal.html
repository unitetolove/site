The Beaches—East York federal by-election: a briefing

Beaches—East York — federal by-election, Monday, August 31, 2026. This page describes; it endorses no one and rates no one, and every candidate on it is treated the same way, by the same method. The practical page carries both east-end races side by side; the parallel briefing is the Scarborough Southwest provincial briefing (September 3).

The race and how it came to be

Nate Erskine-Smith, the Liberal who represented Beaches—East York from 2015, was re-elected on April 28, 2025 with 39,804 votes — 67.7 per cent, the riding's largest-ever share. In February 2026 he announced he was exploring a bid for the Ontario Liberal leadership and sought that party's provincial nomination in Scarborough Southwest; he lost the nomination on May 9, 2026, appealed alleging irregularities, and the party's arbitration committee dismissed the appeal on May 24. His resignation from the House of Commons took effect July 7, 2026, and Elections Canada announced the seat vacant on July 8. (Elections Canada — past results, Beaches—East York; CTV News — Erskine-Smith to resign federal seat; Wikipedia — 2026 Beaches—East York federal by-election (and the reporting it cites); Beach Metro Community News, August 13, 2026)

Prime Minister Mark Carney called the by-election on July 24, 2026, setting August 31 as election day — one of three federal by-elections held that day, alongside Chicoutimi—Le Fjord in Quebec and North Vancouver—Capilano in British Columbia. (Wikipedia — 2026 Beaches—East York federal by-election (and the reporting it cites); Beach Metro Community News, August 13, 2026)

The Liberal riding association chose its candidate at a nomination meeting on July 18, 2026 — a short contest announced roughly a week earlier. Tanveer Shahnawaz won it; 869 votes were cast. Journalist Steve Paikin, reporting a leak of the final tally, described a 13-vote margin over the second-place contestant, environmental lawyer Claire Seaborn; the party itself has not published the count, so treat the margin as reported rather than official. Two earlier entrants — John Tory Jr. and Marc Hull-Jacquin — had withdrawn before the vote, each saying publicly that the early nomination date left no time to campaign. (TVO Today — Steve Paikin analysis, July 28, 2026; Beach Metro Community News, July 13, 2026; Wikipedia — 2026 Beaches—East York federal by-election (and the reporting it cites))

The field is final. Elections Canada issued the confirmed list of candidates on Wednesday, August 12, 2026 — six candidates, no independents. New rules under Bill C-25 applied to this by-election: a candidate needed signatures from 100 electors, each elector could consent to only one candidate, and an official agent could support only one candidate per district. (Elections Canada — confirmed candidates, Beaches—East York; Elections Canada — by-election FAQ; Beach Metro Community News, August 13, 2026)

All-candidates debate

Wednesday, August 19, 6:30–8:30 p.m. — all-candidates debate at Calvary Baptist Church, 72 Main Street, organised by Daily Bread Food Bank with the church and moderated by Community Centre 55’s Reza Khoshdel. As of August 12, the New Democratic, Green and Liberal candidates had confirmed they would attend; the organisers’ attendance list may change.

Reported by Beach Metro Community News, August 12, 2026.

The riding

Electoral district 35007 covers 17 square kilometres between Coxwell Avenue in the west, the Don River and Sunrise Avenue in the north, Victoria Park Avenue in the east and Lake Ontario in the south. Its population was 109,359 at the 2021 census, with 82,102 electors on the preliminary lists for this by-election. Its neighbourhoods include The Beaches, the Upper Beaches, the East Danforth, O'Connor–Parkview and part of old East York — plus Crescent Town at Victoria Park and Danforth, a dense high-rise neighbourhood built around Victoria Park subway station, inside the city neighbourhood now called Taylor-Massey, home to a significant Bangladeshi-Canadian community. (Elections Canada — riding profile, Beaches—East York; Wikipedia — Beaches—East York (federal electoral district); Wikipedia — Crescent Town)

By the 2021 census, 55.8 per cent of the riding's households own their home and 44.2 per cent rent; median individual income was $44,000 in 2020. English is the language most spoken at home (85.5 per cent), with Bengali the largest other home language (2.1 per cent). These figures come from the census as tabulated by the two aggregators linked here, not from a fresh Statistics Canada pull. (338Canada — Beaches—East York (demographic panel, StatCan 2021); Wikipedia — Beaches—East York (federal electoral district))

The seat's history: New Democrat 1988–93 (as Beaches—Woodbine), Liberal 1993–2011, New Democrat 2011–15, Liberal 2015–2026. Turnout in the last four general elections here: 71.99 per cent (2015), 70.16 per cent (2019), 64.91 per cent (2021), 71.94 per cent (2025). (Wikipedia — Beaches—East York (federal electoral district), citing Elections Canada)

The candidates

6

Candidates are listed alphabetically by surname. Every candidate is presented by the identical method: who they are, from their own published materials or the reporting linked beside each fact; previous candidacies, from official results; one official link, chosen the same way for everyone (their own campaign website if they have published one, otherwise their party’s page for them, otherwise the party announcement naming them — and “no official page found” where none exists); and what they have published, quoted verbatim with its source and access date — with no platform found stated plainly, as a fact about the public record on the day it was read, where that is true.

Shannon Devine — New Democratic Party

Who they are. Labour communications professional; lives in Toronto's east end with her wife and two daughters. Her site states she "has spent the last 20 years professionally advocating for rights of working people," including as communications director for the CAW/Unifor, executive director of the Ontario Federation of Labour, head of the United Steelworkers' national communications and political action department, and executive director of the Toronto and York Region Labour Council. (shannondevine.ca — About, accessed August 14, 2026)

Previous candidacies. NDP candidate in this riding in the 2025 general election — 4,027 votes, 6.9 per cent — with campaign roles here since 2015. Announced as the by-election candidate on July 30, 2026. (Elections Canada — past results, Beaches—East York, accessed August 14, 2026; Wikipedia — 2026 Beaches—East York federal by-election, accessed August 14, 2026)

Official page. shannondevine.ca (Candidate website)

What they have published. Her homepage carries a set of commitments, quoted in full below. No standalone riding-specific platform document is published on her site beyond these homepage commitments (as of August 14, 2026).

If I have the honour of representing you in Parliament, I commit to:

  • Freely speaking out on federal decisions that impact Beaches—East York and Toronto. As an NDP candidate I can't (and won't) be silenced by a top-down Carney government.
  • Advocating for the return of climate and environmental protections.
  • Ensuring the Liberals keep their word on Billy Bishop Airport, instead of making more backroom deals with Doug Ford.
  • Firmly opposing new pipeline projects.
  • Calling for diplomatic and economic solutions to end Canada's complicity in the genocide in Palestine.
  • Fighting hard for the things our community needs: Affordable rent and housing; Bringing down costs on groceries, prescriptions and other essentials; More good jobs; Affordable child care; Taking my direction from you.

— shannondevine.ca (homepage), accessed August 14, 2026

“It turns out the Mark Carney government is more conservative than many actual Conservative governments, leaving many feeling betrayed while the cost of living soars.”

— shannondevine.ca (homepage), accessed August 14, 2026

Who they are. His candidate profile on the national party site states: "Michel Lachance is a lifelong resident of Toronto's east end, born in the Beaches and proud to still call the community home. A small business owner, licensed financial professional, and founding member of the People's Party of Canada." It says he has owned and operated MJL Electronics and Computers for more than a decade and later became licensed in securities and insurance. (People's Party of Canada — candidate profile, Michel Lachance, accessed August 14, 2026)

Bruce Livesey — Green Party of Canada

Who they are. Investigative journalist and former TTC bus driver. The party's nomination release states he "spent more than 40 years working as an investigative reporter, television and radio producer, newspaper and magazine writer and bestselling author," including six years as associate producer at CBC's The Fifth Estate; he is the author of Thieves of Bay Street. Born in Vancouver, raised in Fredericton, in Toronto since 1982. (Green Party of Canada — nomination release, July 30, 2026, accessed August 14, 2026)

Previous candidacies. None found. Chosen after a nomination contest; announced July 30, 2026. (Green Party of Canada — nomination release, July 30, 2026, accessed August 14, 2026)

Official page. greenparty.ca (Party announcement)

What they have published. His priorities are set out in the party's nomination release, quoted below. No standalone platform document beyond this release was found (as of August 14, 2026).

His priorities include securing free public transit, expanding social and cooperative housing to lower rents, helping households and businesses reduce their energy bills and supporting community-owned, nonprofit grocery stores.”

Polling

No riding-level poll of this by-election had been published as of August 14, 2026. That is a statement about the public record, not an estimate: the search behind it covered the firms that publish riding polls and the aggregators that track them.

What does exist is a model projection, which is not a poll. 338Canada's seat model — its own page describes it as "a projection based on polls," derived from national and regional surveys with no riding poll as input — rated the seat "LPC safe" as of its August 9, 2026 update: Liberal 61 ± 7, NDP 18 ± 6, Conservative 16 ± 5, Green 3 ± 3, People's Party 0 ± 1. A projection reports what a model expects from wider patterns; it does not measure anything specific to this by-election campaign. (338Canada — Beaches—East York projection)

For national context only: Nanos Research's rolling federal tracking ending August 7, 2026 reported Liberals 45.6 per cent, Conservatives 29.1, NDP 12.9; a Léger poll fielded August 1–3, 2026 reported Liberals 46, Conservatives 34, NDP 8. These are national figures, not riding figures, and our record of both comes from the firms' own published summaries rather than fetched full reports, so sample sizes are not recorded here. (Nanos Research — weekly tracking summary; Léger — federal polling, August 2026)

How to vote

You are electing one Member of Parliament. Polls are open 9:30 a.m. – 9:30 p.m. on Monday, August 31, 2026. Every date, address and rule below was read from the election authority’s own pages on August 14, 2026.

  • Vote now, at the returning officeAny day until Tuesday, August 25, 6 p.m. by special ballot, at 2575 Danforth Avenue, Unit 10.
  • Vote by mailApply online or at the office by August 25, 6 p.m. A completed mail-in ballot has to arrive back by election day.
  • Advance pollsFriday, August 21 – Monday, August 24, 9 a.m. – 9 p.m., at your assigned advance polling station.
  • Election dayMonday, August 31, 9:30 a.m. – 9:30 p.m., at your assigned polling station.

Who can vote, and what to bring

A Canadian citizen, 18 or older on election day, who has lived in the riding since July 26, 2026, and can prove identity and address.

  • One government-issued photo card showing your name and current address — a driver’s licence, for example; or
  • two pieces of ID, both showing your name, at least one showing your current address — the accepted list includes health cards, bank statements, utility bills, leases, student cards and prescription labels; or
  • no ID at all: declare your identity and address in writing and have someone assigned to your polling station vouch for you (one person may vouch for one elector).

Find your polling station and check your registration: Voter Information Service (by postal code) · Online voter registration.

Mobile polls visit most seniors’ and long-term-care residences. Electors with disabilities can request home voting by contacting the local office before August 25, 6 p.m.

The practical page carries the same voting information for both east-end races side by side.

What this page is and is not

It is the public render of our research briefing, built from the sources linked on this page and nothing else. It endorses no one and rates no one; every candidate is treated the same way, by the same method. Where a candidate has published little or nothing, the page says so plainly — that is a fact about the public record on the day it was read, not a criticism: by-elections are short and campaigns publish on their own schedule, and this page gets re-read and corrected as the record changes. We are not a registered third party under the Canada Elections Act or Ontario’s Election Finances Act, and no money has been spent promoting this page or any candidate; it is published as commentary and research, which both statutes distinguish from election advertising. If something here is wrong or out of date, tell us through the Harvest and it gets fixed.
