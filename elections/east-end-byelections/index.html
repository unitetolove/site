Two by-elections, two different ridings. If you live in Beaches—East York you are electing a Member of Parliament on Monday, August 31. If you live in Scarborough Southwest you are electing a Member of Provincial Parliament on Thursday, September 3. They are separate ridings with separate ballots and separate rules — almost nobody votes in both. Every fact on this page was read from Elections Canada, Elections Ontario or local reporting on August 14, 2026.

The east-end by-elections

Two seats in Toronto’s east end are being filled this summer, three days apart. This page is the practical layer and nothing else: the dates, the official candidate lists as the election authorities publish them, each candidate’s own official page, the all-candidates debates, and every way you can cast a ballot. It does not tell you who to vote for, and it does not rate anyone.

How voting works

Our standing guide to who can vote, what ID does, and every way to cast a ballot in Ontario.

Beaches—East York — federal by-election

August 31, 2026

You are electing one Member of Parliament. Polls are open 9:30 a.m. – 9:30 p.m. on Monday, August 31, 2026.

How and when to vote

  • Vote now, at the returning officeAny day until Tuesday, August 25, 6 p.m. by special ballot, at 2575 Danforth Avenue, Unit 10.
  • Vote by mailApply online or at the office by August 25, 6 p.m. A completed mail-in ballot has to arrive back by election day.
  • Advance pollsFriday, August 21 – Monday, August 24, 9 a.m. – 9 p.m., at your assigned advance polling station.
  • Election dayMonday, August 31, 9:30 a.m. – 9:30 p.m., at your assigned polling station.

Who can vote, and what to bring

A Canadian citizen, 18 or older on election day, who has lived in the riding since July 26, 2026, and can prove identity and address.

  • One government-issued photo card showing your name and current address — a driver’s licence, for example; or
  • two pieces of ID, both showing your name, at least one showing your current address — the accepted list includes health cards, bank statements, utility bills, leases, student cards and prescription labels; or
  • no ID at all: declare your identity and address in writing and have someone assigned to your polling station vouch for you (one person may vouch for one elector).

Find your polling station and check your registration: Voter Information Service (by postal code) · Online voter registration.

Mobile polls visit most seniors’ and long-term-care residences. Electors with disabilities can request home voting by contacting the local office before August 25, 6 p.m.

Scarborough Southwest — provincial by-election

September 3, 2026

You are electing one Member of Provincial Parliament. Polls are open 9 a.m. – 9 p.m. on Thursday, September 3, 2026.

How and when to vote

  • Vote now, at the returning officeAny day through Wednesday, September 2 at 2685 Kingston Road, Scarborough — open Monday to Saturday 10 a.m. – 8 p.m. and Sunday noon – 5 p.m., closing at 6 p.m. on the final day.
  • Vote by mailApply by 6 p.m. on August 28. Your completed kit has to be received by Elections Ontario by 6 p.m. on September 3 to be counted.
  • Advance votingWednesday, August 26 – Friday, August 28, 10 a.m. – 8 p.m., at any advance voting location in the riding.
  • Election dayThursday, September 3, 9 a.m. – 9 p.m., at your assigned voting location.

Who can vote, and what to bring

A Canadian citizen, 18 or older on election day, who lives in Scarborough Southwest.

  • ID is required, but photo ID is not — and if you do show photo ID, your appearance does not have to match the photo;
  • you may show your ID on a phone or other mobile device;
  • if you are already on the voters list, one piece of ID showing your name is enough; if you are not, you need ID showing both your name and your current home address;
  • international ID and permanent resident cards are not accepted.

Find your voting location and check your registration: Voter Information Service · Online voter registration.

Assistive voting technology is available at the returning office from August 21 to September 2, and by appointment on election day.

Where this comes from

Candidate names, party affiliations and official links come from the election authorities’ own candidate lists and from each party’s own published pages; dates, office addresses, ID rules and voting methods come from Elections Canada and Elections Ontario; the debates come from local reporting, linked in each section. Every source is linked above rather than summarised, so you can check any line of this page against the body that published it.

This page carries no survey numbers, no forecast and no judgement of any candidate or party. Every candidate in a race is listed the same way, in the same order rule, with the same kind of link. If something here is wrong or out of date — especially after the provincial nominations close on August 20 — tell us through the Harvest and it gets fixed.

For each race we also publish the full briefing behind this page — how the seat came open, the riding, what every candidate has published in their own words, the state of the survey record, and every source: the federal briefing · the provincial briefing.

Separately, we have read what the candidates in both races have published and written it up as commentary: what the east-end candidates have actually published. It endorses no one and rates no one against anyone else.

And because a riding is a place before it is a contest, we have assembled what the public record says about the constituency itself — who lives in it, what neighbours report to the city, the rental stock, how much of it has been voting, and where the public washrooms and taps are: Beaches—East York by the numbers. Every figure there is derived from a named open dataset, and the sections that describe the place carry no candidate content at all.
