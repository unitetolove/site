Two worlds, one street

In April 2025, Beaches—East York voted 71.9% — one of the highest-participation ridings in Canada. This story is about the neighbours that number hides, and the twelve minutes of Main Street that separate two civic worlds.

Scroll ↓

The lake

Start at the boardwalk. The Beaches zones vote at 75–76% — and more than half of those votes are cast early, in advance polls. Homeowners at 63%, median household income $116,000, towers almost absent (7.6% of dwellings).

Civic capacity is thick here: four residents' associations, service clubs, church halls. This is where field programs recruit.

Twelve minutes up Main Street

Drive north on Main, under the GO bridge, to where Main meets Danforth. Advance zone 608 — the Main–Dawes corridor — is the lowest-participation zone in the riding: 55.9%. Same riding, twenty points of canyon.

Nearly half the households here rent. This corridor is the program's first focus — presence, mutual-aid registration, tabling.

The towers of Crescent Town

Cross Dawes into Crescent Town and Taylor-Massey. Each red dot is a single building that is its own polling division. Five Crescent Town towers hold 3,592 electors voting 30–37% on e-day.

Taylor-Massey: 66% renters, 76% of dwellings in towers, 12.2% Bengali at home, median income $68,500. The English hyperlocal media layer does not land here at all (who reaches whom).

What this asks of us

Not a turnout operation for anyone's benefit — a serve-and-invite program: mutual aid, neighbourhood assemblies, and voting information offered identically to every neighbour, concentrated where participation is thin and carried by partners who already hold trust (every tower's three nearest doors: the tower explorer).
