Online Annex — running the Citizens' Assembly in a Box on a video call

📄 Print this kit (PDF, letter, black-and-white-safe)

One page. Any video tool your room already uses. The same evening, online.

This annex turns the Citizens’ Assembly in a Box evening into an online one. It exists because the honest answer to “what platform do we need?” is: none you don’t already have. The tools that matter are the rules, not the software. The people who run online deliberation for a living report the same thing plainly: moving a good room onto a video call without redesigning the technique is what fails — the technique is this page.

Kids welcome. Food shared. Nobody pays. (Online too: kids can sit in; everyone brings their own snack to the screen; nothing here costs anything.)

What you need

  • Any video-call tool your room already uses that has breakout rooms (Zoom, Google Meet, Jitsi — whatever is familiar; familiarity beats features).
  • One shared document everyone can open (Google Doc or any equivalent) — this is your wall.
  • The main kit, read once: the question rules, the mixed-tables rule, the facilitator’s card, and the three-column record all apply unchanged.
  • 12 to 30 people, 2.5 hours, a real question with a real recipient — exactly as in the main kit.

The translations — room to screen

In the room On the call
Mixed tables of 4–6 Breakout rooms of 4–6, assigned by the host, never random-only — mix ages and viewpoints, split people who know each other. Assign them before the call from the signup list if you can.
Silent cards, 5 minutes Everyone writes alone, cameras off if they like, in a private note — then sends it to the facilitator in a direct message (or a form). Not in the group chat: nobody’s card should anchor anyone else’s.
Reading a sample back Facilitator reads a spread of cards aloud, anonymous, before any discussion opens — same rule, same reason: the quiet get heard before the loud.
The wall The shared doc, with the three-column record (WHERE WE AGREE · WHERE WE DIVIDE · WHAT WE’D NEED TO KNOW) as its only structure. The facilitator holds the keyboard; the room supplies the words.
Cross-pollination shuffle Re-assign half of each breakout room at the halfway mark — thirty seconds of admin for the most valuable five minutes of the evening.
The harvest Everyone opens unitetolove.ca/harvest on their own device in the last five minutes — online, the QR is just a link. If the online form isn’t live yet, the shared doc holds a harvest section — the doc is the record, nothing is lost.

Three online-only rules

  1. The chat is not the room. Side-chat during someone’s speaking turn is the online way the loud drown the quiet. Ask for it plainly at the open: reactions yes, running commentary no. The facilitator’s “who hasn’t spoken yet?” matters more on a call — ask it every round, by name if needed, since screens make silence invisible.
  2. Phones are seats. Anyone who can join only by phone audio is a full participant — the facilitator reads the doc’s key lines aloud whenever it changes, so nobody needs the screen to follow the wall.
  3. Recording follows the room’s own rule, decided at the start, said out loud. Online makes recording one click — which is exactly why it must be a decision, never a default. No recording without the whole call hearing it announced.

When your room outgrows this page

Purpose-built platforms for online assemblies exist and are worth knowing: some are access-by-request research tools, some are in beta, some cost real money — and a true citizens’ assembly (lottery-selected, stratified, professionally run — the gold standard the main kit names) is a different undertaking from this evening at any scale. This page is the honest first tier: your own call, your own doc, these rules. When a room runs this three times and wants more, that is a good problem, and the main kit’s honesty box says where to look.
