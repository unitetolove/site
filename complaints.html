Where your complaint actually goes

Something in this city's systems wronged you, or someone you know, and you don't know who's actually supposed to hear it. This page is every formal channel we've found and checked, in one place — what it covers, whether you have to try something else first, what the deadline is, and whether someone can file it for you. Every row links straight to the body's own page, checked 2026-08-10.

This is not legal advice. It's a map of where to go, built and checked the same way as everything else on this site — see how we verify. A few bodies (marked below) legally require a licensed lawyer or paralegal to represent you at a hearing; we tell you which ones and point you to where free help exists. Nothing here should be your only source before a real deadline.

If you need to talk to someone right now, about this or anything else: Toronto Community Crisis Service / 211 Ontario — dial 211. Distress Centres of Greater Toronto — 416-408-4357. Canada's 988 Suicide Crisis Helpline — call or text 988.

The one thing worth knowing before you start

Almost every one of these bodies makes you try the institution's own process first — Ombudsman Toronto won't take your case until you've gone through the City's own complaints process and gotten its final answer; Patient Ombudsman wants you to go to the hospital's patient relations office first; OW/ODSP appeals run on a strict 30-day clock at each of three separate stages. In practice, a complaint here is rarely a single form — it's a case, with a response clock and an escalation step, and the biggest reason people don't get heard is missing a deadline while waiting for step one to answer. Whatever you file, write down the date you filed it and when you're owed a response.

Municipal (Toronto) — 7 mechanisms

Toronto Shelter & Support Services (TSSS) — internal complaint process

Shelter conditions, staff conduct, being turned away from a program.

Try this first?Yes. Each program has its own frontline process first — Central Intake, Shelters/Respites/Warming Centres, Drop-in, or Encampments/Street Outreach. Only after that's exhausted does it go to TSSS by email, form, or voicemail.
DeadlineNot stated.

Still open — Not confirmed either way whether someone can file on your behalf.

Source: Toronto Shelter & Support Services, Compliments & Complaints · checked 2026-08-10

Ombudsman Toronto

City services including shelters, welfare (TESS) administration, the TTC, city agencies, and Toronto Community Housing. Can investigate patterns on its own — not just one case — and publish public reports.

Try this first?Yes — take it as far as you can through the City's own complaints process first (311, below, is the usual starting point) and get the City's final answer before you come here.
DeadlineNot stated.

Still open — Not confirmed either way whether someone can file on your behalf — no consent language found on the site.

Source: Ombudsman Toronto, How to Make a Complaint · checked 2026-08-10

Toronto Auditor General — Fraud & Waste Hotline

Misuse of city funds by staff or contractors — including shelter operators.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineNone stated.

Still open — Anonymous complaints are investigated. Not confirmed whether a third party can file on someone else's behalf.

Source: Toronto Auditor General, Report Fraud, Waste or Wrongdoing · checked 2026-08-10

311 — Toronto's service-request line

The everyday complaint line and portal for city services. This is also literally what Ombudsman Toronto tells people to start with, to build the record they'll later want.

Try this first?—
DeadlineNone.

Still open — Not confirmed whether a third party can file on someone else's behalf.

Source: City of Toronto, 311 Toronto · checked 2026-08-10

TCHC (internal) + Office of the Commissioner of Housing Equity (OCHE)

Toronto Community Housing tenants; help for seniors and vulnerable tenants facing arrears or eviction.

Try this first?Not confirmed either way.
DeadlineNone stated.

Still open — Whether someone can file for you is not confirmed. The current officeholder's name wasn't confirmed either — contact through the office generally.

Source: Office of the Commissioner of Housing Equity, Who We Are · checked 2026-08-10

Integrity Commissioner (Toronto)

Conduct of a member of City Council or a local board.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineWithin 3 months of you becoming aware of the conduct, or 6 months of the conduct itself — whichever applies.

Still open — The complaint form calls for a signed declaration by “an identifiable individual,” which suggests self-filing, but no explicit rule on filing for someone else was found.

Source: City of Toronto, Complaint and Application Procedures · checked 2026-08-10

Petitions and comments to Council committees (“Have Your Say”)

Getting something formally onto the record with a Council committee.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineNone stated.
Can someone file for you?Yes — on someone else's behalf, but only with their confirmed consent attached. Their own quoted rule: “If you are submitting comments on behalf of someone else and do not include confirmation of their consent for you to submit comments on their behalf, we will not distribute them.”

Still open — Anonymous submissions are explicitly rejected — you can't submit without your name.

Source: City of Toronto, Have Your Say at Council & Committee Meetings · checked 2026-08-10

Provincial (Ontario) — 13 mechanisms

Ontario Ombudsman

Provincial ministries and agencies, ODSP/OW administration, police oversight bodies, correctional facilities, tribunal and court service delays, children's aid societies, school boards — over 1,000 organizations. Can open its own investigations into patterns, not just individual cases.

Try this first?Mostly yes — varies by which body you're complaining about.
DeadlineNot stated.
Can someone file for you?Yes.

Source: Ontario Ombudsman, What We Can Help You With · checked 2026-08-10

LECA — Law Enforcement Complaints Agency (formerly OIPRD)

Police conduct, including Toronto Police Service. (LECA does not handle policy or service-level complaints about police — that now goes to the Inspectorate of Policing.)

Try this first?No.
DeadlineBest filed within 6 months — but this isn't a hard legal cutoff. Late complaints can still be accepted (for example: disability, an ongoing proceeding, or public interest).
Can someone file for you?Yes — on someone else's behalf, if you complete their Agent Consent form.

Source: LECA, Screening Complaints · checked 2026-08-10

SIU — Special Investigations Unit

Serious injury, death, or sexual assault involving police.

Try this first?Not applicable — the SIU takes on cases on its own once notified; you don't file a complaint here the way you would elsewhere. If you witnessed something, you can come forward as a witness.
DeadlineNot applicable.
Can someone file for you?Not applicable — this is a witness channel, not a representative-filing one.

Still open — Verified as a notification-based body, not a resident-filed complaint. We couldn't confirm a specific intake URL for this row, so no link is given here — search “SIU Ontario” directly, or ask us and we'll find it.

Patient Ombudsman (Ontario)

Hospitals (including CAMH, as a public hospital), long-term care, home care, and community surgical/diagnostic centres.

Try this first?Yes, in their own words: “First, try to resolve the issue directly with the health care organization. This may be through the patient relations department or the administrator.”
DeadlineNot stated.
Can someone file for you?Yes.

Source: Patient Ombudsman, Before You Make a Complaint · checked 2026-08-10

PPAO — Psychiatric Patient Advocate Office

Rights advice and advocacy inside psychiatric facilities, including CAMH. Part of the Ministry of Health, active at 9 designated facilities.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineNot applicable.
Can someone file for you?Not applicable — they advocate directly rather than taking third-party filings.

Source: Government of Ontario, Psychiatric Patient Advocate Office · checked 2026-08-10

OW/ODSP internal review → Social Benefits Tribunal

Ontario Works or ODSP benefit denials and cutoffs. The single highest-stakes deadline on this whole page.

Try this first?Yes — three separate 30-day clocks: 30 days to request the internal review, 30 days for the ministry to respond, then 30 days to appeal to the Social Benefits Tribunal after that decision.
DeadlineLate appeals are possible up to 1 year with a granted extension — but don't count on it; the 30-day clocks are the real deadline.
Can someone file for you?At the Tribunal stage, representation is gated to a licensed lawyer or paralegal (or a narrow unlicensed exception) — see “Getting help filing” below.

Source: Government of Ontario, Internal Reviews and Appeals — Social Assistance · checked 2026-08-10

HRTO — Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario

Discrimination in services or housing, on Code grounds — disability, race, receiving public assistance, and others.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineWithin one year of when the discrimination happened, or within one year of the last incident in a series. Late applications need an explanation and are accepted at the Tribunal's discretion.
Can someone file for you?Gated to a licensed lawyer or paralegal, or a narrow unlicensed exception — see “Getting help filing” below.

Source: Tribunals Ontario, Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario · checked 2026-08-10

LTB — Landlord and Tenant Board

Tenancy disputes — the sharpest edge for anyone precariously housed. Governed by the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006; applications go through the Tribunals Ontario Portal.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineNot stated.
Can someone file for you?Gated to a licensed lawyer or paralegal, or a narrow unlicensed exception — see “Getting help filing” below.

Source: Tribunals Ontario, Landlord and Tenant Board · checked 2026-08-10

Health regulatory colleges (CPSO, CNO, and others)

A specific clinician's conduct — a doctor (CPSO) or a nurse (CNO), for example.

Try this first?CPSO encourages trying to resolve it with the physician first, where appropriate — not a hard requirement. CNO: no stated requirement.
DeadlineNot stated.

Still open — CNO's wording implies family members or caregivers can raise a concern; a complaint cannot be made anonymously. No formal “representative” rule was found at either college.

Source: College of Nurses of Ontario, Make a Complaint · checked 2026-08-10

IPC Ontario — Information and Privacy Commissioner

Privacy complaints and freedom-of-information appeals against city and provincial institutions. Also useful as a way to obtain records as evidence for another complaint.

Try this first?Varies.
DeadlineNot stated.
Can someone file for you?Yes.

Source: IPC Ontario, Role and Mandate · checked 2026-08-10

Office of the Chief Coroner — inquest / death-review requests

Deaths within the system. In their own words: “a relative or personal representative of the deceased person may request an inquest.”

Try this first?No.
DeadlineNone — there is no time limit between the date of death and when an inquest can be convened.
Can someone file for you?Yes.

Source: Government of Ontario, Coroner's Inquests · checked 2026-08-10

Ministry LTC ACTION Line & RHRA

The long-term-care and retirement-home edge. The LTC ACTION Line (1-866-434-0144, run by the Ministry of Long-Term Care) sits between a home's own process and the Patient Ombudsman. RHRA is the independent regulator for retirement homes.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineNot stated.

Still open — Not confirmed whether someone can file for you. RHRA's own site blocks automated checking, so its details here are high-confidence but not directly receipt-verified the way the rest of this page is.

Source: Government of Ontario, Long-Term Care Home Complaint Process · checked 2026-08-10

Formal Legislature petitions (Queen's Park)

The provincial declaration route — getting an issue formally onto the record at Queen's Park.

Try this first?—
DeadlinePaper-only, signed originals; the government responds within 24 sitting days.
Can someone file for you?You need an MP—actually an MPP—to present it. Any MPP except cabinet ministers and the Speaker can present a petition on your behalf.

Source: Legislative Assembly of Ontario, Petitions · checked 2026-08-10

Federal — 8 mechanisms

Federal Housing Advocate

Housing patterns and systemic issues nationally, including homelessness and encampments — this is the body behind the 2024 national report on encampments.

Try this first?—
DeadlineNone.
Can someone file for you?This is a systemic lever, not an individual-complaint channel — it monitors, advises, gathers submissions, and can ask the National Housing Council to convene a review panel. Individuals and groups can add their submission toward that kind of systemic review; it does not take or decide one person's individual case.

Still open — This is a correction from an earlier draft of this page's own research: the Advocate does not adjudicate individual complaints, even though it accepts submissions from individuals.

Source: Canadian Human Rights Commission, Final Report — Review of Encampments · checked 2026-08-10

CRA service complaints → Taxpayers' Ombudsperson

Problems with CRA service — plausibly including access to benefits like the Canada Child Benefit or GST credit, though not spelled out by name on the mandate page.

Try this first?Yes — file with CRA's own Service Feedback Program first.
DeadlineNot stated.
Can someone file for you?Yes — the Taxpayer Bill of Rights (Article 15) states the right to be “represented by a person of your choice.”

Still open — The current title is “Taxpayers' Ombudsperson,” not “Ombudsman” — a name that's changed recently.

Source: Canada Revenue Agency, Taxpayers' Ombudsperson — Submit a Complaint · checked 2026-08-10

Service Canada → Social Security Tribunal

Denied Employment Insurance (EI), Canada Pension Plan (CPP), or Old Age Security (OAS) benefits.

Try this first?Yes — request reconsideration first: 30 days for EI, 90 days for CPP and OAS.
DeadlineThen 90 days to appeal to the Social Security Tribunal after the reconsideration decision, across all three programs — with a 1-year hard cutoff for late appeals.

Still open — Whether someone can file or appeal on your behalf isn't addressed on the pages we checked.

Source: Social Security Tribunal of Canada · checked 2026-08-10

CHRC — Canadian Human Rights Commission

Discrimination in federally regulated services.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineWithin 12 months of the incident, with exceptions possible.
Can someone file for you?Yes — their own words: “You can file a complaint on behalf of others if you have their consent.”

Source: Canadian Human Rights Commission, File a Discrimination Complaint · checked 2026-08-10

OPC Canada — Office of the Privacy Commissioner

Privacy complaints, both federal (Privacy Act) and private-sector (PIPEDA).

Try this first?Varies.
DeadlineNot stated.
Can someone file for you?Yes — with a signed Authorization form; the representative can then “make binding decisions on my behalf with respect to the carriage of my complaint.”

Source: Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, Report a Concern · checked 2026-08-10

CRCC (not yet PCRC) — RCMP conduct

RCMP conduct only — not CBSA.

Try this first?No.
DeadlineNot stated.

Still open — A significant correction from an earlier draft: the renamed body (“PCRC,” under Bill C-20) received Royal Assent in October 2024 but has not come into force. As of April 2026 the body is still legally the Civilian Review and Complaints Commission (CRCC), still operating under that name, and still RCMP-only. CBSA coverage is expected once PCRC formally takes effect, not yet.

Source: CRCC — Civilian Review and Complaints Commission for the RCMP · checked 2026-08-10

Correctional Investigator (Office of the Correctional Investigator)

Federal corrections and prisons.

Try this first?Encouraged, not required — their own words: “We encourage you to try and resolve issues informally or through CSC's internal grievance process before contacting our Office.”
DeadlineNot stated.
Can someone file for you?Yes — an offender can file on behalf of another offender, or family and friends can, with a required written release designating them.

Source: Office of the Correctional Investigator, Make a Complaint · checked 2026-08-10

House of Commons e-petitions

Getting something formally onto the federal record.

Try this first?—
DeadlineThe sponsoring MP has 30 days to decide whether to present it; the government responds within 24 sitting days once it's presented.
Can someone file for you?You need a Member of Parliament to sponsor and present it — you cannot present one directly. The sponsor doesn't need to be your own riding's MP. A minimum of 500 signatures is required.

Source: House of Commons, e-Petitions · checked 2026-08-10

Left off this page, on purpose

Two real, existing bodies with little overlap with our base today — named here rather than silently left off: OBSI (banking and investment complaints, obsi.ca) and the Veterans Ombudsman (ombudsman-veterans.gc.ca — note the correct address, not veteransombudsman.ca). Both may be added if the demand shows up.

Getting help filing

Guiding you through any of these mechanisms and helping you prepare what you file is safe everywhere on this page — that's the same kind of plain-language, step-by-step help Ontario's own CLEO (Steps to Justice) already provides, unlicensed. Filing as your representative is different: Ontario's Law Society Act makes representing someone at a tribunal — the Human Rights Tribunal, the Landlord and Tenant Board, the Social Benefits Tribunal — licensed activity for a lawyer or paralegal, with only narrow exceptions. For those three, the right next step is a free legal clinic, not us or anyone unlicensed: your local community legal clinic, the Human Rights Legal Support Centre (for HRTO), the Advocacy Centre for Tenants Ontario (ACTO, for LTB matters), or Legal Aid Ontario. Ombudsman offices are different — most accept someone filing on your behalf with your signed consent, no licence required, and we say which ones confirm that above.

How this page was built

Every mechanism above was checked directly against the body's own website, not a directory or aggregator, on 2026-08-10. Where our own earlier research had something wrong — a deadline, a name, which body currently exists — it's corrected here with the receipt, not quietly fixed.
