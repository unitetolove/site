Families by Age: 0–2 · 3–5 · 6–12 · 13–17

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if it serves a Toronto child aged 0–17, or their parent/caregiver, because of the child's age or stage — and it is free, or explicitly labelled subsidized. General household help a family uses the same way anyone else would — food banks, rent help, utility assistance, a general mental-health walk-in clinic, a homeless-youth shelter — belongs to another guide in this fleet and gets a pointer here, not a repeated entry. This guide exists for one reason: you know your child's age and nothing else about what Toronto offers. Find your band below.

How this guide is organized: by the child's age — 0–2, 3–5, 6–12, 13–17 — plus one short cross-cutting section up front for the handful of things that apply at any age (the child benefits, the two recreation-fee mechanisms). Where a program actually spans several bands, it is placed once, in the band where it starts, with its real range stated plainly — so a CampTO listing that starts at age 4 sits in the 3–5 section but tells you it runs through age 16, rather than being copied into four sections or quietly narrowed to just one age.

Applies at any age: the child benefits and the two recreation-fee doors

Canada Child Benefit (CCB) Tax-free monthly payment per child under 18: up to $8,157/yr (under 6) or $6,883/yr (6–17) per child, for the July 2026–June 2027 benefit year, reduced above $38,237 adjusted family net income; plus up to $3,480/yr per child eligible for the Disability Tax Credit. No separate application — filing taxes and applying for the CCB is what starts it. canada.ca — Canada Child Benefit Who it's for: citizens, permanent residents, protected persons, or a temporary resident who has lived in Canada 18+ months with a valid permit — not active refugee claimants (the program's own page excludes anyone holding only a Refugee Protection Claimant Document). Requires ID and a filed tax return. *Reconfirmed 2026-07-31 directly against the CRA's "How much you can get" page: all figures above ($8,157 / $6,883 / $38,237 threshold / $3,480 disability top-up / the July 2026–June 2027 payment period) match exactly. No change.

Ontario Child Benefit (OCB) Tax-free monthly payment for low- to moderate-income families with children under 18. Ontario's own page currently states "up to $1,727 per child each year" — that page was last updated 2026-04-14. No separate application: applying for the CCB through the CRA automatically triggers an OCB assessment, but a family must file a tax return every year, even with no income, to keep receiving it. ontario.ca — Ontario Child Benefit Who it's for: any Ontario family that qualifies income-wise, any status the CCB itself accepts. CRA questions: 1-800-387-1193. Note on the figure: unlike the CCB's own page, Ontario's OCB page does not state a July–June benefit-year cycle for this amount — the $1,727 figure is exactly what ontario.ca states today, not a number this guide adjusted for a claimed cycle. Third-party finance sites cite higher figures ($1,760/yr) attributed to a "2026-2027" cycle; those numbers do not appear on ontario.ca itself and are not used here. *Reconfirmed 2026-07-31 by direct fetch: ontario.ca still states "up to $1,727 per child each year," page still stamped "Updated: April 14, 2026." The figure has not moved. Per this guide's own standing instruction, it is not being reconciled upward to any third-party number — see Flag 7.

Welcome Policy — Recreation Fee Subsidy An income-tested annual credit toward City of Toronto recreation programs, camps, and swim/sport instruction anywhere in the city: $683.04/yr per child or youth to age 24. Not automatic — you apply, though OW/ODSP/Child Care Fee Subsidy recipients skip the income-verification paperwork. Valid 12 months, does not carry over, must reapply. toronto.ca — Welcome Policy Who it's for: low-income Toronto households (tested against Low-Income Measure After-Tax, e.g. $30,255 for 1 person, $60,510 for 4 people). Apply online, by phone (social-assistance recipients only), or in person at Regent Park CC, Keele Community Hub, or Scarborough Civic Centre. *Reconfirmed 2026-07-31 by direct fetch: $683.04 (child/youth to 24) and $314.60 (adult 25+) unchanged; page now stamped "Date modified: June 24, 2026." No change.

Canadian Tire Jumpstart Charities — Individual Child Grant New in v0.3 — a second recreation-fee door, distinct from Welcome Policy above. A grant of up to $300/child/year covering registration and equipment costs for a sport or physical-activity program (minimum five weeks, at least one session a week; a sports camp must run at least five consecutive days). This is a funding mechanism, not a turnkey program: the money is paid directly to the service organization running the activity (the "payee"), never to the family, and the family is the one who has to find and secure the actual program spot. jumpstart.canadiantire.ca — Individual Child Grants Who it's for: children ages 4–18 from families meeting Revenue Canada's Low-Income Cut-Off (LICO) threshold. Proof of need is a current Canada Child Benefit statement; alternative proof is accepted case-by-case for recent immigrants, people fleeing spousal abuse, and similar circumstances. Apply online — a Jumpstart account login is required. Verified 2026-08-01 by direct fetch of the provider's own page: "Individual Child Grants may no longer be available in some Chapters" is stated in exactly those words, with instructions to log into your Jumpstart account to confirm local-chapter availability before applying — coverage is genuinely not guaranteed in every Toronto-area chapter, matching the flag already carried in the atlas row. New finding this review, not previously in the atlas row: the same page states Jumpstart "is not currently accepting new payee registrations" — if the organization delivering your child's program isn't already a registered Jumpstart payee, this grant may not be usable for that program regardless of whether your chapter is otherwise accepting applications. The 2026 cycle is confirmed open today ("The Individual Child Grant program is now open for 2026"). Subsidized, not free.

City of Toronto Free Centres A different door from Welcome Policy, and worth knowing about even if you don't qualify for that subsidy: at 38 named community/recreation centres across all four districts (12 in Etobicoke/York, 6 in North York, 10 in Scarborough, 10 in Toronto/East York), every City-delivered registered and drop-in program is free for every age group — no income test at all. This covers CampTO camps and registered swim/skate lessons when offered at one of these centres. Named centres on the provider's own page include Chalkfarm, Driftwood, Regent Park, Scadding Court, Wellesley, Malvern, and Oakdale, spanning all four districts — the full 38-name list with addresses is on the page linked below, and it's worth checking directly: which specific centre is free is the fact that actually matters, not the general idea that some centres are. Registering at a Free Centre still means creating a recreation account and registering for the program like anywhere else — spots can still fill and waitlist. toronto.ca — Free & Lower-Cost Recreation Options Who it's for: anyone registering at one of the 38 named centres, no income test. Separately, free drop-in leisure swimming and public leisure skating (no lesson, no registration) are free citywide at every City pool and rink, not just these centres — that's a standing City amenity, not tied to this list. *CORRECTED 2026-07-31: this guide's own text previously read "roughly 30" named centres in three places in this guide (this section, the CampTO entry, and the 13–17 band's intro paragraph). A direct fetch of the provider's page today counted the list by district — 12 + 6 + 10 + 10 = 38 — matching the atlas row (FT-1006) exactly. All three mentions are corrected below. See Flag 4.

Free flu shots (Universal Influenza Immunization Program) Free annual flu vaccine for anyone 6 months and older who lives, works or studies in Ontario, at primary care offices, pharmacies and public health clinics. ontario.ca — Universal Influenza Immunization Program Who it's for: any Ontario resident 6 months+. A health card isn't strictly required, though some providers may ask for one; proof of living/working/studying in Ontario can substitute.

0–2: pregnancy through toddlerhood

Toronto Public Health — Prenatal and Parenting Programs Free group-based prenatal programs — the Canada Prenatal Nutrition Program and the Young Parent Prenatal Program — plus virtual parenting workshops. No OHIP card needed. (Healthy Babies Healthy Children, delivered on the same page, has its own entry directly below with its own eligibility band.) toronto.ca — Prenatal Programs Who it's for: anyone pregnant or newly parenting in Toronto — explicitly open regardless of OHIP or immigration status. Register by calling 416-338-7600 or via TPH's eChat with a Public Health Nurse.

Healthy Babies Healthy Children (HBHC) Free, confidential, voluntary home visiting from a Public Health Nurse and/or Family Home Visitor, from pregnancy up to the child's 3rd birthday — pregnancy/birth support, bonding, growth and development, feeding, and links to other services. No OHIP card required. toronto.ca — Healthy Babies Healthy Children Who it's for: anyone pregnant or parenting a child 3 or younger in Toronto, plus at least one added circumstance (newcomer with a first baby here, parenting questions, feeling unsupported, a health concern, or a difficult life situation). Self-refer by calling 416-338-7600, emailing PublicHealth@toronto.ca, or via eChat; a regulated provider can also refer you online. Distinct from Nurse-Family Partnership below, which is narrower and ends a year earlier.

Nurse-Family Partnership (Toronto Public Health) Free, voluntary, more intensive home visiting from a Public Health Nurse, from pregnancy until the child turns two. No OHIP required. toronto.ca — Prenatal Programs Who it's for: a narrower group than HBHC above — age 21 or under, under 26 weeks pregnant, a first-time parent, and experiencing financial concerns (including OW). Register the same way as HBHC: 416-338-7600, email, or eChat.

Toronto Public Health Breastfeeding Clinics Free individual breastfeeding/chestfeeding consultations with a Public Health Nurse or hospital lactation staff at 10 sites across Toronto, including baby weigh-ins; also free phone, video-call and eChat infant-feeding support. toronto.ca — Breastfeeding Clinics Who it's for: anyone in Toronto with a baby. Most TPH sites require booking ahead (no walk-ins); some hospital sites allow a call-ahead walk-in.

The New Mom Project Free new-item baby bundles (diapers, wipes, toiletries, swaddle blankets) shipped home for parents with a baby up to 3 months old; a separate Graco-partnered "Travel Bundle" provides a free infant car seat for newborns 0–6 months. newmomproject.ca Who it's for: referral-only, through a partner agency — a midwife, doctor, nurse, social worker, shelter, community health centre or Toronto Public Health. Not a direct public application; supply is limited and "subject to availability."

SickKids Free Car Seat Education Appointments A free one-on-one appointment with a certified Child Passenger Safety technician to learn how to correctly install and use your child's car seat — a weekly outdoor clinic at SickKids on University Ave, rain or shine. cpsac.org — SickKids listing Who it's for: anyone; book online via the CPSAC event listing. This is installation/use education, not a free car-seat giveaway — see New Mom Project above for that.

Diaper Bank of Toronto Free diapers for low-income families. The row does not state a specific age band — diapers are in practice an infant/toddler need, which is why this entry sits here, but the provider's own page doesn't itself draw an age line. thediaperbank.ca Who it's for: low-income families, but only through a partner agency — the Diaper Bank does not take requests directly from individuals. Ask a shelter, family resource centre, or community health centre whether they're a partner. No public phone number is published; contact is by email or web form only. *Reconfirmed 2026-07-31: the current homepage still states no age band anywhere — this remains an honest gap, not narrowed and not invented. See Flag 3.

EarlyON Child and Family Centres Free drop-in programs for children birth to 6 and their caregivers — play-based learning, parent support, and referrals. No membership or registration fee. toronto.ca — Child and Family Programs Who it's for: any family, any status, citywide — 100% free, no registration, just drop in. Runs through delivery partners including Indigenous-led sites, a 2SLGBTQ+ stream (Glitterbug Mobile EarlyON via The 519), a Deaf/hard-of-hearing stream (Rumball EarlyON), and Francophone sites. Exact hours vary by location — the City's own page says to contact each site directly. Relevant through the 3–5 band too, since it runs to age 6.

Canada-Wide Early Learning and Child Care (CWELCC) Fee Reduction Reduced base child-care fees at enrolled licensed providers, currently capped at $22/day for children under 6 (the eventual federal target is $10/day, not yet in effect). Applies automatically at CWELCC-enrolled centres — find one via the City's "Find a Child Care Near You" locator. toronto.ca — CWELCC Who it's for: any family using a CWELCC-enrolled licensed child-care provider, ages 0–5 (under 6). A separate, deeper, income-tested Child Care Fee Subsidy exists on top of this: 416-338-8888. *Reconfirmed 2026-07-31: the City's page still states "As of January 1, 2025, child care fees are capped at $22 per day" — the $10/day federal target is still not in effect. No change. See Flag 9.

TPL Ready for Reading (Baby Time, Toddler Time, Preschool Time) Free early-literacy drop-in and registered programs at Toronto Public Library branches citywide — Baby Time (birth–18 months), Toddler Time (19 months–3 years), and Preschool Time (3–5 years) — songs, rhymes, stories and play, plus a 24/7 Dial-a-Story line in 16 languages. tpl.ca — Ready for Reading Who it's for: any family, any TPL branch. Some sessions are drop-in, others require registration — check your branch. Dial-a-Story: 416-395-5400. Runs through age 5, so it's also the 3–5 band's early-literacy answer — no need to look elsewhere for that.

TTC Kids Ride Free (12 and under) Free TTC rides — subway, streetcar, bus — for any child 12 and under, no fare or card needed. ttc.ca — Fares and Passes Who it's for: any child 12 and under in Toronto. An optional Child Proof-of-Age photo ID is available for kids who look older than they are. Covers the whole 0–2, 3–5 and 6–12 range — it just stops being true the day a child turns 13.

Dixon Hall Music School Individual and group music lessons on a sliding scale, $4–$22/lesson based on financial assessment, for ages 4 and up; adapted lessons and music therapy for people with disabilities. dixonhall.org — Music School Who it's for: primarily Regent Park, Moss Park and St. Lawrence — but any Toronto address is eligible for the adapted-lessons/music-therapy stream. Subsidized, not free; sliding-scale fee applies.

TPH Catch-Up Immunization Clinics (JK–Grade 12) Free, publicly funded vaccination for the nine shots required under Ontario's Immunization of School Pupils Act, at Toronto Public Health community clinics, for students from junior kindergarten through grade 12. No OHIP card needed — families without insurance can also be directed to a free clinic for uninsured people. toronto.ca — Report Student Vaccination Who it's for: any student JK–12 (roughly ages 4–17) in Toronto — this entry sits here because JK is where it starts, but it runs the whole way to grade 12. Book via the provider's page or call 416-338-7600 (option 2).

13–17: teenagers

Honest gap, stated plainly: this is the thinnest band in the atlas, and it's also the age where a parent has the least idea what already exists — most recreation and tutoring programs in this guide are built around younger children and simply stop around age 12. What follows are the genuinely free, teen-specific doors this atlas currently has. There is no free (not merely subsidized) teen camp option outside the 38 Free Centres listed above — CampTO's own Youth tier (13–16) is fee-based everywhere else. There is no dedicated free mental-health or counselling entry in this guide by design: that need is covered in Guide 11 Mental Health and Addiction and Guide 01 In Crisis Now, not here — this guide sticks to recreation, learning and family-support programs organized by age, and a teen in crisis shouldn't have to find their way through a recreation guide to get there.

This guide records 13–17 as its own reasonable estimate for "teen," not a page-stated fact — don't read it as a hard cutoff in either direction. Each site's Youth Recreation Programmer contact is listed on the page; no single citywide phone number applies. *Reconfirmed 2026-07-31 by direct fetch: still no single stated age band for Youth Spaces overall — the only numeric sub-program bands on today's page remain SEEED (12–15) and the Building Skills Through Recreation employment stream (16–24), exactly as before.
