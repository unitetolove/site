Utilities & Bills: Electricity, Gas, Water, Property Tax & Home Energy Help

If a hydro, gas, water, or property tax bill is the thing keeping you up at night — this page is for that. Selection rule: a resource is included here only if its own subject is a utility, energy, water/waste, or property-tax bill (or a home upgrade meant to shrink one), it is status: ACTIVE in the atlas, and it isn't already the better-homed subject of another guide in this fleet. Broad, multi-purpose benefit programs (tax credits, discretionary funds covering many unrelated expenses) are cited only for the narrow slice of them that is actually about a utility bill — the rest of what they cover is catalogued elsewhere and pointed to, not repeated.

If you're behind on a bill, or about to be disconnected

Low-income Energy Assistance Program (LEAP) emergency grant A one-time emergency grant paid directly to your utility — not to you — to stop an electricity or natural-gas disconnection: up to $650 for electricity ($780 if you heat with electricity) and up to $650 for natural gas. oeb.ca — Low-income Energy Assistance Program Who it's for: Ontario households in arrears or facing disconnection, income-tested on the same table as OESP below. ID, proof of income, proof of address, and your disconnection notice (if you have one) may be requested. Apply through a designated LEAP intake agency, not directly through your utility.

Emergency Energy Fund (EEF) Help to reconnect service, prevent a disconnection, or pay down arrears on your hydro, gas, or oil bill. Call the Neighbourhood Information Post at 647-727-8988, Monday–Friday 9am–4:30pm. toronto.ca — Emergency Energy Fund Who it's for: low-income Toronto residents who are NOT currently on Ontario Works or ODSP — if you are, ask your caseworker about the equivalent benefit instead. Apply directly by phone — the provider's own page describes no separate referral step.

If your electricity bill is just too high every month

Ontario Electricity Support Program (OESP) An ongoing monthly credit applied straight to your electricity bill: $45–$75/month depending on household size and income, or $52–$113/month for Indigenous households, households that heat with electricity, or households using electricity-intensive medical devices. oeb.ca — Ontario Electricity Support Program Who it's for: income-tested by household size. OW and ODSP recipients automatically qualify — apply through your caseworker instead of the general application. ID and proof of income (or CRA consent to verify it) requested for everyone else.

If you live in Toronto Community Housing and pay your own electric heat

Electric Heating Relief Program An automatic hydro-bill credit for rent-geared-to-income (RGI) Toronto Community Housing tenants who have electric heat and pay their own hydro bill. No application — TCHC identifies eligible households and pays the credit straight to Toronto Hydro. Separate from OESP above; you can have both. torontohousing.ca — Energy Relief Program Who it's for: RGI TCHC tenants with electric heat, paying their own hydro. Automatic — no application, no documents.

If you're on Ontario Works or ODSP

ODSP Discretionary Benefit for Low-Cost Energy-Conservation Measures A one-time payment of up to $50 for ODSP recipients toward low-cost home energy items — caulking/weatherstripping, hot-water pipe or tank insulation, low-flow showerhead/faucet aerators, efficient bulbs, a clothesline, motion-sensor lighting — with your caseworker's prior approval. ontario.ca — ODSP discretionary benefits directive Who it's for: ODSP recipients who own and live in the unit, or rent where the landlord isn't responsible for the item. One-time, not recurring — ask your caseworker.

Ontario Works has a parallel piece of its broader discretionary-benefits list: a one-time $50 allowance for home energy-conservation supplies, plus separate money toward smoke alarms and CO detectors — ask your OW caseworker directly. The rest of that discretionary-benefits list (eyeglasses, emergency dental, medical transport, diet allowance, and more) isn't a utility expense and is catalogued in full in Guide 02: Claim Everything, not repeated here.

If you want to cut your energy bill for good — free home upgrades

Enbridge Home Winterproofing Program (HWP) A free home energy assessment, plus free insulation and draft-proofing, for income-qualifying households whose primary heat is natural gas (furnace, boiler, or gas fireplace) — a smart thermostat is added at no extra cost if you receive one of those upgrades. Call 1-844-770-3148. enbridgegas.com — Home Winterproofing Program Who it's for: income-qualifying Ontario households heated by natural gas. Electrically-heated households apply directly to the Energy Affordability Program below instead. Apartments/condos of four or more units and mobile homes without a permanent foundation don't qualify for this specific program. Proof of income (or a benefit statement) requested. Now coordinated with the Energy Affordability Program below into one shared application.

Save on Energy — Energy Affordability Program (EAP) Free energy-saving upgrades for income-eligible households: an LED lighting kit for the moderate-income tier, or a full in-home assessment with appliance, insulation, and heat-pump replacement for the most-in-need tier. Apply online at saveonenergy.ca/EAP or call the same 1-844-770-3148 line as the Enbridge program above. saveonenergy.ca — Energy Affordability Program Who it's for: income-qualifying Ontario households, two tiers depending on income/household size or receipt of a qualifying benefit. Proof of income requested.

Save on Energy (IESO) online tools — discovery tool, not a benefit itself. A free hub linking out to the Home Renovation Savings Program, the Peak Perks smart-thermostat incentive, the Energy Affordability Program above, and a "My Energy Action Plan" planning tool. Useful as a starting search point; the individual programs it links to are the actual benefits, and only the two above are independently verified rows in this atlas. saveonenergy.ca Who it's for: any Ontario resident. Free to use; the programs it links to have their own separate eligibility rules.

Property tax, water, and solid-waste bill relief

City of Toronto Property Tax & Water/Utility Rebate Programs Three linked programs: Property Tax Increase Cancellation, Property Tax Increase Deferral, and a Water/Solid Waste Rebate. Deadline to apply for the 2026 tax year: November 2, 2026 — you must reapply every year even if you received relief last year. toronto.ca — Property Tax and Utility Relief Program Who it's for: combined household income $62,000 or less, AND either receiving a disability benefit, age 65+, age 60–64 with GIS/Spouse's Allowance, or age 50+ with a registered pension (the age-50+-with-pension route applies to Deferral and the Water/Waste Rebate, not Cancellation). Must reapply every year with your CRA Notice of Assessment and, where applicable, proof of age or of your disability benefit.

Every Ontario tax filer: a credit that offsets energy and property-tax costs

Ontario Trillium Benefit (OEPTC + Ontario Sales Tax Credit) A combined tax-free payment for the July 2026–June 2027 benefit year: the Ontario Energy and Property Tax Credit, up to $1,307/yr (age 18–64) or $1,488/yr (65+), toward property tax and the energy portion of sales tax, plus the Ontario Sales Tax Credit, up to $378/yr per person (you, your spouse, and each dependent child under 19). The Northern Ontario Energy Credit component does not apply to Toronto residents. ontario.ca — Ontario Trillium Benefit Who it's for: Ontario tax filers of any income (the amount phases down as income rises). Claimed by completing Form ON-BEN on your tax return — no separate application or documents.

Not what you needed?

If it's your phone or internet bill, not a utility bill, that's the problem — that's this fleet's Phone & Internet guide (fleet #5). It hasn't been built yet as of this draft; coming in a later wave, nothing to link to yet.

If it's your rent, a security deposit, or an eviction — not a utility bill — that's the problem, that help lives in this fleet's Housing guide (Guide 07, fleet #3), not here, including the Toronto Rent Bank and the Housing Stabilization Fund.

Honest gaps

  • No general low-income water or solid-waste rebate for renters or working-age adults. The only water/solid-waste relief in this atlas (above) is bundled into the City's property-tax program and gated to seniors, people on disability benefits, or 50+ pensioners under the $62,000 income line — a working-age renter with a low income and a high water/garbage cost has no atlas-listed door for that specific cost on its own.
  • No ongoing monthly natural-gas bill credit equivalent to OESP. OESP (above) is electricity-only. LEAP (above) covers natural gas, but only as a one-time emergency-arrears grant, not an ongoing monthly discount the way OESP works for electricity.
