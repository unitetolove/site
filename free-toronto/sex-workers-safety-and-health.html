Sex Workers' Safety & Health

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if a sex worker in Toronto can use it without having to stop working and without being treated as a victim by default, and it is free or explicitly labelled subsidized. This rule cut a real number of well-known, well-funded services this wave — organizations doing real work, built around the premise that the person in front of them needs to leave sex work in order to be helped. A service someone cannot use without agreeing to stop working is not a service to them. Every candidate considered and every cut, with its reason, is in this lane's build report.

Four facts decide whether someone reading this actually walks through a door. They come first, before anything else, because everything below depends on them:

  • Non-judgmental and unconditional. Where a provider says this in its own words, this guide quotes it. Nothing below asks you to justify, explain, or apologize for your work.
  • No ID required, where the row states it — and where a row is silent on this, this guide says so rather than guessing either way.
  • No police involvement. Nothing below routes through, requires, or reports to police as a condition of service.
  • Safe without immigration status, where the provider's own page states it. Where a provider's page doesn't say so, this guide does not claim a no-status-check policy it never made — silence is not the same as a promise, and guessing wrong in either direction costs someone real safety.

What you will not find here. You may be offered help elsewhere that requires you to agree to stop doing sex work, or that treats you as a trafficking victim by default. Those programs exist, and some of them do real good for the specific people they're built for — but they are not built for a sex worker who is not being trafficked and is not looking to exit, and this guide does not include them for that reason. If you or someone you know is being trafficked, coerced, or held against your will, that is a different situation and a different guide — Human Trafficking — and conflating the two is exactly the harm this split was made to avoid.

Things you may have been told about that are gone

This section exists because relying on a dead tool for safety information is worse than knowing it's dead. If you were pointed toward any of the following, here is what this lane found, checked directly against each one's own current page or presence, on 2026-08-01.

  • baddatelist.com is gone. This is the URL that outside sources — safety guides in other cities, reference articles — still cite as Toronto's centralized "bad date list" for reporting dangerous clients. As of today it resolves to a parked, for-sale domain page. It is not a stale site still limping along; it is not there. Use Maggie's Toronto instead (below) — Maggie's own current page confirms it keeps its own up-to-date Bad Date safety lists, in physical and virtual copies, as part of its regular programming. The function has not disappeared; the independently branded tool the wider internet still points people toward has.
  • Sex Professionals of Canada (SPOC) — its website still loads, but every dated item on it is from 2013–2014. No current activity, event, or contact update was found. Treat it as dormant.
  • Work Safe Twerk Safe, a real, named, by-and-for Toronto stripper organization founded in 2019, shows no posted events for the current month and no dated activity since November 2024 — over a year and a half stale. It has not posted a closure notice, so this guide does not call it closed, but the signal is too weak to send someone there expecting an active response.
  • Sex Workers Alliance of Toronto (SWAT) does not appear to exist as an independent, contactable organization anymore. It survives only as a phone-number-only listing on Toronto Rape Crisis Centre's own (live, current) resource page — no independent site or activity record was found newer than a very old directory listing.

If you want support from people who have done this work

Peer-led means run by people with the lived experience, not just delivered to them — and it is used differently for exactly that reason. These two organizations are this guide's anchors.

Maggie's Toronto — Harm Reduction, Drop-Ins & Case Management for Sex Workers (peer-led) Free safer-sex and safer-drug-use supplies, including free home delivery anywhere in Toronto; three weekly peer drop-ins plus specialized Indigenous, trans/non-binary and male-sex-worker groups; monthly workshops; short- and long-term case management (life, financial, family planning); and up-to-date Bad Date safety lists — the current, working version of the tool named above. maggiestoronto.org Call 416-964-0150. Peer-led — run for and by current and former sex workers, on a self-disclosure/trust-based model. No proof of sex work is required and there is no exit condition: open to anyone currently or previously doing sex work. Address not publicized, for community safety — call for the current drop-in location and hours (the row notes the main office was undergoing renovations as of its last verification; confirm the location before referring someone in person). Free.

Butterfly — Asian and Migrant Sex Worker Support Network (peer-led) Free crisis intervention and a hotline, health support and referrals, counselling and emotional support, outreach, capacity-building and leadership training, and legal-rights workshops — in English, Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and Thai. No referral required. butterflysw.org Call 416-906-3098 or email cswbutterfly@gmail.com. Peer-led — by and for Asian and migrant sex workers. Safe without immigration status: explicitly serves undocumented sex workers, no status check. Free. Based in Toronto; also connects migrant sex workers to support in Vancouver, Montreal, Saskatchewan, Winnipeg and Manitoba.

If you need health care today, with nothing to prove

Four programs across three community health centres run walk-in or drop-in sessions that state plainly, in their own words, that current sex work is not a barrier — one of them newly confirmed by this guide's seal pass.

Regent Park Community Health Centre — Sex Worker Drop-In A weekly drop-in explicitly open, in the provider's own words, to "women, trans and non-binary people who have engaged in sex work in the past and/or who are currently engaged in sex work" — current engagement is stated as eligible, not just former. On-the-spot clinical services, social-work support and referrals, and snacks. No appointment or intake. regentparkchc.org 465 Dundas St E. Thursdays 10am–12pm. Contact: Nicole Morency, NicoleM@regentparkchc.org, 416-364-2261 ext. 2236. Not peer-led — run by CHC clinical and social-work staff — but explicitly non-judgmental and unconditional in the provider's own words: current sex work is not treated as something to stop before you can be served. No statement about immigration status was found on the provider's own page, so this guide does not claim a no-status- check policy for it. Free.

Sherbourne Health — WINK (Women in Need 'Klinik') A Wednesday-morning drop-in: a hot breakfast, one-to-one primary care and crisis support from an interdisciplinary health team, harm-reduction supplies, goal planning and group health-promotion activities, in a trans-inclusive space. sherbourne.on.ca 333 Sherbourne St. Wednesdays 9–11am. Contact: Jenn, 416-324-4100 ext. 3317. Worth knowing before you go: WINK opened in 2010 as a drop-in built for sex workers; the provider's own page says it has since become a mixed room — homeless and under-housed women, and trans and Indigenous women, now also attend. Sex workers are still explicitly named as served, but expect a shared room, not a sex-worker-specific space. Not peer-led. No statement about immigration status was found on this page. Free.

South Riverdale Community Health Centre — Walk-In Wellness Hub Monday–Friday walk-in access to health care, harm-reduction supplies, support and referrals. The provider's own words: "a harm reduction-based, sex work-positive, and judgment-free space." srchc.ca At the Oakridge Health and Harm Reduction Hub, Cataraqui Recreation Centre, 52 Cataraqui Cres — an East Toronto Health Partners satellite site, not SRCHC's main building. Monday–Friday, 10am–2pm. Contact: Kelly Wu, 437-770-9073. No health card, family doctor or medical referral required — the provider's own words, and the clearest no-status-barrier statement found this wave. Not peer-led. Free.

South Riverdale Community Health Centre — Women's Harm Reduction Drop-in (formerly COUNTERfit Women's Harm Reduction Program) Renamed and rescheduled in SRCHC's mid-2026 site restructure — same site, same door. The provider's own words: "For women (trans inclusive) who use drugs, live with mental illness, and/or have experience with sex work." A weekly drop-in to share a meal, connect with others, and take part in different programming each week, with health and social supports available during the drop-in and guest speakers on other services. srchc.ca 955 Queen St E — SRCHC's main building, a different site from the Walk-In Wellness Hub's Oakridge Hub location above. Tuesdays 12–2pm (changed from Tuesday mornings 9–11:30am). Contact: Dahli Clarke-Partida, Dclarke-Partida@srchc.com — the previously listed contact (Rhiannon Thomas, ext. 2388) no longer appears. Not peer-led — a CHC-run program, not by-and-for sex workers. The former page's "sex-work positive" self-description and its nurse-practitioner-on-site detail do not appear on the successor page and are no longer claimed; the current page does name sex-work experience in who the drop-in is for. No statement about ID, police involvement or immigration status appears, so none is claimed either way. Free (cost as verified 2026-08-01; the successor page states no fee either way). (2026-08-04 link-rot correction: old COUNTERfit program URL 404s; successor fetched today.)

If you need to understand your legal rights

HIV Legal Network — Sex Work Legal Information Free, plain-language and legal-brief resources on the criminal law, municipal bylaws (including body-rub-parlour and personal-service-establishment licensing), and rights affecting sex workers in Canada — developed in consultation with organizations run by and for sex workers. hivlegalnetwork.ca 1240 Bay Street, Suite 600, Toronto; 416-595-1666. This is legal information, not a walk-in legal clinic — the provider's own page does not describe individual case intake or one-on-one legal advice, so treat this as a way to understand your rights, not a substitute for a lawyer. Free. Canada-wide, headquartered in Toronto.

Butterfly (above) also runs legal-rights workshops as part of its broader support — see its entry above rather than a repeated one here.

General services that also serve you, but weren't built for you

The five services above were built with sex workers specifically in mind, or say so in their own words. The services below were not — they are general-population services whose own pages happen to name sex workers among the people they serve, or that serve a closely related need (status-free care, low-barrier drop-in). They are listed here as pointers, not repeated in full, because duplicating a fact doesn't make it more true — each has its complete entry in the guide that is actually its home.

  • Toronto Public Health — Sexual Health Clinics. Free STI/HIV testing and treatment, birth control counselling, emergency contraception, and free condoms and lube — no OHIP or referral required. The City's own page names "people involved in sex work" explicitly among the people these clinics serve, alongside people without a health card, recent immigrants and refugees, and 2SLGBTQ+ people. toronto.ca — Sexual Health Clinics

  • Sistering — 24/7 Low-Barrier Drop-In. Hot meals, showers, laundry, an onsite clinic, harm reduction and crisis counselling, for women and gender-diverse people — 24/7 at its 962 Bloor St W location only. The provider's own page names sex workers explicitly among the people it serves, alongside people without legal status. Safe without immigration status. sistering.org

  • Blue Door Clinic. Free HIV primary care built specifically for people who are uninsured or precariously insured — not gated by sex-work status, but a real door if immigration status, not sex work, is what's standing between you and HIV care. Full entry in Guide 26, Living with HIV. caseyhouse.ca — Blue Door Clinic

  • Hassle Free Clinic. Anonymous HIV/STI testing (no health card required) and a same-clinic PrEP/PEP program — general population, not sex-work-specific on its own page, but a strong door for the occupational sexual-health needs many sex workers rely on. Full entry in Guide 26, Living with HIV. hasslefreeclinic.org

What we did not find

Stated plainly rather than stretched over with a general service that doesn't actually fit:

  • No Black-specific sex-worker organization. Black CAP's outreach programs are gated by HIV-risk category, not by sex-work status; no dedicated Black sex-worker organization was found.
  • No independent Indigenous-specific sex-worker organization. Maggie's own Indigenous drop-in group (cited above, inside its entry) is the only Indigenous-specific door found in this domain; 2-Spirited People of the 1st Nations is active and Indigenous/Two-Spirit-specific but is not gated by sex-work status, and is already homed elsewhere in this atlas.
  • No dedicated sex-worker housing program. Sistering's low-barrier drop-in (above) is the closest available door, and it is a general drop-in, not sex-work-exclusive housing.
  • No second confirmed peer-led organization beyond Maggie's and Butterfly. Every other candidate found across both waves — Sex Professionals of Canada, Work Safe Twerk Safe, the Sex Workers Alliance of Toronto — was dormant, dead, or unconfirmable (see "Things you may have been told about that are gone," above), reconfirmed as such by this guide's seal pass. This guide's one newly-confirmed program this wave, COUNTERfit Women's Harm Reduction Program, is not peer-led, so it adds a health-care door without closing this gap. This may be a real feature of a small, precariously funded field, not a research gap that a future pass will simply close.
  • No independent safety or bad-date tool beyond what Maggie's runs itself. No Toronto-specific safety app, SMS alert, or independent warning-list tool was found operating.

Not in this guide — cross-guide boundary

One fact, one home:

  • Human trafficking — see the separate Human Trafficking guide. Kept separate on harm grounds: treating sex work as inherently trafficking is a documented harm the serving organizations named in this guide themselves reject.
  • General HIV testing, treatment and HIV-specific housing or peer support — Guide 26 Living with HIV, pointer only, except where a service (like Blue Door Clinic and Hassle Free Clinic, above) is cited here for a fact specific to this guide's reader.
  • General 2SLGBTQ2S+ community and identity services (not sex-work-specific) — Guide 21 LGBTQ2S+, pointer only.
  • General harm reduction (needles/syringes, naloxone, drug checking, supervised consumption) — Guide 11 Mental Health and Addiction, pointer only.
  • General emergency shelter and the housing system — Guide 07 Housing, pointer only.
