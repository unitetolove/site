Caring for Someone Else

If you're reading this, you're probably not looking after yourself — you're looking after someone else, and you may never have used the word "caregiver" to describe it. Maybe it's an ageing parent. A disabled child. A partner who's ill. A friend with no one else. You may not think of what you do as a job, a role, or something you're "entitled to get help with" — you just do it. This guide is written for you, not for the person you're caring for. Their services live in other guides (Seniors, Disability, Mental Health, Families by Age) — this one is only about what helps you stay standing.

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if it helps the person doing the caring — not the person being cared for — and it is free or explicitly labelled subsidized, in Toronto (or reachable from Toronto). A service that helps the person you care for, even a very good one, does not belong here — it gets a pointer at most, in the section near the bottom of this guide. That rule cut a real number of near-misses: friendly-visiting and phone-companionship programs for isolated seniors (they serve the senior, not you), several transportation and meals programs (same reason), bereavement supports for someone who has died (that's a different guide, not yet built — named below), and two child-benefit programs that are ordinary parenting support rather than caregiving in the sense this guide means.

A fact worth stating plainly, because it changes who thinks this guide is for them: almost nothing below gates on your age, your income, or your legal relationship to the person you care for. The eligibility test in nearly every entry in this guide is about the person you're caring for — their age, their diagnosis, their needs — not about you. If you've assumed you don't qualify for help because you're "just" a friend or neighbour, because you're young, or because you're not struggling financially, that assumption is usually wrong. Check the specific entry, but the safe default is to call.

FT-9212, FT-9619 and FT-9716 are excluded from this guide. None of the three is a caregiver-specific row (Somali settlement services, Deaf/hard-of-hearing services, and a homeless-shelter meal/respite program), so none was a candidate for this guide in the first place.

If you don't know where to start

Ontario Caregiver Organization — Caregiver Helpline Call 1-833-416-2273, free, 24/7, staffed by real people, with interpretation available in 150 languages upon request. They can connect you to respite options, local support groups, and information about the person you care for — plus free counselling/coaching webinars, peer support, e-learning, and dedicated resources for 2SLGBTQIA+, Black, Indigenous and young caregivers. This is the single most general door in this guide: if nothing else below fits your situation, start here. ontariocaregiver.ca/help/helpline Unpaid caregivers (family, friend or neighbour) of anyone with a health condition, disability or aging-related need, anywhere in Ontario. No documents required. Free — funded by the Government of Ontario. Reconfirmed live on the provider's own page, 2026-07-31. (Also a full entry in Guide 11 Mental Health and Addiction, from the counselling-services angle — this guide and that one both treat it as core, not duplicated by accident.)

Respite — getting actual time off

This is the section that matters most in this guide, because it is the thing caregivers run out of first and the hardest thing to find. Read the constraint on each entry below before you call — several of these doors are real but not always open, and a caregiver who calls a closed door and gives up will not call the next one. Be honest with yourself about which of these you can actually reach: one respite program in this list is not in Toronto at all.

The short version, so you don't have to read six entries to find it out: of the six doors below, most charge something, one of the three adult day programs is not physically in Toronto, and none can promise a free, same-week block of time off in Toronto proper. That said, this verification pass found that two of the three adult day programs now publish an actual daily rate you can budget for before you pick up the phone (SPRINT Senior Care at $31.00/day and Baycrest at $50/day — both inside Toronto); the third, Circle of Care's, still does not publish its rate anywhere online, even after this review checked a second, different kind of page specifically looking for it. Read on for the specifics, and see the honest assessment at the end of this section before you decide where to call first.

Circle of Care — Caregiver Support Services Free: short-term case management and system navigation, caregiver support groups, individual counselling and future-care planning, and hospice/bereavement companion visits. Separately subsidized, not free: Personal Support (PSW) hours and the Adult Day Program (next entry), which this same organization also offers as caregiver-relief options — do not read this as "everything Circle of Care offers is free." No documents required for the free components; no age restriction stated for the caregiver. circleofcare.com/caregivers Caregivers of an aging loved one, Toronto and York Region. No documents required for the free components. (Reconfirmed live, direct fetch, 2026-07-31 — matches Guide 20's identical live-fetch finding the same day; no disagreement between the two guides on this row.)

Circle of Care — Adult Day Program A 7-day-a-week supervised day program (9:30am–3:30pm, lunch included; extended hours Wednesdays until 6pm at additional cost) for people with memory loss and/or physical frailty — gentle exercise, art/music therapy, meditation and social activities, explicitly meant to give family caregivers a break. The physical site — 9600 Bathurst Street, Unit 4 — is in Vaughan, Ontario, not Toronto, even though Circle of Care markets it to its Toronto/GTA caregiver base; confirm this location and the transport it takes to get there before counting on it. Transportation is offered within South of Major Mackenzie, North of Finch, West of Bayview and East of Dufferin — a catchment spanning both Vaughan and North York — and participants from outside that area are still welcome if they can get there another way. Government-subsidized, not free — families pay a portion of the daily cost, and the actual rate is not published anywhere on the provider's page. This seal pass checked a second, different kind of page (a blog explainer about the program, not the program page itself) specifically looking for the number — it still is not published there either. Call (905) 738-1128 to ask what you would actually pay. circleofcare.com/adp No explicit numeric age eligibility stated for the core program (criteria are diagnosis- and function-based: memory loss, physical frailty, managed incontinence, behaviour manageable in a group setting, no severe dietary restrictions). Subsidized, price unknown until you call. (Reconfirmed live 2026-07-31 by both Guide 20's seal pass and this seal lane, via two different pages on the provider's own site — the rate is genuinely not published anywhere Circle of Care has put it online.)

Ontario Health atHome — Home Care Respite (in-home PSW hours) Once a care coordinator assesses and approves the needs of the person you care for, publicly funded personal support worker hours and other home-care services are delivered in the home at no direct cost to your family — hours in your home that are hours you get back. Call 1-833-515-1234 — the provider's own current Contact and Making-a-Referral pages both state this is the number to call, 7 days a week, 365 days a year, anywhere in Ontario, and that it automatically routes your call to your nearest local office. (310-2222, no area code needed, is still associated with the Toronto Central office in other directories and should still connect you — but it is no longer the number the provider's own site leads with, so this guide leads with the toll-free number above.) You can also start with a doctor/nurse/self/family-member referral. Assessment can take days to weeks depending on local demand — this is not a same-day door. A separate long-term-care Short-Stay respite program (a bed in an LTC home) charges a daily co-payment; that is a different program from this one. ontariohealthathome.ca/home-care Any Ontario resident whose care recipient needs home care, referral-based. Free once approved. Contact number corrected 2026-07-31.

Geneva Centre for Autism — Les & Kae Martin Programs (Free Adult Respite & Camp) Fully funded respite and camp sessions for autistic adults 18+ who need one-on-one support: evening and weekend respite, March Break camp, weekday respite sessions, summer camp, and an appointment-based program offering immediate, short-term day respite with 1:1 support. Evening and weekend respite specifically can involve a wait — the provider's own page names people "requiring interim respite care while waiting for evening or weekend respite" as an intended audience for the appointment-based day option, which functions as a bridge while you wait rather than a guarantee that evening or weekend respite opens right away. Call 416-322-7877. This is the narrow free tier — most of Geneva Centre's other adult programs (Adult Day Support, Community Options, Social Club, Learn/Train/Work) require Passport funding or direct fee payment, so do not assume the rest of Geneva Centre's adult offerings are free because this one is. genevacentre.ca — Les & Kae Martin Programs Caregivers of autistic adults 18+ needing 1:1 support, Toronto (112/164 Merton St. and 3036 Danforth Ave.). Free. Confirmed by direct provider-page fetch this verification pass, 2026-07-31 — upgraded from an earlier WebSearch-only verification. (Also a full entry in Guide 19 Disability, from the autistic adult's own program angle — genuinely dual-audience, not accidental duplication.)

Baycrest — Adult Day Program Baycrest runs two adult day programs built for exactly this kind of relief: the Freeman Family Day Centre (for older adults who are well or living with physical frailty or mild cognitive impairment, Monday–Thursday, 9:30am–1:30pm) and the Samuel Lunenfeld Mountainview Club (for older adults living with moderate to advanced dementia, in a more secure setting, Monday–Friday, 9:30am–2pm); both are physically at Baycrest's North York campus (Toronto), both require a minimum of two days a week attendance, and both include a supervised hot kosher lunch, medication administration, and caregiver-facing social work support, including virtual caregiver support groups. The daily program fee is $50 for in-person attendance (which also includes access to daily virtual programming), or $10 per day for virtual-only participation — published on the provider's own program-specific page, which also states fees are subject to change and that only limited subsidies are available. To register or ask about a subsidy, call 416-785-2500 ext. 2858 (this is the program's own registration line; Baycrest's broader Community Navigation and Access intake, 1-877-540-6565, is the number to call if you're not yet sure this is the right door for your situation). Eligibility is generally 65+; people under 65 with dementia are considered case-by-case, and the program states it is not suited to traumatic brain injury or developmental delay. baycrest.org/all-clinics-services/adult-day-programs Age 65+ generally (case-by-case exceptions under 65 with dementia), Toronto (North York). Not suitable for traumatic brain injury or developmental delay. Subsidized — daily fee published as of this verification pass, 2026-07-31 (see above); limited subsidies available. This rate was not found by Guide 20's own seal pass, which checked a more general Baycrest page — see the honest gaps section below.

SPRINT Senior Care A general community-support agency whose caregiver-facing services are concrete and priced, even though most aren't free. Its own Adult Day Program (1 William Morgan Drive, East York — inside Toronto — Monday–Friday) costs $31.00 per day plus $4.55 one-way for door-to-door transportation. Its in-home respite care (caregiver support) — a PSW who comes to relieve you directly, billed hourly rather than through a government assessment queue — costs $19.10/hour with a 1-hour minimum ($52.50/hour on statutory holidays), per the provider's own fee schedule (effective April 1, 2026 – March 31, 2027). SPRINT also runs a Caregiver Support Group (monthly, at its Adult Day Program site, for caregivers of people with dementia) and a Peer Support Group (every other Friday, at its main office, 130 Merton Street) — both require pre-registration with a SPRINT social worker and are not drop-in. Call 416-481-6411 for any of the above, or to book an Adult Day Program tour. sprintseniorcare.org — Caregiver Relief and Support Services · sprintseniorcare.org — Fee Schedule Toronto-area caregivers of seniors. No numeric age gate stated for the Adult Day Program on the provider's own page. Priced as above — not free, and no subsidy is mentioned for these specific rates (unlike Circle of Care's and Baycrest's day programs) — but the price is known in advance, which none of this guide's other paid respite doors can currently offer. Figures confirmed live, direct fetch, 2026-07-31.

An honest assessment of what "respite" actually means in this atlas today: of the six doors above, two remain unambiguously free with no daily fee (Circle of Care's own counselling/navigation component, and Ontario Health atHome's in-home PSW hours once approved) — and both carry real friction: the free counselling door doesn't include the actual relief-care hours, and the free PSW door can take weeks to open. Geneva Centre's respite is free but narrow — built for autistic adults 18+ needing 1:1 support, with evening and weekend slots specifically named by the provider as something people may need to wait for. Of the three adult day programs that would give you the most concrete block of time off, two now publish an actual number you can budget for before you call — SPRINT Senior Care ($31.00/day, inside Toronto) and Baycrest ($50/day, inside Toronto) — while Circle of Care's remains undisclosed anywhere online and is also the one physically located outside Toronto, in Vaughan. If a same-week block of respite time matters more to you right now than whether it's free, SPRINT's day program or its $19.10/hour in-home respite are, as of this verification pass, this atlas's most concretely bookable paid options — call and ask about the wait, since none of the three providers publishes one. If you need it to be free and in Toronto proper, this atlas still does not have a guaranteed same-week door to offer you — call the Ontario Caregiver Organization Helpline above and ask what else exists regionally; do not assume any door above will open on your timeline.

Money you may be missing

EI Caregiving Benefits (Family Caregiver & Compassionate Care Benefits) Employment Insurance income support for workers who take time off to provide care to a family member (or someone considered like family): the Family Caregiver Benefit for Children (up to 35 weeks, for a critically ill or injured child under 18), the Family Caregiver Benefit for Adults (up to 15 weeks), and Compassionate Care Benefits (up to 26 weeks, for end-of-life care). Weeks can be shared among eligible caregivers caring for the same person. canada.ca — EI caregiving benefits Any Canadian resident who meets standard EI eligibility and provides care to a family member or equivalent. Requires medical certification from a doctor or nurse practitioner. Free (this is an insurance benefit, not a program with a client fee). Reconfirmed live 2026-07-31 on a page the provider itself last updated 2026-06-02 — all three benefit names and week-maximums are unchanged.

If the person you're caring for is a child with a severe disability, the Assistance for Children with Severe Disabilities (ACSD) program pays up to $678/month directly to you as the parent or guardian, and the money can go toward costs including respite. Its full detail — because its eligibility gate is the child's disability, not your status as a caregiver — lives in Guide 16 Families by Age and Guide 19 Disability, not here.

A caregiver-specific tax credit likely exists federally (the Canada Caregiver Credit/Amount) but has no row in this atlas. Rather than describe a credit this project has not itself verified, this guide names the gap: ask a free tax clinic (Guide 02 Claim Everything covers CVITP) whether you qualify when you file. Similarly, no row in this atlas describes job-protected caregiver leave under Ontario's Employment Standards Act — if taking the EI benefit above requires time off work, that is a separate legal question this guide cannot currently answer from the atlas. (Both gaps were checked again against the full atlas this verification pass — still no matching row for either. Your own health

Caring for someone else is hard on the person doing it, and this guide treats that as its own need — not a footnote to the crisis line above.

Alzheimer Society Toronto Free support for people with dementia and their caregivers: phone/in-person counselling, referrals to community services, a lending library and online resources in 90+ languages, and public education workshops — including free dementia support groups facilitated by registered social workers. Call 416-322-6560 (Head Office, 20 Eglinton Ave West); a Mid-Scarborough Hub (2658 Eglinton Ave E) is also available by calling ahead for an appointment. alz.to Caregivers of people with Alzheimer's disease or other dementias, Toronto. Free. Phone number added this verification pass, 2026-07-31 (not previously recorded). (Also a full entry in Guide 11 Mental Health and Addiction; both guides treat this as core rather than a duplicate by accident, the same way this guide's OCO Helpline entry is dual-homed.)

If you're supporting someone in the Tamil community, Vasantham — A Tamil Wellness Centre offers free elder-abuse and caregiver support alongside its mental-health and addictions counselling, in English and Tamil. Full entry (with phone number and address) already lives in Guide 11 Mental Health and Addiction; not repeated here to keep one fact in one home.

If your own mental health is in crisis right now — not "I'm tired," but "I don't feel safe" — that's not this guide. Call 988 or a Distress Centre (416-408-4357); both are full entries in Guide 01 In Crisis Now. A caregiver in genuine crisis is not a lesser use of a crisis line.

If you're grieving after the person you cared for has died, that is not this guide either. Fleet guide #21H "After Someone Dies" is being built to hold that ground and is not yet available — this guide only names it so you know where to look next, rather than describing bereavement supports here and duplicating that guide's future territory.

Honest gaps

  • This atlas cannot currently promise a caregiver a guaranteed, free, same-week block of respite in Toronto proper. See the frank assessment at the end of the Respite section above. This remains the single most important honest gap in this guide — though this verification pass found that two of the three adult day programs now publish an actual price, which at least lets a caregiver budget for a paid block of time off in advance rather than discovering the cost only after calling.
  • This guide's Ontario Health atHome entry, and Guide 20 Seniors' identical entry, both previously cited 310-2222 as the number to call. This seal pass found the provider's own current Contact page leads with 1-833-515-1234 instead (310-2222 still appears to work, per other directories, but is no longer what the provider's own site recommends). This guide has been updated; Guide 20 has not yet been rechecked against this same finding — flagged for the wave's integration lane.
  • This guide's Baycrest Adult Day Program entry now disagrees with Guide 20 Seniors' identical entry, both sealed the same day. Guide 20 reports the daily rate as unpublished, because it verified against Baycrest's general Clinics & Programs page; this lane found the rate ($50/day in-person, $10/day virtual) published on Baycrest's own program-specific Adult Day Programs subpage — a different kind of page from the one Guide 20 checked. Flagged for the wave's integration lane to reconcile.
  • No Canada Caregiver Credit/Amount row exists in the atlas. A caregiver who does not know this federal tax credit exists never claims it; this guide cannot describe its current amount or eligibility because no row in this library's resource atlas carries it (rechecked this verification pass — still absent). Ask a free tax clinic (Guide 02) when you file.
  • No row describes job-protected caregiver leave under Ontario's Employment Standards Act. A caregiver taking unpaid time off to use the EI Caregiving Benefits above may need to know their job is protected during that leave; this atlas does not currently answer that question (rechecked this verification pass — still absent).
  • No dedicated hospice organization appears in this atlas as its own row. Circle of Care's Caregiver Support Services (above) includes free hospice/bereavement companion visits as one component of a broader program, but no standalone Toronto hospice (for example, a dedicated end-of-life respite or bereavement-care organization) has its own row to cite.
  • No young-caregiver-specific program exists as its own row, beyond the Ontario Caregiver Organization Helpline's own stated "dedicated resources for... young caregivers," which this guide cannot describe in more detail than the row itself states.
  • Sheena's Place runs a "Caregivers & Loved Ones" program category for people supporting someone with an eating disorder, per its own site — but that specific offshoot is not independently rowed in the atlas (only the person-affected side, FT-17007, is), so this guide cannot cite it as a verified caregiver door without inventing detail the row doesn't carry.
