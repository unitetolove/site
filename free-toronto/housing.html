Housing: Rent Help, Eviction Defence & Shelter (incl. GTA)

If you're behind on rent, worried about an eviction, or trying to get into subsidized or supportive housing — this page is for the part of a housing crisis that isn't tonight's emergency, but next week's or next year's. Everything on it is free. Selection rule: a resource is included here if a reader can start the door themselves — by phone, online, or a known referral pathway (even one that takes a while) — and its own page says honestly who to contact and what happens next. Rows behind a genuinely unclear or undocumented process are left out rather than guessed at.

If tonight is the emergency — no bed, nowhere to sleep, or you or your family are in danger — that's Guide 01: In Crisis Now, not this page. This guide picks up from there: keeping the home you have, defending against eviction, and getting into your next one.

If you need a shelter bed tonight

That's covered start-to-finish in Guide 01: In Crisis Now, including youth-specific, women's, domestic-violence, refugee, and Indigenous-specific shelters, plus what to do if Toronto's shelters are full and you need the GTA regional fallback. This guide doesn't repeat that list — call 211, or Central Intake at 416-338-4766 (1-877-338-3398, or 311), the city's 24/7 line that places people into an available shelter bed or 24-hour respite site and also connects to detox, mental-health, refugee, legal, Rent Bank, and LTB information.

Streets to Homes (S2H) — Street Outreach & Housing Plan Development If you're sleeping outside or in an encampment, 24/7/365 outreach connects you to more than tonight's bed — an outreach worker will also start a housing plan with you, plus help with ID, income supports, wellness checks, and supplies. The SHARC site (129 Peter St) also runs walk-in housing counselling weekdays 9am–3pm. toronto.ca — Streets to Homes Who it's for: anyone sleeping outside, Toronto, 16+. No documents required.

If you're behind on rent, or about to be

Toronto Rent Bank A grant — not a loan — up to $5,000 for rent arrears, or $2,000–$3,500 for a rental deposit depending on unit size (grants issued 2021 or later don't need to be repaid). 2026 income limits scale by household size, from about $46,500/year for one person up to roughly $91,000 for a family of seven. You don't need to be on the lease to apply. toronto.ca — Toronto Rent Bank Who it's for: Toronto renters under the income limits above who are NOT currently on OW or ODSP (see Housing Stabilization Fund below instead). Apply online; proof of income required.

Housing Stabilization Fund (HSF) Funds for rent, moving costs, rent or energy arrears, bed bug treatment, or setting up a new place — for people on Ontario Works, ODSP, or humanitarian Emergency Assistance (this includes some migrants under a humanitarian emergency authorization). toronto.ca — Housing Stabilization Fund Who it's for: current OW/ODSP/humanitarian Emergency Assistance recipients, Toronto. Apply through your caseworker — this is a referral, not a self-serve application.

Neighbourhood Information Post (NIP) — Voluntary Housing Trusteeship Program A free, voluntary program where NIP manages a portion of your income so rent and bills get paid on time (pay-direct), plus budgeting help — available even if you're currently homeless. Call 416-924-2543. Three Toronto-area locations (Gerrard & Parliament, Danforth & Coxwell, Ellesmere & Markham). nipost.org — Trusteeship Program Who it's for: anyone in Toronto. No income test, no documents required.

If you got an eviction notice, or you're worried about one

Understand & Prevent Evictions (City eviction-rights hub + Prevention Handbook) Free plain-language explanation of the 4-phase eviction process, the 7 legal eviction-notice types (N4 through N13), how to spot a bad-faith eviction, and a downloadable multilingual handbook (also available in Tamil, Tagalog, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, and Arabic). The page states directly that renters are protected under the Residential Tenancies Act regardless of immigration status — "newcomers, permanent residents, undocumented migrants, international students and citizens are all covered." toronto.ca — Understand & Prevent Evictions Who it's for: any Toronto renter. Free, no documents needed to read it.

Eviction Prevention in the Community (EPIC) Intensive case management for low-income households facing an imminent eviction: an urgent action plan, help applying for financial supports, and navigation across housing, legal, health and financial services. Referral-only — ask a community legal clinic, the Toronto Rent Bank, a Housing Help Centre, the City's Shelter Support and Housing Administration, or even your landlord or property manager to refer you in. toronto.ca — EPIC Who it's for: low-income Toronto households facing imminent eviction. Referral required — immigration-status policy not stated by the provider.

Commissioner of Housing Equity (OCHE) An independent office specifically for current Toronto Community Housing Corporation (TCHC) tenants facing eviction over rent arrears. oche.ca Who it's for: TCHC tenants only, Toronto. Age and documents required are not stated by the provider.

RentSafeTO — Tenant Complaints & Apartment Building Standards Enforcement Free complaint intake and City enforcement (inspections, orders, fines) against apartment landlords for heat, pest, maintenance and property-standards violations, via 311 or the RentSafeTO online portal, plus an interactive Building Score Map. This is the door for "my landlord won't fix anything," not for eviction itself. Applies only to apartment buildings with 3+ storeys and 10+ units — condos, townhomes, and units inside a private home (e.g. a basement or main-floor apartment) are explicitly not covered; those tenants should use 311's general property-standards complaint channel instead. toronto.ca — RentSafeTO for Tenants Who it's for: tenants in Toronto apartment buildings of 3+ storeys and 10+ units only. No documents required.

** Centre for Equality Rights in Accommodation (CERA), previously cited here under row id FT-9511, is omitted this review: its own url (equalityrights.org/cera) — and its entire domain — now redirects wholesale to a differently-branded site that doesn't independently confirm CERA's name, email, or Ontario-wide housing-discrimination scope. That redirect target turns out to be the same organization behind the Canadian Centre for Housing Rights entry immediately below, which is independently confirmed and cited on its own terms instead.

Canadian Centre for Housing Rights (CCHR) Free lawyer advice and representation for Toronto-area renters in the private market: disputing an above-guideline rent increase, fighting a no-fault N12 (landlord's-own-use) or N13 (demolition/renovation) eviction notice, or making a maintenance application to the Landlord and Tenant Board. Call 416-944-0087 or 1-800-263-1139; live chat available Fridays 1–5pm. Hours: Mon–Thu 9:30am–5pm, Fri 9:30am–1pm. housingrightscanada.com — Our Tenant Services Who it's for: renters in Toronto's private market. No documents required; immigration-status policy not stated by the provider.

Get free help understanding your rights, or a lawyer for the Landlord and Tenant Board

Federation of Metro Tenants' Associations (FMTA) — Tenant Hotline Free tenant-rights information and counselling by phone, email, or social media — evictions, repairs, rent, leases. Counsellors explain the law and your options (this is information, not legal advice) and can refer you to a legal clinic. Interpretation in 150 languages. Call 416-921-9494, Mon–Fri 8:30am–6pm, Sat 9am–4pm; after-hours voicemail returned within 24–48 business hours. Also runs an online Eviction Tracker tool. torontotenants.org/hotline Who it's for: anyone in Toronto. No documents required; immigration-status policy not stated by the provider.

ACTO Tenant Duty Counsel Program (TDCP) Free legal advice, assistance and referrals from a duty-counsel lawyer on or before the day of a scheduled Landlord and Tenant Board hearing, with priority to tenants facing eviction. Sign up online at tdc.acto.ca as soon as you have a hearing date — the provider doesn't publish a specific days-before-hearing cutoff on its own page. acto.ca — Tenant Duty Counsel Who it's for: any unrepresented tenant with a scheduled LTB hearing, Ontario. No income test stated for this specific service; no documents required.

Steps to Justice — Housing Law Guided Pathways (CLEO) Free step-by-step legal information plus online "Guided Pathways" interviews that auto-fill real LTB forms — money owed (T1), repair problems (T6) — and an Eviction Solution Explorer for non-payment-of-rent evictions (defence tips, sample letters, a payment-plan form). Usable as a guest or with a free account. stepstojustice.ca — Housing Law Who it's for: anyone in Ontario. Free, no documents required. For legal advice rather than self-help forms, the site itself directs low-income tenants to a local community legal clinic.

Pro Bono Ontario — Free Legal Advice Hotline Free phone consultation with a volunteer lawyer, up to 30 minutes, one per day: housing, employment, consumer/debt, going to court, corporate law for charities/nonprofits/small business, and powers of attorney (mornings only, 9am–1pm). No family, immigration, or criminal law. Call 1-855-255-7256, Mon–Fri 9am–5pm. Does not accept blocked/anonymous caller ID — agencies calling on a client's behalf should email info@probonoontario.org instead. probonoontario.org/hotline Who it's for: anyone in Ontario needing civil-law help who can't afford a lawyer. Phone; no documents required to call.

JusticeNet — for readers legal aid can't take If your income is too high to qualify for legal aid but too low for standard legal fees, JusticeNet is a not-for-profit directory of lawyers, paralegals and mediators (including for housing/tenancy matters) who've agreed to reduced, income-scaled fees — not free. Register as a client first, then search the directory and contact a professional directly; some now offer remote (phone/video) consultations. justicenet.ca Who it's for: anyone in Canada whose income falls between the legal-aid and standard-fee gap. Online registration required; reduced-fee, not free.

Find a free tenant/legal clinic in your neighbourhood

Toronto's community legal clinics are organized by neighbourhood catchment and each is funded by Legal Aid Ontario — free if you qualify under LAO's financial-eligibility test (every clinic sets its own exact threshold). If your neighbourhood isn't listed below, use the network finder, or call the FMTA hotline above.

Legal Aid Ontario — Community Legal Clinics (network) The umbrella network and finder tool for dozens of independently-operated, LAO-funded clinics across Ontario, organized by neighbourhood and specialty. legalaid.on.ca — Legal Clinics Who it's for: low-income Ontarians, under each clinic's own income test. Walk-in; no documents required to start.

Neighbourhood Legal Services — Downtown East (Yonge St to the Don Valley Parkway, Bloor St to the Toronto Islands). Walk-in intake, but you'll get an appointment for the actual legal help, not a same-day lawyer. Phone: 416-861-0677. Office hours: Mon–Fri 10am–2pm (except Wed), Wed 10am–4:30pm, closed weekends. nlstoronto.org

Kensington-Bellwoods Community Legal Services — west-downtown catchment (Bloor St W north, Lake Ontario south, Ossington/Strachan Ave west, Yonge St east). Call 416-924-4244 ext. 21, or use the online intake form. kbcls.org

Parkdale Community Legal Services (PCLS) — west-end catchment (Bloor St W, Ossington/Strachan Ave, Lake Ontario, Humber River); also covers ODSP, immigration and workers' rights. Call 416-531-2411. parkdalelegal.org

South Etobicoke Community Legal Services — south Etobicoke (Eglinton Ave north, Humber River east, Etobicoke Creek west). Call 416-252-7218. southetobicokelegal.ca

West Toronto Community Legal Services — catchment bounded by Jane St west, St Clair West/Weston Rd/Rogers Rd/Dufferin north, Yonge St east, Bloor St south; also runs a separate Housing Help Services stream. Call 416-531-7376. wtcls.org

Don Valley Community Legal Services — east-central catchment (Lake Ontario south to York Mills/401 north, Yonge St/Don River west to Victoria Park Ave east). Phone intake line only — no walk-ins; leave a message, callback within 3 business days. Schedule changed as of 2026-08-01: the Intake Line now runs Mon–Thu 9:00am–12:30pm only, and in-person intake appointments are now Mondays and Wednesdays (previously Mondays and Tuesdays). Call 416-441-1764 ext. 1. donvalleylegal.ca

Unison Health and Community Services — Legal Services — northwest Toronto (Steeles north, Bathurst St east, St. Clair Ave W south, Keele St/Humber River west), with sites at Lawrence Heights, Bathurst-Finch, Jane-Trethewey, Keele-Rogers, and Oakwood-Vaughan. This is three different services, not one — check which door is yours: - Community Legal Clinic (Keele-Rogers) — general legal advice by phone appointment, weekdays 9am–12pm & 1–3pm, plus an urgent drop-in Wednesdays 1–3pm. Call 416-653-5400 ext. 1244. - Drop-In Legal Clinic (Jane-Trethewey) — last Thursday of each month, 2–5pm; covers social assistance, housing, employment, human rights, and CPP/EI, plus document notarization. Catchment: former City of York only, and some financial criteria also apply. - Weekly Legal Clinic (Keele-Rogers) — Thursdays 9–11:30am, notarization/commissioner-of- oaths only — not general legal advice. unisonhcs.org

Willowdale Community Legal Services (WCLS) — North York, housing/tenant rights for tenants, occupants and renters in public or private housing. Note: does not handle family or criminal law. Tel: (416) 492-2437. Office hours: Mon, Tue, Wed, Fri 8:30am–12pm & 1–4:30pm; Thu 1–4:30pm only. willowdalelegal.com

Downsview Community Legal Services (DCLS) — Downsview/Bathurst-Finch catchment. Tel: 416-635-8388 ext. 0 or 21 (Bathurst-Finch office); Lawrence Heights satellite 416-787-1661 x3263. Hours: Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri 9am–5pm, Wed 9am–1pm, closed daily 1–2pm for lunch. A monthly Housing Help Clinic for tenants before Landlord and Tenant Board action was previously listed here — it isn't currently shown on DCLS's own site. Call to confirm whether it's still running before planning around it. downsviewlegal.ca

Downtown Legal Services — a legal clinic hosted by the University of Toronto Faculty of Law, but not gated to U of T students: open to any low-income Toronto resident under LAO's income test (with an added exemption from that income test specifically for levy-paying U of T students). Covers housing among several practice areas. Call 416-978-6447 — closed to new intake for several weeks each term (late Nov–Dec, April–mid-May, late Aug–mid-Sept). downtownlegalservices.ca Who it's for: low-income Toronto residents (income-tested) or any levy-paying U of T student (automatically exempt from the income test).

Osgoode Hall Law School — Community Legal Aid Services Programme (CLASP) Another law-school-hosted clinic — York University's Osgoode Hall — that is not gated to York students: free help from supervised law-student caseworkers for low-income Toronto residents, covering housing (via the clinic's Tenant Rights stream), administrative law (ODSP, CICB, Human Rights), immigration/refugee, family, and criminal law, plus an ID Clinic and Tax Dispute Clinic. CLASP's own page does not list a standalone employment-law practice area. osgoode.yorku.ca — CLASP Who it's for: any low-income Toronto resident, under an income test. Referral required — the provider's page doesn't say how to get referred in; call and ask.

Honest gap: these eight catchments don't tile the whole city — there's no dedicated neighbourhood tenant-legal-clinic row in this atlas yet for Scarborough, most of Etobicoke north of South Etobicoke's boundary, or central/east North York outside Willowdale and Downsview. If your neighbourhood isn't covered above, start with the FMTA Hotline or the LAO clinic finder, both above.

Get help finding or keeping housing — a Housing Help Centre

Housing Help Centres (citywide network) Free one-on-one help finding and keeping housing: landlord matching, mediation between tenants and landlords, and direct application help for the Rent Bank, Low-income Energy Assistance Program (LEAP), Emergency Energy Fund (EEF), and Ontario Electricity Support Program (OESP). This row's provider — The Housing Help Centre (THHC), also known as Scarborough Housing Help-Centre — runs walk-in and phone access plus itinerant intake at partner sites across Toronto and York Region, including Toronto Employment & Social Services offices, Toronto Public Library branches, ASAAP, Catholic Cross-Cultural Services, TNG Community Services, and University Settlement. shhc.ca — Housing Help Toronto Who it's for: anyone in Toronto. No documents required; call or walk in — no appointment needed.

East York East Toronto Family Resources (EYET) — Housing Help Housing search (incl. private-market apartments), financial-assistance program applications (Rent Bank, OESP, LEAP), tenancy support, and applications for transitional/supportive housing, for residents from Broadview/Danforth to Victoria Park/Danforth and south of Danforth/Queen/Carlaw. Call 416-686-3390. eyetfrp.ca Who it's for: East York-area Toronto residents. Cost and documents not stated by the provider.

Fred Victor — Bridging Housing & Community Stabilization Housing workers who bridge people experiencing homelessness to a network of affordable-housing sites and landlords, then provide at least a full year of follow-up support to help the tenancy stick. Call 416-364-8228 ext. 3361. fredvictor.org — Housing Access and Support Services Who it's for: people experiencing homelessness, Toronto. Referral required; no income test stated.

Apply for subsidized or rent-geared-to-income (RGI) housing

Access to Housing — Rent-Geared-to-Income (RGI) Housing Free application to join Toronto's Centralized Waiting List for RGI housing, where eligible tenants pay roughly 30% of household income toward rent. Apply and manage your account through the MyAccesstoHousingTO online portal. The City's own page states plainly that average wait times are long — treat this as a long-term housing plan, not an emergency fix. toronto.ca — RGI Subsidy Who it's for: Canadian citizens, permanent residents, or refugee claimants, Toronto, with at least one household member 16+; must not owe money to a social housing provider. Apply online; proof of income required.

WoodGreen Housing Access Centre Free help completing and updating an RGI housing application, gathering supporting documents, and referrals to other Housing Help Centres, The Access Point (supportive housing, below), and Central Intake. Call 416-469-4455, Tue–Thu 9am–4pm, phone or appointment only. The provider's own page states current average RGI waitlist times of 5–7 years for seniors and 5–10 years for non-senior/family units — the application-help service itself is immediate and free, but the underlying housing is a long wait. woodgreen.org — Housing Access Centre Who it's for: household members eligible per WoodGreen's RGI-eligibility criteria (citizen, permanent resident, applied-for-PR, refugee claimant, or Convention refugee), age 16+, Toronto. Proof of income required.

Toronto affordable housing lottery (Random Draws for Affordable Rental Homes) An email sign-up for future affordable-rental-housing openings — as of this atlas's last verification (2026-07-28), there are currently no open random draws to enter. When a new affordable development has space, a developer-run Expression of Interest period opens; after it closes, applicants are randomized and invited to apply in that order (the whole process usually takes 60–90 days). The City states it is also building a new Centralized Access System for Affordable Rental Housing to eventually replace this per-developer process — not yet live. toronto.ca — Affordable Rental Homes Who it's for: any Toronto resident; income/document requirements vary by the specific development once a draw opens.

Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit (COHB) A monthly portable rent benefit paid directly to your household — not tied to a specific unit — to help cover private-market rental costs. In Toronto, access is referral-only (not a self-serve application): you must already be on, or qualify for, the Centralized Waiting List for RGI housing, and get referred in through an approved community partner (such as the Housing Help Centres or legal clinics above). The City confirms it can only enroll a limited number of households each year, per the province's annual allocation — treat it as active-but- capacity-limited, not a standing entitlement. toronto.ca — Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit Who it's for: Ontario residents already on, or eligible for, the Centralized Waiting List; apply through a referral partner, not directly. Proof of income required.

Honest gap: this atlas doesn't have a row confirming whether Toronto's co-op housing sector runs a separate application/waitlist system of its own, distinct from the Centralized Waiting List above — if you're specifically hunting for a housing co-op, Access to Housing (RGI) is the closest verified door until that's checked. Readers specifically fighting an above-guideline rent increase or a renoviction (N12/N13) notice: that's the Canadian Centre for Housing Rights, cited above in the eviction-notice section — this atlas's earlier draft held that row at unverified status; as of this verification pass it's confirmed and citable.

Apply for supportive housing (mental health, addictions, or after homelessness)

The Access Point A single free application that acts as the centralized intake for Toronto's mental-health and addictions service network — Intensive Case Management, Assertive Community Treatment Teams, and three streams of supportive housing (mental health, problematic substance use, and mental health and justice). Covers almost all of Toronto (Port Union Road to Highway 427, Steeles Ave to the lake). Self-referral or a professional referral are both accepted, via an online form or a downloadable PDF. The provider's own page is honest that this is a long-term application route, not immediate relief — up to about 5 years' wait for 24-hour high-support shared living, up to about 7 years for an independent low-support apartment. theaccesspoint.ca Who it's for: anyone in the Toronto catchment above, age 14+. Apply online, yourself or through a professional referral.

Habitat Services Matches people with mental health challenges and low income to contracted boarding homes and rooming houses (shared or single rooms with meals and housekeeping, congregate living). Apply through The Access Point, above — call 416-640-1934, not Habitat Services directly. habitatservices.org Who it's for: people with mental health challenges, Toronto. Referral required.

Madison Community Services Operates 7 supportive-housing homes for people with mental health challenges and homelessness, plus collaborations with additional supportive-housing partners, multilingual services (including Spanish, Farsi, Dari, Arabic, and Ukrainian), and a CAMH-Madison High-Support Housing collaboration for hospital-to-community transitions. Call 416-977-1333. madisoncs.org Who it's for: people with mental health challenges and/or experiencing homelessness, Toronto. Referral required.

Houselink Community Homes Permanent affordable/supportive housing plus social programs (addressing poverty, isolation, and food security) for people living with mental illness and addictions. Referral is typically routed through The Access Point, above. houselink.on.ca Who it's for: people living with mental illness and/or addictions, Toronto. Referral required.

Mainstay Housing Supportive housing across 41 residential locations and 1,000+ units in Toronto, with a published "How to Apply" process. mainstayhousing.ca — How to Apply Who it's for: people needing housing-stability supports, Toronto (income-tested). Apply online; a referral document is also listed as required — call ahead to confirm what to bring.

Ecuhome Corporation Supportive housing (57 shared houses, 6 apartment buildings, a 59-unit rooming house, plus a 24-unit family building) for people who have experienced chronic homelessness, with a published 6-step application process. Applications by appointment only — call 416-593-9313 and listen for the prompt. ecuhome.ca — Apply for Housing Who it's for: people who have experienced chronic homelessness, Toronto. Bring 2 pieces of ID — one must show Canadian citizenship, permanent residence, or refugee status — plus proof of income or a shelter/previous-landlord reference letter.

St. Clare's Housing 944 units of affordable/rent-geared-to-income and market-rent housing across 8 Toronto neighbourhoods. Subsidized units are by referral only from a partner agency (such as a Housing Help Centre, above) — you can't apply directly. stclares.ca Who it's for: people needing affordable housing, Toronto (income-tested). Referral from a partner agency required.

Jean Tweed Centre — Addictions Supportive Housing (women) Women-specific supportive housing for women who are homeless or unstably housed with substance-use concerns, including trans women and non-binary people. Two streams: Addictions Supportive Housing (affordable, permanent housing across the Toronto area, with a dedicated case manager) and Elm Centre Supportive Housing (up to 30 self-contained units at the YWCA's Elm residential building — Jean Tweed provides case management, YWCA provides the unit). Both apply through The Access Point, above. A third, separate program — Palmerston House transitional housing, for women and mothers of infants up to 18 months working toward substance abstinence — is currently accepting applications directly via an online form and may require prior treatment attendance. jeantweed.com/supportive-housing Who it's for: women (including trans women and non-binary people) with substance-use concerns, Toronto area. Apply via The Access Point (Addictions Supportive Housing, Elm Centre) or Jean Tweed's own online form (Palmerston House).

Homes First Society One of Toronto's largest homelessness charities (42 years operating): 8 emergency shelters, 17 supportive housing sites, and 2 market-rate housing sites (27 properties total), reaching more than 6,000 people a year and supporting over 3,800 individuals daily, with wrap-around food, health care, harm reduction and life-skills support under a Housing First model. General-public admission typically routes through the City's Central Intake system (above), not direct self-referral — Homes First's own site did not have self-referral mechanics confirmed on the pages fetched. homesfirst.on.ca Who it's for: people experiencing chronic homelessness, Toronto. Access mainly via Central Intake; check homesfirst.on.ca's "I Need Help" page for any direct routes.

Indigenous-led and Indigenous-specific housing doors

Wigwamen Incorporated Affordable and geared-to-income housing across 865+ units in 10+ Toronto/Scarborough buildings plus Ottawa (families, seniors, singles, scattered housing). Ontario's oldest and largest urban Indigenous housing provider — Indigenous-led, but the provider's own page states it's open to both Indigenous and non-Indigenous applicants, not restricted to one community. wigwamen.com Who it's for: anyone under the income threshold, Toronto — open to Indigenous and non-Indigenous applicants alike. No documents specified by the provider for the initial contact.

Nishnawbe Homes Affordable, rent-geared-to-income housing across multiple GTA properties (Toronto and Brampton) for First Nations, Inuit and Métis singles, students, Elders, working poor, women, men, youth, 2-Spirited people, and parents. Call 416-975-5451 (244 Church St office). Honesty note: no independently confirmable official program/eligibility page exists for this provider — nishnawbehomes.ca is an unbuilt GoDaddy placeholder domain, confirmed again on this verification pass; the org's real-world existence is corroborated by 211 Central/211 Ontario listings and its CanadaHelps charity profile. Call to confirm current details before relying on them. Nishnawbe Homes — charity profile Who it's for: First Nations, Inuit and Métis people — community-specific, Toronto/GTA (income-tested).

Housing built for a specific community

YMCA Sprott House One year of supported residential housing for up to 25 young people, ages 16–24, who are 2SLGBTQIA+ — one of the first transitional housing programs of its kind in Canada. Eligibility is determined through an application and interview process; you'll work with a case manager toward your own goals. Call 647-438-8383. ymcagta.org — Sprott House Who it's for: 2SLGBTQIA+ youth 16–24, Toronto. Community-specific. No ID/income documents stated — an application and interview instead.

Fife House — Supportive Housing & Food Security for People Living with HIV/AIDS Long-term and transitional supportive housing (six residential programs plus satellite locations, about 200 units) for people and families living with HIV/AIDS, plus a Housing Help drop-in (Tue and Thu, 1–4pm, 490 Sherbourne St) for people not yet housed with Fife House. Call 416-205-9888. fifehouse.org — Housing Help and Referrals Who it's for: people living with HIV/AIDS, Toronto (income-tested), over 16. Referral required for residential housing; the Housing Help drop-in is a lower-barrier navigation door into that process. Fife House does not provide emergency housing or emergency beds.

Elizabeth Fry Toronto — Phyllis Haslam Community Residential Program A supportive residential placement for women on parole from provincial or federal custody, with staff support to build a release/life plan (education, employment, rebuilding family ties). Call 1-855-924-3708 (toll-free) and ask to speak with a worker for an assessment. efrytoronto.org — Housing Supports Who it's for: women on parole from provincial or federal custody. Referral/assessment required; the provider doesn't publish a fixed rent-geared-to-income fee structure — confirm directly.
