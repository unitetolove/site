Aging Out of Care

Hard selection rule: an entry belongs here only if a young person who has been in Ontario's child-welfare system can use it because of that experience, or it is a support they are disproportionately likely to need at this specific transition — and it is free or explicitly labelled subsidized, reachable in Toronto.

The one fact that shapes this whole page: almost everything here expires on a birthday, and the limits do not line up with each other. Money changes amount every year from 18 to 23. Health, dental and counselling coverage keeps going for years after the money stops — to 25 for one part, to 29 for another. School money runs to 26, or has no age limit at all, or runs to 30, depending which door you're using. A worker is there for you to 24. A drop-in hub is there to 29. The single worst outcome this guide is built to prevent: a 24-year-old who assumes everything ended at 23, and never asks about the counselling they are still entitled to for five more years. Read the table below before anything else, then come back to it whenever your own birthday changes what's true for you.

What ends when — read this first

OSAP Living and Learning Grant 26
Ontario Postsecondary Application Fee Reimbursement No age limit stated

None of these ceilings share a start date or a length. Ready, Set, Go and the Aftercare Benefits Initiative are explicitly sequential — ABI is what happens after Ready, Set, Go's money stops — but nothing forces you to notice that unless someone tells you, which is the entire reason this guide states it twice.

Money while you transition

Ready, Set, Go (RSG) — CAS of Toronto A voluntary agreement with your Children's Aid Society/Agency that provides monthly financial support on a sliding scale by age, plus help with education, employment, housing and life skills. Monthly amount, as published on ontario.ca (updated 2026-04-20, independently corroborated the same day on jfcy.org's own Ready, Set, Go rights page): $1,800 at age 18, $1,500 at 19, $1,000 at 20, $1,000 at 21, $500 at 22 — plus a $500/month top-up for anyone in post-secondary or skilled-trades training/apprenticeship, starting at age 20. You can work up to 40 hours/week at minimum wage without losing this support. Renewable any time up to your 23rd birthday; ending it is reversible until then. (First Nations, Inuit or Métis youth may have separate federal supports to age 26 — a different program, not an RSG extension.) torontocas.ca — Ready, Set, Go Program Youth who were in extended society care, a customary care agreement, or a Voluntary Youth Services Agreement immediately before turning 18. Access runs through your existing CAS/Agency relationship, not a general public intake — outside Toronto CAS, contact your own local CAS.

What continues after Ready, Set, Go ends — read this even if you think you're done

This is the section most likely to be missed, because nothing about Ready, Set, Go's own paperwork tells you it exists.

Aftercare Benefits Initiative (ABI) Vision, prescription-drug, dental and extended health benefit coverage for former youth in care aged 21 to 25 (18 to 25 if you were adopted on or after June 1, 2016) whose Ready, Set, Go financial support has ended and who aren't covered by another health plan — plus counselling and life-skills support services until you turn 29, a full six years past where most people assume everything stopped. A child welfare agency staff member completes the ABI registration on your behalf; there is no direct self-application, so ask your worker by name. Receiving Ontario Works or ODSP income support removes eligibility for the extended health-benefit portion specifically, but not for the counselling/life-skills portion. ontario.ca — Support for youth leaving the child welfare system Former youth in care, Ontario-wide, once your RSG financial-support eligibility has ended. Ask your CAS/Agency worker to register you — this does not happen automatically just because RSG ended.

School

CAS of Toronto — Financial Entitlements (RESP / VYSA / OCBE Savings) Money that may already be sitting in an account in your name. The Children's Aid Society of Toronto is required to hold three savings mechanisms for eligible children/youth: a Registered Education Savings Plan (RESP) for youth 0–18 in extended society care or with 12+ consecutive months of interim/customary care; a Voluntary Youth Services Agreement (VYSA) Savings account for youth 15–17 on a VYSA; and Ontario Child Benefit Equivalency (OCBE) Savings for youth 15–17 in care or on a VYSA. No dollar amount is stated on the provider's own page — funds accumulate through the agency's receipt of your Children's Special Allowance. Released once you complete a financial-literacy course and have an active personal bank account (and, for VYSA/OCBE, once your care or agreement has ended). torontocas.ca — Financial Entitlements Youth who were in CAS of Toronto's care or on a VYSA. Ask your worker directly whether an account exists for you — these are set up automatically by the agency, not applied for. Doesn't help a youth whose worker never opened the accounts, or who was never in CAS Toronto's care specifically; ask your own CAS about the equivalent entitlement.

OSAP Living and Learning Grant Additional OSAP funding above the regular full-time funding limits — up to $2,000 per semester (up to $4,000 across a typical two-semester academic year, though the actual amount is need-assessed by your institution's financial aid office and could be lower) — per ontario.ca (updated 2026-05- 05 and 2026-04-20). Two eligible groups: (1) ages 18–26 currently or formerly in extended society care, a customary care agreement, a protection custody order, or a VYSA — including if you were adopted on or after August 1, 2013; (2) ages 23–26 who received Ready, Set, Go financial support. Automatically considered when you submit a full-time OSAP Application for Full-Time Students — no separate form, though your institution's financial aid office may request supporting documents confirming care status. Stacks with the fee-reimbursement program below. ontario.ca — OSAP for under-represented learners Ages 18–26, per either eligible group above (including the 2013-or-later adoption pathway). Requires a full-time OSAP application plus supporting documents on request.

Ontario Postsecondary Application Fee Reimbursement Program Reimbursement of money spent applying to college and/or university, for a current or former Ontario child in extended society care (formerly a Crown ward): up to 2 of — all Ontario Universities' Application Centre (OUAC) fees for up to 5 university choices; all Ontario Colleges Application Service (OCAS) fees for up to 5 college choices; up to $100 for OSAP-approved private-institution programs; up to $100 for OSAP-approved out-of-province programs. Claims can cover the current academic year plus the 2 previous academic years (as fetched 2026-08-01, still: 2025–26, 2026–27 or 2027–28). The provider's own page states this in as many words: "Your age, income or adoption status does not matter to qualify for reimbursement." ontario.ca — Ontario Postsecondary Application Fee Reimbursement Program Current or former Ontario child in extended society care. No age limit stated anywhere on the page. Apply by mail or email with a signed reimbursement form plus payment receipts.

Children's Aid Foundation of Canada — Scholarships & Graduation Awards Scholarships of up to $5,000, renewable until program completion, for students currently or previously in government care (temporary or permanent care up to age of majority, or permanent care for at least 12 months) who are under 30 — or 30+ if you've never previously received CAFC post-secondary support. Separately, Graduation Awards of $500 per year of a completed program, up to $2,000 total, paid in two installments (90% in summer, the remaining 10% the following February after a short survey), for students who previously received a CAFC scholarship or bursary. As re-checked 2026-08-01, still unchanged: Scholarship Paths 1 and 2 are closed for the 2026–27 school year (reopening December 2026 for 2027–28); Paths 3 and 4 "may reopen... during the summer" of 2026 — that status hasn't moved since the prior wave's fetch, so check the page directly for current status rather than assuming either is open right now. cafdn.org — Educational Support Currently or previously in government care, under 30 (no upper limit for first-time CAFC recipients). Online application plus documentation of care history. Headquartered at 25 Spadina Road, Toronto; delivered locally in part through CAS of Toronto's Pape Adolescent Resource Centre (below).

Somewhere to live

StepStones for Youth — Building Connections, Housing Subsidy & Engagement Programs Free skill-building, mentorship, education/employment support, a housing subsidy program, and counselling for youth 10–25 who are currently or were previously involved in the child welfare system, including youth transitioning out of foster care — the one housing door in this guide that is gated by care history rather than open to any homeless youth. The Housing Subsidy Program itself runs through a youth-facing online portal (the Youth Housing Subsidy Portal): automated eligibility and ID checks, with grants covering first and last month's rent plus monthly rent top-ups for up to 12 months, paid directly to landlords rather than to the youth. Self-refer, or be referred by a youth/social worker, CAS, group/foster home, teacher, guidance counsellor or family member via the "Refer A Youth" form. stepstonesforyouth.com — Youth Support Programs Youth 10–25, currently or previously involved in the child welfare system, Toronto. Free.

General youth homelessness shelters are not care-specific — you don't need child-welfare history to use Covenant House, Eva's Initiatives, Youth Without Shelter, or YMCA Sprott House (for 2SLGBTQ+ youth 16–24) — so full entries for those live in Guide 07 Housing and Guide 01 In Crisis Now, not here. Homelessness immediately after leaving care is a well-documented risk, which is why this guide points to them at all rather than assuming a care-specific reader wouldn't need them.

Honest gap: there is no dedicated social-housing priority category for youth leaving care in Toronto. Checked directly against the City's own rent-geared-to-income priority-access page, still current as of its April 13, 2026 last update when re-checked this verification pass: the 7 ranked RGI priority categories are domestic-violence/trafficking survivors, downsizing households, terminally ill applicants, households exiting supportive housing, applicants experiencing homelessness, age-16/17 heads-of-household, and Indigenous applicants. A youth leaving care can only qualify through the general "experiencing homelessness" category (which requires being in a shelter, a VAW shelter, or an unfit dwelling — couch-surfing does not qualify, per the City's own page) or the age-16/17 category, if it applies. This guide states that plainly rather than imply a door exists that doesn't.

Your documents

Before you leave care, your Children's Aid Society or Agency should help you obtain or correct: your birth certificate, SIN, OHIP health card, Ontario photo ID or driver's licence, an Indian status card if applicable, and immigration documents. Justice for Children and Youth's own Ready, Set, Go rights guide states this as a clear expectation — your CAS/Agency "may have had obligations to collect information and copies of documents for you" and "should also apply for missing or expired documents, and correct errors" in them — ask your worker by name, before your file closes, because almost everything else in this guide (a bank account for your RESP/VYSA savings, an OSAP application, a job) assumes you already have these.

If that didn't happen and you're now on your own: general free ID-replacement help exists citywide, though it is not care-specific. The PAID (Partners for Access and Identification) ID Clinic offers free help obtaining birth registration, a wallet-size Canadian birth certificate, Record of Landing replacement, SIN card replacement, and an Ontario Health Card (if you lack an address and other ID), at 40+ clinic sites GTA-wide, walk-in. It cannot help with PR cards, citizenship cards, foreign birth certificates, passports, or an Ontario driver's licence/photo ID — those need a different process. the519.org — PAID ID Clinic Anyone, including people without a fixed address. Walk-in, first-come-first-served, capped daily capacity per site. Free. Not care-specific — no care-specific ID clinic was found in this atlas; see honest gaps.

Staying connected

TELUS Mobility for Good (youth aging out of care) A free smartphone (or $75 off a phone of your choice, or bring-your-own-device) plus a free 10GB 5G+ mobile plan for 2 years ($25/month regular price after), or a $10/month 20GB plan ($35/month after) with unlimited Canada-wide talk and text, for youth aging out of the child welfare system, ages 18 (in Ontario) to 26. telus.com — Connecting for Good, youth programs Ages 18 (Ontario) to 26. Requires a letter of eligibility from a child-welfare ministry/agency representative. Subsidized, not free after year 2. Runs on TELUS's national network, reaching Toronto — the companion home-internet program is explicitly not available in Ontario (confirmed again on the provider's own page this verification pass: wireline-only, restricted to Alberta, British Columbia and parts of Quebec), so don't count on it for home internet.

Someone in your corner

WoodGreen Youth in Transition Worker (YITW) A free, community-based Youth in Transition Worker who helps youth 16–24 with a history of involvement in the child welfare system navigate housing, education, employment/training, health and mental health services, legal services, and life skills such as financial literacy and household management. Voluntary, flexible and youth-driven — workers meet you in the community, at shelters, or virtually by text, phone or email. Contact: Andrika Sutton, 416-571-5703. woodgreen.org — Youth in Transition Worker Youth 16–24 with a history of involvement in the child welfare system. Free. Formerly run independently by Turning Point Youth Services, now fully integrated into WoodGreen.

Justice for Children and Youth (JFCY) Free legal information, advice and representation on the rights of children and youth in Ontario, including a dedicated Ready, Set, Go / leaving-care rights guide — how to access your own CAS records, what to do if a worker tries to end your RSG support early, and general rights on health, education and leaving home. Serves youth under 18, and up to age 25 for youth who are homeless. jfcy.org — Rights: Ready, Set, Go Under 18; up to 25 if homeless. Ontario-wide, Toronto-headquartered. Free. Reach them by phone or online through "Talk to a Lawyer."

Ombudsman Ontario — Children and Youth Unit A free, independent (non-government) complaints and advocacy service, with a dedicated Children and Youth Unit, for children, youth and young people on their own — covering children's aid societies, foster/group homes, youth justice facilities, and supports for older teens on their own. Can help get documents corrected or sent, connect you with your caseworker, resolve a placement dispute, or find out what supports you can access. Dedicated staff focus on Indigenous, Black, and 2SLGBTQIA+ children and youth. This is the currently operating body for this function — if a CAS/Agency improperly ends your Ready, Set, Go support, this is who JFCY's own rights guide directs you to contact. Toll-free 1-800-263-2841; direct 416-325-5669; TTY 416-325-2648. ombudsman.on.ca — Children, Youth and Families Children, youth and young people on their own, Ontario-wide. No age limit stated on the provider's own page. Free. No documents required.

People who have been through it

Pape Adolescent Resource Centre (PARC) — CAS of Toronto A voluntary, Toronto-specific resource centre exclusively for former youth in care and youth 16–29 leaving the child welfare system — one-on-one case management plus group programming across health/ mental health, education, employment, immigration, nutrition, identity, financial literacy, housing, legal issues, life skills and crisis counselling. Core programs include Connections (a health/mental- health navigation hub with a dedicated nurse navigator), the HSBC Skills and Leadership Program, and RBC Youth Works (pre-employment training, career counselling and job-placement referrals). Reach by phone (416-462-1010) or through your CAS worker. Eligibility is stated on the provider's own page in exactly these words: "former youth in care and youth between the ages of 16-29 years who are leaving the child welfare system." torontocas.ca — PARC Programs & Services Former youth in care and youth 16–29 leaving the child welfare system, Toronto. Free.

Honest gap: a dedicated peer-support network specifically for youth in care — separate from a service hub like PARC — is the thinnest area in this domain. Youth in Care Ontario/Canada (youthincare.ca) shows no verifiable activity since a February 2023 post about an April 2023 AGM, and its own "Youth in Care Ontario" chapter page still names the province's former child-and-youth advocate office (dissolved in 2019) as a current partner — treated as a dormant website, not a live program. Ontario YouthCAN's current operating status could not be confirmed on its own site (a static mission statement with no dates or contact information). One Vision One Voice's Youth Action Committee (for Black children and youth in care) is very likely still active — a March 2024 recruitment post surfaced independently — but its host page sits behind a JavaScript wall this project's tools cannot read, so it was not minted as a row. If PARC above doesn't fit your situation, ask a worker whether OVOV's Youth Action Committee is currently taking members; this guide cannot confirm it directly.

Cross-guide pointers — one fact, one home

  • General youth services and teen programming (drop-in youth spaces, after-school programs, teen recreation) — live in Guide 18 Families by Age; this guide only carries what a young person needs because of a child-welfare history, not general teen services.
  • Housing and shelter generally, including youth homelessness shelters open to any youth regardless of care history (Covenant House, Eva's Initiatives, Youth Without Shelter, YMCA Sprott House) — live in Guide 07 Housing and Guide 01 In Crisis Now.
  • Mental health and addiction generally (crisis lines, ongoing counselling not specific to care history) — live in Guide 11 Mental Health and Addiction and Guide 01 In Crisis Now.
  • Jobs and employment generally — live in Guide 12 Jobs & Employment.
  • School generally (adult credit programs, ESL, academic upgrading not specific to care history) — live in Guide 14 Learn Free.

Honest gaps

  • No dedicated Toronto or Ontario social-housing priority category exists for youth leaving care. See "Somewhere to live" above — a real, structural absence, not an oversight of this guide, re-confirmed against the City's own priority-access page this verification pass.
  • No care-specific identification-document clinic was found. JFCY confirms the CAS/Agency's expected role in helping with documents, but no free-standing clinic dedicated to former youth in care (comparable to PAID's general-homelessness clinic) could be found or fetched.
  • Dedicated peer-support networks for youth in care are the thinnest area in this domain. See "People who have been through it" above — Youth in Care Ontario/Canada appears dormant, Ontario YouthCAN's current activity is unconfirmed, and One Vision One Voice's Youth Action Committee is very likely active but sits behind an unfetchable page.
  • Toronto-specific tuition-waiver programs, as distinct from the province-wide Living and Learning Grant, were not found at individual GTA institutions this review; existing campus-lane rows were not re-audited for a youth-in-care-specific waiver, since that was out of this lane's scope.
  • A new Children's Aid Foundation of Canada "Education Support Fund" for additional or unexpected postsecondary costs is described on the provider's own page (fetched 2026-08-01) as a forthcoming program with no live application yet — not detailed above as an available resource on that basis; worth checking in a future guide version once it opens.
  • Every age ceiling and dollar figure in this guide has now been independently re-verified against each provider's own page as of this verification pass (2026-08-01) —. None of the ceilings or dollar tiers changed from the prior wave's research; the one open item from that wave (the correction owed to FT-9923) has already been applied to this library's resource atlas, and one further correction (StepStones' housing-subsidy detail, on FT-9922) is proposed.
