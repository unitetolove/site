After Someone Dies

This guide covers handling a death in Toronto — the practical tasks and the grief, together. It does not assume you are the executor, the next-of-kin, or related to the person who died. Read the next section before anything else; it is the single most consequential fact in this guide.

Hard selection rule: an entry belongs here only if it helps someone in Toronto handle a death — practically or emotionally — and is free or explicitly labelled with what it costs.

Before you do anything else

Get authorization before you sign anything with a funeral home or cemetery. Toronto Employment & Social Services (TESS) must authorize funeral or burial costs before a contract is signed — not after. This was re-confirmed word for word on the City's own page on 2026-08-01.

  • If the person who died was on Ontario Works or ODSP: tell the funeral director this before any arrangements are made. The funeral director then contacts TESS directly for authorization.
  • If the person who died was not on OW or ODSP: call the Application Support Centre at 416-338-8888, option 4, and get authorization before you sign anything with a funeral provider or cemetery.
  • TESS's Funeral Services Unit is open 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m., Monday to Friday. If the death happens after hours, on a weekend, or on a holiday, tell the funeral service provider you plan to apply for this help on the next business day — say so before anything is signed.

Paying first and applying afterward can mean losing the benefit entirely — TESS may not reimburse costs already paid. Arranging the funeral and then looking for help afterward is the natural order to do things in. It is also the order that costs families thousands of dollars. Call first. Sign second.

Re-confirmed 2026-08-01 on the City's "Health Support" page (a different page from the policy document cited below, checked specifically for this instruction) — the phone number, the option number, and the authorize-before-signing rule all matched exactly. This is the guide's single highest-stakes claim and it held without change.

Money toward the funeral

City of Toronto (Ontario Works) — Funeral and Burial Assistance The City pays a funeral provider or cemetery directly — funeral-service administration, burial (opening/closing the plot), or cremation plus interment or scattering — when the estate of a Toronto resident who has died cannot cover the cost. This applies whether or not the person who died was receiving Ontario Works or ODSP; if the estate has insufficient funds, ask. The provincial policy that sets this benefit — Ontario Works Policy Directive 7.2, page last updated April 14, 2026 — names a "recommended maximum" of $2,250 for funeral, burial or cremation costs combined. This is guidance, not a hard cap; administrators can exceed it at their discretion, and any amount approved above the guideline is still cost-shared at the standard rate. A grave marker required by the cemetery and a "perpetual care" charge are included; a tombstone is not. The City can later recover what it paid from the estate, the CPP Death Benefit, or life insurance — it will mail a formal "Letter of Intent" to each recovery source — so treat this as a bridge, not necessarily free money, if the estate later turns out to have funds. Read "Before you do anything else," above, before you call. toronto.ca — funerals and burials · ontario.ca — Policy Directive 7.2 itself Estate of a Toronto resident, income- and asset-tested (deceased and spouse, at time of death). Proof of income and assets required. Figure: $2,250 recommended maximum, verified word for word on the province's own Policy Directive 7.2 page, 2026-08-01, still dated April 14, 2026 — unchanged. Free — paid by the City, subject to the recovery noted above.

Two broader hardship-benefit doors also cover funeral costs, as one line among several. The City's Hardship Fund (for people not on OW/ODSP — call 416-338-8888, option 3) and OW Discretionary Benefits (for people already on OW — ask your caseworker) both list funeral expenses as one eligible item among many others. Neither is specific to death, and both are covered in full in Guide 02 Claim Everything — named here only so a second door is visible if the dedicated funeral benefit above doesn't fit.

Registering the death and getting the certificate

Register a Death and Get an Ontario Death Certificate Ontario law requires a death to be registered — a Medical Certificate of Death plus a Statement of Death, filed with the municipal clerk, usually by the funeral director — before a burial permit or cremation can happen. Once registered, you can order the certificate itself, which you will need repeatedly: to settle the estate, claim insurance, close bank accounts, or cancel government services. Registration is currently taking up to 16 weeks, stated on the province's own page — the certificate cannot be issued until registration is complete. Once registered, the certificate itself takes a further 15 business days (regular service, $15) or 5 business days (premium service, $45, or in-person emergency service with proof of urgency, $45 plus a further $30 surcharge). Order online, by mail, or in person at Toronto ServiceOntario (47 Sheppard Ave E, Unit 417). Fees — 2026 ServiceOntario schedule, page updated January 14, 2026: $15 regular death certificate, $22 certified copy of the death registration (regular online), $45–$52 premium or emergency service plus courier. No general low-income fee waiver was found. Because nearly everything else in this guide depends on this document, the 16-week registration wait can stall the whole process — start it immediately, before anything else. ontario.ca — get or replace an Ontario death certificate Anyone can order a basic death certificate or death search; a certified copy of the death registration is limited to next-of-kin (parents, spouse/common-law partner, children, siblings — extended next-of-kin only if all closer relatives are also deceased) or the executor. Figures: 2026 fee schedule, re-confirmed word for word 2026-08-01 — the "up to 16 weeks" registration notice and all fee figures are unchanged and the page is still dated January 14, 2026.

Money you may be owed

CPP Death Benefit A one-time lump-sum payment on behalf of a deceased Canada Pension Plan contributor. If there's an executor, they apply — ideally within 60 days. If there's no estate, or no one has applied, the money goes, in order, to whoever paid the funeral costs, then the surviving spouse or common-law partner, then the next-of-kin — so a person who is not the executor can still claim this directly. Under the rate structure effective for deaths on or after January 1, 2025: the basic amount is $2,500, plus a possible top-up of $2,500 — a maximum of $5,000 — but the top-up only applies if the person who died never received a CPP/QPP disability or retirement pension, and has no surviving spouse or common-law partner eligible for the survivor's pension. Most estates do not get the full $5,000: the average amount actually paid to new beneficiaries in January 2026 was $2,572, per the CPP payment-amounts table updated March 31, 2026. Processing takes 6–12 weeks after a complete application. canada.ca — CPP Death Benefit · canada.ca — CPP payment amounts table Deceased contributed to CPP for at least 1/3 of contributory years (minimum 3), or 10 years. Free to apply. Figures: 2025 rate structure; average-paid figure current to January 2026 — every number re-confirmed word for word on both pages, 2026-08-01. Unchanged.

CPP Survivor's Pension An ongoing monthly payment to the legal spouse or common-law partner (cohabiting one year or more) of a deceased CPP contributor, continuing even if the survivor remarries. The amount is fully individualized — it depends on your age at the contributor's death and how much and how long they paid into CPP — so no single figure applies to everyone; use Canada.ca's benefits estimator for a personal number. As a range, from the CPP payment-amounts table updated March 31, 2026 (2026 figures): survivor under 65 — average $545.71/month, maximum $803.54/month; survivor 65 or older — average $334.24/month, maximum $904.59/month. Apply within 12 months of eligibility if you can — CPP pays a maximum of 12 months of retroactive benefits, so delay permanently loses money. You cannot receive a full survivor's pension and a full CPP retirement or disability pension at the same time — they combine, capped at the larger benefit's own maximum. canada.ca — CPP Survivor's Pension Legal spouse or common-law partner (1+ year cohabiting) of a deceased CPP contributor. Free to apply. Figures: 2026 table, updated March 31, 2026 — CPP amounts are revised every January. Re-confirmed word for word 2026-08-01. Unchanged.

Allowance for the Survivor (OAS) A monthly, tax-free payment for people aged 60 to 64 whose spouse or common-law partner has died, who have not remarried or entered a new common-law relationship, and whose income is under the threshold. It runs alongside — and is legally separate from — the CPP Survivor's Pension above; you may be able to receive both. The overview page states up to $1,702.34/month for the July– September 2026 quarter (page updated July 14, 2026) — a ceiling, not a typical amount; actual payments are income-tested and lower for most recipients. Re-checked on 2026-08-01, this remains the figure the government's own overview page leads with today. This figure changes again in October 2026 — OAS and Allowance rates adjust every quarter. Honest flag, still unresolved: the eligibility page itself states two different income thresholds — "$30,336" in one bullet list and "$30,696" in the table just below it, both on the same live page, both captured on 2026-07-31 and re-captured, unchanged, on 2026-08-01. This guide does not guess which is correct; check the current page or call before assuming you're over or under the line. A second, related inconsistency found this review: the government's own "How much you could receive" detail page has not been updated since May 1, 2026 and still displays the previous quarter's table (April–June 2026: $1,682.15/ month, threshold $30,336) — a different figure again from the overview page above. Canada.ca's own pages are currently out of step with each other on this benefit; this guide reports what each page states rather than picking one for you. Not automatic, even if you're already getting the CPP Survivor's Pension — you must apply, and can do so up to 11 months before turning 60. canada.ca — Allowance for the Survivor · canada.ca — Do you qualify · canada.ca — How much you could receive Age 60–64, surviving spouse or common-law partner, not remarried/repartnered, 10+ years' Canadian residence since age 18. Free to apply. Figure: July–September 2026 quarterly rate, re-confirmed current on 2026-08-01. This is the fastest-dating and least internally consistent figure in this guide — see the honest gaps section.

The estate, and its honest limits

Free help administering an estate — actually administering one, not just advice about it — does not exist in Toronto for a general low-income resident. What exists instead is free, short legal advice. Say that plainly to yourself before calling: a 30-minute conversation is not a caseworker who will walk your family through probate. This seal pass tried again to disprove that, checking a different kind of page than before — Legal Aid Ontario's own current list of the areas of law it covers (family, criminal, refugee/immigration, domestic violence, mental health, youth) — and wills and estates is not one of them. The gap holds.

Law Society Referral Service A free referral service that matches you with a lawyer or paralegal for up to 30 minutes of free legal advice, in any area of law including wills, estates and probate — open to anyone in Ontario, no income test, and you can ask for a lawyer who speaks your language. Alternative: the Ontario Legal Information Centre offers a similar free 30-minute meeting (1-844-343-7462 or legalinfocentre.ca, also no income test), but its own terms say it cannot apply the law to your specific situation or review your documents — narrower than a lawyer referral. lso.ca — Law Society Referral Service Anyone in Ontario. No documents required, no income test. Free for the first 30 minutes; further work is typically a private fee. This review's direct fetch of the provider's page returned mostly site navigation rather than the service description itself (a tool limitation, not a content change); the free/30-minute/no-income-test facts were corroborated instead against the same provider's own referral-portal terms and independent legal-directory summaries on 2026-08-01. The Ontario Legal Information Centre alternative was not independently re-fetched this review — carried forward unchanged, flagged for reconfirmation next time.

Office of the Public Guardian and Trustee — Estate Trustee of Last Resort Ontario's government office will administer an estate itself, as "estate trustee of last resort," when there is no will (or the named executor has died or become incapable) and no eligible next-of-kin in Ontario — including when the next-of-kin who exist are minors or mentally incapable adults. This answers "what happens when there's no will and no one to handle it" — it is not a free lawyer for a family that does have a willing executor but no money to pay for help. The OPGT's own page states it "cannot provide legal advice or assist individuals in preparing court applications." Call 1-800-891-0504 or email OPGTEstatesAdmin@ontario.ca to ask whether it applies to your situation. ontario.ca — administering estates Deceased was an Ontario resident or owned Ontario real estate; no other eligible person available. Free to inquire — the OPGT charges its own regulated fees to the estate only once appointed, not to you directly. Re-confirmed word for word 2026-08-01, including the "cannot provide legal advice" language and the estate-only fee structure. Unchanged.

Your rights as a funeral consumer

Bereavement Authority of Ontario — funeral price disclosure and complaints Ontario law requires every licensed funeral home, cemetery, crematorium and transfer service to show you a full price list before you sign anything, disclose any commissions or referral arrangements, and honour a 30-day full-refund right after signing. After that 30 days, a cancellation fee applies, capped at 10% of the contract or $350, under the Funeral, Burial and Cremation Services Act, 2002. The BAO licenses providers and publishes a free public register you can check before you buy (portal.thebao.ca/public-register — a live-looking website is not proof a business is licensed). Its complaint process is free to file, by phone, email, mail or an online form — but the Registrar "has no authority to award damages or compel licensees to give refunds or discounts where services and/or merchandise have been provided," so it can mediate, warn or discipline a provider, not force a refund for services already delivered. The BAO also publishes a free Consumer Information Guide to Death Care in Ontario — a two-page overview is available in 10 languages, and an audio version exists in English and French. ontario.ca — your rights · thebao.ca — Consumer Information Guide Phone 647-483-2645 (Toronto) or 1-844-493-6356 (toll-free). Anyone in Ontario. Free — the price-list disclosure is a legal right at no cost, and filing a complaint carries no fee. The 10%/$350 cancellation-fee cap and both phone numbers were re-confirmed word for word 2026-08-01; no legislative change was found. Unchanged.

Grief support, including for a child

Bereaved Families of Ontario — Toronto Free, small (8–12 person), closed-membership mutual-support groups organized by type of loss, plus one-to-one grief support. Self-refer — no professional or agency referral needed; call 416-440-0290 or use the online intake form. A mandatory one-to-one screening session happens before you join a group. Groups run only three times a year (fall, winter, spring) — not currently forming mid-summer, and there is often a wait for a spot. bfotoronto.ca Anyone in Toronto. No documents required. Free. Dorothy Ley Hospice — grief and bereavement support Free grief companion volunteers, a monthly "Understanding Grief" webinar, relationship-specific groups (spousal loss, parent loss, a men's coffee chat and more), up to eight sessions of individual grief counselling (in person or by videoconference), and wellness offerings. No prior connection to this hospice is required — the provider's own page confirms you can self-refer, at no charge. Call 416-626-0116 or use the online "Get support" form; 24-hour helpline 905-667-1865. Located at 220 Sherway Drive, Etobicoke. dlhospice.org Anyone grieving, primarily west Toronto/Etobicoke-based (some services by videoconference). Free. This is the only Toronto-area hospice this project has directly confirmed follows an open-to-the-public pattern — others (Kensington Health/Journey Home, Hospice Toronto, Casey House) were still not checked this review; see honest gaps. Re-confirmed word for word 2026-08-01, including the "up to eight sessions" figure. Unchanged.

Yorktown Family Services — Rapid Access Therapy (grief and bereavement) Free, walk-in counselling — no appointment, no referral, no health card needed — for children, youth and young adults, including grief after a death, at the West Toronto Youth Hub (1541 Jane St). A second walk-in site now also exists downtown: "what's up walk-in®" at St. James Town Community Corner, 200 Wellesley St E — call 416-917-9356 before arrival. Also runs individual/family grief therapy by intake, a separate path from the walk-in clinics. Call 416-669-3521 for the Jane St. walk-in service, or 416-394-2424 for the general agency line. yorktownfamilyservices.com — Rapid Access Therapy hours Ages 0–29 (a parent qualifies for this specific service only if the parent themselves is under 29 — otherwise it's for their child). Toronto. Free — re-confirmed word for word 2026-08-01 on the provider's current hours page, which also surfaced the second downtown walk-in location above (not previously recorded).

Camp Erin Toronto — bereavement camp for children and teens A free weekend (two nights, three days) overnight camp for children and teens 6–17 grieving the death of a parent, caregiver, sibling or other significant person — grief education and peer support alongside ordinary camp activities, led by bereavement professionals and trained volunteers. Applications are not open right now — the next window opens Monday, November 2, 2026, for a camp running June 11–13, 2027. Join the notification list in the meantime. If this specific weekend doesn't fit your family, the same provider's page names three other bereavement camp options: Camp Skein (Camp Maple Leaf, a paid one-week camp for people who have already attended Camp Erin), Camp Kerry (Lumara Society, a family bereavement retreat, applications typically open in April), and Camp Keaton (Bob Kemp Hospice, free, ages 7–17, held annually in June). Also a full entry in Guide 18 Families by Age, from the age-and-stage angle — both guides treat it as core. camperintoronto.com Bereaved children and teens 6–17. GTA. Free. Support for a specific kind of loss

Distress Centres of Toronto — Survivor of Suicide or Homicide Loss Support One-to-one grief support plus a follow-up series of group sessions for family and friends bereaved by a suicide or homicide, and a Suicide Resource Centre. Day or evening, at three Toronto locations (downtown, North York, Scarborough). Self-referral — contact Alex Shendelman at 416-595-1716. torontodistresscentre.com/survivor-support Anyone in Toronto. Healing Hearts (Moms Stop The Harm) — grief support for an overdose or substance-use death A free, peer-led, open-format bereavement support group by Zoom, specifically for people grieving a death from substance-use-related harms including overdose. Facilitators have lived experience of this same kind of loss; no time limit on attending — join or leave as needed. Email info@healingheartscanada.org. momsstoptheharm.com/healing-hearts-groups Adults 18+, accessible from Toronto by Zoom (national organization). Free — re-confirmed word for word 2026-08-01. PAIL Network — pregnancy and infant loss support Free, peer-led support for anyone in Ontario who has had a pregnancy loss, stillbirth, infant death, or an abortion for medical reasons: loss-specific online groups, in-person "Circles of Support" (including a Toronto midtown group at Bathurst St & St. Clair Ave W, every 2nd Tuesday of the month, 7:30–8:30pm), one-to-one peer phone support (four 45-minute sessions), and specialized groups for grandparents, partners, Indigenous families, Black African Canadian and Caribbean families, and 2SLGBTQ+ families. No time limit — you can access this at any point after a loss, not only right after. Call 1-888-303-7245 or fill out the request form at pailsupport.sunnybrook.ca. pailnetwork.sunnybrook.ca Anyone in Ontario who has experienced this kind of loss. Free. Honest gaps

  • Free help administering an estate — not just advice about one — does not exist in Toronto for a general low-income resident. The Law Society Referral Service and Ontario Legal Information Centre both offer roughly 30 minutes of free advice; neither walks a family through probate. The OPGT steps in only when no one else is eligible or available, and it is a government backstop charging the estate, not a free lawyer for a family that does have a willing executor. This seal pass tried again to disprove this, checking Legal Aid Ontario's own current services list (a different kind of page than anything checked at build) — wills and estates is still not one of the areas of law Legal Aid Ontario covers. Stated plainly here rather than implying an ongoing service exists.
  • No verified free or subsidized faith-based or community burial option was found for Toronto. Community burial societies came up only in third-party summaries, never on a fetchable page stating explicit terms — a real gap, not an oversight, and not claimed here until directly confirmed. Not re-attempted this verification pass.
  • The Allowance for the Survivor's income threshold is inconsistent on the government's own page ($30,336 vs. $30,696, same page) and remains unresolved — re-checked on 2026-08-01 and found unchanged from an earlier finding. A second inconsistency was found this review: the government's "How much you could receive" detail subpage has not been updated since May 1, 2026 and still shows the prior quarter's rate ($1,682.15/month) and a third repetition of the $30,336 threshold, while the Overview page (updated July 14, 2026) shows the current quarter's $1,702.34/month. This guide reports what each government page states rather than resolving the inconsistency for you.

  • Only Dorothy Ley Hospice was directly confirmed to offer grief support open to the public without a prior connection to its own hospice patients.
