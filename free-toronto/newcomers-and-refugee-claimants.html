Newcomers & Refugee Claimants

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if a person who arrived in Canada recently — including refugee claimants, protected persons, sponsored immigrants, students and workers on temporary status, and people with no status — can use it because of their newcomer or immigration situation, and it is free to them. A service every Torontonian uses equally belongs in another guide and gets at most a one-line pointer, not a repeated entry.

The single most important thing in this guide is immigration status, not need. Refugee claimants, protected persons, permanent residents, temporary residents (workers and students) and people with no status at all face genuinely different doors. Getting this wrong in either direction has real consequences: telling someone a door is closed when it isn't makes them self-exclude from something they qualify for; telling them a door is open when it isn't wastes a trip that may have taken hours to make. Every entry below states what the row itself says about who qualifies — no more, no less. Where a row is silent on status, this guide says so rather than guessing. Where a row explicitly says a service is open regardless of status, that sentence is printed plainly, because it is the single most valuable sentence in this guide for a reader with no status.

If you have no legal status in Canada right now

These are the doors in the atlas that explicitly do not turn away someone without status.

Access Alliance — Non-Insured Clinic (NIC) Free confidential primary care (nurse practitioner, nurse, midwife) by appointment, for immigrants and refugees in Toronto with no health insurance and no primary care provider — this includes people with no legal status, an expired or rejected refugee claim, or temporary low-wage work without private insurance. You do not need to show ID or immigration documents to book or be seen — eligibility is self-declared based on your situation (the one exception: a specific ID/visa-status check applies only to Mexican nationals, per a notice on the provider's own page). accessalliance.ca Call 416-324-9380 Mon 9:00–9:30am (books Mon/Tue appointments) or Thu 9:00–9:30am (books Thu/Fri); appointment-only, no walk-ins. 340 College St, Suite 500. Not eligible: current visitors, students, or work-permit holders who already have private insurance, IFH, or OHIP coverage.

FCJ Refugee Centre — Primary Health Clinic Free primary care and psychiatry for people with no insurance at all who reside in Canada, regardless of immigration status — the provider's own page states plainly: "everyone deserves access to health care, regardless of their insurance and immigration status." fcjrefugeecentre.org/health-clinic Appointment by phone, Tuesdays only — not a walk-in/daily clinic; wait times vary. Eligibility is simply "no insurance at all" plus living in Canada. FCJ's other services (settlement, shelter, English classes, food security) are also free and serve refugee claimants, convention refugees, migrant workers and undocumented people alike.

Blue Door Clinic (hosted at Casey House) Free, confidential HIV primary care for people in the GTA who are uninsured or precariously insured — explicitly including newcomers awaiting a status review, migrant workers, visitors, international students, and Canadian-born people lacking adequate ID. caseyhouse.ca — Blue Door Clinic By appointment (Mon 9:30am–12pm; Wed/Thu 9:30am–3:30pm), 119 Isabella St. Accepts self-referral or referral from a health provider/community agency via an online form.

Butterfly — Asian and Migrant Sex Worker Support Network Free crisis intervention/hotline, health support and referrals, counselling, outreach, and legal-rights workshops for Asian and migrant sex workers, in English, Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and Thai. No referral required. butterflysw.org Call 416-906-3098 or email cswbutterfly@gmail.com. Supports roughly 500 migrant sex workers on an ongoing basis; also connects people to support outside Toronto.

FCJ Refugee Centre — Anti-Human-Trafficking Programs Free, holistic support for people who have been trafficked (labour or sex trafficking): help applying for Open Work Permits for Vulnerable Workers and Temporary Resident Permits for victims of trafficking, referrals to counselling/food security/legal support, and orientation on labour rights and signs of exploitation. The provider's own page states: "we do not work with agents or representatives, and all our services are free of charge." fcjrefugeecentre.org/human-trafficking Office line 416-469-9754; WhatsApp 647-971-2153 (this specific number reaches the Migrant Workers Mobile Program stream, not a general crisis line). If you need a 24/7 crisis phone line for trafficking right now, call the Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-833-900-1010 (24/7, 200+ languages, independent of police) alongside or instead of FCJ. Reconfirmed 2026-07-30: the provider's own trafficking page still publishes no general crisis phone — only the WhatsApp number above, for the Migrant Workers Mobile Program stream specifically. This guide's framing (pair with the Hotline for anything time-sensitive) is unchanged and current.

Legal Aid Ontario — Community Legal Clinics (network) Free legal information, advice or representation in housing/eviction, income support, workers' rights, human rights, and immigration/refugee law, through dozens of independently-run, LAO-funded clinics organized by neighbourhood. Each clinic's own eligibility test is income-based (LICO), not stated as an immigration-status test on the network's own page — the row does not confirm status eligibility either way, so this guide states only what's known: income-tested, not a stated status gate. legalaid.on.ca/legal-clinics General LAO lines: 1-800-668-8258 (toll-free), 416-979-1446 (GTA); use the Find a Legal Clinic tool for the clinic serving your neighbourhood. Access varies by clinic — some are appointment-only, none confirmed as unrestricted walk-in.

If you're a refugee claimant

Shelter, reception and settlement support built specifically for people making a refugee claim in Canada. Every shelter below is refugee-claimant-specific, not a general homeless shelter — for shelter beyond this list, see Guide 07 Housing and Guide 01 In Crisis Now (which also carries the City's 24/7 Central Intake line, 416-338-4766 — reconfirmed against the City of Toronto's own Shelters page, fetched 2026-07-30, which states plainly: "call 311 or Central Intake at 416-338-4766").

Sojourn House — Emergency Shelter Free 24/7 emergency shelter (76 beds across 38 rooms) for refugee claimants — including separated youth, single adults, women and families — with settlement/housing counselling, refugee-determination and landing-process support, and case coordination with legal services. sojournhouse.org Call (416) 864-0515 ext. 221/222; 101 Ontario St. Clients are referred by community agencies or can call directly to inquire (calling directly is recommended to reduce wait). Important: Sojourn House's shelter runs a waitlist model, not guaranteed same-night placement — "you will be contacted on a priority basis as soon as a bed becomes available," per the provider's own site. If Sojourn House can't take you tonight, Central Intake (416-338-4766) is the fallback. A separate 78-room Emergency Family Shelter Program exists but is reached only through City of Toronto Central Family Intake (416-397-5637), not by calling Sojourn House directly. Reconfirmed 2026-07-30 via a fresh fetch of the provider's own Emergency Shelter page: the waitlist language above is word-for-word what the page states today. No change.

Romero House Transitional housing across 4 houses for newly arrived refugee claimants (residents typically stay about a year), with help navigating the refugee-determination and settlement systems, referrals to legal and social services, a clothing boutique, and a weekly food hamper for residents. Also runs a Walk-in Program for claimants not living on-site, and a Community Host Program matching claimants needing emergency shelter with a volunteer host. romerohouse.org 1558 Bloor St W; office hours Mon–Wed 10am–4pm, Thu–Fri by appointment. Housing placement itself is intake-managed, not simple walk-in, even though office hours are walk-in for information and support. Explicitly a welcoming space for LGBTQ+ refugee claimants.

Matthew House Toronto A reception/intake shelter for refugee claimants who have been in Canada less than 28 days (about 80 people/year), including unaccompanied minors, plus five long-term transition homes for refugee youth. Runs ReCourse, a distinctive program that stages simulated refugee hearings to prepare claimants for their real IRB hearing. matthewhouse.ca Access is referral-required, not a direct-dial walk-in door — this guide still cannot confirm who can make that referral or how fast it moves for someone who just landed with nowhere to go; the provider's own site (re-fetched 2026-07-30, "What We Do" and "Contact Us" pages) does not publish a self-referral process or an intake-specific phone line. New today: the provider's own Contact Us page does list a General Inquiries line, 416-203-7848 (admin@matthewhouse.ca), and a mailing address (981 Dundas St W, Toronto M6J 1W4) — neither was in this guide before this verification pass. Call General Inquiries and ask directly whether same-day intake is possible; do not assume it is. If you can't reach Matthew House, Central Intake (416-338-4766) or one of the other four claimant shelters in this section may be faster.

Christie Refugee Welcome Centre Emergency shelter (76 beds / 24 private rooms across three buildings) for refugee claimant families of any ethnic, racial or religious background, with meals, clothing, settlement services, an onsite health clinic, after-school children's programs, and a dedicated Centre for Refugee Children for unaccompanied/separated minors. christiestreetrc.com 43 Christie St; office 416-588-9277 (Christie's own general contact line — not a guaranteed intake line). Do not use 416-338-4766 to reach Christie specifically — that number is the City's Central Intake line and routes to the general shelter queue, which may or may not have a Christie bed available. Reconfirmed 2026-07-30: both the Christie homepage and its own "Find Shelter" page still list 416-588-9277 as Christie's number. Note for the atlas, not a change to this entry: Christie's own "Find Shelter" page currently lists Central Intake's number as 1-877-338-3398, which does not match the City of Toronto's own Shelters page (416-338-4766, fetched the same day) — flagged in this lane's report for whoever maintains the Central Intake row (FT-9106); not resolved here since it may simply be a toll-free alias, and this guide never repeats that specific number from Christie's own page.

Adam House Short-term shelter (21 people at a time) for refugee claimants, with meals, information and referral, legal-aid/immigration and social-assistance help, plus four transitional homes for continued support. Explicitly welcomes claimants "regardless of race, ethnicity or creed." adamhouse.org 430 Gladstone Ave; call (416) 538-2836 for intake. (A second number on the same contact page, 416-538-3416, reaches current residents only — do not use it to ask about a bed.) Average length of stay, ages served, and intake hours are not published by the provider; phone first to confirm what's actually offered. Interim Federal Health Program (IFHP) — how to access it Temporary federal health coverage for refugee (asylum) claimants: hospital services, health-care-professional visits, vision and emergency dental care, prescription medications, and one Immigration Medical Exam, lasting until you get provincial health insurance or leave Canada. canada.ca — IFHP health coverage IRCC determines eligibility automatically when your claim is filed and issues an IFHP letter/Acknowledgement of Claim you can use to access care. Manage coverage online via an IRCC account, or by mail to the nearest IRCC office. Coverage is cancelled automatically if your claim is withdrawn or declared abandoned.

Legal Aid Ontario — Refugee Law Office Free help preparing your Basis of Claim form and representation at your refugee-determination hearing before the Immigration and Refugee Board, written appeals of denied claims, Federal Court judicial-review applications, and danger-opinion submissions — for refugee claimants and protected persons who qualify financially. legalaid.on.ca — Refugee Law Office Toronto office: 20 Dundas St W, Suite 201, 416-977-8111. Must meet LAO's standard financial- eligibility test; interpreters and document translation arranged as needed. Canadian Centre for Victims of Torture (CCVT) Free mental-health counselling (in-house psychiatrists, psychologists, family doctors; crisis intervention; individual, family and group counselling), settlement services, and legal advocacy for survivors of torture, war, genocide and crimes against humanity — predominantly refugees and protected persons, though the provider's own page does not restate an immigration-status policy, so this guide states the service as described rather than assuming a status gate either way. ccvt.org 194 Jarvis St, 2nd Floor; 416-363-1066, toll-free 1-877-292-2288. CultureLink Bike Host Free cycling mentorship pairing newcomers with an experienced cyclist mentor, including a temporary loaner bike (for the program's duration, not a keep-forever giveaway) and group rides. Two status facts worth knowing before you count on a benefit: - The Canada Child Benefit explicitly excludes active refugee claimants. Per the federal program's own page: "An individual with a Refugee Protection Claimant Document is not eligible for the CCB" — only protected persons (a positive Notice of Decision from the IRB), PRs, citizens, a temporary resident who has lived in Canada 18+ months with a valid permit in month 19 that doesn't say "does not confer status," or a person registered/entitled to be registered under the Indian Act, qualify. A family budgeting on the assumption CCB will arrive before their claim is decided will be wrong. Reconfirmed 2026-07-30 via CRA's own "Who can apply" page (last modified 2025-11-20): the full eligibility list and the refugee-claimant exception above are exactly as CRA states it today. No change.

  • Ontario Works and ODSP are open to refugee claimants from the date the claim is filed — not only after a positive decision — per Ontario's own policy directives; visitors are not eligible unless they've filed a refugee claim or a PR application. For the full income-support picture, see Guide 02 Claim Everything.

General settlement help — whatever your papers say

These are settlement agencies whose core, named service is helping any newcomer navigate life in Ontario — orientation, documentation help, referrals, interpretation. Kababayan Multicultural Centre Completely free settlement counselling, employment services, youth, seniors' and women's services. Work, English classes, and getting your credentials recognized

These three needs are covered in full elsewhere in the fleet, to avoid stating the same fact twice in two guides:

  • Employment help built for newcomers — ACCES Employment, Kababayan, Arab Community Centre of Toronto, Afghan Women's Organization, Newcomer Women's Services Toronto, and internationally-trained-professional credential guidance (CICIC, WES) are all covered in Guide 12 Jobs & Employment's "If you're a newcomer to Canada" section, including which employment doors require a scheduled intake versus a phone call.

  • English classes (LINC) — the eligibility question this guide flagged is now settled. LINC's eligibility, per the Toronto District School Board's own "Requirements" page (fetched 2026-07-30): you must be a Permanent Resident, a Protected Person, or a Convention Refugee, at least 18 years old, not already registered in a CLIC program, and you must take a placement test at a Language Assessment Centre. Naturalized citizens, temporary residents (work/study permit holders), and refugee claimants who have not yet received a positive decision are not eligible for LINC — this is stated as a positive eligibility list, not a hedge. Confirmed independently the same day by the Toronto Catholic District School Board's own page, which puts it even more plainly: "Permanent residents and convention refugees, 18 years or older, are eligible. (LINC courses are not open to Naturalized Canadian citizens and refugee claimants)." If LINC's door is closed to you because you're a naturalized citizen or a refugee claimant, a broader door exists at the same board: TCDSB's own (non-LINC) Adult ESL program is open to "Naturalized Canadian citizens, permanent residents, refugee claimants and convention refugees" 18+, plus domestic workers admitted under the Live-in Caregiver program — visitors are still not eligible for either program, and temporary work/study permit holders outside the Live-in Caregiver stream remain excluded from both. Full class details live in the Learn Free: ESL to university guide (fleet #11); this guide carries only the status fact, since it cuts across both guides. tdsb.on.ca — LINC Requirements · tcdsb.org — ESL and LINC eligibility

  • Credential recognition and bridge training for internationally trained professionals (Ontario Bridge Training Program) belong to the Free Training & Credentials guide (fleet #10), not here — this guide is about status and settlement, not skills training.

  • Occupation-specific Language Training (OSLT) is workplace language training, so it sits with the language and learning programs in Learn Free: ESL to university (fleet #11), not with the trades and credential programs in fleet #10.
