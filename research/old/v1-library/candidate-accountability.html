Candidate Accountability Guide

FIVE-PILLAR FRAMEWORK (campaign scoring system)

Each candidate is scored against the five key campaign arguments:

Pillar What "Aligned" Looks Like
P1 — AG Strategy Publicly supports a provincial homelessness strategy with targets; endorsed Bill 28 or equivalent
P2 — OW Advocacy Has pushed province to raise OW/ODSP; advocated for shelter-allowance deduction fix
P3 — HART Hub Distinguishes treatment (HART) from housing; calls for independent evaluation and guaranteed housing pathways
P4 — COHB Restoration Publicly demanded provincial COHB restoration to $54M (300 households/month)
P5 — HSCIS Support Voted Yes on EC7.7 (Nov 2023), PH23.3 (July 2025), and EC13.8 (June 2024)

Overall ratings: HIGH (aligned on 4-5 pillars) | MODERATE (2-3 pillars) | MIXED (split record) | LOW (0-1 pillars / opposed)

THE FOUR DECISIVE COUNCIL VOTES

Item Date Motion Result Key Dissenters
EC7.7 Nov 8, 2023 HSCIS adoption — build 20 permanent shelters 22-1 ✅ Holyday (sole No)
EX9.3 Nov 8, 2023 HousingTO expansion to 65,000 homes 24-1 ✅ Holyday (sole No)
EC13.8 June 27, 2024 Encampment strategy — people-first protocol 21-2 ✅ Bradford + Holyday
PH23.3 July 24, 2025 Advance 6 HSCIS shelter sites 22-3 ✅ Bradford + Holyday + Pasternak

A 2021 precedent, found via Task S's TMMIS sweep, July 2, 2026 — extends this pattern two years earlier than any other confirmed instance. At the same meeting where Council formally reiterated "its commitment to taking a housing first and human rights approach," Councillor Holyday moved a competing, carried amendment: "City Council adopt a goal of zero encampments." Councillor Joe Cressy moved a separate carried amendment at the same meeting: "City Council adopt a goal of ending chronic homelessness." ✅ [City of Toronto Council, direct primary source, item 2021.CC34.1: https://secure.toronto.ca/council/agenda-item.do?item=2021.CC34.1] Both amendments carried at the same meeting — not a vote Holyday lost, but two different councillors successfully attaching two different framings to the same underlying motion. This is qualitatively different from the dissent pattern in the four rows above (a losing "No" vote) — it's an early, on-the-record instance of Holyday advancing an enforcement-first framing successfully, three to four years before the pattern this table otherwise documents. Worth noting precisely: this doesn't extend the "sole dissenter" streak itself (this wasn't a dissent), but it does establish Holyday's specific policy position — "zero encampments" as the stated goal, distinct from "ending chronic homelessness" — as consistent and long-standing, not a recent development.

Source: City of Toronto official council voting records; corroborated by CBC Toronto, TorontoToday, Progress Toronto

MAYORAL RACE

Olivia Chow — Incumbent Mayor (seeking re-election)

Background: Mayor since June 2023 by-election; former MP (2006-2014); lifelong housing advocate.

Housing/Homelessness platform: - Champions HSCIS as her signature capital program ("This 10-year infrastructure strategy for shelters will allow the City to stabilize its emergency accommodations") - Led the 2024 people-first encampment protocol (EC13.8) - Primary City voice on COHB restoration — her Sept 26, 2025 letter to the Province documented the cliff: "$7.95M for 2026 vs $19.75M for 2025 and $38M for 2024" and demanded restoration to $54M (enabling 300 households/month to exit shelter) - Advocates for permanent provincial funding on IHAP and refugee claimant costs - Removed Brad Bradford from Planning & Housing Committee Dec 16, 2024

Key statements: - "You can't jail your way out of a homelessness crisis." - "There are 15,000 people who are homeless in Toronto… and the city only has capacity to house 12,000 of them." (PH23.3 meeting, July 24, 2025) - "COHB [is] the single most effective tool we have for freeing up beds in our shelter system so that more people can come indoors from streets and parks." (Sept 26, 2025 letter)

Five-pillar score: P1✅ P2✅ P3✅ P4✅ P5✅ — HOUSING ACCOUNTABILITY: HIGH

Brad Bradford — Challenger (ex-Ward 19 Beaches-East York)

Background: Urban planner; councillor 2018-2026; removed from Planning & Housing Committee Dec 2024; 8th in 2023 mayoral race; not re-standing for W19.

Housing/Homelessness platform: - Campaign framing: "Crime, Congestion, and Cost" — frames encampments as public safety failures - Website: "Parks that have become tent cities, unsafe for children and pets" - Original proposal: encampment clearing within 48 hours when within 200m of schools/playgrounds (MM34.4) — corrected a second time, July 2, 2026 — the first correction here (framing this as "narrowed to 50m") was itself wrong, caught by checking the official Council Decision text directly rather than trusting the earlier fix. The 200-metre scope was never removed. What Councillor Fletcher's carried amendment actually did: changed the timeline from 48 to 24 hours, and added a 50-metre prioritization tier — encampments within 200m are addressed on the 24-hour timeline, with those within the closer 50m treated as highest priority. Both numbers are part of the same adopted policy; it's a two-tier structure, not a boundary that shrank from 200m to 50m. Passed November 12-13, 2025. - No documented support for COHB, OW advocacy, or Housing First

Key votes: - EC7.7 (Nov 2023): Yes ✅ (supports HSCIS in principle) - EC13.8 (June 2024): No ❌ — one of two dissenters on people-first encampment protocol - PH23.3 (July 2025): No ❌ on final item; moved (lost) to strip Third St site entirely; "voted against every single shelter" — Progress Toronto ✅

Five-pillar score: P1❌ P2❌ P3❌ P4❌ P5⚠️/❌ — HOUSING ACCOUNTABILITY: LOW/OPPOSED
