# The plan for the plan

Toronto's plan lives at [/plan](https://unitetolove.ca/plan/) — the goals, the scenario, the
ladder any organization can climb. This page is the reasoning underneath it: why these five
goals, what the arithmetic actually looks like, how the adoption architecture and the coalition
ladder fit together, and the calendar the whole sprint runs on. If /plan is the plan, this is the
plan for the plan.

## The five goals, tied together

Five goals, working as one system, not five separate projects:

- **The conversations** — ten thousand smaller conversations across Toronto in three months, each
  one returning a harvest: a vision, a complaint, or a nomination.
- **One Big Conversation** — the city-scale synthesis moment, roughly mid-October, where the
  harvest becomes one public plan.
- **The plan** — the crowd's own plan, published with receipts, discussed as widely as the city
  will carry it.
- **The candidates** — a suite of candidates who publicly commit to implementing the plan,
  self-assembled by their own adoption.
- **The coalition** — a thousand-plus organizations agreeing to sprint the next year together.

None of the five works alone. The conversations feed the plan; the plan gives candidates
something concrete to adopt; the coalition is what actually runs ten thousand conversations,
because equipping a thousand organizations to host ten conversations each is the whole arithmetic
of goal one. Pull one goal out and the machine stalls.

## The honest math

Targets are labeled targets everywhere on this project, and here is the arithmetic behind them,
shown rather than just asserted.

**Ten thousand conversations** works out to roughly a hundred and ten a day for ninety days. That
number is not met by anyone showing up to host events full-time — it's met by conversion. Toronto
already holds thousands of gatherings every week: congregations alone number in the thousands,
before counting teams, classes, meetups, and dinners. The plan doesn't create ten thousand new
events; it converts a fraction of the gatherings the city already holds into ones that harvest
something, plus seeds new circles where none exist yet. That conversion needs four things
everywhere at once: kits, a visible counter, a harvest that's one QR code away, and organizations
willing to adopt the pattern — which is exactly what the coalition ladder below is for.

**Tens of thousands co-authoring the plan** means signatures, vision submissions, room harvests,
and participants in the city-scale synthesis, added together. A crowd that size is consistent
with what a festival-scale civic moment in a city like Toronto can actually draw.

**A million or more discussing it** is a reach number, not a participation number — media cycles,
shared posts, embeds, diaspora channels carrying it further than Toronto's own borders. It is
never published as something achieved. It's published as an invitation: help us get there.

**A thousand-plus organizations** is the most achievable number on this board, because the tally
of organizations already mapped across the city — congregations, unions, clubs, associations —
already runs past a thousand before a single new one is added. The ask ladder (below) starts at
zero cost, which is why this is also the number that makes the conversations arithmetic work: a
thousand organizations hosting ten conversations each is ten thousand conversations. That's the
whole machine, and it needs no permission, including ours.

One naming note, held deliberately: the specific instrument used for the city-scale opinion
mapping isn't named publicly yet. It's a real, proven, open-source tool already used at city and
national scale elsewhere — we're naming it once our own pilot has run and we can back the name
with our own receipt, not before. Naming a tool before we've run it invites a gotcha; naming it
after is a receipt.

## The adoption architecture

The short version lives at [Records, not
endorsements](https://unitetolove.ca/strategy/open/03-records-not-endorsements.html) — the full
playbook, written for anyone to copy. Here is how it plugs into the five goals: the plan (goal
three) is the thing candidates are asked to adopt; the adoption record (goal four) is what
publishes as a result; and the whole architecture exists because a commons that picks favourites
stops being a commons. Adoption, not endorsement, is what makes the crowd's plan carry weight at
the ballot box without this project ever telling anyone how to vote.

## The coalition ladder

How a thousand organizations happens: one ask at a time, each one optional, each one a real
receipt.

1. **Pin** — a zero-cost map presence. An organization shows up on the map; that's the whole ask.
2. **Host** — one conversation, in the organization's own room, under its own name, using a kit.
3. **Quest** — adopting one of the six citywide quests (happiest, healthiest, safest, cleanest,
   friendliest, most beautiful) with a public scoreboard.
4. **Sprint covenant** — the year-one commitment, signable, public: "we will point at the same
   goals at the same time." The [covenant itself](https://unitetolove.ca/plan/covenant.html) is
   live and signable now.

Every rung is optional; organizations climb at their own pace; only the aggregate count of each
rung publishes — never a roster, never a ranking of which organization has climbed furthest.

## The calendar

The full narrative version, and why the year runs the shape it does, is at [A year,
school-shaped](https://unitetolove.ca/strategy/open/05-a-year-school-shaped.html). Here is the
arc itself, month by month:

- **August** — setup. The library, the kits, the rooms, the counter, and the candidate field all
  standing before the city takes its September breath.
- **September** — ignition. Every network in the city opens at once during the first week; all
  six quests launch together as evolving threads, each with a live scoreboard.
- **Late September into October** — the six weeks of wins: the conversation counter runs public,
  the coalition climbs from pins to hosts, the harvest window is open, and the adoption question
  goes out to every candidate in every race.
- **Mid-to-late October** — One Big Conversation and the State of the Conversation publish; the
  adoption record goes public. October 26 is the ballot — the mid-sprint scoreboard, not the
  finish line; the sprint is already six weeks old and does not pause for the count.
- **November** — the new council is sworn in with the plan and the adoption record already on its
  desk.
- **Winter** — the committee-room months, where commitments hold their dates or don't.
- **Spring** — the work surfaces where everyone can see it.
- **Summer** — an honest public audit, failures published in the same typeface as wins.
- **December, every year** — rest, on purpose. No scoreboards, no asks. The gatherings don't stop;
  they just stop counting.
- **The following September** — a second ignition that nobody has to light, because last year's
  participants are this year's hosts.
- **Year two and beyond** — the arc keeps running to the next reckoning, and eventually to
  export: the whole playbook translated, versioned, and handed to any city that wants to fork it.
