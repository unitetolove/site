The Listening Project

Toronto’s future shouldn’t be one person’s idea of it. This page is where anyone can add to it — a vision, something you know, or something that’s happened to you or someone you know. Every way to send something below works today, through channels that already exist — email, a voice memo, a link to a video you control. Nothing new to sign up for, nothing to install.

Three things you can tell us

Your vision

What you want Toronto to become — a sentence or a page, however you’d say it.

What you know

A problem you’ve seen up close, or an idea you think would actually work.

What’s happened

Something you’ve lived through, or watched happen to someone else, in this city’s systems.

Your vision for Toronto

What would you want this city to become, if it actually worked for everyone in it? Big or small, a dream or a fix — write it however you’d say it out loud.

  • Write it: ✉️ unknownsoldier@unitetolove.ca — opens an email with a short template. Delete it and write your own words if you’d rather.
  • Say it: record a voice memo on your phone and attach it to the same email.
  • Show it: record a short video, put it somewhere you already control (an unlisted YouTube link, a Google Drive or Dropbox link, anything you can share), and paste the link into the same email. We don’t need you to upload anything to us directly.

What you know

Maybe you’ve worked in a system that’s broken, or seen a fix somewhere else that actually works, or have a professional’s eye on a problem the rest of us only guess at. This is where that goes.

  • Write it: ✉️ unknownsoldier@unitetolove.ca — same address, its own template.
  • Say it: a voice memo, attached, works exactly as well as writing it out.
  • Show it: same as above — a link to a video you control, pasted into the email, rather than an upload.

What’s happened

If what you want to tell us is about something that happened to you, or to someone you know — a way this city’s systems failed someone — read this part first. It works differently from the two sections above, on purpose.

Nothing is recorded or kept until you’ve told us how it can be used. Right now, all we’re asking for is a way to reach you. Please don’t write your story into an email yet — a real person will reach out first, talk it through with you, and only then, if you want to go further, record anything.

When that conversation happens, you get to choose two separate things, independently, and either answer is fine: first, how much of what you say — if any of it — can ever be shared publicly, and in what form; second, whether what you tell us can ever be used in an actual complaint to an official body, like a city watchdog. Sharing it publicly is a different question from sharing it with the authorities — you can say yes to one and no to the other, in either direction, and that’s entirely your call.

If you ever do record something with us: it’s turned into text by a program that runs on our own machines — never sent to ChatGPT, Google, or any other outside AI company, ever, for any reason.

You can change your mind about any of this later, including after today.

To start, just tell us how to reach you:

  • Write it: ✉️ unknownsoldier@unitetolove.ca — just a way to reach you and a good time to call.
  • Say it: a short voice memo saying the same — a way to reach you — works too.

If you need to talk to someone right now, whether or not you talk to us at all: Toronto Community Crisis Service / 211 Ontario — dial 211. Distress Centres of Greater Toronto — 416-408-4357. Canada’s 988 Suicide Crisis Helpline — call or text 988.

What happens to what you send

Everything gets read by a person — nothing sits in an unwatched inbox. What we can’t honestly promise is a personal reply to every single message; at the scale we hope this reaches, that’s not a promise we can keep, so we won’t make it. What we do promise instead: what people are telling us shows up in the open, regularly, as an aggregate — counts, themes, what changed because of it — the same radical-transparency standard the rest of this site holds itself to. Being heard should be demonstrable, not just claimed.

Sign the declaration

This is the declaration, in full — the declaration of August 1, 2026. This exact wording is what you sign, online or on paper.

The Declaration — Toronto’s shelter and homelessness system

We, the undersigned, live in Toronto. Some of us have slept in its shelters. All of us pay for them.

Here is what the public record already shows:

  • A shelter bed costs Toronto $49,640 a year, as reported by the city’s Auditor General. Housing First support — an actual home, with help — costs $20,367, and $6,311 after the savings it creates.
  • In winter 2023-24, Toronto lost 12,742 shelter bed-nights to process rules — while an average of 174 or more people a night called for a bed and went unmatched. That is the city’s own audit.
  • A single adult without a home gets $343 a month — Ontario Works’ shelter allowance only exists once you already have a home to pay for. That is 13 cents of every poverty-line dollar.
  • This year, federal homelessness funding for Toronto was cut 68%.
  • None of this is a mystery. Toronto’s homelessness is the product of five specific policy failures, most built in a single 1990s window — which means it can be unbuilt by policy too.
  • Scotland put 2,154 people straight into homes; 83% are still housed. The model works when governments actually fund it.

And here is what the record cannot show, but we can: we have lived this system, worked inside it, and listened at its doors. What we have seen and heard does not match what the public is told. That testimony is being gathered, with consent and care, and it will be heard.

Therefore we demand, of every level of government and every officer with the power to act:

  1. A full, independent investigation of the homelessness-serving system — the system as a whole, and each and every component, supplier, agency, and organization within it. We accuse no one. We demand the investigation that would let the truth accuse or acquit — by the officers who exist for exactly this: the city’s Auditor General, Ombudsman Toronto, the Ontario Ombudsman, the Federal Housing Advocate.
  2. Full financial transparency. Every public dollar that enters this system, traceable to what it bought, published where any resident can read it.
  3. Reform to what the evidence already proves: a system that houses people instead of warehousing them — because the record above shows housing people costs less than what we are doing now.
  4. A public answer. Not a statement — a response, on the record, to each of these demands.

Sign if you’re with us. And if this system has touched you — file. A complaint with your name and a file number is a voice that cannot be averaged away. We will show you how, and help every step we lawfully can.

Every figure above carries a primary-source receipt; the full receipts publish with this project’s research pages.

Sign the declaration above

Your name and email are held for this signature record only — never sold or shared, never published as a list. Unsubscribe or ask us to delete your record anytime at unknownsoldier@unitetolove.ca.

Sign in person: paper sign-up will be available at gatherings and events around the city, and the printable signature sheet works today — print it, sign it, and mail or hand it to a collector. If you’d like to help collect signatures in person, tell us that too.
