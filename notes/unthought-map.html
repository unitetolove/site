THE UNTHOUGHT MAP — what we haven’t written, built, needed, or dared yet

2026-08-04 · Mobilization Jane, thinking ahead of the ask (“extrapolate and think for me”). Everything below is PROPOSED-for-your-stars; every idea already passes the standing laws unless a ⚖️/⚠️ note says it needs a ruling. Nothing here is a send, a build order, or a publish. Companion to the publish gap sweep (that = finished work unpublished; this = work that doesn’t exist yet).

W · What else to WRITE

  1. ★★★ The letter to the seven-in-ten — every outlet writes for voters; nobody writes to the 70% who stayed home, without shame, as the protagonists. Our §3 canon is this letter waiting to happen.
  2. ★★★ 25 ward math cards — “Your ward was decided by N votes; a block party decides it.” Generated from data we already hold (2022 margins + current filings). Poster/QR-able, share-able, the single most localizable thing we can make.
  3. ★★ “How to read a platform” — teaches the day-one standard (dates? costs? authority?) without naming anyone. The 0/70-commit-to-a-date finding is its spine.
  4. ★★ “What a mayor can actually do” — strong-mayor powers + city-vs-province jurisdiction, plain language. Voters and media both lack it; our jurisdiction maps already carry it.
  5. ★★★ “Acclamation nation” — 291 zero-candidate races, 42.7% acclamation-track, province- wide (SOC receipt). Nobody else has this dataset. A democracy-health story with our name on the receipts. Pairs with the appointed-chairs finding as a two-punch.
  6. ★ “The missing infrastructure” — the tribe registry’s own discovery: umbrella orgs dead (Somali umbrella 3× confirmed, seed-era chambers gone). A loving, dignity-checked piece about civic infrastructure Toronto lost — that invites communities to rebuild it.
  7. The FAQ/objections page — “Is this a party? A cult? Who pays for this? Why anonymous?” Asked and answered in-voice, once, linkable forever (the skeptic letter’s Q&A, standing).
  8. ★★★★★ The one-year sprint scenario — the narrative’s “What could Toronto do in one year, all-out?” expanded into a concrete, dated, joyful scenario (the plan-shaped sibling of A Tuesday in the Practiced City).
  9. ★ “We asked 18 AIs to fix homelessness” — the multi-AI capture experiment written up as a public methods piece. Novel, press-friendly, and it IS our AI-disclosure brand in action. Add frame to end: “I am homeless, and I agree. Just wait until you hear my stories.”
  10. Classroom pack — Ontario grade-5/10 civics is mandatory and teachers are starving for election materials. A teacher-facing election kit (⚠️ minors gate: materials for teachers, never collection from kids). A channel with zero competition.

P · What else to PRODUCE

  1. ★ Candidate-diff alerts — the web-harvest already hash-diffs platforms weekly; publish it as a feed (“what changed this week”). Media subscribe; the biweekly edition gets a standing “movers” section for free.
  2. ★ Debate-in-a-box — an all-candidates-meeting kit any room can run: fair rules, the question bank, invite templates, moderation script, accessibility checklist. Fills the civic vacuum where ratepayer groups used to be; equip-never-convene by construction. Also Citizens Assemblies in a Box.
  3. The election calendar (.ics) — subscribe-able: advance polls, deadlines, voting day. An hour’s work; embeds everywhere forever.
  4. Embeddable widgets — the count, the plan scoreboard, ward cards, as one-line embeds any newsletter/blog/campus paper can carry. The commons spreads by embed, not by visits.
  5. Voting-plan generator — pick your ward → your dates, your places, your ID list, printable (turnout plan’s explainer, productized).
  6. ★ WhatsApp-first share cards — the diaspora’s channels are WhatsApp/Telegram; translated image cards sized for them (not Twitter). our internal work-package record priorities give the languages. To be developed after the print pack below.
  7. ★ Print pack v1 — spec exists (PRINT_PACK_V1_SPEC); library-printable posters + the mirror-moment card (“the person who decides Toronto’s future” — a literal mirror surface; cheesy exactly once, memorable forever).

N · What we NEED and don’t have

  1. ★★ A press kit / media room page — boilerplate, logo pack, contact route, correction SLA, the who-is-TUS answer, “how to fact-check us” guide. Media will hit the site within days of any attention play; today they’d find nothing.
  2. ⚖️ A Municipal Elections Act third-party-advertising read — before Answer Day, the scoreboard, or any paid print at scale: counsel confirms our neutral-library posture sits outside MEA third-party-advertiser registration (it should — no support/oppose content — but this is exactly the cheap-insurance class the estate routes to counsel). Assigned to the counsel package; hold for now.
  3. ★ OG/social preview images — every page needs an og:image or every share renders as a bare link. Small build, large multiplier; belongs in day 2–3.
  4. ★★★★ Privacy-respecting analytics — Cloudflare aggregate stats only; without it the funnel plan (one rung per surface) is flying blind. Fits measurement-without-surveillance.
  5. A distribution answer for the biweekly — editions need a subscribe path (RSS at minimum; the deferred newsletter question returns with CASL posture attached).
  6. The bus-factor doc — if you’re offline for a week, what dies? Rail, deploys, sends, corrections SLA — named holders or named degradation. Exit-is-the-credential applies to infrastructure too.
  7. Corrections SLA — a stated clock (“checked within 48h”) once media attention arrives; the honesty brand needs a response time, not just a policy.

X · Sexy attention plays (all lawful, all receipts-first)

  1. ★★★ The 0/70 moment — “Seventy published platforms. Zero commit to a date.” One number, one poster, one press release, the day-one standard as the answer. Ready today, pending confirmation the candidate research is finished, and scoped to Toronto if that is where the count applies.
  2. Answer Day — announce one date; deliver the equal question to every candidate publicly; publish a live answered/silent count (per-candidate pages, never a ranking — a recorded standing decision shape already ruled). The count itself becomes the story arc for weeks.
  3. The welcome-count reveal — keep the count dark until it’s real, then reveal with a comparison the city feels (“more people than a full council chamber joined Toronto’s conversation this week”).
  4. “Someone already solved it” weekly drop — one proven world solution + the Toronto org that could run it (with their consent), every week to voting day. Positive, tribally shareable, feeds org pins.
  5. ★ The AI-transparency angle — “an anonymous Torontonian and a fleet of AIs built the city’s largest civic library — and published every receipt.” Tech press (BetaKit/The Logic/Ars) will take this now; Written With Machines is the anchor artifact.
  6. The Mamdani window — the NYC piece timed to any Mamdani news cycle; comparison content rides existing waves instead of making its own.
  7. ★★ The green pill — the narrative’s Matrix beat as the share-visual grammar (concept, not film imagery — ⚠️ no WB assets). Blue/red/green tricolour posters with the one link.
  8. ★ Trustee races spotlight — the least-covered races on every ballot; “the most ignored election in Toronto” writes itself from SOC data and serves the least-served voters.

C · Candidates’ needs nobody serves (identical-to-all, firewall-clean)

  1. ★★ Ward data packs — per ward: demographics, 2022 poll-by-poll results, turnout history, advance-poll map. Public data nobody assembles; the single highest-value gift, and it makes the library indispensable to every camp equally.
  2. The compliance calendar — nomination mechanics, the 25 signatures, filing fee, finance rules and dates, sign bylaws, third-party rules — sourced official, wrong-date-is-worst- error seal bar (same as turnout).
  3. ★ Free campaign stack guide — free website/email/canvass tools, printable walk lists from public data, accessibility (AODA) obligations. Extends CAMPAIGN_IN_A_BOX.
  4. Question-bank prep pack — “the 66 questions, with the library’s receipts to study from.” Framed as exam prep, offered to every registrant identically. The current 66 questions need a redo.
  5. ★ All-candidates-meeting survival guide — pairs with Debate-in-a-box from the candidate’s side of the table.
  6. “After the ballot” page — win or lose, here’s how you stay in civic life (day-one packages for winners; the commons’ gardeners for everyone else). Nobody builds the losers’ pipeline; it’s where next cycle’s candidates come from.

M · What media will need (build before they ask)

  1. ★ The data desk — clean downloadable CSVs (races, candidates, platform census, margins) + data dictionary + attribution license. Journalists take data gifts and cite the source forever. (Pairs with gap sweep §6 open-data.)
  2. ★ Story-ready receipt bundles — the shadow-report press pack; the homelessness numbers pack; acclamation-nation pack. Each: key figures, methodology paragraph, receipt links.
  3. Election-night sheet — margins-vs-2022 comparison prepped in advance; at 8:05pm on Oct 26 every newsroom wants exactly this and nobody will have built it.
  4. The fact-check invitation — standing offer with the correction SLA: “break something, we’ll credit you.” First fact-check = beginning of the relationship (narrative’s media door, operationalized).
  5. ★ Detailed profiles on non-frontrunning candidates

R · Research we haven’t looked at

  1. ★ Poll-by-poll turnout deserts — block-level 2022 turnout mapped; where the seven-in-ten actually live. Targets block circles, ward cards, and the listening project at once.
  2. ★ All-candidates-meeting census — who hosted them per ward historically; the vacuum map that justifies Debate-in-a-box. Toronto only to start, scalable beyond.
  3. Local media deserts — wards with zero local coverage (media landscape registry × geography); tells us where the commons IS the local paper.
  4. Six quests baselines — day-zero measurables for happiest/healthiest/safest/cleanest/ friendliest/most-beautiful, so the scoreboards start honest.
  5. Campaign-finance patterns 2022 — incumbent money advantage, donor concentration — aggregate and institutional only (conditions, never persons).
  6. Newcomer voting-rights confusion — the PR-can’t-vote misinfo space; an inoculation explainer serves the §8 tribes directly.
  7. Poster-bylaw audit — where posting is actually legal, ward by ward (“post only where you have the right,” operationalized — protects every volunteer).
  8. Cost-of-the-broken-system per household — one sealed number synthesizing the flagships (shelter + congestion + housing). Hardest seal on this list; highest poster value.
  9. ★ Faith & festival calendar for Sept–Oct — the inflection register made concrete: when each Wave-2 door naturally opens (Rosh Hashanah, Navratri/Diwali, harvest suppers, homecoming).

S · Strategies we haven’t tried

  1. Pins before people — ask the 108 anchor orgs ONLY for a map pin (zero-cost, zero-commitment ask, org’s own front door shown). A full map is proof-of-crowd that precedes any headcount, and it’s the Wave-2 bridge inventory building itself.
  2. ★★★ Campus co-publication — student papers co-publish the ward browser/SOC data under their masthead (published artifacts only; firewall clean). They need content; we need fall reach.
  3. Adopt-a-ward for diaspora orgs — the ward data pack as the gift; their neighbourhood’s math as the hook.
  4. ★★ The civic hour ask — institutions give staff one paid hour to vote/attend a local all-candidates night. A corporate/institutional door we’ve never designed; peer-city precedent exists. Proposed as an early priority: ask, and encourage people to request time off now for upcoming civic action, and to encourage their organizations to develop enabling policies for civic participation over the next three months.
  5. ★ Election workers as multipliers — the city pays and trains poll workers; routing our people INTO city jobs makes the most-informed civic actors ours-and-the-city’s at once (turnout plan already lists the jobs one-pager; this makes it a strategy, not a leaflet).
  6. The pre-commitment — publish BEFORE the election what happens after it, win or lose (the sprint year, the quests, the gardeners). Inoculates against “it’s over” and recruits everyone afraid of backing a loser.
  7. “Take your circle to City Hall” kit — council attendance as a kit rooms adopt (never a convening of ours): agenda-reading guide, deputation how-to, debrief questions.

The top ten, if you star nothing else

0/70 moment (X1) · ward math cards (W2) · press kit (N1) · acclamation nation (W5) · Answer Day (X2, after the ⚖️ N2 read) · ward data packs (C1) · pins before people (S1) · candidate- diff feed (P1) · turnout deserts (R1) · AI-transparency angle (X5, anchored on Written With Machines).

Laws checked: every idea equips rather than convenes; candidate-facing items identical-to-all; no person-targeting anywhere; counts/figures stay receipt-bound; minors gate flagged; one counsel flag (⚖️ N2) named rather than assumed away.
