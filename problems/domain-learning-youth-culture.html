PROBLEMS — Learning, youth & culture

Five rows: the education system's decay, the school-board governance shell around it, youth opportunity and belonging, a four-problem compact cluster of quality-of-life layers (arts/nightlife, libraries, heritage, public realm), and professional sports ownership — the second of the two newest rows in the whole register, landed the same session this rollup was written.

X05 · The education system's decay

The problem. Ontario faces a teacher shortage the ministry's own documents expect to worsen from 2027, felt most in the GTA. The Auditor General found a 72% unfilled-absence rate for educational assistants at Toronto Catholic in 2023–24, with special-education support buckling. EQAO's own release shows 51% of Grade 6 students met the provincial math standard in 2024–25 — a third year at roughly half. Per-pupil funding fell about 1% in real terms for 2026–27, with a $6.5B cumulative shortfall since 2018–19. Students — special-needs students first — and burning-out teachers and EAs are hit hardest. Full receipts: register entry X05.

What we're asking. With a teacher shortage set to worsen from 2027 and a 72% unfilled-absence rate for EAs already documented, what does "more teachers, better paid" actually cost — and is anyone building toward that number, or just absorbing the shortfall? - The teacher-fear question, answered honestly — teachers are being told AI will replace them, and that fear is real; what's the deal that makes a teacher's life better — AI takes the grading, prep, and paperwork; the human keeps the relationship, the belief, the accountability — and is the honest workforce story actually more teachers, better paid, doing the human part? (education EDU-Q74) - What education money actually buys — free doesn't mean free, it means someone else pays; what's the honest funding model for a city where learning is a public utility, given the finding that more spending doesn't linearly buy more learning — what does the money genuinely need to fund versus what it currently buys in signaling and gatekeeping? (education EDU-Q90) - What is the actual staffing math behind the ministry's own 2027 teacher-shortage projection — how many additional teachers and EAs does closing the 72% unfilled-absence rate require, and has anyone published that number? - Of the $6.5B cumulative real-terms funding shortfall since 2018–19, how much of that gap tracks directly onto the EQAO score decline, and how much is a separate story (staffing, classroom violence, absenteeism)? - Is there any Toronto-specific (not province-wide) breakdown of the 72% EA unfilled-absence rate — does it concentrate in specific schools or neighbourhoods?

The shelf. education-reimagined-k12 (full set) · most-educated-population-learning-city (full set).

Unexplored. Register: "none blocking — EQAO + funding receipts landed above (AG's 72% EA figure still secondary-corroborated; primary AG pull owed at mint)." Worth flagging: the education program's 58-question extended bank is rich but leans philosophical — literacy-in-an-AI-era, experiential learning, "the city as classroom" — rather than the specific crisis metrics (teacher shortage, EA absence rate, EQAO scores, funding decline) the register's own X05 entry is built on. That mismatch is itself worth noting for whoever next extends the EDU bank.

H3 · School-board governance

The problem. The governance shell around X05: the provincial funding formula and supervision powers versus elected trustees' shrinking real authority. School data joins a broader inventory being built for 2030-decision tracking. Full receipts: register entry H3.

What we're asking. Trustees on this fall's ballot govern boards whose real fiscal room the province sets — so what does a trustee actually control, versus what only looks like control? - What specific powers do elected trustees retain under the current provincial funding-formula and supervision regime — and which of those powers have been narrowed or removed in the last two provincial terms? - Has the province ever placed a Toronto-area school board under supervision, and if so, what changed for students and staff during that period versus before and after? - Is there a voter-facing explainer anywhere of what a school trustee can and cannot actually decide — given how little real fiscal room the register says the province leaves them?

  • Who governs schools best in the world — and is the honest lesson from Finland and the high-performers that governance structure matters less than the standing of the teaching profession itself? What would that reframe mean for what a Toronto trustee should even try to do?

  • What would it take for a school-board election to be an informed vote — a public, plain-language record of what each board decided, spent, and achieved this term — and who should build it?

X11 · Youth opportunity & belonging

The problem. Post-education pathways, first-job precarity (overlapping X03/X01), and belonging as a violence-prevention substrate (overlapping A6). The register names youth un/underemployment as its own receipt gap. Full receipts: register entry X11.

What we're asking. What actually happens to a Toronto young person in the first two years after high school or post-secondary — and does anyone track it as one pathway rather than three separate silos (education, labour, safety)? - What is the youth un/underemployment receipt the register names as missing — does any Toronto-specific number exist, disaggregated from the provincial or national youth-unemployment rate? - Where does the overlap with A6 (gun and youth violence) actually show up in the data — is there any receipt connecting first-job precarity or post-education disengagement to the neighbourhoods where violence concentrates? - Is there a single body (municipal, provincial, or a program like this one) that tracks a youth cohort across the education-to-work transition, or does the data end at graduation and pick up again only if something goes wrong?

  • Iceland's Planet Youth collapsed teen substance use by redesigning youth life itself — organized activity, parental time, late-night hours — not by messaging. What is the Toronto version of redesigning the conditions of being young here, and which pieces are municipal?
  • What happens when young people design the programs themselves — where has youth co-design produced measurably better uptake and outcomes than adult-designed youth programming, and what would Toronto hand over to try it?

The shelf. youth-opportunity-post-jobs-belonging (full set).

Unexplored. Register: "Receipt gap: youth un/underemployment." The question bank is essentially silent — searches across every program for youth-unemployment, first-job precarity, and youth belonging returned nothing aimed at this problem specifically, only tangential hits about trust in city-led technology.

H9 / E5 / D5 / D1 · Arts & nightlife · libraries & local services · heritage · public-realm maintenance

The problem. Municipally-owned quality-of-life layers; H9 (arts and nightlife) and E5 (libraries) double as connection infrastructure (see PR-X02 in the health domain). Full receipts: register entry H9/E5/D5/D1.

What we're asking.

H9 — Arts & nightlife. Is Toronto's nightlife and cultural-venue collapse a receipt anyone is actually tracking, or is it still just the vibe everyone agrees is true? - The party's physical anchor, made concrete — what are the real economics and licensing of a standing weekly all-ages, substance-optional dance institution, where does it live, who runs it so it survives past the founders' energy given the Artscape-receivership lesson, and how does an alcohol-optional model pay its rent? (connection C66) - Healthy nightlife beyond alcohol as the only social lubricant — do we save the dying venues, or repurpose dead night-time square footage for sober-optional social life? (connection C23) - Enjoyment of culture as a public-health strategy, not just consumption — festivals, food, ritual and celebration as vitality inputs, funded accordingly, reaching the isolated rather than only entertaining the already-connected. (vitality V25)

E5 — Libraries & local services. - TPL as the people's university, already trusted, free, and everywhere — what does modernizing it with an AI guide at every branch and a maker-and-experience grid actually mean, without losing the humanness — the librarian who knows you — that makes it trusted? (education EDU-Q68) - Who owns the night — night mayors, night transit, night libraries: the connection case for a 24-hour city, honest about its costs. (connection C24)

D5 — Heritage. - What is Toronto's current heritage-designation backlog or built-form preservation receipt — is there any public number for buildings at risk of demolition while awaiting a heritage evaluation? - Does heritage preservation policy conflict with the housing-supply push (C1) in any measured way — how many housing units have been delayed or blocked specifically by a heritage designation in recent years?

D1 — Public-realm maintenance. - Is there a citywide public-realm cleanliness or maintenance-response-time receipt, broken out by neighbourhood, or does this problem have no measured baseline at all in the current corpus?

The shelf. arts-culture-creative-economy-spaces (full set, H9, plus joyful-city-nightlife-festivals, full set) · libraries-civic-commons (full set, E5, plus local-services-libraries-animal-control, full set) · heritage-preservation-built-form (full set, D5) · public-realm-cleanliness-maintenance (full set, D1, plus urban-beautification-public-art, full set).

Unexplored. Register: "municipally-owned quality-of-life layers" with H9/E5 doubling as connection infrastructure — no problem-specific gaps line named for any of the four. D5 (heritage) and D1 (public realm) came back essentially silent from the question-bank search despite full shelf coverage — the corpus has written about these problems without the program banks ever asking questions aimed at them directly.

G4 · Professional sports ownership & civic power

The problem. Toronto's four major professional franchises (Leafs, Raptors, Blue Jays, TFC) are consolidating under a single private owner: Rogers Communications moved from 37.5% to 75% of Maple Leaf Sports & Entertainment in 2025 (buying BCE's stake for $4.7B) and in 2026 signed — but had not yet closed — an agreement for the remaining 25%, which would put one company in control of assets valued at $13.1B combined. Public money and land sit underneath this private consolidation: BMO Field's 2007 build was federal/provincial/city-funded, the City retains ownership, and the land's governance is now contested by Ontario's proposed Bill 68. The general academic literature on stadium/arena subsidies is close to unanimous that they do not deliver the local economic-development benefits proponents claim, and one widely-cited study found official reporting undercounts the true public subsidy by roughly 40% once land, infrastructure, and foregone tax are counted. Full receipts: register entry G4.

What we're asking. As one company moves toward full control of Toronto's major-league sport assets, does the city's ownership of the land underneath them (BMO Field, Exhibition Place) still mean anything once Bill 68 settles the authority question? - The gambling-money conflict, named honestly — gambling revenue is now structurally braided into pro sport, so partners-never-adversaries with the pro clubs runs straight into an addiction-industry funding question the program can't wish away; what's the honest ask to the pro ecosystem, and is refusing gambling sponsorship a line the grassroots commons can hold precisely because it's built on different money? (sport SP126) - What is the actual resolution of Bill 68's authority question over Exhibition Place and BMO Field — does the province end up with any operational control, or is this purely a naming/oversight dispute? - Does the roughly-40%-undercount finding on public stadium subsidies (land, infrastructure, foregone tax) apply to BMO Field's own 2007 build — has anyone actually run that specific number for Toronto? - With MLSE ownership consolidating toward a single company, what leverage — if any — does the city retain over ticket pricing, community-access programming, or local economic-development commitments tied to the venues it still owns the land under?

The shelf. pro-sport-ownership-civic-power (backgrounder only — no dayone, brief, or card exists yet for this slug).

Unexplored. Register's own gap flag: the claim set is backgrounder-sourced only, not independently re-verified for this register entry — 21 verified / 16 still being checked/2 disputed of 39 claims in the backgrounder itself, with no Toronto-specific public-subsidy rationale or lobbying-registry claim located in that file's own pass. This is one of the two newest rows in the whole register (landed the same session as this rollup) and the question bank has nothing aimed at it directly — the one adjacent hit (sport SP126, on gambling money) is the closest the corpus comes.

Across this domain — steal from the world, collect everything, find the best minds

  • Who does learning, youth, and culture best — Finland's teacher profession and Estonia's digital schooling, Iceland's Planet Youth model that collapsed teen substance use by redesigning youth life itself, Helsinki's Oodi library as the city's living room, the cities whose percent-for-art and night-economy offices actually produce culture — what EXACTLY does each do, and which pieces does Toronto steal?
  • Collect ALL existing recommendations: every education-funding analysis, youth-strategy review, arts-investment case, and library study already written for this city and province — what was recommended, costed, adopted, ignored?
  • Who are the best minds and organizations on public education, youth development, and civic culture anywhere — and which Toronto educators, youth workers, and cultural builders are already world-class and unfunded?
