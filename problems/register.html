PROBLEM REGISTER — what are Toronto and Torontonians' problems? (one of this library's own project records)

The framing of record for Toronto and Torontonians' problems: what each problem is, who's hit, scale with receipts, which government holds the lever, and the gaps — one entry per problem, organized by domain. Receipt-tier legend and conventions: see the footer.

§1 · The lived front door

PR-X14 · Cost of living (NEW v0.2 — the umbrella Torontonians actually experience)

The squeeze is the aggregation, and no single-file row captured it: general inflation has settled near 2% while the components people can't avoid haven't. Food prices are forecast up another 4–6% in 2026 — a family of four spending $17,572 on food, up ~$995 in one year [https://www.dal.ca/sites/agri-food/research/canada-s-food-price-report-2026.html], with Ontario named among provinces above the national average and the trade war itself listed among the drivers. Ontario electricity rates jumped ~29% in 2026 (Tier 1 12.0¢/kWh) even as gasoline got policy relief (consumer carbon price to $0.00 April 2025; federal fuel excise tax suspended April 2026, ~10¢/L) [https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/04/14/prime-minister-carney-suspends-federal-fuel-excise-tax-gasoline-and]. The wage floor hasn't kept up with the city: GTA living wage $27.20/hr vs minimum wage $17.60 (rising to $17.95 Oct 2026) — a $9.60/hr ≈ $14,500/yr gap for a full-time worker [https://www.ontariolivingwage.ca/updated_2025_rates]. Nearly half of Toronto food-bank users live in households with someone employed [https://www.dailybread.ca/blog/whos-hungry-2025-record-breaking-hunger/]. Who's hit: every renter and low/middle-income household; hardest at the bottom (see X06). Levers: split by component — F (fuel taxes, monetary/trade), P (electricity rates, minimum wage, rent guideline), M (property tax, transit fares, water rates, rec fees — the city's own price lines). Depth: decomposes into C1 (housing), C5 (rents), C7 (utilities), H2 (food), X06 (poverty); this umbrella row exists because the lived problem is their sum. Verified & MINTED 2026-08-08: food-price forecast → CL-50000 · GTA living wage $27.20 → CL-50009 (OLWN primary, Nov 2025 — its basket method doubles as the all-in cost-of-living receipt this entry's gap asked for) · minimum wage $17.60→$17.95 Oct 2026 (ontario.ca). Gaps: wage-distribution receipt.

§2 · Housing & homelessness

C1★ · Housing supply & affordability

Toronto remains one of the least affordable major markets in the country: CMHC's 2024 affordability ratio puts Toronto at 74% of income to carry the average home vs 54% Canada-wide (CL-0015), on a national base that is itself the lowest housing-per-capita in the G7 — 424 units/1,000 residents vs a G7 average of 471 (CL-0018). Supply is moving backwards against the province's own target: starts fell 89.3k → 74.6k → 65.4k (2023–25) against the ~150k/yr the 1.5M-by-2031 target needs, and the province projects just 64.8k for 2026 — at the 2025 pace the target arrives around 2046 [https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/ontario-housing-start-projections-9.7145188]. Meanwhile Ontario municipalities carry 77% of social-housing expenditures vs 14% provincial / 9% federal (CL-0002). Who's hit: first-time buyers priced out; renters absorbing the spillover; the GGH's next 4M residents (57% growth 1996–2021, CL-0028). Levers: M (zoning, approvals, DCs — Planning Act authority), P (PPS, Building Faster targets, RTA), F (CMHC programs, $6B infrastructure fund CL-0016). Depth: backgrounder 38 claims/37 verified (Housing Supply & Affordability — Backgrounder). Need-vs-delivery (filled 2026-08-08 ✓): HousingTO targets 65,000 rent-controlled homes by 2030; ~30,000 approved (46%) by end-2024 — but only 632 affordable homes COMPLETED in 2024 [https://www.toronto.ca/news/unlocking-affordable-housing-city-of-toronto-provides-annual-progress-update-on-housing-action-plans/] — approvals outpace completions ~47:1 (MINTED: CL-50003; starts trajectory CL-50002, 2026 Ontario Budget Ch. 2 primary). Gaps: completion-rate trend over multiple years.

C2★ · Homelessness & encampments

The system count alone: 12,304 people in Toronto's shelter and bridging programs at the 2024 Street Needs Assessment, 51.6% refugee claimants (CL-120089) — and the city's public figures for "actively homeless" currently contradict each other across its own pages (10,569 vs 9,251 for the same metric and month; the 2024 SNA's 15,418 is a different population measure — the live 🚩 docket: figure contradictions). Indigenous people are overrepresented in every SNA cycle ever run (CL-120077); 27.3% of Indigenous adults in Toronto have experienced homelessness (CL-120086). The capital plan to fix the shelter system is itself short: $166.9M unfunded of the $674.5M ten-year HSCIS budget, 13 of 20 sites funded (CL-120114), while HousingTO's outstanding capital commitments grew between its own annual reports (CL-120123). Who's hit: the people themselves first (mortality, dignity); Indigenous residents and refugee claimants disproportionately; every neighbourhood where encampments become the visible default. Levers: M (shelter operations, encampment policy, HSCIS capital), P (HPP funding — the AG found provincial share fell from 68% toward parity, CL-80304), F (Reaching Home, IHAP — cut 68% for 2026, CL-120035). Depth: the estate's deepest asset — flagship BOOK v1.0, sealed 2026-08-07 (L5), root causes I1, solutions evidence I2. Gaps: resolve the population-figure contradiction (docket item 1) before any publish.

C4 · Development charges & approval timelines

Toronto's DC rates and permit timelines sit inside every unit's cost and every project's feasibility math; the province has repeatedly rewritten the DC framework mid-game. Who's hit: buyers/renters (costs pass through), builders, the city's own capital budget (DCs fund growth infrastructure). Levers: M (DC bylaw, approvals), P (DC Act amendments). Receipts: CMHC/ BILD timeline studies named in THE ISSUE MAP — Toronto's 67 ratified research issues; claims register thin here — a real gap given how load-bearing DC reform is in every housing debate. Gaps: current Toronto DC schedule + timeline receipts; province's latest DC amendments.

C5 · Rental market & tenant protections

Roughly half of Toronto households rent, and the protection machinery is visibly behind: the LTB resolves within its own 90-day standard only 51–53% of the time against an 80% target (CL-0281); nationally 20% of renter households were in core housing need in 2021, 89% of those on affordability (CL-0286). New-lease rents softened in 2025 (2–8% declines in advertised 2-bed rents, CL-0290) while rents-paid on sitting tenancies kept rising — churn is the penalty. Who's hit: renters broadly; hardest, tenants facing eviction into a market repriced above them. Levers: P (RTA, rent guideline, LTB), M (rental licensing, RentSafeTO-class enforcement). Gaps: Toronto eviction-filings receipt; above-guideline-increase volume.

C8 · Short-term rentals · (compact)

Housing-stock leakage into STRs, municipally licensable and enforced (or not); scale receipt for Toronto's current registered/estimated-unregistered split is a gap. Levers: M.

E4 · Newcomer settlement & shelter pressure

Federal policy sets the flow; the city absorbs the cost. Refugee claimants were 51.6% of the shelter-system population at the 2024 SNA (CL-120089); Toronto's federal refugee-response funding was then cut ~68% for 2026 — $300M → $97.1M (CL-120035), a $202.9M hole the city either backfills or converts into service cuts (CL-90727). Peel's overflow-hotel contracts show the regional spillover (CL-90834). Who's hit: claimants themselves; the whole shelter system's other users competing for the same beds. Levers: F (immigration policy, IHAP), M (delivery). Gaps: 2026 in-year actuals once budget variance reports land.

I1/I2 · Homelessness root causes & solutions evidence · (research spine)

Not separate lived problems — the knowledge layer under C2: documented federal/provincial withdrawals that built the crisis (I1) and the Housing-First/peer-jurisdiction evidence base (I2). Depth: flagship CH2–CH3, CH5, CH8. Scored under C2.

§3 · Economy, work & the trade war

PR-X09 · The US trade war & geopolitical rupture (DEEPENED v0.2)

The live, quantified shock v0.1 buried in a one-liner: Ontario's FAO projects the US tariff regime costs Ontario 68,100 jobs in 2025 rising to 119,200 in 2026 (✓ primary re-fetched 2026-08-08) if restrictions hold, with manufacturing GDP down 8.0% in the first full year — primary metals (−17,700 jobs), auto parts, machinery hardest; 40% of Ontario manufacturing production exports to the US [https://fao-on.org/wp-content/uploads/report/impacts-of-us-tariffs/Impacts-of-US-Tariffs-on-the-Ontario-Economy-EN.pdf · https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/fao-report-ontario-impact-us-trade-war-1.7522571]. Provincial growth roughly halves vs the no-tariff path (1.2% vs 1.9% 2026). Toronto is exposed through its role as the region's head-office/financial/logistics core and through GTA manufacturing belts; the food-price report lists the trade dispute itself among 2026 grocery drivers (§1). Behind the tariffs sits the wider rupture — a US turned unreliable as ally and market, supply-chain re-shoring, defence/procurement realignment — the corpus's Geopolitical Resilience & Trade Shocks — Day-One Brief frame. Who's hit: manufacturing workers (Windsor–GTA belt), export-linked services, every consumer via prices. Levers: F (trade policy, retaliation, support programs), P (worker supports, procurement shifts), M (local economic development, "buy Canadian" procurement, affected-worker services). Toronto exposure (filled 2026-08-08, ⚠️ search-tier — CB page blocks scripted fetch): Conference Board metro analysis: Toronto real GDP −1.6% vs baseline (Q2 2025); the city facilitates ~$123B/yr in US trade; City survey: 42.1% of employment-area jobs tariff-exposed [https://www.conferenceboard.ca/insights/the-true-cost-of-trump-tariffs-city-impacts/]. Gaps: 2026 in-year actuals vs FAO projection.

PR-X01 · AI and the future of work & income (DEEPENED v0.2)

The exposure is broad and the realized signal is now visible at the entry level: the Conference Board classes 57.4% of Canadian jobs as highly exposed to AI (49.0% competing / 51.0% augmenting — ✓ re-fetched from the CBoC/FSC primary PDF [https://fsc-ccf.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/canadas-workforce-in-transition_sept2025.pdf]) and StatCan's early-evidence series tracks employment effects [https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2026001/article/00003-eng.htm]; the one displacement signal the futures corpus verified economy-wide is hiring softening for 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations (WORK & ECONOMY FUTURES — where work and livelihoods are heading, honestly scored (one of this library's own project records FUTURES THIRD PASS)). The deeper problem is the transition question the dayone quartet frames — income replacement, meaning and life beyond work, whether Toronto captures AI's upside or only its displacement (AI's Labour-Market and Income-Replacement Effects — Day-One Brief · Purpose, Flourishing, and a Post-Work Transition — Day-One Brief · Toronto's AI-Sector Economic Opportunity and Sovereignty — Day-One Brief). Who's hit first: young workers entering clerical/admin/junior-content roles; mid-career white-collar next if competing-class adoption scales. Levers: F/P (income security, labour law, education), M (workforce development, its own adoption H4, convening). Gaps: Toronto-CMA exposure receipt; realized-displacement tracking beyond the entry-level signal.

PR-X16 · Automation of driving work (NEW v0.2)

Waymo is preparing an application to Ontario's AV pilot program (live since 2016, currently running through 2027) to bring robotaxis to Toronto, and has met with city officials; the mayor has publicly conditioned any deployment on not costing drivers their livelihoods (MINTED: CL-50018 ⚠️; CBC page returns empty to scripted fetch — browser-rendered pull confirms the article live, dated Apr 15 2026) [https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/waymo-ontario-self-driving-cars-9.7163535]. Driving is one of the largest male-dominated occupation clusters in the region — ride-hail, taxi, delivery, trucking — and the futures corpus treats driving automation as a when-not-if under uncertain timelines. The policy lever (G1: MTO pilot rules, municipal right-of-way and licensing) decides the pace; the problem is the livelihood cliff if pace outruns transition support. Who's hit: counted 2026-08-08 — Toronto alone has 80,429 approved ride-hail (PTC) driver licences (MINTED: CL-50008; city staff report, Dec 2024) plus ~11,265 taxi drivers, before counting truck/delivery drivers (no published Toronto-CMA figure; StatCan table 98-10-0593 is the source to mine) [https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2024/ex/bgrd/backgroundfile-251312.pdf]; disproportionately immigrant men. Levers: P (O.Reg 306/15 pilot terms), M (vehicle-for-hire licensing, curb rules), F (EI/retraining). Gaps: truck/delivery count; Waymo application status as it lands.

PR-X03 · Precarious work & job quality

Beneath the gig headline (4.7% of ON workers' main job, LFS 2022–23) sits the wider contested layer — StatCan counted 871k gig workers plus ~1.2M temporary/casual/contract in 2024; definitional fights (Fraser vs PEPSO framings) are themselves part of the problem's politics [https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/esdc-edsc/documents/corporate/portfolio/labour/programs/labour-standards/reports/gig-workers-what-we-heard/WWHR-Developing-greater-labour-protections-for-gig-workers.pdf]. Who's hit: platform workers, newcomers overrepresented in app work, young workers. Levers: P (ESA, Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act's real force), F (EI reform). Depth: Gig Economy & Precarious-Work Labour Standards — Day-One Brief · Worker Co-operatives and Community-Wealth Building — Day-One Paper. Gaps: Toronto-CMA precarity share receipt.

PR-X06 · Deep poverty & income security

Toronto is the child-poverty capital of large urban Canada: 25.7% of children — 119,690 kids — below the CFLIM-AT line (2023), two points above the next-worst big city, half of one-parent families poor, 27.1% of Indigenous children [https://www.socialplanningtoronto.org/childpoverty2026]. Provincial income floors erode beneath it: The Number That Hasn't Moved Since 2018 — Day-One Brief documents OW's real-terms decline. Food-bank depth data (§4 H2) shows the same households at the sharpest edge. (all sub-figures ✓ re-fetched from the SPT primary 2026-08-08, incl. Winnipeg 23.6% next-worst). Who's hit: children first; single parents; racialized and Indigenous households; social-assistance recipients. Levers: P/F (OW/ODSP rates, child benefits — the big levers), M (poverty-reduction strategy, fee subsidies, the city's own service prices). Depth: Deep Poverty and Income Security — Day-One Paper; G2 carries the basic-income evidence file. Gaps: all-ages Toronto poverty receipt; depth-of- poverty (average distance below line) receipt.

G2 · Basic income & income-security evidence · (compact)

The evidence file for X06's biggest lever: pilots diverge (Finland/Stockton/OpenResearch), Ontario's cancelled pilot returned no verdict (futures); levers F/P. Scored under X06.

PR-X07 · Immigrant integration & the diaspora economy

Beyond shelter pressure (E4): credential recognition, underemployment of skilled newcomers, integrity of the intake system, and the upside the city forgoes when integration fails — the dayone trio's frame (Immigration and the Diaspora Economy — Day-One Paper · Immigration System Integrity and Exploitation — Day-One Paper · Immigration, Mutual Learning, and Integration — Day-One Paper). Demographic policy swings (X12) now cut the flow while settlement capacity remains strained. Levers: F (levels, credential frameworks), P (regulated professions), M (settlement services, hiring). Gaps: underemployment receipt (newcomer earnings gap).

D6 · Downtown & main-street vitality · (compact)

Downtown = 3% of land, ~40% of non-res floor area, 500k+ jobs (CL-90588/90589) — its post-2020 recalibration (office conversion, main-street vacancy) is a fiscal-base problem as much as a streetscape one. Levers: M (+BIA machinery), P/F grants (CL-90556/90557/90613). Gaps: current Toronto office/retail vacancy receipt.

F1 · City workforce & labour relations · (compact)

Service delivery runs on ~40k+ city workers; bargaining rounds and vacancy rates set what residents actually get. Ledger thin; receipts gap. Levers: M/P.

J1 · Financial-system resilience & rails · (compact)

Payments modernization, fraud growth, open banking's 2026 rollout — federally decided, locally felt (CL series under J1's own wave; DECISIONS BEFORE 2030 — the full inventory (Phase C, one of this library's own project records) §3). Levers: F/BoC/OSFI.

§4 · Health, care & food

PR-X04 · Primary-care access

Ontario's college projected 4.4 million Ontarians — 1 in 4 — without a family doctor by 2026 (✓ OCFP primary re-fetched; its own last hard count on that page: nearly 2.3M, Sept 2022); the widely-reported current estimate of ~2.5 million remains ⚠️ press-tier after provincial connection pushes (15k+ Torontonians connected mid-2026), still among the worst primary-care droughts in the province's history, with 52% of family physicians surveyed considering retirement within five years [https://ontariofamilyphysicians.ca/news/more-than-four-million-ontarians-will-be-without-a-family-doctor-by-2026/ · https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/07/29/more-than-15k-torontonians-have-recently-been-connected-to-a-family-doctor-heres-how/]. Downstream: ER load, later diagnosis, chronic-condition drift. Who's hit: unattached patients — disproportionately newcomers, low-income, and young adults. Levers: P (OHIP funding models, team-based care), F (health transfers), M (almost none directly — advocacy and siting). Depth: Family Doctors and Primary Care — Day-One Brief. Gaps: Toronto-specific unattached count receipt.

H2 · Food insecurity

4.1 million food-bank visits in Toronto in one year — +636k YoY, +340% since 2019; more than 1 in 10 Torontonians now use food banks; 1 in 4 clients are children; 18% of client households with kids report a child going hungry at least weekly; 46% of client households have someone working (MINTED: CL-50001 [4.1M visits/employed share] · CL-50010 ⚠️ [+635,962 YoY visits] · CL-50011 ⚠️ [+340% since 2019] · CL-50012 ⚠️ [1-in-4 clients are children] — all four report-PDF deltas now landed, the three ⚠️ new ones at “still being checked”-tier pending Lane-V promotion) [https://www.dailybread.ca/blog/whos-hungry-2025-record-breaking-hunger/ · https://www.dailybread.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/WH-Single-Spread-29OCT2025.pdf]. The 2026 food-price forecast (§1) makes the direction worse before better. This is X06's sharpest daily surface and X14's harshest component. Levers: P/F (income policy — food charity can't close an income gap), M (student nutrition, community food infrastructure, land for gardens/markets). Depth: FOOD & AGRICULTURE FUTURES — feeding people in the futures we actually face (one of this library's own project records FUTURES THIRD PASS) (farmland loss 319 acres/day, provincial lever). Gaps: household food-insecurity rate receipt (StatCan CIS) to pair with the visits count.

A4 · Opioid & drug-poisoning crisis

464 Toronto residents died of opioid toxicity in 2024 — down 16% from the 2020–22 plateau (~555/yr) but still +50% vs pre-2020 (CL-80314); provincially, opioids were in 85.2% of 8,767 substance-toxicity deaths 2018–21 (CL-80315), fentanyl/analogues dominate (CL-0179), and northern cities' rates more than tripled (CL-40024). Provincial policy swung hard (consumption- site closures → HART hubs) mid-crisis. 2025 preliminary: 258 opioid toxicity deaths, a 45% drop from 2024's 468 (MINTED: CL-50015 ⚠️; TPH's Toronto Overdose Information System, Tableau dashboard, browser-rendered pull — note its own 2024 count of 468 runs slightly above CL-80314's 464, an unreconciled preliminary/final-data-vintage gap). Who's hit: people who use drugs, their families; shelter system overlap; downtown east concentration. Levers: P (treatment capacity, site approvals, HART model), M (TPH programs, paramedic response), F (scheduling, safe-supply policy). Gaps: treatment-wait receipt.

E3 · Aging, home care & long-term care

The demographic wall is booked: ON 65+ went 2M → 2.8M (2013–22) and heads to 4.4M by 2045 (CL-80041) while the LTC bed base grew ~1% in a decade (same claim family); COVID's LTC mortality catastrophe (64.8% of provincial deaths from 20.2% of cases at Aug-2020, CL-80038) exposed the model, and by late 2023 the ministry had fully implemented only 49% of the AG's preparedness recommendations (CL-80042). City-run homes outperform provincial averages on antipsychotic use (CL-0498). Who's hit: seniors waiting for beds/home-care hours; unpaid family caregivers, mostly women. Levers: P (licensing, funding, home-care), M (10 city-run homes, seniors services), F (transfers). Depth: Aging Well: Home Care and Aging-in-Place — Day-One Brief. Gaps: current LTC waitlist receipt; home-care hours gap.

C6 · Child-care access under CWELCC

Fees fell; spaces didn't keep up: 31% of parents of 0–5s report a waitlist child (56% for under-1s) nationally in 2025 (CL-0299), participation still below 2019 (CL-80177), while ON+federal funding stepped to $5.24B combined 2024-25 (CL-80163) and Toronto's Children's Services budget grew $567.8M in a year on CWELCC flow-through (CL-80188). The binding constraints are spaces and workforce, not price. Levers: M (service-system manager), P (funding formula, workforce), F (CWELCC envelope). Gaps: Toronto waitlist receipt.

H1 · Mental-health crisis response

Toronto built the alternative — TCCS citywide from July 2024, the fourth emergency service, ~18,000 calls since 2022, 78% of transferred 911 calls resolved without police in year one (citywide date ✓ July 8 2024 from the city's own page) [https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-executive-committee-adopts-plan-to-expand-toronto-community-crisis-service-citywide-after-successful-first-year/]. Current-year receipt landed: call transfer percentage (911 calls transferred to and completed by TCCS without observed police involvement) 76.7% in 2025; 1,766 calls for service / 1,465 mobile dispatches in May 2026 (MINTED: CL-50016 ⚠️, CL-50017 ⚠️; City's TCCS Dashboard, Tableau-embedded, browser-rendered pull) — and the problem is now consolidation: coverage depth, TTC expansion, sustainable funding each budget cycle. Who's hit: people in crisis (disproportionately racialized residents in police-response outcomes — the reason the service exists). Levers: M (TCCS budget), P (health-system handoffs). Gaps: none blocking — current-year receipt landed above.

PR-X02 · Social isolation, connection & wellbeing

13% of Canadians report always/often feeling lonely; 24% among those living alone (StatCan CSS — ✓ re-fetched; Q1-2024 CSS still shows 13%, figure current) [https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/211124/dq211124e-eng.htm], with Toronto's tower-and-condo form concentrating single-person households. The corpus treats connection as civic infrastructure (Connection, Community, and Belonging — Day-One Brief · Mental Wellbeing and Flourishing — Day-One Brief · The Joyful City: Nightlife, Festivals, and Play — Day-One Brief) — and it's one of the few problems where municipal levers (space, programming, permits) are genuinely strong. Gaps: Toronto-specific loneliness receipt.

A2 · Fire & paramedic response · (compact)

Paramedics hit response targets ~80% of the time (2023) but highest-acuity averages ran over target and 60% of service time is spent inside hospitals on offload (CL-90142/90144) — a health-system interface problem wearing a municipal uniform. Levers: M/P.

PR-X13 · Social-services navigation · (compact)

The thinnest-trace row in the register (assembly-kb title only): fragmentation across systems as its own harm. Needs corpus and evidence both before scoring.

§5 · Safety & freedom

A1★ · Community safety, policing & surveillance

The budget is the biggest municipal discretionary line — $1.43B net 2026, +7.0%, 143 net new officers (CL-120026) — and the oversight board is where the surveillance decisions land: plate readers already scan 1M+ plates/day under board-revisable policy, and board pre-approval governs any high-risk AI incl. facial recognition (2030 piece, verified). Once installed, surveillance is nearly never uninstalled — the tolerance window is pre-procurement, i.e. this term. Who's hit: everyone policed; racialized communities carry documented disparities; every resident's data footprint. Levers: M (board + budget — among the strongest city levers anywhere in this register), P (CSPA standards, IG of Policing), F (criminal law). Depth: backgrounder 44 claims (Community safety: policing budgets, staffing, oversight, and results — Backgrounder). Gaps: per-capita harm trend receipt; current TPS surveillance-asset inventory.

A6 · Gun & youth violence

461 shootings/discharges in 2024 (+33.6% YoY), 43 deaths (+48.3%) (CL-90039); handguns are the instrument in 86% of Toronto firearm crime (CL-90015); $16.6M in federal prevention funds flowed 2023–26 (CL-90219) against a prevention landscape (F.O.C.U.S., 190+ agencies, CL-90043) whose evidence base the register's own issue question interrogates. Who's hit: young men in a small number of neighbourhoods, overwhelmingly; their communities' sense of safety citywide. Levers: M (prevention grants, policing), F (firearms law, border), P (courts/bail). Gaps: 2025 YTD trend; program-evaluation receipts.

B2 · Road safety / Vision Zero

Deaths concentrate on the vulnerable: seniors are 29% of Toronto pedestrian collisions vs 17% of population (2021–25, CL-0749); nationally pedestrians are 15.2% of road deaths (CL-0136); the speed-injury physics are settled (15% speed ↑ → 65% fatal-collision ↑, CL-0753). This is one of the most municipally-controllable problems in the register — road design is the lever. Levers: M (design, speed limits, automated enforcement), P (HTA). Gaps: Toronto KSI 2025 receipt.

A3 / A5 · CSWB plans & bylaw enforcement · (compact)

The mandated community-safety plan (CSPA s.243) and the everyday-order layer (noise, property standards, vacant buildings). Delivery-audit receipts are the gap for both. Levers: M.

H7 · Equity strategies delivery

The city has adopted the strategies (anti-Black racism, gender equity, 2SLGBTQ+ inclusion, disability beyond AODA); the problem the register tracks is funded-vs-delivered — the audit shape, not a scale number. Receipts live in progress reports and Ombudsman findings; a dedicated dedicated audit-report review is owed (named in archived pass record). Levers: M. Who's hit: the named communities when commitments quietly under-deliver.

F3 · Indigenous relations & reconciliation

The receipts that exist are stark — 27.3% of Indigenous adults in Toronto have experienced homelessness (CL-120086), chronic-homelessness rates worse than non-Indigenous peers (CL-120085) — and the city's own commitments (20% of housing/homelessness grants to Indigenous-led projects, CL-120158) need the same funded-vs-delivered audit as H7. The $4B federal urban Indigenous housing strategy remains unreleased (CL-120159). Levers: M/P/F + Indigenous-led governance (the estate's own Indigenous-authored rule). Depth: dayone Indigenous suite.

D7 · Accessibility (AODA and beyond) · (compact)

8M Canadians 15+ with disabilities; 56% hit barriers in public spaces; 62% vs 78% employment rate (CL-0175/0172); AODA's 2025 deadline passed with compliance gaps unresolved. Levers: M (built form, services), P (AODA enforcement).

§6 · Climate, environment & infrastructure

E2 · Climate adaptation, flooding & heat

Ontario's FAO puts the cost of doing nothing at +$4.1B/yr average on public infrastructure vs $3.0B/yr under proactive adaptation (CL-90815/90817) — municipalities own 71% ($506B) of the portfolio (CL-90816). Toronto's precedents keep arriving: July 2013 ($1B insured, CL-90769), July 2024 (~$990M GTA insured, futures ⚠️); the 2080s projection is 55–60 days/yr above 30°C in southern Ontario (same file). Who's hit: basement-flooded homeowners, heat-exposed seniors and tower residents without cooling, every ratepayer funding repairs. Levers: M (stormwater capital, heat strategy, drainage sphere + CAs), P (CA framework, building code), F (DMAF). Depth: backgrounder 62 claims (Climate Adaptation — Stormwater & Flood Infrastructure — Backgrounder). Gaps: Toronto heat-mortality receipt.

F4 · Climate mitigation & retrofits

Council adopted −65% GHG by 2030 (vs 1990), net zero 2040 (CL-140950); the retrofit machinery funded so far touches ~15 buildings/2,200 units at −21% emissions (CL-80339) against a building stock of hundreds of thousands — the pace-vs-target gap is the problem. Programs exist at every level (GMF up to $10M/project CL-80336; CMHC deep-retrofit CL-120006; TATR $13.4M CL-30066/80340). Levers: M (TransformTO funding each cycle, TGS), P (building code, IESO procurement), F (GMF/CMHC). Gaps: citywide emissions-trend receipt vs interim targets (2023: 16.1 Mt, −43% vs 1990 — futures ⚠️).

H10 · Water systems & buried capital

The invisible balance sheet (MINTED: CL-50005): $93.3B replacement value, SOGR backlog ~3.6% of it, $9.28B of the 2026–35 capital plan on renewal (✓ backlog/replacement-value/renewal figures re-fetched from the 2026 budget-note primary); watermains averaging 61 years old (9% 80–100yr, 15% >100yr) — MINTED: CL-50014 ⚠️, its own primary landed (City's "Watermains" page, updated May 2026) [https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/bu/bgrd/backgroundfile-261774.pdf · https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/building-construction/infrastructure-city-construction/understanding-city-construction/water-sewer-mains/]. G3 · Municipal climate endowments & financing vehicles · (compact)

Toronto's own climate-investment vehicle, the Toronto Atmospheric Fund (TAF), is small relative to the scale of the retrofit/mitigation gap F4 names above: a $96.3M total fund balance (2024), built from three one-time public contributions rather than any repeatable revenue stream — City $23M (1992), Ontario $17M (2016), Canada $38M (2020) — generating ~$9.45M/yr investment income to deploy [corpus]. TAF sits inside a federally-endowed six-hub peer network (Low Carbon Cities Canada, $183M total, Toronto's $40M the largest single allocation) that shares the same structural limit: permanent-endowment income-only spending, not a revolving or leveraged capital pool [corpus]. A parallel, separate instrument — the City's own green-bond program (8 issuances, 2018–2025) — finances Council-approved capital projects directly rather than through endowment income. Who's hit: every resident depending on the pace of retrofit/mitigation programming this fund and its parallel financing tools help underwrite — the mismatch between a ~$96M endowment and a building stock of hundreds of thousands of units (F4) is the scale problem, not any single-year underperformance. Trend: three one-time top-ups over three decades (1992/2016/2020), not a repeatable formula; whether Toronto gets a fourth tranche, or a structurally different (revolving/leveraged) vehicle, is undecided. Levers: M (TAF governance — 11-member Council-appointed board, City holds 3 seats incl. the Mayor), P (endowment-top-up precedent, local-board regulatory framework), F (GMF/LC3 capitalization decisions). Gaps: ⚠️ claim set is backgrounder-sourced only, not independently re-verified for this register entry — Municipal Climate Endowments — Backgrounder carries 25 verified / 21 still being checked claims; portfolio asset-allocation and sibling-hub comparative-performance data are named gaps in that file.

§8 · Learning, youth & culture

PR-X05 · The education system's decay (DEEPENED v0.2)

The receipts have hardened since v0.1's "estimate" tag: Ontario faces a teacher shortage the ministry's own documents expect to worsen from 2027, felt most in the GTA [https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7339837]; the AG found a 72% unfilled-absence rate for educational assistants at Toronto Catholic in 2023-24 with special-education support buckling (MINTED: CL-50013 ⚠️; AG Special Report 2026 primary now landed, replacing the secondary-only press citation) [https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/specialreports/specialreports/en26/2026_SpecEd_EN.pdf · https://torontoobserver.ca/2026/06/18/its-not-safe-anymore-educational-assistant-crisis-in-ontario-schools/]; EQAO's own release (MINTED: CL-50006): 51% of Grade 6 students met the provincial math standard in 2024-25 — 49% below, third year at ~half [https://www.eqao.com/about-eqao/news-release/assessment-results-2025/]; per-pupil funding fell ~1% in real terms for 2026-27 with a $6.5B cumulative shortfall since 2018-19 (MINTED: CL-50007; CCPA/Tranjan, May 2026) [https://www.policyalternatives.ca/news-research/ontario-school-funding-shortfall-continues-to-grow-reaching-6-4-billion-since-2018/]; classroom violence and absenteeism recur across boards. Trustees on this fall's ballot govern boards whose real fiscal room the province sets (H3). Who's hit: students — special-needs students first; teachers/EAs burning out; the city's long-run human capital. Levers: P (funding formula, curriculum, college of teachers pipeline), boards (delivery), M (almost none — siting/partnerships). Depth: K-12 Education Reimagined — Day-One Brief · Most Educated Population / Learning City — Day-One Brief. Gaps: none blocking — EQAO + funding receipts landed above (AG's 72% EA figure still secondary-corroborated; primary AG pull owed at mint).

G4 · Professional sports ownership & civic power · (compact)

Toronto's four major professional franchises (Leafs, Raptors, Blue Jays, TFC) are consolidating under a single private owner: Rogers Communications moved from 37.5% to 75% of Maple Leaf Sports & Entertainment (MLSE) in 2025 (buying BCE's stake for $4.7B) and, in 2026, signed — but had not yet closed — an agreement for the remaining 25% from Kilmer Sports Inc.; full consolidation would put one company in control of the region's major-league sport assets, valued at $13.1B combined (Sportico, July 2026) [corpus]. Public money and land sit underneath this private consolidation: BMO Field's 2007 build was federal/provincial/city-funded and the City retains ownership, with the land governed by the City's own Exhibition Place agency — governance now contested by Ontario's proposed Bill 68, which names BMO Field explicitly even as the province says it is not "taking over" the site [corpus]. The general (not Toronto-specific) academic literature on stadium/arena subsidies is close to unanimous that public subsidies for professional venues do not deliver the local economic-development benefits proponents claim, and one widely-cited study found official reporting undercounts the true public subsidy by roughly 40% once land, infrastructure and foregone tax are counted in [corpus]. Who's hit: taxpayers/ratepayers backing venue land and construction costs; the public interest in municipal control of civic land, now contested provincially. Trend: ownership concentration rising (toward 100% of MLSE under one company); the municipal-vs-provincial land-authority question is live and unresolved as of this writing. Levers: M (Exhibition Place governance, zoning, venue-land agreements), P (Bill 68's proposed authority shift, subsidy approvals). Gaps: ⚠️ claim set is backgrounder-sourced only, not independently re-verified for this register entry — Pro Sport Ownership & Civic Power — Backgrounder carries 21 verified / 16 still being checked/2 disputed of 39 claims; no Toronto-specific public-subsidy rationale or lobbying-registry claim was located in that file's own pass.

§9 · Governance, democracy & the meta-problems

C3 · Municipal fiscal capacity — the enabler under every row

The structural gap has been documented for nearly two decades (2008 PMFSDR: ≥$5.9B/yr × 10 yrs just for infrastructure, CL-80284) and the toolkit hasn't changed: property tax + provincially-controlled everything else (assessment via MPAC, OMPF envelope CL-0059). Every Tier-1 problem's municipal answer runs through this constraint — four budget cycles this term. Levers: M (rates within P rules), P (the actual power: new revenue tools, uploads), F (transfers, CCBF CL-80275). Gaps: Toronto's current structural-deficit receipt.

PR-X08 · Provincial power over the city (municipal autonomy)

The meta-lever: the province can and does redraw the city's powers mid-game (1998 amalgamation over local objection — futures; strong-mayor regime; council-size cut mid-election). Whatever the ranked list says the city should do, this row decides whether the city may. Depth: Municipal Autonomy and the New Deal — Day-One Paper · Regional Governance GTA — Day-One Paper. Levers: P (the point); M (advocacy, charter-city argument).

PR-X15 · The polycrisis (NEW v0.2 — the frame over the register, not a competing row)

The Cascade Institute (Canadian-anchored: Homer-Dixon et al.) defines a global polycrisis as "the causal entanglement of crises in multiple global systems in ways that significantly degrade humanity's prospects" [https://cascadeinstitute.org/polycrisis/ · https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/global-sustainability/article/understanding-polycrisis-definitions-applications-and-responses/970EC4CDD3698E3D5F0162C94900246A]. Toronto's register is itself an instance: the trade war (X09) drives grocery prices (§1) which deepen poverty (X06) which loads shelters (C2) while climate shocks (E2) hit the same budgets (C3) that fund the responses — entanglement means single-row solutions under-perform and resilience (X10) is a cross-cutting investment, not a row. Register law this row adds: the ranked list ranks problems; portfolio judgments should read the entanglement map, not just the ranks. The metacrisis variant (the crisis in our collective sense-making/response capacity itself) is the deepest version of the same point — and is exactly the estate's own thesis (understanding as the cultural engine). Depth: INDEX — this library's internal records/ (entry point) + DR3 — Directory Index corpora. Gaps: a Toronto entanglement map (candidate Phase-E artifact).
