THE PROBLEM — The cost of living in Toronto

1 · The problem

A Toronto family of four now spends $17,572 a year on food — up about $995 in a single year, with another 4–6% coming in 2026 and the trade war itself named among the drivers. Ontario electricity jumped roughly 29% in 2026. The GTA living wage is $27.20 an hour; the minimum wage is $17.60 — a $9.60-an-hour, roughly $14,500-a-year hole for someone working full time. Nearly half of Toronto's food-bank users live in a household where somebody works. That is the squeeze: not one price but food, rent, power, and fares stacked on a paycheque that stopped keeping up — while headline inflation reads a calm ~2%, which is exactly why the official numbers keep telling people the crisis they are living isn't happening. And nobody owns the whole problem: Ottawa holds fuel taxes, money, and trade; Queen's Park holds electricity rates, the wage floor, and the rent guideline; City Hall holds property tax, fares, water, and rec fees. Each can blame the others; the household pays all three at once. This file is the umbrella — housing (C1), rents (C5), utilities (C7), food (H2), and deep poverty (X06) each have their own file — because the lived problem is the stack, and the stack is what no single row answers: how does a Toronto household get to afford its own city? Full framing and receipts: register entry (§1, PR-X14).

2 · What we're asking — the question hierarchy

The primary question: how do we make Toronto a city its own people can actually afford to live in — not survive in, live in — and what would it take to be the best city in the world at that within a decade?

Where are Torontonians ACTUALLY at — measured, not imagined

  • Where is the squeeze ACTUALLY landing — real rents paid, real grocery bills, real utility arrears, real commute costs, by ward and by income decile, stacked the way a household feels them rather than reported one line at a time — and why does no such combined picture exist yet? Who should build and maintain it?
  • What is Toronto's real income picture behind the citywide averages — working poverty, underemployment, precarity — broken out by neighbourhood and immigration cohort? (livelihood L4)
  • What are Torontonians actually giving up to absorb the squeeze — deferred dental care, paused retirement savings, dropped insurance, skipped meals — and is any of that displacement measured anywhere, or does it only surface later as hunger and homelessness statistics?
  • What are the early-warning signs that a household is going under — eviction filings, first-time food-bank visits, payday-loan volumes, benefit churn — and how do we publish them together as one honest index, as openly as a poverty line? (mci MC45)
  • Is the living-wage gap widening or narrowing? Nobody can currently answer that for Toronto with a multi-year real-wage-versus-real-cost trend line — build it.

How did it get this expensive — root causes traced to the bottom

  • For each component of the stack — food, shelter, energy, transport, care — what is the honest diagnosis of WHY it costs what it costs here: market structure, policy choice, underbuilding, concentration, geography, all of it? Which of those causes were decisions someone made — and who made them?
  • How much of Toronto's cost of living was intentionally designed to enrich landowners and investors — and how compliant have older generations been in pricing out the young? How do we bring costs down to sane levels without destroying the retirement safety nets those same generations built for themselves? (homes seed, verbatim model)
  • Which of today's relief measures — the suspended federal fuel excise tax, the $0 consumer carbon price, the minimum-wage step-ups — are structural fixes, and which are temporary politics with a cliff at the end? What happens to a household budget when the temporary ones lapse?
  • How exposed are Toronto grocery and goods prices specifically to the tariff war — is there a Toronto-level pass-through estimate at all, beyond the province-wide job numbers?

Who profits from the squeeze — and what breaks if we solve it

  • What is the actual structure of Canada's grocery market — concentration, margins, the Competition Bureau's own findings — from published sources, honestly stated? (harvest H32)
  • Walk the household's money upstream: who is on the receiving end of each line of the squeeze — the grocery oligopoly, the landlord layers, the utility, the platform taking its cut of gig wages, the payday lender at the bottom? Which of those intermediaries genuinely earn their keep, and which are pure extraction — what's the honest, both-ways test? (automata AU78)
  • The affordability industry itself: who profits from the squeeze staying managed rather than solved — and what breaks, and for whom, if Toronto actually becomes affordable? Name the losers honestly: FOR and AGAINST.
  • How many Toronto workers are paid below minimum wage off the books, under threat of a report to immigration — and what severs the link between a worker's status and an employer's leverage over their wage? (newcomers N16 · N17)

Who does this best in the world — and what do we shamelessly steal

  • Which cities and countries have actually kept life affordable at scale — Vienna's rents, Singapore's hawker-centre food economy, fare policy in Tallinn and Luxembourg, the EU cities that tied wage floors to real living costs — what exactly do they do, what did it cost, and what do we shamelessly copy? What does nobody dare copy, and why?
  • Where has "free at the point of use" worked at real city scale — transit, recreation, internet, tool libraries — and where has it quietly broken the thing it freed? (mci MC61 · abundance AB74)
  • Where has a real income floor been tried at scale, and what do the cash-transfer, basic-income, and social-supermarket literatures honestly show — caveats included? (mci MC46)
  • What is the full inventory of existing recommendations on Toronto affordability — every task force, commission, academic centre, and community organization that has already studied this — and what has been recommended, costed, and ignored? Stop re-deriving what is already on a shelf somewhere: collect it all first.
  • Who are the best minds and best organizations on urban affordability anywhere — the researchers, the practitioners, the institutes whose work we should be reading, importing, and inviting in — and who is doing the best work in Toronto right now that the city ignores?

The floor under everyone — and what it would actually cost

  • What would an honest, minimum-viable dignity floor look like for Toronto right now — food that doesn't require a food-bank line, direct rent relief — and could it stand up inside a single year? (livelihood L57)
  • What does Toronto currently spend downstream of scarcity — policing survival behaviour, courts, emergency departments, shelter churn — versus what closing the floor would cost upstream, both columns built honestly? (mci MC49)
  • Ontario cancelled its Basic Income pilot mid-flight — do we argue the income-floor case FOR and AGAINST on the evidence, build a city-scale instrument that doesn't depend on the province, or both? (livelihood L3)
  • Which pieces of a needs floor are actually municipal levers (recreation, transit, library, connectivity, food programs) versus provincial (OW/ODSP rates) versus federal (income architecture)? Draw the addressee map before recommending anything. (mci MC50)
  • The targeted-versus-universal chain, honestly run: what would a free TTC pass for every OW/ODSP recipient cost and change (mci MC69) — why is uptake of existing low-income programs so low (mci MC70) — where does targeting beat universal, and where does targeting's stigma and friction cost more than making it free for everyone (mci MC71) — who actually has to say yes (mci MC72) — and can a low-income pass be indistinguishable from anyone else's, so nobody has to re-prove their poverty every year? (mci MC73) (transit_foundations TF-Q71 · TF-Q73 · TF-Q78)
  • Is direct rent relief the single highest-leverage intervention available for most households — honestly costed, with who bears the cost named — given rent is the largest line in almost every squeezed budget? (livelihood L37)
  • Most of Toronto's rental stock is owned by small landlords who aren't wealthy — what tenant protection works without making every landlord the villain? (livelihood L76)
  • Should every city-funded improvement that predictably raises nearby rents carry a binding anti-displacement commitment at design time, not bolted on after? (regeneration RG27 · RG76)

The family, the worker, the future paycheque

  • How do we take the cost out of birth and a child's first year, so a family's experience of it isn't decided by what they can afford? (genesis G39) And what would it take for a Toronto child to never have to think about money — food, shelter, transit, care, learning, reliably there without a transaction a kid has to navigate? (abundance AB83)
  • Child care can consume an entire salary — what actually makes it accessible and affordable for a Toronto parent? (genesis G40)
  • How do we value the unpaid labour of caregiving — for kids and for aging parents — so caring for your own family doesn't become the path to your own old-age poverty? (elders E13)
  • What does dignity look like for gig and platform workers once a platform can simply exit the market the way Foodora did — and does protection extend to the temp-agency layer that intermediates so much precarious work? (livelihood L18 · L19)
  • Could Toronto's gig platforms actually run as worker- and community-owned cooperatives, with the platform's cut staying with the people doing the work — and what does a gig worker become in that transition: a co-owner with portable reputation and real bargaining power? (torontoos T34 · T35)
  • Retraining has a historically weak record — what is the fast-but-safe transition Toronto actually owes a worker displaced by automation: an income bridge and a real placement, not retraining theatre? (livelihood L25) Which jobs, in which neighbourhoods and cohorts, are most exposed — the honest contested estimate, not the scariest headline? (livelihood L72)
  • When the displaced worker is the expert on their own job, how do we position them to design what replaces it? (automata AU69) And who catches their purpose and daily structure, not just their rent? (automata AU83)
  • If Torontonians would rather talk about rent and groceries than about a "future of work," the conversation meets them there — at the rent and the groceries. (automata AU99)

The honest counterargument — built in, not bolted on

  • Where are the affordability critics right? Do broad subsidies feed the very price inflation they compensate for; do wage-floor increases cost the marginal job; does rent relief flow through to landlords? Take the strongest version of each case, concede what deserves conceding, and say what evidence would change our mind.
  • What would a real food-resilience posture require so a bad month, a broken supply chain, or a closed border doesn't turn directly into hunger (harvest H20) — and if the goal is making food banks unnecessary rather than better-run, what has to change upstream: income, not charity? (harvest H35) What does "free food that isn't poor-quality food" require as a design standard? (harvest H33)
  • Before any new municipal revenue tool rides in on the affordability argument, what is the city's actual fiscal starting position — budgets, reserves, debt, and the structural gap covered by one-time measures every year? (abundance AB53)

4 · What's unexplored

The register's own named gap for this row: wage-distribution receipt — X14's entry cites the GTA living-wage figure as an all-in cost-of-living stand-in but has no receipt for how wages are actually distributed across the city, only the single living-wage-vs-minimum-wage comparison.

Because X14 is an umbrella, its component rows carry gaps that bear directly on the lived squeeze but are tracked under their own entries, not duplicated here: C5 rents (Toronto eviction-filings receipt; above-guideline-increase volume), H2 food (household food-insecurity rate receipt to pair with the visits count), and X06 poverty (an all-ages Toronto poverty receipt; a depth-of-poverty receipt).

Everything §2's "Raised this review" names has no dedicated evidence shelf yet: there is no published Toronto cost-of-living index broken down by decile and component (NEW-1); no visible tracking of what households cut to absorb the squeeze (NEW-2); no stated cliff-scenario analysis for when temporary relief measures lapse (NEW-3); no Toronto-specific tariff price-pass-through estimate (NEW-4); no combined-burden ward-level map (NEW-5); and no multi-year real-wage-versus-cost trend line for the city specifically (NEW-6).

Source tags like (homes HM12) mark questions carried from the project's own question banks (QUESTION_HARVEST.csv); untagged questions were raised in the 2026-08-11 rewrite.
