Ontario's Homelessness Prevention Program funding history, and the Auditor General's accountability follow-up

The $700 million figure often cited for Ontario's Homelessness Prevention Program has a real, traceable funding history across two budget years, excludes a category of shelters some might assume it covers, and sits alongside a formal Auditor General finding that 40% of the province's own 2021 homelessness recommendations show little to no progress two years later.

The finding

Ontario's Homelessness Prevention Program (HPP) was created in Budget 2022 with a base of approximately $493 million. Budget 2023 added $202 million, bringing the total to approximately $695 million — the figure commonly rounded to $700 million CSRL2-136. Violence-against-women shelters and domiciliary hostels are explicitly not funded through HPP (administered by the Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing); they are funded separately through the Ministry of Children, Community and Social Services CSRL2-137.

Separately, Ontario's Auditor General's December 2021 Value-for-Money Audit on homelessness made Recommendation 1 in exact terms: that the province develop a comprehensive, coordinated provincial strategy to prevent and reduce homelessness, with specific actions, targets, and timelines — the audit's own finding was that Ontario did not have one CSRL2-139. The Auditor General's 2023 follow-up on that same audit found 37% of recommended actions fully implemented, 23% in process, and 40% showing little or no progress CSRL2-140.

Why it matters

Knowing HPP's own two-budget-year funding history matters for citing the $700 million figure accurately — it is a combined total built from a 2022 base plus a 2023 addition, not a single new allocation, and its own program boundaries exclude VAW shelters and domiciliary hostels specifically, a distinction easy to miss when citing total provincial homelessness spending. The Auditor General's own 40% little-or-no-progress finding, two years after a formal recommendation for a comprehensive provincial strategy, is a documented accountability gap from the province's own independent audit office — not this library's own assessment, but the government's own oversight body's finding.

What this page does not cover

This page does not verify whether the Auditor General has issued a further follow-up beyond the 2023 report addressed here — a search for a possible later update did not locate one specific to this 2021 audit. It does not evaluate whether HPP's funding boundaries (excluding VAW shelters) are an appropriate program design choice. It does not carry a "case against" the Ministry's implementation record beyond citing the Auditor General's own published follow-up findings, since those findings are the province's own independent oversight body's assessment, not this library's independent judgment.
