Toronto's 2025-2030 Homelessness Strategic Plan: a twice-delayed release, and what its own consultation found

The City's own strategic plan for homelessness services has slipped twice — first from 2025 to 2026, then to 2027 — while its own stakeholder engagement process found a majority of respondents believe services have gotten worse, not better, over the past five years.

The finding

The City of Toronto's 2025-2030 Strategic Plan to Address Homelessness had not been approved by Council as of 2026, and was listed as a 2026 deliverable in the City's own budget documentation CSRL2-104. That timeline itself slipped further: a City webpage update dated March 4, 2026 confirmed the release had been extended again, to 2027 — having previously already slipped from 2025 to 2026 CSRL2-158.

The plan's own stakeholder engagement process, described in its "What We Heard" report, surveyed a wide range of participants including City staff, service providers, sector partners, Indigenous partners, and people with lived experience of homelessness CSRL2-106. The findings from that engagement are candid about the current system's trajectory: only 6% of respondents said services were better than five years ago, while 66% said they were worse; 47% identified reducing chronic homelessness as the top priority for the plan to address CSRL2-159.

Related, concrete commitments sit alongside the delayed plan: the City is developing its first Black-mandated shelter CSRL2-107, the existing HousingTO 2020-2030 Action Plan targets 18,000 new supportive homes by 2030 CSRL2-109, and average nightly shelter demand for 2026 is projected at 9,808 people CSRL2-110.

Why it matters

A strategic plan slipping twice, from 2025 to 2026 to 2027, while a related capital target (18,000 supportive homes by 2030) and rising demand projections (9,808 people per night in 2026) continue regardless, is a real, disclosed gap between planning timelines and operational pressure. The consultation findings matter on their own terms too: a stakeholder base that is 66% more likely to say services have gotten worse than better over five years is a serious signal the plan itself is meant to respond to — and worth stating plainly rather than only citing the plan's existence without its own findings.

What this page does not cover

This page does not evaluate whether a 2027 release timeline is itself reasonable or unreasonably delayed, since the sources reviewed do not provide that judgment. It does not verify whether the Strategic Plan's eventual release has occurred as of any date after this page's own March 2026 source. It does not carry a "case against" the City's planning process beyond reporting its own consultation findings, since those findings are the City's own documented stakeholder input, not this library's independent assessment.
