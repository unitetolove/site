Measuring progress: Toronto's By-Name List, Built for Zero, and the fiscal cost of inaction

How Toronto tracks who is homeless — and how completely — shapes what "progress" even means. Toronto has built the data infrastructure the national Built for Zero framework asks for, has genuinely achieved one of its two headline milestones and not the other, and sits inside a national measurement standard that itself changed in 2025. Underneath the measurement question is a concrete fiscal one: public healthcare spending on homelessness in Toronto runs roughly seven times higher per person than for housed residents.

The By-Name List and its known gap

Toronto's active By-Name List (BNL) — the individually-tracked roster used to coordinate housing placements — stood at 10,331 people in October 2024 CSRL2-171. The List has a disclosed, structural undercount: an estimated 18-20% of people experiencing homelessness in Toronto are not on it, primarily people sleeping exclusively outdoors rather than using the shelter system the List is built from CSRL2-171.

Two different milestones, one often conflated with the other

Toronto has achieved Quality By-Name List (QBNL) status for chronic homelessness in the Built for Zero Canada framework — Toronto's Chronic Dashboard shows "Yes - 4.0." It has not achieved the equivalent milestone for veteran homelessness, where Toronto's Veteran Dashboard shows "In progress." Toronto is one of only three Ontario communities still "In progress" on veteran QBN data, versus every other listed Ontario community (Cochrane, Durham, Kingston, London, Niagara, Nipissing, Ottawa, Peel, Sault Ste. Marie, Simcoe, St. Thomas-Elgin, Sudbury, Waterloo, Wellington-Guelph, Windsor-Essex), all showing "Yes" CSRL2-172. Achieving QBNL status for one population does not imply it for another — a distinction this library's own ledger initially conflated before a direct primary-source check corrected it, and worth stating plainly here since the same conflation is an easy one for any reader to make.

The measurement standard itself changed in 2025

Built for Zero's national framework retired its original single "functional zero" threshold in late 2025, replacing it with a multi-dimensional "verified reductions" framework tracking whether reductions in homelessness are rare, brief, nonrecurring, safe, and believable — verified with both quantitative evidence and community/coach confirmation, rather than six consecutive data points against one number CSRL2-178. "Functional zero" is no longer an active designation within Built for Zero; any comparison of a city's status to a pre-2025 "functional zero" claim should be read against this changed standard, not the old one.

The fiscal case: what inaction costs

A 2024 ICES/Homeless Hub linked-data study found average public healthcare costs of $12,209 per year for a homeless person in Toronto in 2021-2022, against $1,769 for a housed resident and $1,912 for a low-income housed resident — roughly 6.9 times and 6.4 times higher, respectively. Applied across Toronto's homeless population, the study estimates $69.8-99.7 million in excess annual healthcare costs for approximately 10,000 people CSRL2-191. This figure captures healthcare spending specifically — it is not a claim about total public cost of homelessness, which would also include shelter operations, income supports, and other services covered elsewhere in this library.

Who does this work

The Toronto Alliance to End Homelessness (TAEH) is a collective-impact coalition of more than 100 member organizations leading the Toronto Supportive Housing Growth Plan's 18,000-supportive-homes-by-2030 target, and co-chairs Coordinated Access with Toronto Shelter and Support Services and the city's Housing Secretariat CSRL2-303. The Canadian definition of homelessness used throughout this library originates with Dr. Stephen Gaetz, a York University professor, Director of the Canadian Observatory on Homelessness and Homeless Hub, and Member of the Order of Canada CSRL2-304. On workforce development specifically, the Toronto Shelter Network already operates a Shelter Sector Pipeline Project for lived-experience workforce development — an existing precedent worth citing directly whenever a "peer navigator" or similar new workforce proposal is evaluated, since it predates most such proposals CSRL2-305.

What this page does not cover

This page does not track Toronto's real-time BNL count or QBNL status past this writing — both change regularly and are better queried live at their own dashboards than restated here as a fixed number. A genuine S1 live-discovery pass (WebSearch, then direct WebFetch) found one candidate updated figure — a claimed decline from roughly 15,400 to roughly 12,200 homeless individuals in "late 2025" — but the source citing it did not identify the underlying organization, researcher, or primary dataset ("a recent analysis circulating online," no further attribution given), so it is not cited here; this project's own sourcing discipline treats an unattributed figure as not yet meeting the bar for citation, not as a shortcut to a seemingly newer number. The Built for Zero Canada by-name-lists overview page itself confirmed no newer Toronto-specific dashboard data beyond what CL-172 already captures.
