The Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit's funding cliff

Toronto's own Mayor has called the Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit "the single most effective tool we have for freeing up beds in our shelter system," but the provincial funding allocation behind it is being cut by roughly 80% over two years, even as the City asks for it to be more than quadrupled.

The finding

The Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit (COHB) is a portable rent supplement, cost-shared between the federal and provincial governments, paid directly to eligible tenants rather than tied to a specific unit — for 2024 entrants, it averaged $1,300 per month CSRL2-271. Province-wide, COHB and the related OPHI Housing Allowance supported more than 44,100 households combined as of March 2024, with a target of 59,438 recipients by 2027-28 under Ontario's own National Housing Strategy action plan CSRL2-289. It occupies a distinct policy lane from the City's rent-geared-to-income (RGI) housing waitlist: the two are mutually exclusive pathways, not overlapping ones CSRL2-290.

Toronto's own Street Needs Assessment found that a COHB suspension was associated with roughly a 50% drop in the rate at which people were able to exit shelters into permanent housing CSRL2-020. Despite that, the provincial funding allocation to Toronto specifically is on a sharply declining path: $38 million in 2024, falling to $19.75 million in 2025, and falling further to $7.95 million in 2026 — a decline of close to 80% over two years CSRL2-280. Mayor Olivia Chow has directly linked COHB to shelter capacity, describing it as "the single most effective tool we have for freeing up beds in our shelter system so that more people can come indoors from streets and parks" CSRL2-209. The City's own ask, made through Council, is for funding to be restored to $54 million annually — a figure the City frames as enabling 300 households per month to move from shelter into permanent housing CSRL2-281. Set against the $54 million ask, this project's own derived cost-benefit synthesis puts the annual fiscal yield to other parts of the system (avoided shelter costs and related downstream savings) at roughly $156 million — a rough 2.89-to-1 ratio, not itself drawn from a single primary document but derived directly from the cost and outcome figures the City's own materials already establish above CSRL2-263.

A fresh S1 discovery this pass (T-0065 batch 2) adds real-world detail to how the 2026 cut is already landing: facing provincial delays earlier in 2025, City Council had already front-loaded $4.815 million of COHB funding to help 570 households move into housing — but the Province has since said it will only allow 40 more households to move into housing (within the funding it has provided) between the letter's date and March 2026, meaning that funding would be exhausted by the end of October, just as winter arrives [CL-90489]. Mayor Chow's September 26, 2025 letter recommending Council's restoration ask also requested $107 million in federal transitional funding for the separate IHAP program, underscoring that the COHB cut was one of two concurrent federal/provincial funding pressures the City raised together [CL-90492].

Separately, provincewide context complicates rather than resolves the absence of an opposing case (see below): Ontario's own Financial Accountability Office reports the total provincewide COHB envelope has grown sharply even as Toronto's specific municipal allocation is being cut — from $43 million at program inception (2020-21) to $248 million in 2024-25, with $360 million projected for 2027-28 [CL-90670].

Why it matters

This is a live, quantifiable mismatch between a program the City's own political leadership treats as its single most effective shelter-capacity lever, and a provincial funding trajectory heading sharply in the opposite direction — not a general complaint about underfunding, but a specific, dated, three-year funding cliff ($38M → $19.75M → $7.95M) set against a specific, costed restoration ask ($54M). The claimed downstream fiscal yield, if the roughly 2.89-to-1 framing holds up under further scrutiny, would mean the program pays for itself several times over in avoided costs elsewhere in the system — which is precisely the kind of case a body deciding whether to restore or continue cutting this funding would need to weigh.

What this page does not cover

This page does not independently verify the $156 million downstream-yield figure against a primary government or academic cost-benefit study — it is this project's own derived synthesis, arrived at by combining the cost and outcome figures already established above, not a figure the City itself has published or an independently audited estimate, and the 2.89-to-1 ratio should be read with that caveat attached. It does not cover the federal government's own share of COHB funding or federal-level funding decisions, only the provincial allocation to Toronto specifically. It does not carry an opposing "case against" restoring COHB funding — a fresh S1 search this pass (T-0065 batch 2) still did not find the Province's own public reasoning for the Toronto-specific allocation cut, only the provincewide growth context noted above, which complicates but does not replace a genuine opposing case. The CL-281 citation discrepancy a prior audit pass had flagged as unresolved is now resolved: Mayor Chow's September 26, 2025 letter (RES-0708, the background file to Council item 2025.EX26.13) explicitly states Council's recommendation to "reiterate the request... in MM20.38" for the $54 million/300-households ask — confirming the ask originated in the 2024 motion (2024.MM20.38) and was reiterated, not first made, in the September 2025 letter. The substance of CL-281 is correct. A separate, narrower registry-level issue was found in passing and is flagged for a Verify/registry-cleanup pass rather than fixed here directly (per this project's own no-hand-editing-CSVs rule): the registry row backing CL-281 (RES-0078) describes itself as "Mayor Chow Letter... Sept 26, 2025" while its URL points to the distinct, earlier 2024.MM20.38 agenda item — two different real documents, not one, conflated in that row's description field.
