Ontario's homelessness count, 2024-2026: revisions, regional disparity, and funding scale

Ontario's own AMO/HelpSeeker homelessness estimates were revised between reports, grew fastest in Northern and rural communities specifically, and now sit alongside more than $4 billion in combined provincial and municipal funding — more than double the 2018 level.

The finding

A note on revisions, stated before the current figures: AMO/HelpSeeker's January 2025 report first estimated Ontario's 2024 chronic homelessness at 41,512 people, describing homelessness as having "tripled" CSRL2-120, with a total 2024 estimate of 81,515 CSRL2-026. The organization's own January 2026 update revised the 2024 chronic figure down to 38,739 CSRL2-121, and separately clarified that 78,802 is the "observed full-year 2024 total," distinct from 81,515, which was the earlier report's own projection — the two figures serve different purposes and one does not simply correct the other CSRL2-111.

The current, most recent figures: total known homelessness in Ontario for 2025 was 84,973 (rounded to "nearly 85,000" in AMO's own January 2026 release) CSRL2-112, CSRL2-023, with 45,111 people — 53% — chronically homeless, replacing an earlier approximate "42,000+" figure CSRL2-113. Ontario's chronic homelessness has roughly doubled since 2019, per the 2025-revised figures CSRL2-027.

Growth is sharply uneven across the province. From 2024 to 2025, Northern Ontario's known homelessness grew 37.3%, and has grown approximately 117.5% since 2021 — against 49.1% provincially over the same period. Mostly-rural Service Manager areas saw a 31.0% increase in 2025 alone. Service Managers report approximately 2,000 encampment sites province-wide. Combined provincial and municipal housing and homelessness funding reached over $4.0 billion in 2025, more than double the $2.1 billion reported in 2018 (+103%), with municipal funding specifically rising from $1.1 billion to $2.0 billion (+90%) [CL-90661].

Why it matters

The revision history matters on its own terms: a reader citing "41,512 chronically homeless in 2024" without knowing AMO/HelpSeeker's own later update revised that figure to 38,739 would be citing a superseded number, not a wrong one — the distinction matters for how corrections should be handled elsewhere in this library, and this page states both figures rather than picking one silently. The regional-disparity figures reframe a single provincial percentage: homelessness "up X% province-wide" obscures that Northern Ontario and rural areas are growing far faster than urban centres, a genuinely different policy picture than a flat provincial average suggests. The funding scale figure — more than doubling since 2018 — is a real, substantial investment increase that should be read alongside the homelessness growth figures, not in isolation.

What this page does not cover

This page does not reconcile every methodological difference between AMO/HelpSeeker's successive reports beyond what each report itself states about the other. It does not break down the $4.0 billion or $2.0 billion municipal figures by individual municipality. It does not carry a "case against" the funding response, since the sources reviewed are AMO/HelpSeeker's own reporting, not a documented opposing position on adequacy.

  • AMO/HelpSeeker, "Municipalities Under Pressure," 2025 (RES-0106).
