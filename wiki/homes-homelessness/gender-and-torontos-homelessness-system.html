Gender and Toronto's homelessness system: mortality, shelter design, and federal funding

Women make up a minority of Toronto's homeless population, but a 2026 peer-reviewed study found they die younger, die of overdose at a much higher rate, and have access to disproportionately less dedicated shelter capacity than their share of that population would suggest.

The finding

Toronto Public Health's full-year 2024 data puts the median age at death for people experiencing homelessness at 50 for men and 38 for women, against a general-population median of 78 for men and 85 for women — a gap of 28 years for men and 47 years for women CSRL2-716. A 2026 peer-reviewed study (BMC Public Health), drawing on Toronto Public Health's mortality dashboard, found 215 total deaths among people experiencing homelessness in Toronto in 2024: 163 men, 46 women, and 5 gender-diverse individuals — meaning men accounted for 76% of all deaths, roughly matching their larger share of the homeless population. Cause of death diverged sharply by gender, however: overdose accounted for 76% of women's deaths specifically, and 80% of gender-diverse individuals' deaths, compared with 48% of men's deaths CSRL2-477.

The same study found Toronto's shelter system structurally under-serves women relative to their share of the homeless population. Women make up 41% of Toronto's homeless population, but of the city's 93 adult shelters as of Q3 2025, only 25% (23 shelters) were women-only, compared with 35% (33 shelters) men-only and 40% (37 shelters) mixed-adult. The gap is sharper in bed capacity than in shelter count: women-only shelters held only about 18% of total adult shelter beds on average — the smallest share of any shelter type — and harm reduction services were available in only 26% of women-only shelters, the lowest rate across all shelter types CSRL2-478.

On funding, the federal government's 2021 budget committed $315.4 million over seven years, starting in 2021-22, through the Canada Housing Benefit, specifically to increase direct rent assistance for low-income women and children fleeing violence. The same budget separately reallocated $250 million under the National Housing Co-Investment Fund toward an estimated 560 units of transitional housing and shelter space for women and children fleeing violence CSRL2-131.

A fresh S1 discovery this pass (T-0065 batch 3) resolves this page's own previously-excluded citation: YWCA Toronto operates two violence-against-women shelters, a shelter for homeless women, and a transitional housing project, sheltering over 2,000 women annually; its most recent financial profile shows $46.5 million in revenue (66.9% government-funded), a 34.1% overhead rate, and $56 million in mortgage debt financing its shelters and housing — structured as negative operating reserves because that debt is leveraged directly into housing assets, not a sign of financial distress on its own CSRL2-199. This source was previously excluded from this page as a dead link; a direct live re-check this pass confirms it now resolves (HTTP 200), so it is included here. Separately, a search this pass also surfaced an unsourced claim — reported without its own primary citation — that "163 women, girls and children lost their lives to gender-based violence in Toronto" in 2024. That figure is not included here: its scale is implausible for a single city in a single year without independent confirmation, no primary source for it was found, and this project's own standing discipline is to verify serious claims computationally before repeating them, not on a secondary aggregator's say-so.

Why it matters

The shelter-capacity and cause-of-death findings describe a specific, structural mismatch: women die of overdose at a substantially higher rate than men within the same homeless population, while having access to a smaller share of shelter beds — and less harm reduction service within the shelter type most available to them — than their share of the population would predict. The age-at-death gap shows this isn't a marginal disparity: homeless women in Toronto are dying, on median, at an age less than half that of the general population's women. The federal funding commitment shows a targeted policy response exists at the funding-allocation level, but the shelter-system data describes what that funding has not yet closed on the ground.

What this page does not cover

This page does not evaluate whether the $315.4 million federal commitment or the $250 million co-investment reallocation have been fully deployed — a fresh S1 search this pass checked specifically and found no federal progress reporting (including the government's own March 2026 National Housing Strategy progress report) that reconciles these two specific 2021-budget line items to a current deployment figure; this remains a genuinely open question, not just an unexamined one. It does not evaluate service quality within provincially-administered violence-against-women shelters specifically, which operate under a separate system from the City-administered shelters this page's capacity data describes.
