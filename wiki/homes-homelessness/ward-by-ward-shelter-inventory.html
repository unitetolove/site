Toronto's base emergency shelter system: where it actually is, ward by ward

Toronto's 59 base emergency shelter sites are not distributed evenly across the city's 25 wards — 7 wards have zero base shelter capacity, while a single ward holds nearly a quarter of all sites including the system's single largest location.

The finding

A direct export from the City of Toronto's own official shelter locator tool, current as of July 2026, confirms 59 base emergency shelter sites citywide with 3,849 total named capacity (54 of the 59 sites have fixed published capacity figures). Seven of the city's 25 wards have zero base shelter sites: Don Valley West, Etobicoke Centre, Humber River-Black Creek, Scarborough North, Scarborough-Agincourt, York Centre, and York South-Weston. Toronto Centre alone holds 14 sites — 24% of the citywide total — including the system's single largest site, Seaton House Main, at 580 beds. The three highest-count wards together hold 31 of the 59 sites, 53% of the total CSRL2-771. An earlier, less precise pass over the same City export had already identified the core finding (59 sites, 18 of 25 wards with at least one site, 7 with none) before this more complete pass added the specific ward names, exact capacity figures, and concentration percentages above CSRL2-710.

A note on this data's own nature, stated plainly rather than glossed over: this is user-supplied primary data — a direct export from the City's own interactive tool, not a published report independently re-verifiable the same way a PDF or dataset file is. The appropriate verification method applied here was confirming the internal arithmetic checks out precisely (ward counts summing to the citywide total, percentages matching the underlying counts) — which it does — rather than an independent web re-fetch of the same interactive tool.

This is base shelter capacity specifically — the permanent, City-administered or Purchase-of-Service system — distinct from the broader occupancy dataset that also includes temporary hotel and warming centre capacity, which can substantially change a ward's total sheltering capacity in ways this specific inventory does not capture.

Why it matters

"7 of 25 wards have zero base shelter capacity" is a genuinely different, more actionable framing than a citywide total alone: it identifies specifically where the system's permanent capacity is not, not just how large it is overall. The concentration figures (one ward holding a quarter of all sites, including the single largest site) are directly relevant to any assessment of geographic equity in service placement, or to a ward councillor's own constituent-facing accountability on this question. The explicit caveat about this data's own primary-source nature matters too: a reader relying on this figure should understand it traces to a City tool export, verified by internal consistency, not an independently re-fetched published document — a real, disclosed distinction from most of this library's other citations.

What this page does not cover

This page does not cover the broader occupancy dataset that includes temporary hotel and warming centre capacity — a ward with zero base shelter sites may still have temporary sheltering capacity through that separate system, not captured here. It does not independently re-verify the City's shelter locator tool export through a second, separate re-fetch, for the reasons stated above. It does not carry a "case against" the current geographic distribution, since the sources reviewed are a primary data export and its own internal-consistency check, not a documented opposing position or a policy argument about where new capacity should go.
