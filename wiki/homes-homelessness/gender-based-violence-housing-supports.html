Housing supports for survivors of gender-based violence and Indigenous-led Housing First: three different models

Canadian jurisdictions are funding gender-based-violence-specific housing support through genuinely different mechanisms — a direct monthly benefit in Alberta, a provider- and training-funding model in British Columbia — alongside a separate, Indigenous-led Housing First program with its own documented outcomes in Toronto.

The finding

The federal government's Canada Housing Benefit for survivors of gender-based violence allocated $315.4 million over seven years, beginning with Budget 2021, with provincial rollout continuing through 2024-25 CSRL2-053. Alberta's own implementation provides a direct monthly benefit of $600 (paid over 12 months) or $1,200 (paid over 6 months) to survivors, totalling $7,200 per year CSRL2-052.

British Columbia's implementation takes a genuinely different form: rather than a direct-to-survivor monthly benefit, a 2026 federal-provincial partnership announced $4.6 million total — $4.3 million distributed through BC Housing's Women's Transition Housing and Supports Program to nearly 90 non-profit providers, and $300,000 specifically to develop a new staff training program, "Supporting Survivors: Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Housing," launching summer 2026 [CL-90663].

Separately, Na-Me-Res's Mino Kaanjigoowin program — an Indigenous-led Housing First model in Toronto — a 2019 evaluation found 66% of participants achieved positive housing transitions, with 54% moving into private housing, among 24 active members at the time of evaluation CSRL2-046.

Why it matters

Alberta's and BC's programs address the same population (survivors of gender-based violence) through genuinely different funding mechanisms — a direct cash benefit versus provider capacity and staff training — which matters for anyone assessing which model reaches survivors most directly versus which strengthens the service system around them. The Mino Kaanjigoowin outcomes give this library a real, specific data point for Indigenous-led Housing First delivery in Toronto specifically, distinct from generic Housing First outcome data that does not account for cultural safety or Indigenous-specific program design.

What this page does not cover

This page does not compare the three funding models' relative cost-effectiveness or reach, since the sources reviewed do not provide directly comparable metrics across jurisdictions. It does not track whether other provinces have implemented their own version of the Canada Housing Benefit for GBV survivors beyond Alberta and British Columbia. It does not carry a "case against" any of these programs, since the sources reviewed are each government's or organization's own program reporting, not a documented opposing position.
