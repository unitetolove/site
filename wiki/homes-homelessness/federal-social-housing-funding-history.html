From withdrawal to partial return: federal social housing funding, 1973 to 2029

The federal government's exit from direct social housing funding in the 1990s, and its much later, partial return through the National Housing Strategy and targeted encampment/unsheltered-homelessness programs, frames the funding gap this library's Ontario- and Toronto-specific pages describe in a longer national arc.

The finding

At its peak, from 1973 to 1993, Canada's federal social housing program built approximately 20,000 units per year CSRL2-039. That federal role ended between 1993 and 1996, when the federal government stopped funding new social housing CSRL2-038.

The federal government's re-entry came decades later and in a different form: under the National Housing Strategy, Ontario's bilateral agreement with the federal government now totals more than $5.75 billion from 2018 to 2028 — the original $4.2 billion, 10-year 2018 bilateral agreement, plus the Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit layered on top in 2019 CSRL2-066. Separately, a targeted federal program addressing unsheltered homelessness and encampments specifically — the Unsheltered Homelessness and Encampments Initiative (UHEI) — provides a $250 million base program from 2024-25 to 2025-26 (announced in Budget 2024, cost-matched by provinces), with a further $125 million extension announced April 1, 2026, bringing the running total to $375 million through 2028-29 CSRL2-133.

Why it matters

The scale contrast is stark on its own terms: roughly 20,000 units per year at peak federal involvement, against a funding gap of decades before the National Housing Strategy's bilateral agreements began partially restoring a federal role — at a different scale and through a different mechanism (cost-shared agreements and benefit payments, not direct unit construction at the prior pace). Reading Ontario- and Toronto-specific funding figures against this longer federal arc clarifies which funding gaps are a municipal or provincial policy choice versus a downstream consequence of a federal withdrawal that predates most current municipal officials' terms in office.

What this page does not cover

This page does not evaluate whether the National Housing Strategy's bilateral funding or UHEI represent an adequate replacement for the pre-1993 federal program's scale — that is a policy judgment this page's own sources do not make. It does not track UHEI's or the Ontario bilateral agreement's disbursement against their own stated targets beyond the top-line figures given here. It does not carry a "case against" any government's funding choices, since the sources reviewed are primary government program pages and academic/journalistic historical accounts, not a documented opposing position.
