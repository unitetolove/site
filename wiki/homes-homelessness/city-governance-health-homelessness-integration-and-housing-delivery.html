City governance: linking health and homelessness policy, and tracking housing delivery

Two of Toronto's own governance mechanisms sit behind the more visible programs this library covers elsewhere: a formal structure connecting health policy to homelessness policy, and a dedicated office now reporting, with real numbers, on how much of the City's housing pipeline is actually on track.

The 2021 service plan, and an open follow-up question

City Council adopted Toronto Shelter and Support Services' three-year Homelessness Solutions Service Plan in November 2021 (Item 2021.EC25.5), alongside the 2021 Street Needs Assessment results. As part of that same decision, Council directed the City Manager to report back by the January 26, 2022 Executive Committee meeting on the cost of a one-year rental subsidy for everyone then living in shelters, hotels, and respite sites. Whether that report-back was ever actually produced has not been confirmed by any research pass to date — a real, still-open question, not resolved here CSRL2-823.

The Health and Homelessness Working Table and its framework

Toronto's Board of Health formally adopted the Homelessness Health Services Framework on January 20, 2025 (Item 2025.HL21.5), following an eight-meeting Health and Homelessness Working Table that ran from August 2023 to May 2024, and established an HHSF Steering Committee. The same decision directed a further status report back to the Board of Health in Q1 2026, and asked the federal and provincial governments to jointly establish an intergovernmental collaborative group on homelessness and health that includes people with lived and living experience of homelessness [CL-824, CL-833]. Among its recommendations was a proposal for "peer support navigator programs to navigate the health care system" — a third independent City precedent (alongside the 10-Point Harm Reduction Plan and the Working Table's own summary report) for the kind of peer-navigator workforce model this library tracks elsewhere as REC S-06 CSRL2-833. Whether the Q1 2026 status report actually materialized has not been checked as part of this page.

Housing delivery: the Build Canada Homes ask and what's actually landing

The City's Housing Development Office — established in 2025 to unify public development efforts across CreateTO, TCHC, the City Real Estate Management division, and non-profit, Indigenous, and private housing partners — requested $557.5 million from the federal Build Canada Homes program in item PH30.6 (considered by the Planning and Housing Committee May 7, 2026, adopted by Council May 20-22, 2026) to support roughly 4,000 rental homes over 12-18 months; a separate $21.6 million from the same federal program was already secured for Dunn House Phase 2 CSRL2-826. The same report disclosed real 2025 delivery results: the City-led portfolio (83 sites, delivered via CreateTO/TCHC/CREM agreements or procurement) reached construction starts on 3,606 homes (1,497 net-new affordable or rent-geared-to-income) and completions on 1,259 homes, plus 908 more under construction; a separate City-supported portfolio of third-party and Rental Housing Supply Program projects reached 4,115 starts and 1,993 completions CSRL2-831.

Not every one of those 83 sites is on track. The HDO's own first annual report — of 74 projects contacted for updated status information, 54 responded — found 6 projects stalled (12 or more months from construction, or no longer proceeding at all), 20 with genuinely uncertain status because they hadn't submitted materials, and 26 combined projects at risk of losing previously-approved incentives. Across the full portfolio, the City's own contribution runs to roughly $900 million: $625.3 million in waived fees, development charges, and tax exemptions, plus $245 million in land value and foregone revenue [CL-50011].

What this page does not cover

This page does not resolve either of the two open follow-up questions it identifies — whether the 2022 rental-subsidy cost-back report was produced, and whether the Q1 2026 HHSF status report materialized — both flagged as genuinely unconfirmed rather than assumed either way. It also does not track PH30.6's own Recommendation 23, which directs staff to report back to the June 24-26, 2026 Council meeting comparing the City's most recent Build Canada Homes submission against its December 2025 one; that report-back was already due as of this page's writing but was not independently checked here.
