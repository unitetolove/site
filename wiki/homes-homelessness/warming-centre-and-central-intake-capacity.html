Warming centre costs and Central Intake capacity in Toronto

Toronto's warming centres are its most expensive form of overnight shelter per night, yet ran with thousands of unused bed-nights even as Central Intake — the phone line people call to find a shelter bed — has documented years of calls going unanswered or unmatched to a bed.

The finding

A City Auditor General review found 4,331 unused warming-centre bed-nights over the period reviewed CSRL2-005, out of a total system spend of $22.2 million on warming centres, of which $12.5 million flowed to third-party operators CSRL2-009. Warming centres are the most expensive shelter type in the system on a per-night basis CSRL2-008 — press coverage has commonly cited $359 per night as the figure, but this library's own re-check of the Auditor General's actual chart found that figure is press's own citation of the AG's range, not the AG's own single headline number: the AG's chart itself shows a $336-$491 per-night range, and a distinct, earlier-circulated $352 figure that had been attributed to hotel shelters was a mislabeling this library caught and corrected — the $350s-per-night range belongs to warming centres, not hotel shelters, in the AG's own data CSRL2-546 CSRL2-567. A specific case example from the same audit: one warming centre's catering costs came to $116,127 for what was budgeted as a 153-day operation but only ran 89 days CSRL2-317. More recently, the winter 2025-26 season saw warming centres open 98 nights, up from 78 the previous winter CSRL2-363.

On the intake side: a 2020-21 freedom-of-information data release found an average of 38 people per night turned away from Central Intake, peaking at 46 per night in January 2021 CSRL2-307, with 2,260 calls declined and 3,646 calls disconnected before being answered over the period examined CSRL2-308. More recent City data, from its own submission under the federal Unsheltered Homelessness and Encampments Initiative, put Central Intake's unmatched-caller figure at 216 per day as of November 2024 CSRL2-652. Council itself has been divided on warming-centre policy: a February 8, 2023 vote on a warming-centre motion passed narrowly, 15-11 CSRL2-683.

Why it matters

The core tension here is internal to the City's own numbers, not an outside critique imposed on them: its most expensive per-night shelter option ran with thousands of bed-nights unused, while its own intake phone line was independently documented — twice, years apart, via different data sources — as turning away or failing to connect a meaningful volume of callers to any bed at all. Whatever the right policy response is, the AG's own case example (a 64-day gap between budgeted and actual warming-centre operating days, with a fixed catering cost regardless) is a specific, concrete illustration of a cost structure that does not flex efficiently with actual need.

What this page does not cover

This page does not attempt to reconcile the AG's $336-$491 per-night range against the specific $359 figure into one single "true" number — both are real, sourced figures serving different purposes (a range from the underlying audit data versus a specific figure commonly cited in press coverage of it), and presenting either alone as the single correct number would overstate the precision either source actually offers. It does not independently verify whether the specific unused-bed-nights and turned-away-caller figures overlap in time or population (the sources cited span different specific periods, from 2020-21 through November 2024), so this page describes a persistent pattern across multiple data points, not a single reconciled annual total. It does not carry a "case for" the current warming-centre cost structure, since the sources reviewed are the City's own Auditor General findings and direct news/FOI reporting, not a defence of current practice.
