Two supportive housing site histories: Dunn House's hospital-partnership model and Strachan House's closure and rebuild

Dunn House and Strachan House show two different but related stories in Toronto's supportive housing landscape — one a proof-of-concept hospital-discharge model now expanding, the other a closed shelter site now being rebuilt at more than four times its original scale.

The finding

Dunn House, at 90 Dunn Avenue, opened in October 2024 as Canada's first hospital-led supportive housing site, a partnership with the University Health Network (UHN). UHN's own stated cost rationale traces to a specific, striking figure: its top 100 homeless patients accounted for 4,309 emergency department visits in a single year CSRL2-743. Early outcome data bears out the model's premise: Dunn House tenants showed a 52% reduction in emergency department visits and a 79% drop in hospital-bed days after roughly a year, generating an estimated $2.1 million in annual healthcare cost savings. Building on this, a January 20, 2026 tri-government announcement confirmed Dunn House Phase 2: $21.6 million in federal funding through Build Canada Homes, up to $2.6 million per year in provincial operational funding, and the City of Toronto leading delivery as public developer — 54 rent-geared-to-income studio units using modular construction. Phase 2 marks a real shift in population focus, from the original model to at-risk seniors specifically, with on-site health and social services access [CL-90656].

Strachan House, at 805 Wellington Street West, was a Homes First-operated supportive housing site that closed in 2022, displacing its residents. It is now being redeveloped by CreateTO CSRL2-746. The redevelopment's full scope, present in the same source already archived for that finding but not previously captured in this library's own figures, is substantially larger than "restoration" alone suggests: two buildings, an 8-storey structure with 81 deeply affordable supportive housing units (the figure previously on file), and a separate 30-storey, 331-foot tower with 240 market rental units plus 61 affordable rental units — 382 total residential units across both buildings, with a unit mix of 93 bachelor, 153 one-bedroom, 106 two-bedroom, and 30 three-bedroom units, and 52 accessible units [CL-90657].

Why it matters

Dunn House's UHN partnership is a genuine, functioning proof-of-concept for the hospital-discharge housing model this library has tracked as a policy proposal elsewhere — it is not hypothetical, it has operating outcome data, and it is now being expanded and adapted (to a senior-specific population) rather than left as a one-off pilot. Strachan House shows the opposite trajectory in the same broad category: a supportive housing site closed outright in 2022, its previous 81-unit figure now confirmed as only the supportive-housing component of a much larger, 382-unit mixed-income redevelopment — a genuinely different picture than "81 units restored" alone would suggest, and a concrete illustration of how a closed site can return at a different scale and with a different funding/tenure mix than it had before.

What this page does not cover

This page does not verify Dunn House Phase 2's construction timeline, since the source itself does not specify project completion dates. It does not independently confirm the 52%/79%/$2.1 million original Dunn House outcome figures beyond the sources already on file for that finding. It does not track Strachan House's redevelopment through City Council's final decision, expected at its July 29-31, 2026 meeting per the most recent reporting found — a live process this page's own snapshot predates. It does not carry a "case against" either project, since the sources reviewed are government press releases and direct industry reporting, not a documented opposing position.
