Federal housing-funding mechanisms: Build Canada Homes and UHEI

Two federal programs now supply direct funding into Toronto's housing and homelessness system: Build Canada Homes (BCH), a federal housing developer, and the Unsheltered Homelessness and Encampments Initiative (UHEI). Both fund capital and projects rather than running programs themselves — the provincial and municipal partners who receive that funding remain responsible for the operating dollars and service delivery layered on top.

The finding

The federal government introduced the Build Canada Homes Act on February 5, 2026, converting BCH from the Special Operating Agency it launched as in September 2025 into a permanent Crown corporation. At introduction, BCH had six Direct Build projects underway — in Dartmouth, Longueuil, Ottawa, Toronto, Winnipeg, and Edmonton — plus four major partnerships (with the City of Ottawa, the provinces of Nova Scotia and Quebec, and a tripartite agreement with Nunavut and Nunavut Tunngavik Incorporated), representing more than 7,500 homes in total CSRL2-806.

BCH is headed by Ana Bailao, a former Toronto city councillor and TCHC board chair. The Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO) has publicly called on the province to match BCH's federal capital dollars with provincial operating funding, specifically for the wraparound supports supportive housing needs to function CSRL2-808. BCH's own public FAQ describes its role more softly than AMO's sharper capital-vs-operating framing: it says BCH "will collaborate with key provincial, territorial, municipal, and Indigenous partners to pair these federal investments with employment and health care supports," without using the words "capital only" itself CSRL2-807. The clean capital/operating split is AMO's own characterization of how the funding should work, not a rule BCH's FAQ states outright — this claim is carried at the ledger's VERIFY (not yet fully verified) confidence tier for that reason, and the sharper framing should be attributed to AMO, not to BCH directly.

Dunn House Phase 2, announced January 20, 2026, is a concrete Toronto example of how BCH funding actually layers with other partners in practice: $21.6 million in federal (BCH) capital financing, up to $2.6 million per year in Ontario operating funding, and the City of Toronto acting as public developer — its housing corporation CreateTO is delivering the project using modular construction. The building will provide 54 rent-geared-to-income studio units for at-risk seniors, built on University Health Network's Gattuso Centre for Social Medicine model of integrating health care directly into supportive housing CSRL2-809.

Separately, on April 1, 2026, the federal government announced $125 million to extend UHEI, its program funding responses to unsheltered homelessness and encampments. Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada delivers this money through agreements with provincial, territorial, and municipal partners, funding specific projects identified through each partner's own Community Encampment Response Plan CSRL2-810.

A fresh S1 discovery this pass (T-0065 batch 2) resolves this page's own previously-flagged open question about whether Toronto's Community Encampment Response Plan (CERP) — the document that determines which of Toronto's own projects UHEI funding actually reaches — has been obtained. It has: Toronto's CERP sets a target of reducing encampments to below 300 by March 2026 and commits to six new permanent shelters with up to 450 shelter spaces over two to five years, alongside added front-line and outreach staffing [CL-90668]. This is drawn from the earlier $25.8 million UHEI tranche (2024-25/ 2025-26), not directly the later $125 million April 2026 extension above — the two are related but distinct UHEI funding rounds, and this page does not assume the 2026 extension's CERP-equivalent commitments are identical.

A second fresh finding names the specific Toronto project inside BCH's six-project Direct Build slate, which the announcement behind CSRL2-806 never itself named: Arbo Downsview, 540 new homes on the former Downsview Airport/Canadian Forces Base site, delivered via BCH's Direct Build approach, with a builder Request for Qualifications issued October 2025 [CL-90669].

Why it matters

These four claims together show the same funding pattern repeating at both the national-program level (BCH, UHEI) and the single-project level (Dunn House): the federal government supplies capital, and provincial or municipal partners are expected to supply the operating money and service delivery that make a capital investment actually function as housing or as an encampment response. For Toronto specifically, this means federal capital announcements are not by themselves a complete funding picture — whether a given project's operating and support funding has actually been secured is a separate question this cluster of claims does not, by itself, answer for every case (Dunn House being the one exception here where the provincial operating commitment is documented alongside the federal capital dollars).

What this page does not cover

Toronto's CERP has now been obtained and its content confirmed (see above) — no longer an open question as of the T-0065 batch 2 pass. This page still does not verify whether the CERP's own commitments (the <300-encampments target, the six new permanent shelters) have been met, only that the plan itself exists and states them. It does not track construction progress on Arbo Downsview or any of BCH's other five Direct Build projects or four partnerships beyond Dunn House, since the underlying sources are point-in-time announcements, not a running tracker — Arbo Downsview's own RFQ process had closed as of this pass with no construction-start date confirmed. Note also that a widely repeated but unsupported claim — that the City of Toronto contributed an additional $10 million to Dunn House Phase 2 — does not appear in the primary source for that announcement and is deliberately not repeated here.
