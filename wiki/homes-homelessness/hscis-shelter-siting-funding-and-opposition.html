HSCIS: Toronto's purpose-built shelter strategy — funding, votes, and site opposition

Toronto's Homelessness Services Capital Infrastructure Strategy (HSCIS) is a ten-year plan, adopted by City Council in November 2023, to build 20 smaller, purpose-built shelters (roughly 80 people each) to replace the city's reliance on shelter hotels. Two years in, the funding is real but partial, the Council votes advancing individual sites have been close enough to produce a consistent opposition bloc, and at least one site is now in active litigation over how it was selected.

Funding and site progress

As of the City's 2026 budget, $507.6 million of the program's $674.5 million capital budget is approved, funding 13 of the planned 20 sites — a jump from an earlier 2025-vintage figure of $258.1 million and 7 sites, driven by $209.4 million in new funding specifically prioritizing the design and construction of 11 sites acquired in 2024 and 2025 CSRL2-456. $166.9 million remains unfunded, to be considered in future budget processes; the federal government's request for full project-cost funding was still unconfirmed as of the 2026 budget CSRL2-163. Of the first sites confirmed with public addresses, six appear in the July 2025 Planning and Housing Committee decision report: 66-66Y Third St, 1220-1222 Wilson Ave, 2204-2212 Eglinton Ave W/601 Caledonia Rd, 1615 Dufferin St, 68 Sheppard Ave W, and 2535 Gerrard St E — a 7th site (on Bloor St W) is referenced in the 2026 budget's ~750-bed total but does not appear with an address in that same decision report CSRL2-162. Openings are staggered: the first sites are expected in 2027, with most following in 2028-2030.

The Council votes

HSCIS was adopted in principle at Council Item EC7.7 in November 2023. The only individually recorded vote that day was on three specific recommendations Councillor Stephen Holyday requested be split out (Recs 9, 10, and 13), which passed 22-1 with Holyday as the sole dissent; the HSCIS adoption itself carried as part of the balance of the item without a separate recorded vote CSRL2-277. The more consequential vote came at Council Item PH23.3 in July 2025, advancing the first six specific shelter sites: it passed 22-3, with Councillor Brad Bradford voting against every individual site and the item as a whole, joined by Holyday and Councillor Jaye Robinson (via Councillor Pasternak's vote against at least the Wilson Ave site) [CL-279, CL-282, CL-684]. Bradford also moved, and lost, motions to strip individual sites from the item.

Site-selection transparency: a genuine two-sided tension

City staff have a deliberate practice of withholding specific new HSCIS site locations from both the public and local councillors until a location is finalized — a policy staff describe as intended to de-politicize site selection and avoid the delays that public pressure campaigns against a specific address tend to produce. The practice is documented, not disputed: what's contested is whether it's the right tradeoff. Councillor Holyday, whose own ward saw a site purchase decided without his input for several weeks, pushed a motion (which failed) to return site-approval authority to Council; a narrower motion from Councillor Pasternak to strengthen the consultation process around already-announced sites passed instead CSRL2-738. This page states the practice and the positions on it without taking a side on which value — speed/depoliticization versus local input — should prevail, consistent with this project's own neutrality discipline.

Litigation: the 66 Third Street site

The clearest concrete test of that tension is now in court. The New Toronto Initiative (South Etobicoke Community Association) filed a $1 million claim in the Ontario Superior Court of Justice against the City, Ward 3 Councillor Amber Morley, and consultant Bruce Davis of Public Progress, over the planned 50-bed seniors' shelter at 66 Third Street — one of the six sites advanced at PH23.3 CSRL2-574. The claim alleges a negligent and procedurally unfair selection process; its central, specific factual complaint is that the site is 9,200 square feet against the City's own stated 15,000-square-foot minimum guideline, roughly 40% under [CL-50008]. Group spokesperson Dan Perdue has said residents received information sessions but no real engagement; Councillor Morley has stated she continues to support the shelter and is working to ensure it is "thoughtfully designed, responsibly operated" [CL-50008]. The shelter is expected to open between 2028 and 2030 if it proceeds as planned.

What this page does not cover

This page does not track the litigation's outcome — it was still active as of this writing. It does not resolve the site-count discrepancy at CL-162 (the missing 7th, Bloor St W address) beyond reporting it as an open, previously-flagged gap; a dedicated follow-up would need the City's own updated site list, not guesswork. It also does not cover HSCIS sites beyond the first six advanced at PH23.3, since later sites have not yet been individually researched for this library. A genuine S1 live-discovery pass (WebSearch) confirmed the funding figures already in the ledger ($507.6M/$674.5M, 13/20 sites) are current as of this writing and surfaced nothing materially new on that front; the lawsuit search, by contrast, surfaced real new detail now captured above as CL-50008.
