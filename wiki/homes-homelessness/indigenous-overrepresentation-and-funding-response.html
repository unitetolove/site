Indigenous overrepresentation in Toronto's homelessness system, and the funding response

Indigenous people are a small share of Toronto's general population but a disproportionate share of its homeless population, its shelter workforce's experience of racist abuse, and — provincially — its overdose deaths. Toronto has a specific, named funding mechanism responding to part of this picture.

The finding

The City of Toronto's 2024 Street Needs Assessment found 9% of survey respondents identified as Indigenous, against 3% of Toronto's general population — roughly three times overrepresented; the same survey found 58% of respondents identified as Black, against 10% of the general population CSRL2-770. Provincially, a 2024 Auditor General report on opioid strategy found the death rate for Indigenous people in Ontario was 11.4 per 100,000, against 1.6 per 100,000 for non-Indigenous people — roughly a sevenfold disparity — documented in the same report that found the province closing a supervised consumption site in Thunder Bay without adequately assessing the resulting harm CSRL2-686. In northern Ontario specifically, 45% of people experiencing chronic homelessness identify as Indigenous, in a region where reported homelessness rose an estimated 204% between 2016 and 2024 CSRL2-157.

Inside the shelter system, a 2024 CAMH shelter safety study found 43.2% of Black and Indigenous shelter staff reported experiencing racist verbal abuse or threats on a daily basis CSRL2-802.

On the funding side, City Council has mandated a minimum 20% set-aside of all Reaching Home, provincial Homelessness Prevention Program, and City Homelessness Investment Fund grant funding for an Indigenous (First Nations, Inuit, and Métis) funding stream, administered through a Memorandum of Understanding with the Aboriginal Labour Force Development Circle as the Indigenous Community Entity CSRL2-367. A concrete, site-specific example of this funding stream in practice is 720 Bathurst St., a property the City has purchased to provide services for Indigenous people experiencing homelessness, with an anticipated capacity of approximately 80 people; it will be operated by Na-Me-Res (Native Men's Residence), which has served Indigenous men experiencing homelessness in Toronto since 1985, with an estimated opening in 2028-2029 CSRL2-712. A fresh S1 discovery this pass (T-0065 batch 5), limited deliberately to this page's factual/financial sub-questions and not its separately-flagged governance content, finds the purchase price this page had not previously confirmed: the City paid $16 million for the 720 Bathurst St. building [CL-90680].

Why it matters

The overrepresentation and disparity figures here span three separate systems — general homelessness counts, provincial overdose mortality, and shelter workplace safety — and each independently shows Indigenous people affected at a rate well beyond their share of the general population. The City's own 20% minimum funding set-aside is a specific, binding policy response to part of this picture, and 720 Bathurst St. is a concrete example of that funding translating into a named, dedicated site with an Indigenous-led operator, rather than remaining only a budget line.

What this page does not cover

This page deliberately does not cover the governance-authority questions around Indigenous self-determination bodies in Toronto's homelessness system (such as the Toronto Indigenous Community Advisory Board's specific decision-making powers, or how they compare to non-binding advisory committees) — this library's own prior review flagged that content as needing real Indigenous consultation before further research or publication, not just citation-checking, and it is deliberately left for that process rather than drafted here. 720 Bathurst St.'s purchase price is now confirmed (see above). This page still does not include a stated match-rate target beyond the 20% funding set-aside — that specific figure was re-checked this pass and still does not appear in any source verified for this page, so it remains genuinely unconfirmed rather than assumed absent.
