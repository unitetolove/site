Learning & Growing Up

Published 2026-07-30 · part of the research library.

Start with the honest fact: the city you vote in doesn’t run your child’s school. Who teaches, what gets taught, and how a school is funded are decisions made at Queen’s Park and by school boards — not by City Hall. What the city does control is narrower but still real: recreation programs, libraries, before- and after-school care, and shared use of school buildings. Right now, Toronto’s own school board is also in an unusual state, under direct provincial supervision.

This is the thinnest-researched category in this library. That’s reflected honestly below: fewer figures, and open questions named rather than papered over.

Where things stand

  1. The Toronto District School Board has been under a Ministry-appointed supervisor since mid-2025, after a financial investigation found the board had been balancing its budget partly through property-sale proceeds. The investigator’s own report found no evidence of mismanagement but recommended supervision anyway — and elected trustees currently have no vote on anything the board does.
  2. A related provincial law, since passed, sets a province-wide range of five to twelve elected trustees per school board — reported as cutting the TDSB’s own trustee count roughly in half, though the law itself sets a range rather than the TDSB’s specific resulting number.
  3. Toronto’s popular children’s recreation programs commonly sell out quickly after quarterly registration opens. The backlog of overdue repairs to the city’s recreation centres, arenas, and pools stood at roughly $986 million at the end of 2024.
  4. About half of newcomer children can’t swim, compared with about nine in ten Canadian-born children — and nine in ten of those newcomer kids who can’t swim aren’t currently taking lessons at all. Toronto’s own director of community recreation has said the city needs about 1,100 lifeguards; only 750 were confirmed.
  5. The city already has working examples of shared use between itself and the school board: Davisville Public School’s new community aquatic centre was built on board land under a City–TDSB lease, and the province-funded Community Use of Schools program gives “reduced or free equitable access” to school space.

One honest gap: no source in this library has yet produced a solid, sourced figure for what happens to a Toronto child between the school bell and a parent getting home from work, or for who actually gets a recreation-program spot when it sells out quickly. This isn’t because nobody looked — it’s the thinnest-researched category in this whole project, and this page says that plainly rather than filling the gap with unsourced material.

What the city controls vs. what needs the province

The city can, on its own: run and fund recreation programs, libraries, and before/after-school care (the After-School Recreation Care and Community Leadership After-School Program are two existing examples); negotiate shared-use deals for school gyms, pools, and fields outside class hours; decide how it staffs and prices recreation registration, since the sell-out problem and the lifeguard shortage both trace to the city’s own budget and hiring choices.

The city can only ask, not require: anything about what happens inside a classroom — curriculum, teacher hiring, class sizes, or how a school board spends its money. Those decisions currently sit with a provincially appointed supervisor, not with anyone Toronto voters elected.

Only the province decides: school funding formulas, curriculum content, and — as of this page’s writing — how many elected trustees the TDSB has and when (or whether) elected trustees get their governing authority back.

How this connects to the election

The municipal ballot elects school trustees, even though the city itself does not control curriculum, staffing, or school funding. A resident who wants to ask a candidate for city council, mayor, or school trustee what they would do about any of the levers described above can use the neutral, non-partisan candidate question bank for this category — every question there works identically for any candidate, any party. This page does not endorse, score, or rank any candidate or party, and does not direct a reader to any organization or campaign.
