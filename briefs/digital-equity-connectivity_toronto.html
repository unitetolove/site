Digital Equity and Connectivity — Toronto

DRAFT Jurisdiction: Toronto (single-tier city; CD and CSD codes coincide) · Data as of: 2026-07-14 (ConnectTO's July 2025 Program Update and October 2025 milestone release; CRTC April 2026 decision; secondary-sourced 2025 StatsCan and Ofcom figures)

Status: DRAFT · Date: 2026-07-14 Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/digital-equity-connectivity.md · Data as of: 2026-07-14 (ConnectTO's July 2025 Program Update and October 2025 milestone release; CRTC April 2026 decision; secondary-sourced 2025 StatsCan and Ofcom figures) Jurisdiction: Toronto (single-tier city; CD and CSD codes coincide) · Trust classes: INHERITED (leaf-promoted this leaf's inherited master briefing (digital equity connectivity), cited as-is) + NEW-LOAD-BEARING (this pass's live-discovery findings, cited NEW-2026-#, pending formal ledger promotion)

TL;DR

Toronto's public Wi-Fi program (ConnectTO) has grown to more than 250 City-owned locations plus 100 library branches by late 2025, covering 89% of Neighbourhood Improvement Areas and Emerging Neighbourhoods, on top of the roughly 7,600 daily connections already measured in 2024 [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-2]. The City's own most current record, however, shows no City-administered broadband subsidy for OW/ODSP recipients has launched — this leaf's inherited briefing's "exploring a subsidy" framing does not hold as of this pass; the City's actual posture is referral to pre-existing federal (Connecting Families) and private-carrier (Rogers Connected for Success) programs [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-4]. About 58,000 Torontonians (roughly 2% of the city) are cited by the City's own commissioned research as lacking affordable in-home internet [NEW-2026-1] — a different framing from this leaf's inherited ~40%-of-lowest-income-households figure, not reconciled in this pass. Federal/CRTC action in 2025-2026 has focused on general-market competition (opening incumbent fibre networks to competitors, finalizing wholesale rates in April 2026) rather than income-targeted affordability specifically [NEW-2026-6].

FOR (the case that Toronto's current approach is real, functioning progress)

  • ConnectTO's Wi-Fi footprint has more than doubled since 2022 (from 100 library-only sites to 252+ City-owned sites, 300 by end of 2025), with 88-89% coverage of the specific neighbourhoods the City's own equity framework prioritizes [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-2].
  • Usage data shows real, sustained reliance: 7,600 daily connections in 2024, 69% repeat users, with nearly 20% of sessions lasting over 8 hours — consistent with people using the service as a genuine digital workspace, not an occasional convenience [NEW-2026-1, inherited].
  • The City is actively directing OW/ODSP-eligible residents toward subsidies they already qualify for (Connecting Families, Connected for Success) through a dedicated Human Services Integration channel, rather than leaving enrollment to chance [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-4].
  • Federal/CRTC competition action is real and dated: opening incumbent fibre networks to competitors (February 2025) and finalizing the wholesale access rates that make that competition durable (April 24, 2026) — not merely proposed, but decided [NEW-2026-6].

AGAINST (the case that the core affordability gap, and this leaf's own inherited "exploring a subsidy" claim, remain unaddressed or overstated)

  • The specific OW/ODSP broadband subsidy this leaf's inherited briefing described as being explored has not launched, per the City's own most current (July 2025) primary source — the City's actual activity is referral-only, not subsidy creation [NEW-2026-1].
  • A structurally similar referral-only mechanism elsewhere (UK Ofcom-regulated social tariffs) shows only ~8.6% uptake among eligible households with roughly 70% unaware the option exists, despite the tariffs being available from every major provider — a documented failure mode this pass found no evidence Toronto's own approach has built measurement against [backgrounder International context §2(b)].
  • The City's own two cited scale figures for the divide (58,000 people/~2% citywide, versus this leaf's inherited ~40%-of-lowest-income-households figure) are not reconciled, leaving the true scope of Toronto's disconnected population genuinely unclear from the sources reviewed in this pass.
  • No 2025-2026 CRTC or federal action specifically reforming Connecting Families' eligibility, data cap, or price point was located — the program's terms remain as this leaf's inherited briefing already described them, even as general-market competition measures advanced [NEW-2026-6].
  • Independent (non-incumbent) wholesale internet providers' market share fell from 8.4% to 4.2% between 2020 and 2024, a trend the Competition Bureau's own market study frames as evidence of a persistent two-network (telephone/cable) duopoly limiting competitive pressure on price [NEW-2026-7, NEW-2026-10] — the structural driver of affordability this leaf's inherited briefing already names is, on this evidence, not visibly improving at the market-share level even as regulatory activity increases.

Both sides draw on real, cited facts. The FOR case leans on ConnectTO's own measurable Wi-Fi-expansion and referral activity; the AGAINST case leans on the gap between what this leaf's inherited briefing claimed was in progress (a subsidy) and what the City's own most current record actually shows (referral only), plus structural market evidence that the underlying affordability driver has not visibly eased. This brief does not adjudicate which side the evidence favours on balance — only states the asymmetry plainly, consistent with this leaf's own neutrality discipline.

Precedents

This leaf's inherited briefing documents ConnectTO's own history (the 2021 municipal-broadband vision, the 2022 cancellation of the affordable-network plan, the pivot to public Wi-Fi) as the central Toronto-specific precedent, cited there rather than re-derived here [inherited from the master briefing]. This pass's own live discovery adds two non-Toronto precedents: San Jose, California's SJ Access program (library-foundation-administered since April 2023, publishing a specific tracked device-gap metric — 25,500 "digitally disadvantaged" households — alongside its connectivity figures, with roughly 3,200 devices distributed via library rental against a 50,000-household connectivity goal) [NEW-2026-9]; and the UK's Ofcom-regulated broadband/mobile social-tariff system (available from all major providers since regulatory encouragement, but reaching only ~8.6% of eligible households as of June 2025 due to a documented ~70% awareness gap) [backgrounder International context §2(b)]. Both are cited with their own stated limitations rather than as unqualified successes, per this leaf's own evidentiary discipline.

Upward Ask

This leaf's own inherited framing is explicit that the deepest lever — internet affordability — is set by a concentrated national telecom market and federal/CRTC regulation, not the City [inherited from the master briefing]. This pass confirms real federal/CRTC activity in the relevant window (competitor fibre-network access opened February 2025; final wholesale rates set April 24, 2026; fee-elimination and contract-notification reforms in March-April 2026) [NEW-2026-6], none of which is targeted specifically at OW/ODSP-level or income-tested affordability — that remains Connecting Families' role, and this pass found no evidence of Connecting Families itself being reformed or expanded in 2025-2026. research/data/municipal_asks.csv was not checked against a row specific to this issue in this pass — a genuine gap, not confirmed either way. No Toronto City Council motion specifically asking the CRTC or federal government to expand Connecting Families' eligibility (e.g., to OW/ODSP recipients directly, rather than only CCB/GIS recipients) or to require telecom incumbents to fund a low-income-specific tariff (the Canadian analogue to the UK's Ofcom-mandated social tariffs) was identified in this pass. The clearest documented federal-level lever available for an upward ask, per this pass's own findings, is: Connecting Families' current eligibility (CCB/GIS recipients) does not explicitly include OW/ODSP recipients as a distinct eligibility category the way Rogers' own Connected for Success program does [NEW-2026-3, NEW-2026-4] — a specific, nameable gap between the private-carrier program's eligibility and the federal program's own eligibility that a Council ask could target concretely, though this brief does not assert such an ask has already been made.

Bottom line

Toronto's own public Wi-Fi program is real, measured, and expanding on schedule — but the specific OW/ODSP subsidy this leaf's inherited briefing described as being explored has not materialized; the City's actual 2025 record shows referral to existing federal and private programs instead. The deepest lever, affordability set by a concentrated telecom market, remains federal and largely outside municipal control, and 2025-2026 federal/CRTC action, while real, has targeted general-market competition rather than the income-tested affordability gap this leaf centres.

Uncertainties

  • Whether the City's OW/ODSP subsidy-exploration work described in this leaf's inherited briefing has been formally abandoned, merely deprioritized, or never progressed past an internal exploratory stage was not confirmed in this pass — this brief states only what the City's own most current public report shows (referral, not subsidy), not the internal reason why.
  • The reconciliation between the master briefing's ~40%-of-lowest-income-households figure and the City's own 58,000-person/2%-of-Torontonians figure remains open — genuinely unresolved, not merely unexamined.
  • The narrower 95%/62% income-quartile access figure (NEW-2026-5) is sourced via a secondary aggregator, not independently fetched from StatsCan's own table in this pass — ⚠️ VERIFY before public use.
  • Whether any Toronto-specific enrollment data exists showing how many OW/ODSP recipients have actually taken up Connecting Families or Connected for Success (as opposed to merely being eligible) was not located in this pass — this is precisely the gap RC-DEC-01 proposes measuring.
  • The Competition Bureau's broadband market study's ultimate disposition (whether a final public report was ever published, beyond the 2018-2019 notice and update pages this pass located) was not confirmed — flagged in the backgrounder's Cui Bono quote-context block.
