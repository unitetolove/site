Mental Health Crisis Response — Toronto

DRAFT Jurisdiction: Toronto (single-tier city; CD and CSD codes coincide, sgc_code 3520005/3520) · Data as of: 2026-07-13 (2026 Social Development and Toronto Police Service Budget Notes; 2025-2026 CAHOOTS/Denver STAR news reporting)

Status: DRAFT · Version: v1.1 · Date: 2026-07-13 Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/mental-health-crisis-response.md · Data as of: 2026-07-13 (2026 Social Development and Toronto Police Service Budget Notes; 2025-2026 CAHOOTS/Denver STAR news reporting) Jurisdiction: Toronto (single-tier city; CD and CSD codes coincide, sgc_code 3520005/3520) · Trust classes: INHERITED (leaf-promoted docs, pre-existing ledger claims) + NEW-LOAD-BEARING (this pass's quote-context findings, cited NEW-2026-#, pending formal ledger promotion)

TL;DR

Toronto's non-police mental-health crisis responder, the Toronto Community Crisis Service (TCCS), has been operating citywide since July 8, 2024, is piloting embedded subway-platform crisis teams since November 2025, and is moving its own call dispatch into Toronto Paramedic Services in 2026 [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-5, NEW-2026-7]. TCCS's reported diversion rate (calls completed without police involvement) has risen from 78% in 2024 toward a 90% target by 2027, alongside a rising call-volume trajectory [NEW-2026-2]. Toronto Police Service's own 2026 budget documents independently report an 18.5% year-over-year fall in mental-health calls and a 63% rise in referrals to TCCS over the same period [NEW-2026-11]. A genuine, unresolved arithmetic discrepancy exists between this leaf's own inherited call/diversion figures and this pass's directly-quoted primary source, flagged rather than silently resolved. Separately, this pass found that the two headline non-Toronto comparator programs typically cited to validate this model — CAHOOTS and Denver's STAR — are each, as of 2025, in materially worse shape than commonly presented: CAHOOTS ended in its founding city of Eugene in April 2025, and Denver STAR has not reached its own stated 24/7 coverage goal, citing Denver's "budget woes" [NEW-2026-17, NEW-2026-18].

FOR (scale and formalize the non-police response)

  • TCCS is real, citywide, and actively expanding: Council-approved citywide expansion (November 2023), live citywide operation (July 2024), a subway-platform pilot (November 2025), and a 2026 dispatch-integration move into Toronto Paramedic Services [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-5, NEW-2026-7].
  • TPS's own budget documents corroborate, from the police side, a real complementary trend: an 18.5% fall in mental-health calls and a 63% rise in TCCS referrals in the same 2024-2025 window [NEW-2026-11].
  • The City's own Auditor General has already identified six further non-mental-health 911 call types (check address, check well-being, dispute, noisy parties, landlord and tenant dispute, unwanted guest) that could potentially be routed to a non-police responder — a concrete, City-sourced roadmap for expansion, not a hypothetical [CL-90662].
  • Toronto's own 2022 pilot-era data shows a low (2.5%) police-attendance-request rate and a low (1.7%) ambulance-request rate for TCCS-attended calls, alongside 799 referrals to mental-health, housing, and healthcare supports in just the first six months — a genuinely favourable Toronto-specific safety and referral record, even if now several years old [NEW-2026-16].

AGAINST (caution on over-reliance on the comparator evidence and on TCCS's own figures)

  • The two comparator programs (CAHOOTS, Denver STAR) most often used to validate the "right responder" model are, as of 2025, each less stable than typically presented: CAHOOTS no longer operates in Eugene, its founding city, following a funding/contract collapse in April 2025; Denver STAR has not reached its own 24/7 coverage goal and names its own city's budget crisis as the reason [NEW-2026-17, NEW-2026-18]. Citing either program's older headline figures (CAHOOTS's ~24,000-call, 0.6%-backup year was 2019; Denver STAR's zero-backup figure was its first six months in 2020) without this update risks presenting Toronto's model as riding on more stable external proof points than currently exist.
  • TCCS's own headline figures do not fully reconcile: this leaf's inherited claim describes "diverting 7,800" of 10,339 calls, while this pass's directly-quoted 2026 Budget Notes state 7,800 as the total 911-sourced calls received (not the diverted count), with 6,891 as the actual number diverted — a real, documented discrepancy in the program's own reporting, not resolved by any source in this pass [NEW-2026-3]. Separately, that same primary-source sentence contains its own internal arithmetic error: 6,891 ÷ 7,800 = 88.3%, not the "78%" the sentence itself states — a ten-point gap too large to be rounding. This brief states the source's figures as reported and flags the discrepancy explicitly rather than picking a number to trust.
  • No source located in this pass isolates a TCCS-specific dollar budget figure from the broader $50.7 million Community Safety and Wellbeing service line it sits inside, and a circulating $31.7 million (2025) / $35.3 million (2026) figure was not independently confirmed via direct primary-source quotation in this pass [NEW-2026-9].
  • No current (2025-2026) TCCS police/ambulance-backup-rate figure was located to compare against the 2022 pilot-era 2.5%/1.7% figures, leaving open whether that safety profile has held as the service scaled citywide [NEW-2026-16].

Both sides draw on real, cited figures from the same or comparable primary sources; the AGAINST side leans more heavily on figures this pass's own live-discovery specifically surfaced (the comparator-program status check and the arithmetic discrepancy), while the FOR side leans more heavily on this leaf's already-inherited synthesis — stated here as the honest shape of the asymmetry rather than smoothed over.

Precedents

  • CAHOOTS (Eugene, OR): the most frequently cited U.S. precedent for this model; discontinued in Eugene itself in April 2025 due to funding/contract collapse, continuing only in Springfield (11am-11pm) [NEW-2026-17] — cited here as an outcome claim (a real, dated result), not a bare "other cities have done this."
  • Denver STAR (Denver, CO): operating five years, 8 vans, 32 staff, responded to 25,144 incidents June 2020-June 2025, but has not reached its own stated 24/7 coverage goal and cites budget constraints as the reason [NEW-2026-18].
  • Toronto Community Crisis Service (2022-, citywide 2024): the Toronto-specific case this brief is otherwise about — included here for direct comparison against the two non-Toronto precedents above, since (unlike them) it is currently expanding rather than contracting.

Bottom line

Toronto's non-police mental-health crisis response is real, citywide, and expanding — TPS's own budget documents independently corroborate a falling mental-health call share and rising referrals to it — but the program's own headline figures contain an unresolved internal discrepancy, its own current safety/backup-rate profile is not confirmed past 2022 pilot data, and the external U.S. comparator programs most often cited to validate this model are, as of 2025, each in materially worse shape than usually presented. The defensible reading is that Toronto's own program is the stronger, currently-more-stable case for this model right now — not a footnote to CAHOOTS or Denver STAR, but arguably ahead of both — while several of TCCS's own reporting gaps remain genuinely open rather than resolved.

Uncertainties

  • The arithmetic discrepancy between TCCS's inherited 10,339/7,800-diverted figure and this pass's directly-quoted 7,800-total/6,891-diverted figure is not resolved by any source reviewed (see backgrounder "Key tensions / tradeoffs"). In addition, the primary source's own "78%" label does not match its own 6,891/7,800 figures (which compute to 88.3%) — flagged as the source's own internal error, not resolved here either.
  • No TCCS-specific isolated dollar budget figure, independently confirmed via direct primary-source quotation, was located in this pass.
  • No current (2025-2026) TCCS police/ambulance-backup-rate figure was located to update the 2022 pilot-era 2.5%/1.7% baseline.
  • Whether any Ontario provincial mental-health funding stream contributes to TCCS's own budget specifically was not confirmed by any source in this pass.
