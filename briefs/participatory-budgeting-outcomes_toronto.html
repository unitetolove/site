Participatory Budgeting Outcomes: What Does It Deliver, and What Makes It Work? — Toronto

DRAFT v1.0 Jurisdiction: Toronto, Ontario (single-tier city; CD/CSD codes coincide) · Data as of: 2026-07-14 (newest cited source: NYC Council FY2027 PB cycle, in progress as of April 2026)

Backgrounder: participatory-budgeting-outcomes (research/backgrounders/participatory-budgeting-outcomes.md) · Data as of: 2026-07-14 (newest cited source: NYC Council FY2027 PB cycle, in progress as of April 2026) Jurisdiction: Toronto, Ontario (single-tier city; CD/CSD codes coincide) · Status: DRAFT v1.0 Trust classes: INHERITED (this leaf's promoted master briefing document) and NEW (2026-07-14 live discovery, quote-context blocks in the backgrounder).

TL;DR

Participatory budgeting works when it controls meaningful, durable money and fails when tokenistic — a lesson Toronto's own 2015-17 pilot ($150,000-$250,000 per ward, declining turnout, discontinued) already taught at small scale [the inherited master briefing's §Executive summary]. This pass live-verified a lead a prior session had flagged but not confirmed: Montreal's citywide participatory-budgeting program — the master briefing's own tentative "strongest large-Canadian-city comparator" — ended in 2025-26 after three editions and $101.5 million invested, with the City citing budget austerity tied to eliminating 1,000 municipal positions, not declining participation (its final edition still drew 28,000+ voters) [backgrounder, "Montreal: confirmed program discontinuation"]. This materially complicates the "Montreal as strongest comparator" framing: Montreal shows that even a well-sized, actively-engaged PB program is not immune to political reversibility absent legal or budgetary entrenchment — sharpening, not softening, the master briefing's own durability warning.

FOR

  • The cleanest causal evidence for PB comes from Brazil: Gonçalves (2014) found PB adoption shifted municipal spending toward sanitation and health and reduced infant mortality by re-weighting existing budgets, though under conditions (low-baseline services, specific political dominance) that do not transfer directly to Toronto [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case FOR, item 1].
  • Meaningful-scale PB draws meaningful participation: Paris committed roughly 5% of its capital budget (~€100M/year, 2014-2020) and saw participation rise from ~40,000 to ~158,000 voters; NYC's Council PB has run continuously since 2011 and drew over 93,000 voters in its FY2026 cycle alone [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case FOR, item 3; NEW, NYC Council, 2025-05-30].
  • Toronto already has a large, multi-year, equity-weighted local precedent hiding in plain sight: Toronto Community Housing's tenant PB (2001-2017, ~$9M/year via a needs-and-equity formula) — though its current, post-2017-pause status is unconfirmed in this leaf's evidence [the inherited master briefing's §Toronto-specific factors].
  • Montreal's now-ended program is itself evidence PB can sustain large-scale engagement at Toronto-relevant Canadian scale: its final 2024-25 edition drew 880 proposed ideas and 28,000+ voters on a $45 million budget, comparable in structure to what a serious Toronto citywide program could look like [backgrounder, "Montreal"].

AGAINST

  • Toronto's own 2015-17 pilot is the clearest local cautionary case: turnout fell even as the per-ward budget grew, residents said projects "felt too small," and it was not continued [the inherited master briefing's §Executive summary, §Toronto-specific factors].
  • Montreal's confirmed discontinuation is the single most important new finding this pass. A program investing $101.5 million across three editions (2020-2025-26), with turnout holding at 28,000+ voters in its final round, was still ended — not because engagement declined, but because the City administration cited the need to cut 1,000 municipal positions to balance the budget. This is direct, live-verified evidence that scale and engagement alone do not protect a PB program from reversal absent structural entrenchment [backgrounder, "Montreal: confirmed program discontinuation"].
  • Porto Alegre, PB's birthplace, suspended its program in 2017, and the number of Brazilian PB programs has more than halved since the mid-2000s — the master briefing attributes this to identifiable institutional interests (councillors guarding discretionary control, cautious bureaucracies) and political-coalition changes, not resource scarcity as such [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case AGAINST].
  • NYC's own program, while durable, has recently contracted rather than grown: 24 districts and $30 million in the FY2026 cycle fell to 22 districts and $22 million in FY2027 — a reminder that even the most institutionally durable North American comparator is not expanding [NEW, 2026-07-14].

Symmetry note: both sides draw on multiple independent, now-partly-updated sources; the AGAINST case is, if anything, strengthened by this pass's Montreal verification rather than weakened, and this brief states that plainly rather than downplaying it to preserve balance.

Precedents

  • Porto Alegre, Brazil (1989-): the birthplace; strong early pro-poor gains, later suspended in 2017 — the founding cautionary arc on durability [the inherited master briefing's §Real-world precedents].
  • Toronto, City pilot (2015-17): small pilot, declining turnout, "projects too small," not continued — the local reality check [the inherited master briefing's §Real-world precedents].
  • Toronto Community Housing (2001-2017, status post-2017 unconfirmed): Canada's longest-running PB, ~$9M/yr on an equity formula — the most relevant local model, currently under-leveraged pending a status check [the inherited master briefing's §Toronto-specific factors].
  • Montreal (2020-2025/26, now ended): $101.5M across three editions, strong final-round turnout (28,000+ voters), discontinued for stated fiscal-austerity reasons unrelated to engagement — now confirmed, and the most directly cautionary large-Canadian-city data point in this leaf's evidence, materially different from the master briefing's tentative "strongest comparator" framing [backgrounder, "Montreal: confirmed program discontinuation"].
  • Paris (2014-2020, restructured since): the most ambitious sustained example by budget share (~5% of capital), with a formal 30% equity guarantee that did not fully prevent affluent-area skew in outcomes [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case FOR, item 3].
  • New York City Council PB (2011-present): the most durable continuously-operating North American model, though recently contracting in scale (24 districts/$30M in FY2026 to 22 districts/$22M in FY2027) [NEW, 2026-07-14].

Upward Ask

Per research/issue-map.md, participatory budgeting is a method the City can adopt and fund unilaterally within its own capital-budget authority — no provincial or federal action is required for the core mechanism, distinguishing this leaf from city-hall-democratic-reform's ranked-ballot and strong-mayor-ratification asks, both of which do require provincial legislative change. research/data/municipal_asks.csv has not been checked against this specific issue slug this pass — flagged as an open item.

Bottom line

Toronto does not need new evidence that token-scale PB fails — its own 2015-17 pilot already established that. What this pass's live-verified Montreal finding adds is sharper and more uncomfortable: even a well-resourced, actively-engaged, multi-year PB program is not safe from discontinuation absent structural protection from ordinary budget politics. Montreal's $101.5 million, three-edition program did not fail on its own terms — its final round still drew 28,000+ voters — and was ended anyway, by administrative choice, during a fiscal-austerity budget cycle. A credible Toronto PB program, per this leaf's combined evidence, needs not only meaningful money (the master briefing's original lesson) but some form of durability protection against exactly the kind of single-budget-cycle reversal that ended Montreal's program — a design requirement this brief states as the evidence's own clearest new implication, not as a recommendation this document's own voice is structured to make.
