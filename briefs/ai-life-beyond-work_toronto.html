Purpose, Flourishing, and a Post-Work Transition — Toronto

DRAFT Jurisdiction: Toronto (single-tier city; CD and CSD codes coincide) ·

Status: DRAFT · Version: v1.0 · Date: 2026-07-14 Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Data as of: 2026-07-14 (Toronto Social Capital Study 2022, City of Toronto 2026 budget documents, January 2026 CFO capital-plan statement, German Pilot Projekt Grundeinkommen, Central Iowa Basic Income Pilot) Jurisdiction: Toronto (single-tier city; CD and CSD codes coincide) · Provenance: carried-forward (carried forward from this page’s own sources this page’s carried-forward master briefing (A)one of this library's internal records, cited as-is) + New load-bearing findings (this review's live-discovery findings, cited NEW-2026-#`, pending formal formal registration)

TL;DR

Toronto's own measured social-capital baseline — the substrate any post-work "flourishing infrastructure" plan would need to work with — has been declining, not holding steady: the Toronto Foundation/Environics Institute Social Capital Study 2022 (n=4,163) found volunteering fell from roughly 40% to 25% of adults between 2018 and 2022, charitable donation fell from 75% to 63%, and almost 40% of respondents reported having no one they can depend on for help, up from 27% in 2018 [NEW-2026-1]. Against that, Toronto Public Library usage has grown — 81% of Torontonians reported using their library in the past year as of a 2026 City budget document, up from 68% in 2019 [NEW-2026-2] — even as the City's own Chief Financial Officer confirmed in January 2026 that roughly $300 million in capital plans for community centres and libraries had been deferred, with some projects under consideration for cancellation [NEW-2026-3]. Separately, newer basic-income pilots (a completed German three-year study; a 2026 Iowa pilot report) continue to find wellbeing improvements among recipients, consistent with the pilots the carried-forward briefing already cites [NEW-2026-4, NEW-2026-5 ⚠️ still being checked].

FOR (the case that Toronto has a real foundation to build post-work flourishing infrastructure on)

  • Toronto Public Library usage has grown, not declined, since the Social Capital Study's 2022 data:

81% of Torontonians reported using their library in the past year (up from 68% in 2019), with 91% customer satisfaction, and in-branch visits reported to have surpassed pre-pandemic levels [NEW-2026-2].

  • A completed German basic-income pilot found the treatment group scored above a control group on

the WHO Well-Being Index consistently over a full three-year study, with reduced stress, improved sleep, and higher-rated social-relationship quality [NEW-2026-4].

  • A 2026 report on the Central Iowa Basic Income Pilot (110 participants, Des Moines area, $500/month

since May 2023) describes "high levels of hope" among recipients, an outcome the report frames as not found in other comparable US pilots — though this specific finding was not independently fetched from primary reporting in this review and is flagged ⚠️ still being checked [NEW-2026-5].

  • The carried-forward briefing's own framing — that flourishing infrastructure is "cheap... and much of it

is municipal," with much of it already existing and needing "expansion and connection rather than invention" — points to libraries and community centres specifically as a keystone already partly in place [From this library’s earlier research from the master briefing].

AGAINST (the case that the underlying civic substrate is measurably weakening, and its keystone infrastructure is under fiscal threat)

  • The Toronto Social Capital Study 2022 documents a real, measured decline: volunteering fell from

roughly 40% to 25% of adults, charitable donation fell from 75% to 63% (a potential loss of more than 300,000 donors), general trust fell from 55% to 42%, and almost 40% of respondents reported having no one to depend on for help, up from 27% in 2018 — implying roughly 200,000 Toronto residents with no close friends to turn to for support [NEW-2026-1].

  • Toronto's municipal election turnout fell from roughly 41% (2018) to roughly 29% in the most

recent election referenced by the same report [NEW-2026-1] — directly relevant given the carried-forward briefing's own emphasis on citizens' assemblies and participatory budgeting as flourishing-infrastructure mechanisms [From this library’s earlier research from the master briefing].

  • As of January 2026, the City's own CFO confirmed roughly $300 million in capital plans for

community centres and libraries had been deferred, with the latest budget reducing the capital plan for parks and recreation by $214 million and libraries by $76 million, and some projects under consideration for outright cancellation — driven by a projected $1.9 billion shortfall in development-charge revenue over the next ten years [NEW-2026-3].

  • The report underlying the Social Capital Study's findings frames the decline as a pandemic-era

effect, not an AI-era one [NEW-2026-1] — this brief does not assert an AI-specific causal link the source itself does not make.

Both sides draw on real, cited facts. This brief states the asymmetry plainly: the FOR case rests on one genuine, specific counter-trend (library usage) and consistent basic-income wellbeing findings from named pilots elsewhere, while the AGAINST case rests on Toronto's own broader, primary-sourced, directly-measured social-capital decline and a concurrent, confirmed fiscal threat to the specific infrastructure category (community centres, libraries) the carried-forward briefing names as the flourishing-infrastructure keystone — without adjudicating which risk should weigh more heavily in any future flourishing-infrastructure investment decision.

Precedents

The German Pilot Projekt Grundeinkommen is the strongest completed, directly comparable precedent this review adds beyond the carried-forward briefing's existing citations (Stockton, Germany 2021-24, Kenya, Finland, Ontario): it ran its full three-year term and found sustained WHO Well-Being Index gains, reduced stress, and improved sleep and social-relationship quality among the treatment group [NEW-2026-4]. The Central Iowa Basic Income Pilot (Des Moines area, 110 participants, $500/month since May 2023) is a newer, smaller US comparator reporting "high levels of hope," though this specific outcome claim has not been independently fetched and verified from primary reporting in this review [NEW-2026-5, ⚠️ still being checked]. The carried-forward briefing's own citations (Stockton, Kenya, Finland, Ontario's 2017-2019 pilot) remain at their original carried-forward status and were not re-checked in this review [From this library’s earlier research from the master briefing].

Upward Ask

Income-floor policy itself (basic income, guaranteed income, EI, tax policy) sits with the provincial and federal governments, not the City — the backgrounder's scope note is explicit that this page documents Toronto's own civic/social-connection baseline and current infrastructure fiscal risk as the substrate for judgment, not the income-floor mechanism [backgrounder Scope]. This review did not locate a confirmed, currently-active municipal ask (resolution or motion) specifically directed at either level of government on flourishing-infrastructure investment tied to a post-work transition; this library's municipal-asks table was not checked for a running count in this review and should be consulted before this section is filled further.

Bottom line

Toronto's own measured social-capital baseline has been declining since 2018, and the specific infrastructure category (community centres, libraries) the carried-forward briefing names as the flourishing-infrastructure keystone is, as of January 2026, facing real capital-plan deferrals and possible cancellations — even as library usage itself grows and newer basic-income pilots continue to find wellbeing benefits consistent with the carried-forward briefing's existing evidence base. Whether Toronto can currently afford to build the complementary civic infrastructure the carried-forward briefing argues is equally necessary to any post-work transition is a genuinely open, current fiscal question this brief surfaces but does not resolve.

Uncertainties

  • Whether a newer (2024-2026) wave of the Toronto Social Capital Study exists beyond the 2022 data

cited here was not confirmed in this review — this brief does not assume the 2022 data remains the most current available baseline [backgrounder Open questions].

  • The Central Iowa Basic Income Pilot's "high levels of hope" finding [NEW-2026-5] was

WebSearch-discovered and not independently fetched from primary reporting in this review. ⚠️ still being checked before public use.

  • The broader synthesis claim that "every major UBI or guaranteed income pilot that has measured

mental health has found significant improvements" [NEW-2026-6] rests on a secondary synthesis source, not an independently checked primary meta-analysis, and the "every" framing specifically should not be treated as verified.

  • Which specific community-centre or library capital projects, if any, have moved from deferred to

outright cancelled since the January 2026 CFO statement was not confirmed in this review — stated as a genuine gap, not assumed either way [NEW-2026-3, backgrounder Open questions].

  • The library-usage growth [NEW-2026-2] and the broader social-capital decline [NEW-2026-1] measure

different things over different but overlapping periods; this brief does not resolve them into a single trend line, consistent with the backgrounder's own treatment.

  • The carried-forward briefing's own citations (Jahoda's latent-deprivation model, Self-Determination

Theory, Keynes/Russell, the Stockton/Kenya/Finland/Ontario pilots) were not re-checked against primary sources in this review and remain at their original carried-forward status.

  • All NEW-2026-# findings in this brief were discovered via WebSearch and direct page fetch in the

backgrounder's own pass (2026-07-14); NEW-2026-1 and NEW-2026-3 were fetched and read directly from primary sources, while NEW-2026-2, NEW-2026-4, NEW-2026-5, and NEW-2026-6 were WebSearch-discovered and not independently fetched in full primary-source form within that pass's budget — treat the latter group as sourced-but-not-yet-independently-verified.
