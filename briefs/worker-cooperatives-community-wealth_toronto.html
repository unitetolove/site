Worker Co-operatives and Community-Wealth Building — Toronto

DRAFT v1.0 Jurisdiction: Toronto, Ontario (single-tier city; CD/CSD codes coincide) · Data as of: 2026-07-14

Backgrounder: worker-cooperatives-community-wealth (our research file for that page) · Data as of: 2026-07-14 Jurisdiction: Toronto, Ontario (single-tier city; CD/CSD codes coincide) · Status: DRAFT v1.0 Provenance: carried-forward (this page’s carried-forward master briefing document) and NEW (2026-07-14 live discovery, including a directly-fetched 2025 peer-reviewed academic study).

Voice note, inherited from the backgrounder: the model is proven and under-used; the honest puzzle is the financing/founding-ecosystem gap, not performance — and this page’s single strongest piece of evidence (Preston's 2025 employment study) turns out to be primarily a procurement-and-wages result, not a worker-ownership result, a genuine finding this brief states plainly rather than glosses over.

TL;DR

Worker co-operatives survive as well or better than conventional firms and deliver markedly better job stability and pay equity, but remain rare because of a documented financing-and-founding-ecosystem gap, not a performance problem [the inherited master briefing's §Executive summary]. This review directly fetched the actual 2025 peer-reviewed academic study behind the Preston Model's most-cited claim and found a statistically significant, disability- and minority-ethnic-group-concentrated employment effect (4%, 95% CI 2.4–5.7%) — but the same study explicitly characterizes Preston's own worker-co-op development as "modest," meaning the strongest evidence in this page supports the procurement-and-Living-Wage lever more directly than worker-ownership itself [backgrounder, "Preston," directly fetched 2026-07-14]. Toronto has one clearly identified worker co-op (Hypha) and no confirmed sector-wide count.

FOR

  • Worker co-ops survive as well or better than conventional firms — Mondragón's long-run survival record and Italian worker-buyout evidence both support this, though the master briefing's own honest caveats (Fagor's 2013 collapse, non-member conventional-labour conditions abroad) are preserved, not smoothed over [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case FOR, item 1].
  • At regional scale, a co-operative economy can anchor a prosperous, low-inequality region — Emilia-Romagna generates roughly 30% of regional GDP through co-operatives, though this figure covers all co-op types, not worker co-ops alone, and rests on over a century of specific Italian legal development the master briefing itself calls the least generalizable comparator here [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case FOR, item 3].
  • Preston's community wealth building programme produced a statistically significant, peer-reviewed, controlled-study-confirmed employment effect — directly fetched this review — with the largest gains concentrated among people with disabilities (+16.4 to +22.1 percentage points) and minority ethnic groups (+6 percentage points), exactly the populations an equity-focused strategy should reach [Rose et al. 2025, Table 1, directly fetched 2026-07-14].
  • Toronto has real anchor institutions (major hospital networks, universities, the City itself) and an existing Social Procurement Policy (2016) that could be weighted toward worker- and community-owned suppliers at near-zero new budget cost [the inherited master briefing's §Toronto-specific factors].
  • A "silver tsunami" of retiring business owners offers a lower-risk conversion path to co-op creation than starting from scratch, since the business already has revenue and a track record [the inherited master briefing's §Toronto-specific factors].

AGAINST

  • Worker co-ops are rare precisely because the entire financial and legal system is built for investor ownership — banks are wary of lending without an outside equity owner, and raising growth capital normally means selling the control that defines a co-op [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case AGAINST].
  • Preston's own worker-co-op development is explicitly characterized as "modest" by the very peer-reviewed study documenting its strongest employment result — the page’s best evidence is not primarily a worker-ownership success story, and this brief states that plainly rather than reading Preston as proof worker co-ops specifically drove the gains [Rose et al. 2025, "Setting and intervention," directly fetched 2026-07-14].
  • Governance is demanding: co-ops struggle to attract senior management at market rates, face insider/outsider tensions, carry structural risk-aversion (members' jobs and savings in one firm), and trade democratic legitimacy against competitive agility [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case AGAINST].
  • Redirecting City procurement to worker- and community-owned suppliers displaces incumbent suppliers, some of whom employ local workers under decent conditions — a real cost, not a free lunch [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case AGAINST, "counterfactuals and displacement"].
  • Cleveland/Evergreen, the other named flagship model, remains active in 2025-2026 but has no comparably rigorous, independently peer-reviewed outcome evaluation located this review — a genuine asymmetry with Preston's now directly-fetched study, not assumed away [backgrounder, "Cleveland/Evergreen," “still being checked”].
  • Toronto's own worker co-op sector size is unconfirmed: Hypha is the one clearly identified, currently-operating example found this review, and no source establishes a total count [backgrounder, "Toronto's worker co-op sector," “still being checked”, genuinely uncovered].
  • Ontario's Co-operative Corporations Act reform is real but partial and dated to 2019, not 2025-2026 (a non-member-business threshold change and audit-rule updates, explicitly excluding housing co-ops; re-dated independently adjudicated 2026-07-17, by a recorded judgment ruling) — materially narrower than the broader modernization the master briefing calls for, and its current in-force status is itself unconfirmed [backgrounder, "Ontario's Co-operative Corporations Act," “still being checked”].

Symmetry note: both sides draw on the master briefing and this review's direct fetch of the Preston study; the AGAINST case is sharpened by this review's discovery that Preston's own worker-co-op component is "modest" — a finding that complicates the FOR case's strongest citation rather than simply supporting it, stated plainly rather than smoothed over.

Precedents

  • Mondragón, Basque Country, Spain: world's largest worker-co-op federation; strong long-run survival, with Fagor's 2013 collapse and non-member overseas labour preserved as honest complications [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case FOR, item 1].
  • Emilia-Romagna, Italy: ~30% of regional GDP from all co-op types; least generalizable comparator by the master briefing's own account [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case FOR, item 3].
  • Preston, England: the strongest, most rigorously evaluated result in this page’s evidence base — a peer-reviewed, controlled 2025 study, directly fetched this review, finding a real but procurement-and-wages-driven (not primarily worker-co-op-driven) employment effect [Rose et al. 2025, directly fetched 2026-07-14].
  • Cleveland, Ohio (Evergreen Cooperatives): the original anchor-procurement worker-co-op model, still active, no comparably rigorous evaluation found this review [backgrounder, “still being checked”].
  • Hypha Worker Co-operative, Toronto: the one clearly identified, currently-operating Toronto worker co-op found this review, incorporated 2019 [backgrounder, “still being checked”].

Upward Ask

Per this page’s master briefing, the City's own procurement-weighting lever (a recommendation card) is within existing municipal authority, but broader-public-sector anchor institutions (hospitals, universities) are bound by Ontario's Broader Public Sector Accountability Act and Procurement Directive, and cannot simply direct contracts to local co-ops — meaning any strategy relying on hospital/university anchor spend requires provincial advocacy to widen that latitude [the inherited master briefing's §Toronto-specific factors]. Separately, the master briefing calls for provincial modernization of the Co-operative Corporations Act beyond the partial 2025-2026 reform already enacted (a non-member-business threshold change, audit-rule updates), specifically Québec-style co-op investment tax incentives [the inherited master briefing's §Policy recommendations, item 7; backgrounder, "Ontario's Co-operative Corporations Act," “still being checked”]. this library's municipal-asks table was not checked against this specific issue slug this review — flagged as an open item.

Bottom line

Toronto has real, low-cost, evidence-backed levers available (procurement-weighting, a modest co-op development pilot modeled on Preston's own realistic scale) but should not oversell what the international evidence actually supports: the strongest single piece of evidence in this page’s base — Preston's directly-fetched, peer-reviewed employment study — is a procurement-and-Living-Wage result more than a worker-co-op-formation result specifically, and Mondragón's and Emilia-Romagna's more dramatic figures rest on ecosystem and legal conditions built over decades to a century that a Toronto pilot could not replicate quickly even with full political will. This is the single most defensible synthesis sentence this brief can state without exceeding a claim_type:recommendation framing it does not have the structure to carry here.

Uncertainties

  • Toronto's own total worker-co-op count is not established by any source found this review — Hypha is the one confirmed example.
  • Cleveland/Evergreen's 2025-2026 activity is confirmed via organizational/press-coverage search synthesis only; no comparably rigorous outcome evaluation was located, a genuine asymmetry with Preston's now directly-fetched study.
  • Ontario's Co-operative Corporations Act reform is now dated to 2019 (re-dated independently adjudicated 2026-07-17, by a recorded judgment ruling), but its exact scope and current in-force status are not confirmed against primary legislative text — FSRAO's own change-log page does not list it at all.
  • The commonly-quoted "Mondragón 90% vs. conventional 20%" survival comparison remains flagged unconfirmed in the underlying master briefing and was not independently re-verified this review.
  • Preston's own peer-reviewed study's authors flag a real limitation this brief preserves: the University of Central Lancashire's own concurrent £200M campus investment is a plausible confound the difference-in-differences design cannot fully separate from the CWB programme's own effect.
  • No Toronto-specific quantification exists for the realistically redirectable share of City/anchor-institution procurement, or for the scale of Toronto's retiring-owner business-succession wave — both named as open gaps by the master briefing itself and not closed this review.
  • No L3-normalized, SGC-keyed fiscal data rows exist yet for this issue in this library's Toronto data layer.
  • this library's municipal-asks table has not been checked against this specific jurisdiction/issue combination this review.
  • This backgrounder does not cover housing or shared-equity co-operatives, which the sibling leaf coop-shared-equity-housing owns entirely — a reader looking for housing-co-op evidence should consult that leaf directly, not assume this one covers it.
