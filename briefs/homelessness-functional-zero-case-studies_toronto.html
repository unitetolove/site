Functional Zero Case Studies — Toronto

DRAFT Jurisdiction: Toronto (single-tier city; CD and CSD codes coincide, Statistics Canada geographic code 3520005/3520) · Data as of: 2026-07-14

Status: DRAFT · Version: v1.0 · Date: 2026-07-14 Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Data as of: 2026-07-14 Jurisdiction: Toronto (single-tier city; CD and CSD codes coincide, Statistics Canada geographic code 3520005/3520) · Provenance: carried-forward (1 carried forward from this page’s own sources doc) + New load-bearing findings (this review's Medicine Hat and Built for Zero source quote findings, pending formal formal registration)

TL;DR

Medicine Hat's 2021 "functional zero" chronic-homelessness achievement and its 2022 reversal are both real and already documented [CL-175, CL-176]; this review's live discovery found the city's Point-in-Time count has continued to fluctuate since — 120 (2022), 104 (2024), 136 (2025), of whom only 33 were counted outdoors, the rest in shelters, transitional housing, or custody [NEW-2026-1]. London's 2021 veteran-homelessness achievement included its own documented 12-of-24-month sustainment gap, later attributed to a data-quality improvement rather than a real increase [CL-177, CL-90666]. Most consequentially, this review discovered that Built for Zero — the organization that defined "functional zero" — formally retired the designation in 2025-2026, replacing it with a broader framework measuring whether homelessness is becoming "rare, brief, nonrecurring, safe, and believable" [NEW-2026-2]. Any Toronto-facing use of "functional zero" as a forward-looking target should account for this: the term now describes a historical milestone, not an active standard.

FOR (the underlying methodology and its lessons remain valuable for Toronto)

  • The by-name-list/coordinated-entry infrastructure that "functional zero" built — real-time, person-specific tracking rather than periodic snapshots — remains, per Built for Zero's own account, "at the core of how communities reduce homelessness" even after the specific threshold retired [NEW-2026-2].
  • London's own gap-months episode produced a genuinely transferable lesson, now formalized industry-wide in Built for Zero's "verified reductions" methodology: distinguishing a real change on the ground from an artifact of improved data collection [CL-90666, NEW-2026-2] — directly relevant to any Toronto metric relying on administrative data quality that could itself improve over time.
  • Three US communities (Rockford, Bergen County NJ, Abilene TX) proved sustained dual-population success was achievable at a municipal level without Toronto's scale or resources [NEW-2026-2] — evidence the underlying goal (not the specific numeric threshold) remains a credible target.

AGAINST (citing "functional zero" today risks using a retired standard, and neither Canadian case is a clean success story)

  • Built for Zero itself states the single-threshold model had real practical limits in large urban systems and could cause "confusion about whether progress was real" when visible encampments persisted alongside a technical achievement for one population [NEW-2026-2] — a caution directly relevant to any framing of a Toronto-scale target using the old standard.
  • Medicine Hat's post-2021 record is not a clean reversal-and-stay-bad story or a recovery story — it fluctuated (120→104→136) across three counts, meaning any single-year citation of Medicine Hat risks telling only part of the story [NEW-2026-1].
  • London's status beyond October 2022 was not located in this review — a genuine, stated gap, not evidence either way.
  • The 136-person 2025 Medicine Hat figure, if cited without its own breakdown, could overstate unsheltered homelessness specifically (only 33 of 136 were counted outdoors) [NEW-2026-1].

Costs

No cost figures apply to this page’s own subject matter (a measurement methodology and two non-Toronto case studies); Toronto's own shelter-system cost figures are already tracked in shelter-system-capacity-strain's own Costs table and are not duplicated here.

Precedents

Medicine Hat (2021 chronic-homelessness functional zero, 2022 reversal, continued fluctuation through 2025) [CL-175, CL-176, NEW-2026-1] and London (2021 veteran-homelessness functional zero, 2022 gap months, October 2022 recovery, status beyond that not confirmed) [CL-177, CL-90666] are this page’s own two Canadian precedents. The three US communities that reached both veteran and chronic functional zero (Rockford, Bergen County NJ, Abilene TX) are named in Built for Zero's own current record [NEW-2026-2] — Rockford's case is covered in depth, including its own live federal-funding threat, in this page’s sibling homelessness-international-comparators.

Bottom line

Medicine Hat's and London's 2021 achievements were real, both had real complications the original announcements didn't capture, and the "functional zero" standard itself — not just these two cities' specific results — has since been formally retired by the organization that defined it in favour of a broader, more rigorous framework; any Toronto-facing reference to "functional zero" going forward should describe a historical milestone, not cite it as a still-active target.
