Business, BIAs, and Homelessness Policy — Day-One Brief

DRAFT Date: 2026-07-14 · File: research/dayone/homelessness-business-community-overlay.md

Status: DRAFT · Version: v1.0 · Date: 2026-07-14 · Layer: L-PLATFORM Sources: research/backgrounders/homelessness-business-community-overlay.md, research/cards/homelessness-business-community-overlay.md only — no fresh citations introduced here.

There is a standard assumption in homelessness policy work: downtown business groups want the encampments cleared and don't much care about the ten-year plan. Toronto's own largest downtown business coalition's actual, written record does not support that assumption — and checking that record directly, rather than assuming it, is the entire point of this brief.

What the Downtown Toronto BIA Alliance actually said

The Downtown Toronto BIA Alliance — six BIAs representing, by the coalition's own current public figures, $117 billion in downtown GDP contribution — wrote to Prime Minister Carney in August 2025 asking the federal government to keep funding the Interim Housing Assistance Program, the program that pays for refugee-claimant shelter capacity. Their letter said they agreed with the Mayor's Office's own request for the same thing. A year earlier, six of those same BIAs wrote to City Council about the encampment strategy, and their stated top concern wasn't enforcement or clearance speed — it was this: what happens if the federal and provincial funding this strategy depends on doesn't show up, or shows up late?

That is, almost word for word, the same argument this project makes independently from the City's own budget documents: Toronto's ten-year shelter capital plan is funded 100% by the City itself, with zero direct dollars from either senior government. A business coalition and a homelessness-research project reached the identical conclusion, from different starting concerns, without talking to each other.

Where this alignment is real, and where it stops

It's real on two specific things: keep IHAP funded, and don't build a homelessness strategy on funding promises nobody can guarantee. It is not evidence of broader agreement. No BIA letter found anywhere in this research addresses this project's harder asks — outcome-based contracting for shelter operators, a wage floor for shelter staff, Indigenous-led housing investment. The coalition's own letters are narrow and specific, and treating two real points of agreement as if they added up to a shared platform would be dishonest, and the kind of thing a business reporter checking the primary sources would catch in about five minutes.

It's also worth being honest that the coalition's own letters are not the whole business community. Individual shopkeepers and op-ed writers can sound a lot more like the "clear it now" stereotype than the DTBIAA's own carefully worded institutional letters do. The formal coalition and the informal sentiment on the ground are not always the same thing, and this brief doesn't pretend they are.

The development-charges report: real, but not the endorsement it might look like

The Toronto Region Board of Trade published a report in December 2025 arguing development charges have gone up 176% since 2011 and that municipalities are billions of dollars short of what they need for infrastructure. That's a genuine point of alignment with this project's own view that municipalities are underfunded. But read the report's actual five recommendations and it becomes clear the Board isn't asking for what this project asks for elsewhere — restoring the specific development-charge authority the province took away under Bill 23. The Board wants to restructure who pays for what, including lowering charges on new construction by moving water and sewer costs off the development-charge system entirely. Citing this report as if it endorsed the Bill 23 position would be wrong, and it's the kind of wrong a reader with the report open in another tab would catch immediately.

What this doesn't settle

Two of the specific letters this whole argument rests on — the May 2024 encampment-strategy letter and the August 2025 IHAP letter — could not be re-read in full in this pass; the direct links returned nothing readable. Everything else about the coalition (that it's real, that it writes exactly this kind of letter routinely, that its self-reported scale figures check out) was independently confirmed. But the exact wording of those two specific letters is being taken on the strength of this project's own prior sourcing, not independently re-verified word for word this week. And nobody has yet mapped where landlord associations or police associations stand on any of this — that's a different piece of work, not started here.

The two moves this leaf's cards propose

1. Cite the DTBIAA's own letters, by name and date, in every future federal/provincial funding ask this project makes (RC-BCO-01) — a free, zero-cost move that makes the funding-reliability argument land harder because it isn't just this project saying it. 2. Never cite the Board of Trade as endorsing Bill 23 restoration (RC-BCO-02) — a discipline check, not a spending ask, aimed at making sure this project doesn't hand a hostile fact-checker an easy win.

Neither move requires new research or new money. Both require reading what's already public, carefully, and not stretching a real but narrow alignment into something it isn't.
