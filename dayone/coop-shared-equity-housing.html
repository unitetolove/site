Co-op, Shared-Equity, Student & Senior Housing — Day-One Paper

DRAFT v1.0 — L-PLATFORM layer, The Unknown Soldier synthesis Date: 2026-07-14 ·

Status: DRAFT v1.0 — L-PLATFORM layer, The Unknown Soldier synthesis · Date: 2026-07-14 Built only from that page's recommendation cards (RC-coop-01–02) and our research file for that page; every claim below traces to one of those two documents.

What a mayor does in week one on this file

612 units of permanently affordable co-op housing are already built or underway in this city right now — Kennedy Green in Scarborough and the Ruth and Terry Grier co-op in South Etobicoke — aimed at exactly the people who earn too much for social housing and too little for market rent: nurses, teachers, social workers, construction workers. Toronto isn't starting from zero on co-op housing — it's mid-revival, with real federal money already landed and a real project on the ground. The week-one job is removing the two things actually slowing it down: how much City land is committed, and whether the sector has the staff capacity to spend the money it already has.

1. Legislate a real land commitment, not just a partnership. CreateTO's work with CHFT already produced Kennedy Green (600 units) and the Ruth and Terry Grier co-op (192 units) — proof the model works at scale. What doesn't exist yet is a standing, legislated commitment to what share of future City land goes to non-market housing versus market-rate disposition. Land is 20–40% of total development cost in Toronto — removing it is the single most powerful lever City Hall has. Week one: direct CreateTO to bring a defined set-aside percentage to Council (RC-coop-01).

2. Fund the capacity, not just the buildings. CHFT itself says its own organizational bandwidth — not the federal money — is what's actually capping how fast this sector can grow. Quebec figured this out decades ago and built GRT organizations to provide sustained technical assistance to co-op developers; Ontario has no equivalent. Week one: start the conversation with the Province about a joint capacity grant for CHFT, modeled on what Quebec already proved works (RC-coop-02).

3. Say plainly that this doesn't fix the whole crisis, and say why. The federal $1.5 billion over four years will produce thousands of units against a need in the hundreds of thousands. This isn't a reason to stop — it's a reason to be honest that co-op housing is one piece of a "missing middle" income band (above social housing, below market), not a citywide affordability fix on its own.

What this office does not control, and won't pretend to

The single biggest risk to this entire file is whether Ottawa renews the CHDP past 2028 — a federal decision Toronto cannot make for itself. The City can spend its capital-adjacent (land, capacity) chips well in the meantime, but it can't replace a lapsed federal capital stream with municipal money at anything like the same scale. Naming that honestly matters more than pretending the City alone can carry this file if the federal funding disappears.

The honest bottom line

Toronto has landed real federal money, has a working land-partnership model, and knows exactly what's slowing it down — CHFT's own capacity, not lack of political will or lack of a funded federal program. Week one is about giving the sector more land and more staff capacity to actually build with the money that already exists, while being honest that this doesn't reach the deepest-need households and depends on a federal funding decision three years out that nobody in this city controls.

Uncertainties this paper inherits from its cards

Neither RC-coop-01 nor RC-coop-02 quantifies a specific fiscal cost — the land set-aside's foregone-revenue cost and the capacity grant's dollar scale are both named as open in the backgrounder. The scale of deferred-maintenance risk in Toronto's existing 1970s–80s co-op stock is explicitly "not yet comprehensively documented," per the inherited master briefing source, and nothing in this paper resolves that gap. None of these gaps are papered over — they are named so this paper isn't read as more certain than its own sources are.
