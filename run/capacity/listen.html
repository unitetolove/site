The Listen playbook

Anyone can promise to listen. This playbook is how a candidate demonstrates it before voting day, with receipts: real consultation sessions run in your own room under your own name, a standing platform your constituents can actually reach you on, and a published record of what you heard. Free and identical for every candidate and team in every race in Ontario’s 2026 municipal elections.

Part of Show Your Work — two ideas for every candidate. This page suggests; it never convenes. Every session described here is yours: your room, your name, your record.

The assembly — one room, one evening, a real question

The whole workflow already exists as a free kit: the Citizens’ Assembly in a Box — one evening, 12 to 30 people, mixed tables, independent input before discussion, disagreement recorded instead of voted away. Candidates run it exactly the way any host does, with three adaptations:

  • Your name on the door. A candidate assembly is convened by your campaign, in a room you arranged, and it says so. Nothing behind this kit convenes, attends, or co-signs your session.
  • The honest floor travels with the kit. An evening’s room speaks for the people in that room — never for the ward, and never for “the people.” Say it at the start; print it on the record. A candidate who claims a room of twenty as a mandate has misread the exercise.
  • Pick a question you don’t control the answer to. The kit’s question discipline applies doubly to candidates: a session designed to produce applause for your platform is a rally, not a consultation, and readers can tell the difference.

The kit’s harvest standard — how to record what the room said, dissent included — is offered, never required. Use ours, use your own, but publish something: an unpublished consultation is indistinguishable from one that never happened.

The listening KPIs

Four definitions, published identically for everyone. What we publish is the definitions; the numbers are yours to earn and publish on your own page:

  • Sessions held — consultation events actually run, dated and place-named. Any honest format counts: an assembly, a town hall, a listening table at a market. A leaflet drop is not a session.
  • Attendance — people in the room, counted at the door, per session. Count once, at the time; never estimate afterward.
  • Questions answered on the record — questions from constituents you have answered in writing or on camera, where the question and the answer are both public. (The citizen question bank is one ready-made source, identical for every candidate; any public question counts.)
  • What-we-heard notes published — a written note after each session: what was asked, what was said, where it disagreed with you, published where your constituents can read it. This is the receipt that separates listening from being seen listening.

A standing platform — the honest table

A campaign that vanishes after October has demonstrated nothing. A standing constituent platform — a place your constituents can reach you, all term — is a listening receipt that keeps renewing. No platform is required, none is endorsed, and every common choice has real trade-offs, so here they are, plainly. Vendor terms change; check the current ones before you commit.

PlatformBest fitThe honest trade-offs
Discord An open constituent space Free, joinable by link, real moderation tooling (roles, channels, audit log). Skews younger; older constituents may need a walkthrough. A public server is discoverable — which is the point, and also the moderation load.
Signal Organizing your own crew Private, end-to-end encrypted, no ads, no data harvesting. Group size is capped and there is no public discovery — by design. The right tool for coordinating volunteers; the wrong tool for an open constituent space, precisely because nobody can find it.
Slack Flagged — read this first Comfortable for anyone who works in an office, but on the free tier your older messages become inaccessible as the archive fills — which makes it a poor public record: a constituent space whose history evaporates undercuts the receipt this playbook is about. Paid tiers fix that, at a real monthly cost.
WhatsApp Meeting people where they are Very widely installed across ages and languages — often the honest answer to “where are my constituents already?” Moderation tooling is thin, large communities are hard to keep civil, and it is Meta-owned — its data practices are its own and worth reading before you route constituents there.
Facebook Groups Where many wards already talk Many neighbourhoods effectively already live here, and discovery is strong. What members see is ranked by feed algorithms rather than shown plainly, moderation is a real ongoing job, and the same Meta data practices apply. Fine as a meeting place; weak as a record.

Whatever you choose, publish the choice as part of your receipts: where the space lives, how a constituent joins it, and who runs it.

The safety floor — rides every space, non-negotiable

  • A named moderator. A human being, named on the space itself, who is responsible for what the space tolerates. “The community moderates itself” is how spaces rot.
  • A minors policy, stated up front. The simple default: your constituent space is for adults, 18-plus, unless you have deliberately built otherwise — and a space that welcomes teenagers needs its own rules, written down: no one-to-one private contact between the campaign and a minor, guardian awareness for participation, and no photos of identifiable minors without a guardian’s okay. If you haven’t done that work, run an 18-plus space and say so.
  • A code of conduct, posted where people enter. Short is fine: what gets a warning, what gets removal, who decides, how to appeal. Ours is free to copy from any of the kits and reword as your own — your space, your name, our template if you want it.

Publish your receipts

Sessions, attendance, answered questions, what-we-heard notes — all of it belongs on your own page, under your own name, standing for the whole term you’re asking to serve. That’s the entire mechanism: receipts your constituents and your local press can check for themselves. Nothing flows back to us — no submissions, no registry, no scoreboard here. The comparing belongs to your constituents and the press, which is exactly where it should be.
