Two weeks to file

Nominations for Ontario’s 2026 municipal elections close at 2:00 p.m. on Friday, August 21 — the same afternoon, the same law, in every one of the province’s 444 municipalities. If you have ever finished a sentence that started with “somebody should” — about your street, your school board, your ward, your town — this page is for you, and the clock on it is real.

Published August 2026 · dates per the Municipal Elections Act, 1996 and Ontario’s 2026 Candidates’ Guide — verified in the full candidate starter kit. This page recruits for no side and no candidate: it recruits for the ballot itself, and it says the same thing to everyone.

The honest math of this moment

In the 2026 field as it stood in early August, hundreds of races across Ontario were on track to be decided without a vote — a single candidate, or none at all, for real seats holding real budgets. (The receipted figures live on the State of the Election page.) Whatever else that means, it means this: in a very large share of this province, the question isn’t whether you could win a seat at the table. It’s whether anyone will even be at the table. One more name on a ballot changes that arithmetic by itself.

Running is the fastest way to start the work

You don’t file first and figure out what you stand for later. It’s the other way around: the work you already wish someone would do is the campaign. From the day you file, two free playbooks — the same for every candidate, identically — suggest a concrete first week: convene a crew, put a kilometre of clean sidewalk on the record, hold one real listening session, publish the receipts. Win or lose, that work is real, it stays done, and your community saw who did it.

What filing actually takes

  • $100 (councillor or school board trustee) or $200 (mayor / head of council) — refunded when you file your post-election financial statement on time. A deposit, not a price.
  • 25 signatures from eligible voters, for mayor/councillor candidates in municipalities with 4,000-plus electors — smaller municipalities are exempt, and trustee candidates never need them.
  • Filing in person (you or an agent) at your municipal clerk’s office, with ID, before 2:00 p.m. on August 21. On the day itself, filing hours are 9 a.m.–2 p.m., province-wide.
  • A campaign bank account only if you raise or spend any money at all.

Your own municipality’s clerk page, race list, and filed candidates: the 444-municipality lookup. Everything else — the rules, the money mechanics, the full calendar, 54 vetted guides, free tools: the starter kit, free and identical for every candidate.

Why this council term, of all terms

The councils and school boards elected October 26 sit until 2030 — through the window in which choices about AI in public services, surveillance and procurement, data governance, climate infrastructure, safety, and freedom will be made, or made by default, a remarkable share of them at the municipal table. The base of the pyramid is where the steering is closest to hand. Whoever files by August 21 is who gets a hand on the wheel.

Send this to the person you thought of while reading it

Most good candidates run because someone they trusted asked them, plainly, once. Be the someone. Send this page with one sentence of your own: “I think you’d serve well, and there are two weeks left.” This site recruits and endorses no one, serves every candidate identically — and thinks the asking itself belongs to neighbours like you.

The door closes at 2:00 p.m., August 21
