The guides shelf

54 external resources for anyone running for municipal office or school board trustee in Ontario in 2026 — primary/official sources first, then established nonpartisan campaign schools and candidate-support organizations, then accessibility resources. Every entry is dated and annotated: what it covers, who publishes it, and when it was last confirmed live. No vendor-paid placement, no partisan source — every entry was screened, and what was excluded is listed at the bottom along with why, so the screen itself is auditable.

54 entries (9 seeded, 45 from the full v0.3 curation sweep) — every one independently fetched and confirmed live 2026-08-05.

Government and official sources

  • Ontario’s 2026 Candidates’ Guide — Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing, published April 1 2026, updated June 22 2026. The full legal-rules reference this whole kit is built on; 12 chapters covering eligibility, nominations, campaigning, finance, and after-voting-day rules.
  • Ontario’s 2026 Voters’ Guide — the companion document for voters, useful for a candidate’s own volunteers explaining the process to constituents.
  • Municipal elections (ontario.ca hub) — MMAH, published April 1 2022, updated April 2 2026. The province’s front-door page for 2026: links the Candidates’, Voters’, and Third-Party Advertisers’ guides in one place plus the headline 2026 dates.

AMO (Association of Municipalities of Ontario)

  • AMO — Municipal Elections — a plain-language overview confirming election-day date, eligibility, and fees, plus a running list of AMO’s own 2026 election resources.
  • AMO — 2026 Municipal Election Webpages — a directory covering every one of Ontario’s 444 municipalities’ own election pages.
  • Lead Where You Live, FINAL April 2026 — AMO’s own comprehensive candidate guide: council structure, key 2026 dates, campaign finance basics, election-day/scrutineer rules, plus AMO’s own curated list of candidate-support organizations. (An earlier December 2025 version of this file may still be findable online and could 404 or serve a stale copy — use this link.)
  • AMO — We All Win: Diversity on Council — AMO’s resource on under-represented candidates, linking to eight further candidate-support organizations, all independently re-verified — see “Women and under-represented candidates” and “Racialized, Indigenous, and faith-community candidate support” below.
  • AMO — Election Resources: Supporting Your Run for Municipal Office in 2026 — AMO’s 2026 workshop hub: a four-part “Ready, Set, Run!” video series, a free “Underrepresented Candidate” workshop, an Urban Indigenous candidates’ session, a youth-candidate session, and a resilience series. Some listed live sessions have already passed; the standing guide/video/cheat-sheet resources are the durable value.
  • AMO — Healthy Democracy Project Resources — index page linking Turn Up the Turnout, the Local Democracy Solutions Bank, civic-education resources, and AMO’s “Mapping the Experiences of Diverse Candidates” research report.
  • AMO’s Leading with Respect Handguides — practical guidance on civility and managing harassment/hostility in local government; more relevant to sitting councils than a first-time candidate, but a reasonable civic-culture reference for a campaign team.
  • AMO — Municipal Campaign Cheat Sheet (PDF) — a condensed one-pager: platform, team/timeline, fundraising, voter engagement, debate/media prep, council-vs-staff role distinctions. Its own links list still cites the 2022 (not 2026) Candidates’ Guide — a minor stale reference inside an otherwise current document.

Not listed: AMO’s Local Leadership Foundations — a real AMO program, but registration is restricted to already-elected officials, not a pre-election candidate resource.

Professional/clerk body

  • AMCTO — 2025–2027 Municipal Elections Calendar (PDF) — the province’s municipal clerks’ own professional association’s full statutory-date reference. This is the document the calendar on the main Run page is cross-verified against.

Finance & third-party advertiser guidance

  • 2026 Third-Party Advertisers’ Guide — MMAH, published March 24 2026, updated April 1 2026. Who can/can’t register as a third-party advertiser, the 2026 restricted period (May 1–Oct 26, 2026), spending limits, and financial-statement obligations.
  • Where to find forms referred to in this guide — MMAH. Direct links to Forms 5–8 on the Central Forms Repository.
  • Financial Statement — Auditor’s Report Candidate (Form 4) — Ontario’s Central Forms Repository. The official downloadable form every candidate whose contributions or expenses exceed $10,000 must file.

Accessibility & inclusion

  • CNIB — Accessible Election Guide for Municipalities (Ontario) — accessible voting locations, assistive voting terminals, braille templates, and voter/candidate accessibility rights under AODA and the MEA.
  • The Candidates’ Guide to Accessible Elections (PDF) — AMCTO, developed with the Accessibility Directorate of Ontario. Checklists for accessible campaign literature, audio/video media, campaign-office accessibility, and a plain-language primer on disability types.
  • Toronto — Election Accessibility Plan — City Clerk’s Office, modified May 7 2026. Toronto-specific, but a useful model of what every Ontario clerk must produce under MEA s.12.1.

Gap disclosed, not papered over: no distinct, standalone language-access guide (multilingual campaign-material requirements, translation resources) was found beyond what these accessibility guides touch on tangentially — flagged as an open item, not answered here.

Municipal clerk offices — candidate guides by city

  • City of Toronto — Become a Candidate — modified Aug 4 2026. Full nomination checklist, ID requirements, campaign finance rules, a live “candidates in default” list, recorded info sessions.
  • City of Ottawa — Candidates — offices up for election, filing logistics, and eligibility rules; Ottawa’s own dedicated guide sub-page mostly redirects to the province’s guide.
  • City of Hamilton — Become a Candidate — statute-referenced eligibility, full list of contested offices by ward, detailed nomination-procedure PDF.
  • City of Mississauga — Become a Candidate — modified July 8 2026. Dedicated election microsite, full nomination-package checklist, direct links to every required form.
  • City of Brampton — Becoming a Candidate — offices/wards being contested, qualification/filing sub-pages, direct link to AMO’s Lead Where You Live.
  • Town of Caledon — Candidate Election Information — issued May 11 2026. Unusually thorough for a smaller municipality — effectively a full candidate handbook.
  • Region of Peel — 2026 Municipal Election — not a filing guide (Peel directs candidates to Brampton/Mississauga/Caledon for that) — a regional-governance orientation package.
  • City of Vaughan — Candidates — especially detailed on campaign-finance mechanics; some rebate-deadline content appears carried over from the prior cycle, worth a currency check.
  • City of Oshawa — Information for Candidates — the strongest Durham-area substitute found; Durham Region itself does not publish its own regional candidate guide.
  • City of London — Information for Candidates — one of the most complete guides found: legal filing requirements plus a genuine explainer of a councillor’s job, plus a recorded info session.
  • City of Windsor — Information for Candidates — clean single-page guide covering qualifications, the agent-filing process, and the full form inventory.
  • City of Kingston — Candidates — notably strong on accessibility/inclusion — links AMO training aimed at underrepresented, youth, and urban Indigenous candidates.

Gap disclosed: York Region and Durham Region have no distinct regional candidate guide of their own — both point to constituent municipalities. Oshawa above stands in for Durham; no York-area substitute was independently verified.

School board trustee associations

  • OPSBA — “Making a Difference for Kids”: Running for Election as a School Board Trustee, 2026 guide (PDF) — Ontario Public School Boards’ Association, July 2026. Eligibility, the ~15-hr/week time commitment, and the full 2026 election calendar, for English public board trustee candidates.
  • OPSBA — 2026 Municipal and School Board Elections — modified July 21 2026. Hub page linking the guide above plus AMO, AMCTO, MMAH, Elections Ontario, Equal Voice, and CIVIX resources.
  • OCSTA — “A Call to Service” (PDF) — Ontario Catholic School Trustees’ Association, May 2026. Legal qualifications specific to Catholic school board trustee candidacy, plus trustee duties.
  • OCSTA — Municipal Election 2026 — published May 27 2026, modified June 5 2026. Key-dates table matching the provincial calendar, links the brochure above.
  • ACÉPO — Élections 2026 — Association des conseils scolaires des écoles publiques de l’Ontario. Original French-language content: eligibility, a four-step “how to run” outline, links to both official French and English provincial guides. French-only.
  • AFOCSC — “Élections des conseiller(ère)s scolaires catholiques francophones : êtes-vous prêts?” — posted April 1 2026. An 11-part video series; episodes 6–7 cover the election process and becoming a trustee. French-only, video format.
  • Ontario.ca — School board trustee elections — updated May 13 2026. The distinct trustee-specific landing page; links all four associations above.

Women and under-represented candidates — campaign schools & mentorship

  • FCM — Municipal Elections in Canada: A Guide for Women Candidates (PDF) — Federation of Canadian Municipalities.
  • Women in Politics (Thunder Bay / Northwestern Ontario) — independent regional non-profit. Mentorship and education for women in Northwestern Ontario considering any level of elected government.
  • PoliNOW / PoliticsNOW — “Electing Women in the North” — independent grassroots non-profit led by Amanda Kingsley Malo. Sells a paid 2026-edition workbook; also runs free, explicitly non-partisan events.
  • electHER Now — independent regional non-profit (Grey-Bruce). States explicitly it is not affiliated with or financially supported by any political party or lobby group, and does not endorse individual candidates. Runs a campaign school and connects women in municipal office with prospective candidates.
  • FCM — Canadian Women in Local Leadership (CanWILL) — funded by Women and Gender Equality Canada. The named program ran 2021–2024 and has concluded; a retrospective page, but its free GBA+ course, resource library, and “Run, Win, Lead” parity framework remain live and usable.
  • Municipal Campaign School (Guelph / Oxford / Wellington) — volunteer-led initiative, part of a province-wide campaign-school network. 2026 sessions dated Feb–April; includes an “Allies in Action” session for male allies.
  • Seat at the Table (Niagara Region) — Niagara Region with the Greater Niagara Chamber of Commerce, Women in Niagara, YWCA, and other partners. The named program ran April 2022–April 2023 and has concluded; archived workshop videos and a project report remain accessible.
  • Equal Voice — Campaign School — Equal Voice Foundation, a registered multi-partisan charity (participants learn from elected officials representing all political parties). Full campaign-lifecycle training; a spring 2026 session was billed municipal-election-focused.
  • Equal Voice — Indigenous Campaign School — Equal Voice Foundation. Indigenous-specific cohort spanning municipal through federal elections. Most recent instance ran Oct–Nov 2025; worth rechecking closer to Oct 26, 2026 for a 2026 instance.
  • The Jean Collective — Southwestern Ontario women’s leadership initiative (Middlesex, Elgin, Lambton counties). Curated resource hub plus its own campaign school and annual conference.
  • Learn How to Run — Waterloo Region Women’s Municipal Campaign School, volunteer-led, running since 2005. Free, explicitly non-partisan two-day in-person program; 2026 dates confirmed (May 2 and June 13, 2026).
  • Leadership féminin Prescott-Russell — “Politique au féminin” — francophone women’s organization, Eastern Ontario, supported by all 8 municipalities of the United Counties of Prescott and Russell. French-only.

Racialized, Indigenous, and faith-community candidate support

  • Operation Black Vote Canada — hands-on training for Black candidates at any level of government, working with all political parties rather than one.
  • Run4OfficeWR — Waterloo Region initiative supporting Black residents considering candidacy at any level; level-of-government explainers and a “Project 3000WR” candidate pipeline.
  • Equal Voice — Indigenous Campaign School — see full entry above.
  • The Canadian-Muslim Vote — Civic Leadership Institute — national registered charity, explicitly strictly non-partisan and non-endorsing. A fellowship pairing young Canadian Muslims with politicians, staffers, and journalists. Reads more as a mentorship/career pipeline than direct campaign-mechanics training.

Civic leadership & general candidate-readiness resources

  • Samara Centre for Democracy — “Are You Candidate Curious?” — self-described leading non-partisan organization focused on strengthening Canadian democracy, published Sept 12 2024. Nine short videos on deciding to run, gathering support, and fundraising. Note: framed around federal candidacy, not municipal-specific — useful for the psychology of “should I run,” not municipal mechanics.
  • Innovations in Democratic Leadership (formerly the Institute for Future Legislators) — Toronto Metropolitan University, publicly run, no party affiliation. Six-week intensive for aspiring legislators from underrepresented groups. Note: federal/provincial-oriented, not municipal-specific, and costs $2,000 with limited bursaries.

Excluded from this shelf — the non-partisan screen and other exclusion classes

Every resource found this sweep was checked against a non-partisan screen and a “genuinely candidate-facing” screen before inclusion above. What follows is excluded, with the class of exclusion stated so the screen itself is auditable, not just its results:

  • Fails the non-partisan screen outright: Nominee (gonominee.com; overtly partisan-coded mission language); Pillar Nonprofit Network / “London United for Change” Candidate School (screens participants for alignment with “progressive policy ideas” despite calling itself non-partisan).
  • Non-partisan mission, but current page content doesn’t clear the bar: ProudPolitics (proudpolitics.org) — stated mission explicitly welcomes leaders “of all political stripes,” but the live homepage carries an op-ed criticizing a named former federal Conservative leader and a “Stop Ford” petition link hosted by a partisan advocacy group (Progress Toronto), and the site has not relaunched since a promised September 2025 refresh — independently re-confirmed by a second reviewer, 2026-08-05, ~11 months overdue. Exclusion confirmed twice, not overturned.
  • Not genuinely candidate-facing (real organizations, but general leadership/governance training, voter-education/advocacy, or paid work-experience, not campaign or candidacy-specific): AMO’s Local Leadership Foundations (post-election only); ONWA Indigenous Women’s Leadership Program; Urban Alliance on Race Relations’ Diverse Youth Fellowship; DiverseCity onBoard (appointed board/commission seats, not elected office); ARCH Disability Law Centre’s “Elections in Ontario” (voter/platform content, not candidate training); TNO – The Neighbourhood Organization; March of Dimes Canada’s candidate-pledge advocacy; City for All Women Initiative (CAWI) Ottawa’s candidate-pledge work; Toronto Metropolitan University’s Diversity Institute (corporate diversity research, not political).
  • Wrong jurisdiction (federal-only): Elections Canada’s “Inspire Democracy”; Chiefs of Ontario’s Election Toolkit.
  • Stale or dormant despite a live domain (no confirmable 2025/2026 activity): Canadian Women Voters Congress (content frozen circa 2012–2014); FCM’s “Diverse Voices for Change” (a 2017 pilot, no ongoing programming); Future Majority (a GOTV/voter-turnout operation, not candidate training); Open Democracy Project / DemocracyKit (no current events found).
  • Unverifiable currency, not content: Women’s Caucus of Middlesex County — the only link AMO provides is a stale 2022 event-registration form; no live organizational homepage was found.

v1.0 remaining gaps, disclosed: a dedicated language-access resource distinct from the accessibility guides above; a York-Region-specific regional guide; further campaign-school/civic-leadership coverage in Northern and Eastern Ontario beyond what surfaced this sweep.
