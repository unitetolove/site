Invite me in.

Not to speak at you — to sit with you. Your room, your rules, your name on the gathering. I come alone, at your invitation only, to speak, to listen, to answer anything you want to ask, and — with permission — to carry what the last room taught me into yours.

Invite me into your Discords, your WhatsApp and Signal chats, your group DMs and forums. Invite me into your churches, mosques, temples, synagogues and gurdwaras. Invite me to your swimming, biking, running and climbing clubs. Invite me to your campuses, your classrooms, your club meetups. Invite me to your boardrooms and your private clubs. Invite me to your bars, cafés, restaurants, music nights and festivals. Invite me to your work chats, your family chats, your hobby servers. Invite me to your prayers, meditations, feasts and holidays. Invite me to speak, to listen, or just to participate.

Tell me your stories. Teach me. Ask me anything — I’ll speak on any topic.

Why you’d want to

Chances are, I’m already one of you. Through sport, food, faith, trade, travel, craft, ideas, music, adventures, the homeland, the neighbourhood — somewhere in your room’s life and mine, the paths already cross. I don’t arrive as an outsider with a pitch; I arrive as the member you haven’t met yet.

And honestly? I’m fun. I’m interesting. I tell good stories, and I love to listen, learn and laugh. I love talking, sharing, and building understanding and ideas together — that’s not the sales pitch, that’s the whole event. The rest of the credibility comes the only honest way: from the visit itself.

What I ask of your room

Nothing. No signup sheet, no pitch, no collection plate, no photo for our feed. There is nothing to join and no one to follow — I hold no stage and convene nothing. And if the visit leaves your room wanting more — the free blueprints, the research, the wider conversation this city is starting — just ask. Everything we’ve built is free, open, and yours to take apart.

A guest in every sense

One honest note: I can’t buy the ticket, the cover, or the plate. Like most of this city, I know Toronto’s festivals, stages, conferences and kitchens mostly from the outside of a price tag — years of reading the listings and thinking I would love to be in that room. (Most of us live there. The people who can afford this city have no idea how much of its beauty is gatekept by a wallet.) So this offer runs on the oldest economy there is: you open the door, and I bring everything I have — the stories, the ideas, the questions, both ears, and whatever the last hundred rooms taught me. Feed me and you have a friend for life. If that trade sounds fair, we’ll get along beautifully.

How it works

There’s a calendar with my real availability. You (the host) request a slot; I confirm or decline — I never book myself into your room. In person I run a handful of rooms a day, clustered by neighbourhood so travel never eats the listening — seven days a week, with Fridays through Sundays shaped around congregations and the rooms that only gather on weekends. And the standing offer is bigger online: I live comfortably in chat channels — invite me into your server or group and I’m reachable there long after any visit, no travel required, hundreds of rooms at once.

The record — decided at your door, not after

Your room owns the record. If anyone records, it’s you — I never film your room. At booking, you pick from a small menu:

  • No recording — words stay in the room.
  • Kept in-room (the default) — recorded for your members, never published.
  • Public, after a delay — released after 30 days and a privacy pass.
  • Public now — rare, and only by everyone’s explicit choice.

Nothing ever goes public without both keys turned — your room’s consent and mine. Either of us can downgrade a release anytime; nobody can upgrade one without the room. And voice travels before face: anything public defaults to audio and transcript — video is a separate, explicit yes, each time. One rule protects your room’s candour and your guest’s privacy alike.

Who I am

This page describes an offer, not a person — that’s deliberate, and it’s permanent. No face here, no feed to follow, no name in print. In person you’ll get a first name and a handshake; if you know, you know. Online I’m Unk Sol; the work itself speaks as @unitetolove.

And this role is designed to end. A season of rooms, and then it dissolves — what remains is what it was for: rooms that know each other, and paths any neighbour can walk without me. Any room can name its own connector tomorrow; the pattern is free, like everything else here.

The only thing I ask for is invitations. Invite me in.
