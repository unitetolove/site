1. What mutual aid is — the doctrine base

Dean Spade's Mutual Aid: Building Solidarity During This Crisis (and the Next) (Verso, 2020) is the canonical modern text. Three facets: survival needs met collectively while building shared understanding of root causes; mutual aid as mobilization — people who help each other learn to organize together; organization by direct participation, not service delivery. The line from charity: participants define the aid; no eligibility tests, no giver/receiver caste, no deservingness screening — eligibility-creep is the documented failure mode that turns mutual aid back into punitive charity. Group-survival principles: humility, feedback, conflict-resolution skill, transparency, consensus-heavy decisions. Sources: Verso · Social Text essay/131/160175/Solidarity-Not-CharityMutual-Aid-for-Mobilization) · APA blog history

The lineage is longer than any pandemic: Kropotkin's 1902 evolutionary case; Black fraternal and co-op traditions (Nembhard's Collective Courage); the Black Panther survival programs (free breakfast — which shamed the US into expanding school breakfast — clinics, ambulances); Common Ground 2005; Occupy Sandy 2012; the COVID wave 2020. The estate's own deepest artifact stands over all of it for Toronto purposes: FLAGSHIP_purok_hyperlocal_organizing.md (the ~10,000-cell hyperlocal briefing + 8 multi-model critiques in report feedback/), including the dual-use warnings (China grid management, Cuba CDRs) that shape our §4 consent design.

2. LA — the operator's named focus, org by org

2.1 The connector: Mutual Aid LA Network (MALAN)

mutualaidla.org — the reference connector-hub: connects people to the group that fits (location/schedule/resource/skill), maintains the Group Listings Directory and an Airtable Resource Catalog anyone can query or add to, publishes The Dispatch newsletter (EN+ES, archive), runs a few small internal programs (Produce in the Park, Hygiene Hello!, Open Art Supply, poli-ed reading group). Volunteer-run, explicitly "solidarity not charity." The Dispatch's listing microformat — WHO / YOU HAVE / YOU NEED / WHEN / WHERE / $$$ — is the best request-offer grammar we found anywhere; our directory adopts it. (Honesty note: the Dispatch archive issue we fetched is July 2023; MALAN's site and catalog are live today, but newsletter cadence in 2026 is unconfirmed — R1 checks.)

2.2 The specialists (mined from MALAN's own network + 2026 checks)

  • Ktown for All — volunteer Koreatown homeless outreach + advocacy;

verified active 2026: weekly outreach continuing, and in Feb 2026 co-won a federal case against the City of LA over destruction of unhoused people's belongings (People's Tribune · ktownforall.org). The model: outreach teams + policy teeth.

  • Water Drop LA — weekly water/supply distribution to Skid Row, Sundays, plus

"backbone infrastructure and capacity-building workshops" for OTHER mutual-aid groups (la2050 profile) — a group whose product is partly other groups' capacity; our connector layer's job description.

  • Polo's Pantry — food logistics collective; runs distributions WITH partners (Las Fotos

Project, Alianza Nacional de Campesinas farmworker distro) rather than solo — coalition logistics as the craft (LA Public Press).

  • J-Town Action & Solidarity — Little Tokyo collective: mutual aid fused with

anti-displacement organizing — aid rooted in a neighbourhood's fight for itself.

  • Home-y Made Meals — home cooks + drivers delivering meals to unhoused neighbours

(homeymademeals.com) — the lowest-barrier volunteer on-ramp we found: "cook one extra portion."

  • Also in the constellation (from the Dispatch + Radical Database LA): Echo Park Mutual Aid,

Palms Unhoused Mutual Aid, All Power Free Clinic (street medicine), Mutual Aid Action LA (community space + food co-op), The People of South Gate, Farm2Power (BIPOC-farm CSA pantries), LA Community Fridge network, Long Beach Food Not Bombs. In This Together LA's Radical Database is the city's other directory layer.

2.3 The stress test: January 2025 fires → 2026 long-term recovery

Crisis phase (Jan 2025): when the Eaton fire destroyed much of Altadena (31 dead across Altadena/Palisades, 9,000+ structures), the housing-crisis-era networks pivoted into disaster hubs within days — while FEMA/Red Cross/insurance spun up, mutual aid was the reliable immediate layer. Practices worth stealing directly: hubs not warehouses (pop-up distribution run by the affected neighbourhood); free stores arranged like shops so displaced people choose (dignity mechanics); ask-first outreach at encampments (check-ins, then supplies). Sources: Truthout · Knock LA · LA Public Press · NPQ first-person · CalMatters

Recovery phase (2026, one year on): the spontaneous wave institutionalized WITHOUT dying — the arc Toronto's COVID wave never managed. The Eaton Fire Collaborative long-term recovery group coordinates orgs from The Collaboratory (540 W Woodbury Rd), a physical hub where survivors meet every helping org in one room; Dena Care Collective (founded by two former mayors) moves $1M to 500+ families; DENA Heals runs healing circles; My Tribe Rise runs material recovery; community resource lists stay maintained (eatonfirecollaborative.org · United Way LA · denafirehelp.com · 2026 resource roundup · AfroLA on uneven recovery). The LA lesson in one line: a network that exists before the crisis responds in hours; a response that builds a physical hub and a coalition survives the year after.

3. The evidence base — what sustains a group, what kills it

The UK ran the largest natural experiment (~4,000+ COVID groups) and studied it properly (Drury/Sussex, Ntontis/Open University, National Preparedness Commission):

  • Decay is the default: ~75% activity drop from the March-2020 peak by June 2020;

collapse when lockdowns eased.

  • What sustained groups: real continuing local needs (not crisis-only framing); trust +

alliances with existing organizations; a culture of care inside the group; regular meetings; positive emotion — joy, pride, efficacy, belonging.

  • What killed groups: no funding, no structure ("chaotic"), burnout.
  • Scaffolding works only string-free: councils/agencies can provide money, space,

connections — but absorbed groups lose the trusted identity that made them work.

Sources: NPC · Frontiers · Sussex case study · J. Civil Society · wellbeing study

Occupy Sandy is the scale proof: ~60,000 volunteers trained and mobilized, out-performing legacy relief so clearly that a DHS-funded study (The Resilient Social Network, HSSAI 2013) wrote it up as the model — the network grew out of Occupy's pre-existing relationships, same pre-existing-network mechanism as Altadena (Truthout · Resilience). Common Ground Collective (New Orleans, 2005 — Malik Rahim, scott crow) is the lineage proof: Panther survival-program DNA → clinics, legal aid, gardens, tool libraries, wetland restoration; the largest anarchist-inspired US organization of its era (Wikipedia · Waging Nonviolence). And Toronto's own CareMongering is the cautionary proof — the fastest-growing mutual-aid movement ever measured (PLOS ONE) and among the fastest to die (The Conversation).

4. Toronto — corrected

v0 of this file repeated COVID-era descriptions of Toronto groups as if current. That was wrong. The full live-verified landscape — who is dead, who is dormant, who actually operates in and near the riding today — is now its own receipt-bearing file: 2026-08-14_TORONTO_LANDSCAPE_VERIFIED.md.

The short version: the COVID cohort (CareMongering-TO, the pod networks, ESN's umbrella) is dead or dormant; the survivors specialized (Community Fridges TO, Bike Brigade — both still needing a recency confirm) or institutionalized; the verified-active layer in the riding is institutional (Community Centre 55 · Eastview · Salvation Army East Toronto · Neighbourhood Link/TNG · 211). Nobody runs a general neighbour-to-neighbour network in BEY in 2026. Field open; graveyard = design brief. Toronto's data layer for the pilot remains estate-held: EC21.6 (330 program rows), Free Toronto (~6,600 resource rows with elig_geo), ward-19 profile, 2024 Street Needs Assessment city numbers (toronto.ca · report PDF · CBC).

5. Design implications (carried into the plan — updated v1)

  1. Connector-hub + block cells, not a new charity (MALAN at riding scale; estate Block

Circle kit as the cell layer; verified-active institutions amplified, never duplicated).

  1. Build for the decay curve — weekly rhythm, care steward, joy as infrastructure; the UK

findings and Toronto's graveyard agree these are survival requirements.

  1. Ask-first, dignity-first — LA check-in method + free-store choice + no eligibility tests

= the needs-map method (know every need, not every name).

  1. Specialize the functions, lighten the umbrella — Toronto's survivors are specialists;

ESN's cells outlived its umbrella. Our sharp functions: the loop, the walk, the Hub Hour.

  1. Institutions are the continuity layer — weave Centre 55/Eastview/TNG/211 in from day

one, string-free in both directions.

  1. Own the platform, not Meta — every dead Toronto group lived on Facebook. Discord/Signal/

site + paper/phone front doors (our ruling stands).

  1. Adopt the Dispatch grammar for every listing: WHO / YOU HAVE / YOU NEED / WHEN / WHERE / $$$.
  2. Plan the year-after now — LA's fire response survived because it built a hub and a

coalition; our W3 replication-kit step is that move made early.

  1. The riding frame was proven here (Davenport 2020) and died with its crisis framing —

ours anchors to permanent need at the base of the pyramid, not to an emergency.
