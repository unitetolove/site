Ten-ish neighbours who know each other’s names

Every big network that lasted was really a mesh of small ones. A block circle is 8–15 neighbours, close enough to walk to each other, who do small things together: a shared meal, a heat-wave check-in list, a hand with the couch, ten people who already trust each other when something real happens. Not a membership. Not a committee. Free to join, free to leave, nothing to sign, nobody pays.

Start one this week — the whole recipe

  1. Name your three. Three people on your street you actually know. Invite them yourself — a neighbour’s word is the entire technology.
  2. First gathering = food, one hour, zero agenda beyond one round of “what would make this street better?” Kids welcome, food shared.
  3. Knock the doors between you — the doorstep script is in the Block Circle starter kit, and it works.
  4. Pick the next host at the first meeting. And say the split rule out loud: at fifteen people, you split into two circles — that’s not a breakup, that’s the street doubling its capacity.
  5. Keep one gathering a month unprogrammed. No ask, no task. The circle that is fun outlives the circle that is worthy — this is a design rule, not a nicety.

Your circle speaks for itself, in its own name. It isn’t a branch of anything — including us. We just keep the kit sharp and connect circles that want connecting.

We never handle money. No donations are taken here or anywhere — if you want to give money, give it directly to the verified organizations in the directory.

Volunteer-run and non-partisan — no party, no candidate, no memberships, nothing for sale. Beaches–East York Mutual Aid Networks (“BEY Mutual Aid”) is part of Toronto’s Mutual Aid Networks: a network of networks — nobody owns mutual aid.
