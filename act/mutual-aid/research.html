We did the homework before we knocked on anything.

Before building, we studied every serious mutual-aid effort we could verify — and, just as carefully, the ones that collapsed.

  • Los Angeles — the continent’s deepest mutual-aid ecosystem, from Ktown for All to the 2025 fire response, where neighbour networks moved faster than any institution and then, crucially, kept going.
  • Occupy Sandy — 60,000 volunteers out-delivering formal relief after the 2012 hurricane; even a Department of Homeland Security study conceded the decentralized network beat the centralized ones.
  • The UK’s COVID groups — four thousand of them, and the survivors shared three traits: regular rhythm, a culture of care for the volunteers themselves, and joy. The worthy-but-grim ones died.
  • Toronto 2020 — our own wave, honestly assessed: CareMongering-TO went dormant, the umbrella networks dissolved, and the lesson is stamped into our design — cells outlive umbrellas, rhythm outlives launches, and a directory nobody re-verifies becomes a list of dead phone numbers.

Three rules came out of all of it and govern everything we do: no eligibility tests, ever (the solidarity floor — help that makes people audition isn’t mutual aid); amplify what exists, duplicate nothing (this neighbourhood already has real institutions — we verified which ones); and the network is a kit plus a rhythm plus a page, never a headquarters.

Read the pack itself

  • The Research Foundation — the full evidence base: LA org-by-org, Occupy Sandy, the UK literature, the global cases, and the design rules they forced.
  • Toronto’s Landscape, Live-Verified — every named organization checked alive-or-dormant, method and date per row.
  • The Reading List & Toolkit Library — the books and toolkits this is built from, and what each taught us.

Published v1 on the operator’s word, 16 Aug 2026. Independent re-verification of every claim continues; corrections publish here, in place, as always.

We never handle money. No donations are taken here or anywhere — if you want to give money, give it directly to the verified organizations in the directory.

Volunteer-run and non-partisan — no party, no candidate, no memberships, nothing for sale. Beaches–East York Mutual Aid Networks (“BEY Mutual Aid”) is part of Toronto’s Mutual Aid Networks: a network of networks — nobody owns mutual aid.
