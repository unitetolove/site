Act

Toronto decides its future over the next few years — with us or without us. You are the ones who matter, and it’s your actions that matter. This is the ladder: every rung is optional, every rung is a real door that works today, and every rung returns a receipt. Nothing here requires a committee, a membership, or anyone’s permission. The first rung costs thirty seconds. Climb exactly as far as you want.

The live list of what moves the project this week is Next actions — if you only bookmark one page, bookmark that one. This page is the ladder itself.

Thirty seconds

Sign up for the conversation. A name and a way to reach you. The running public count is how the city watches itself show up — your signature literally moves a number everyone can see.

Add your three lines to the Harvest. “Toronto in 2030 should ___,” what’s broken on your street, and a neighbour who deserves recognition. The form is live and takes a minute; what you send becomes part of the commons, credited if you want credit.

An hour

Book the October hours now. The city decides things between October 7 and 26 — and time off is won in advance. Ask for a few hours this week, while calendars are still open, then tell one co-worker why. Run a workplace, union, school, or congregation? The same page carries a one-page policy any of them can adopt this month.

File your complaint where it legally counts. Every complaint channel that actually has to answer you — city, provincial, federal — in one place, so “what’s broken” lands on a desk that must respond, not in a void.

Say what you’ve seen. If you work inside the systems this project studies, or you’ve lived what the reports only describe, the listening page takes your account carefully, on your terms — including anonymously.

Arrive with a better question. The citizen question bank arms you for the all-candidates night, the door-knock, and the family group chat — real questions, sourced and receipted.

An evening

Host one of the ten kits. A vision dinner at your own table. Three questions after the service. A block circle on your street. A complaint clinic at a folding table. A citizen-run all-candidates debate. Free, printable, and yours to run in your own name — nothing behind them convenes anything. Every kit ends in the same harvest.

Go where Toronto already gathers. Join stuff. Attend stuff. Make friends. The tribes, rooms, and platforms where the conversation already lives — this project routes you to their doors, never through its own.

A week

Take a work packet. The public map of Toronto’s organizations — the congregations, leagues, associations, and clubs that hold this city together — is built one verified row at a time. A packet is one slice of the city, sized for a week of spare moments, and there is one waiting with your neighbourhood’s name on it.

Spread it. Send one page from the Library to the friend who argues about exactly that issue. Tell one person, face to face. A shelf of print-ready posters and share cards is built and in its final checks now — until it lands, the Harvest is where to send the ones you make yourself. Post only where you have the right to post.

A season

Run for office. Nominations close August 21 at 2 p.m. — and in October every council seat in Ontario is on the ballot, many barely contested. This site equips any candidate, of any politics, on identical terms, for free. If not you, vote — and help one person you trust decide to run or to vote.

Invite me into your room. If you already have a room — a team, a congregation, a class, a crew — host one conversation and I’ll come to it.

A year

Take your organization into the year. Organizations agreeing to sprint the next year together — same goals, same time, own names, shown work. Read The Plan for the whole story of the year, then bring it to the room you belong to.

Some rungs aren’t built yet, and this page will never pretend otherwise: the poster and share-card shelf, the organization map you can pin yourself, and tribe merch nobody profits from are all in the works. When they’re real, they’ll appear here as doors — not before.
