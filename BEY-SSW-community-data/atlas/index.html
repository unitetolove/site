The community, told through data

Two neighbourhoods vote on the same day and behave like two different countries — sometimes on the same street. This atlas is the evidence layer behind that sentence: six data stories built from Elections Canada's own poll-by-poll returns, the City of Toronto's 2021 neighbourhood profiles, the City's own address repository, and a registry of the institutions that actually hold these neighbourhoods together. It maps participation — who gets reached, who gets counted, and where the invitation never arrives — and nothing else. Start with the chart below: it is the finding the stories unfold.

Source: Elections Canada, 2025 general election poll-by-poll results (Beaches—East York, 35007), aggregated from polling divisions to advance-poll zones by this project's own data pipeline · as of 2026-08-15 ·
This figure described in words (alt text) Horizontal bar chart of the 15 advance-poll zones of Beaches—East York, ordered from lowest to highest total turnout. The four lowest zones — 608 (East End-Danforth), 603 (Taylor-Massey), 602 (O'Connor-Parkview) and 600 (O'Connor-Parkview/Victoria Village), the riding's tower corridor — run from 55.9% to 59.0%. The six highest — 614, 611, 612, 606, 613 and 609, covering The Beaches, the Woodbine Corridor and Danforth East York — run from 71.4% to 75.7%. The distance from the lowest zone to the highest is 19.8 percentage points, and the low end is a contiguous block of high-rise geography rather than a scatter.

Read it this way. The canyon is not apathy and not a mystery: it is tenure, tower geography and language, and it tracks them closely enough to plan around. The four red zones hold roughly 21,000 electors between them. Nothing here identifies a person, a household, or a vote — the smallest unit anywhere on this site is a polling division.

What this is, and what it is not

It is a participation map: turnout by zone, by neighbourhood, by building; the demographic layer that explains it; the measured street doors behind the arithmetic; and the coverage maths that says what a volunteer base can actually reach. It is not a targeting product. There is no candidate data, no party data, no individual-level record, and no persuasion model anywhere in it — by construction, not by policy. Every figure carries its source, its as-of date, and a download link to the same CSV the figure was drawn from, so anyone can check the claim against the data instead of trusting the chart.

Non-partisan: this site maps participation only — no candidate, party, or individual-level data appears anywhere. Offered identically to every neighbour of every politics.
