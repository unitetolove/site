The Listening Seat — charter

No Indigenous person or organization has reviewed, endorsed, or participated in this work, and this page says so.

§0 — The mission in one breath

"This is not our land." We are building a civic commons on it anyway, because we live here too. So this project exists to do the one thing a guest can do without being asked: listen completely, repeat exactly, point loudly, and build the seat — then leave the clock in their hands.

The mission, operationally: become the most complete, most faithful, most useful listener to Toronto's Indigenous communities that any settler civic project has ever been — the best possible research on their behalf delivered not as speaking for them, but as making what they have already said impossible to miss.

§1 — What we are, and what we are not

We are four things:

A record. Everything Indigenous nations, organizations, and knowledge-holders in and around Toronto have publicly published about themselves and their priorities — catalogued, provenance- classed, quoted verbatim, verified against the living source, and kept current. Not a sample. Not highlights. As close to everything as honest exhaustion reaches — completeness is itself a form of respect: a partial record is an editorial act.

A router. Surfaces that move Torontonians to Indigenous doors, not to our descriptions of those doors: the services guide, the organization directory, THE INDIGENOUS SHELF, the traditions page built on invitation — answered only the way communities themselves answer it: on their terms, by their invitation. Every route terminates at the Indigenous source itself.

An overlay on everything else we do. No issue this movement researches ships without the published Indigenous record beside it. Where that record is rich, the overlay carries it. Where it is silent, the overlay says so plainly — a documented absence is a finding, not a gap to paper over — and may be the most useful thing we hand a future reader.

An empty seat, kept ready. The infrastructure of relationship — the door, the disclosure mechanics, the co-governance instrument, the honoraria contingency — built, maintained, and left unoccupied. Not a next phase. Not a milestone we advance toward. A seat at a table we set and do not sit at. This charter deliberately retires the phase-ladder framing: there is no next phase. The work is complete in itself, and the door is theirs.

We are not: a voice for Indigenous peoples; a partner (no partnership exists and none is implied); a completed thing (a listening practice does not finish); a neutral archive (we choose to make Indigenous publication loud in a city that has practiced not hearing it — that is a stance, and we own it as ours, not theirs).

§2 — Audiences, and the outcome we owe each

1. Indigenous nations, communities, organizations, and knowledge-holders — the audience the work is for, whether or not they ever look at it. Owed: exact quotation or silence, never paraphrase into our agenda; traffic to their own doors; their distinctions kept (a First Nation is not the Métis Nation is not Inuit is not "urban Indigenous"; each named as it names itself); no monetization, no leverage, no implied association; the seat kept governing-grade, never decorative. The test: if any Indigenous reader ever audits this work, the reaction we are building toward is — "they did the reading, they linked us instead of describing us, they never pretended we were involved, and when we said different things they let us differ."

2. Torontonians — neighbours, readers, the movement. Owed: that any resident can, in three clicks from our surfaces, find an Indigenous service that serves them, an Indigenous author to read, an Indigenous organization to support, and the actual text of what Indigenous communities have said about any city issue they care about. The test: a Torontonian who spends an hour on our surfaces leaves knowing whose land this is, holding at least one door they didn't know existed — and having read Indigenous words, not our gloss on them.

3. The city's institutions and the people who seek to lead them. Owed: a published Indigenous record so complete, so verified, and so precisely sourced that no Toronto policy conversation on our surfaces can honestly proceed without it — receipts that survive hostile checking. The test: our accountability facts get used by people who disagree with us, because they cannot fault the sourcing.

4. The world — other jurisdictions, researchers, civic projects. Owed: the method itself, open: provenance classes, the fabrication guard, the never-speak-for checklist, the two-layer independent review, this charter. The standard other settler civic projects get held to because we proved it possible. The test: the method is adopted, cited, or held up somewhere we never reached ourselves.

5. Ourselves — the people building this. Owed: that every claim survives hostile checking, and that the record shows the listening began long before there was anything to gain by it.

§3 — How we get there

The engine is proven — one operating period built a full first wave of overlays, a shelf of Indigenous-authored works, an organization lane, and a verified source corpus, all under seal. The path is that engine, run in waves with gates, never dates: dates are clocks, and this project holds no clocks over anyone.

Wave discipline, standing: every wave = build lanes + an independent review lane that re-fetches primary sources + a judgment-seat seal with same-sitting fixes. Nothing ships un-reviewed; no builder reviews itself.

The coverage arc: overlays extended across all of Toronto's civic issues — carried where sources exist, absence-documented where they don't (both are completions); the corpus refreshed on a living cycle (sources rot — re-verification is scheduled work, not incident response); the shelf grown and rendered; the organization lane extended each time a new directory axis surfaces.

The surface arc: everything identified gets communicated — published under the same status gate as all our work (no othering carve-outs in either direction), every page linking Indigenous sources directly, every page carrying the honest-limitation caveat. Identification without communication is hoarding.

The seat arc: the relationship infrastructure is maintained current and never activated by us. No outreach by any AI, ever. First contact, if it comes from our side at all, is a human being, in person, in the open — Indigenous communities were placed in the first wave of owed respect on the project's own ruling that it is "a respect thing." First invited, never hurried.

§4 — The standards, in full

The complete stack. Every rule below is enforced by at least one of: a checklist run at build, an independent review at seal, a greppable invariant at close, or a routing rule when judgment is needed. A standard that nothing enforces is a wish; we don't keep wishes.

S1 — Voice. We never speak for Indigenous peoples. Every characterization of an Indigenous position is a verbatim quote or a tight attributed paraphrase, sourced inline. WHO published WHAT, always: no "Indigenous people believe," no blending of distinct voices into consensus — where sources diverge, the divergence is carried as named positions, explicitly unadjudicated. Nations, communities, and organizations are named as they name themselves.

S2 — Truth. No quote lands without same-session verification against the living source, raw- fetched and cross-checked — a confident-looking extraction is a candidate, never a verification. What cannot be verified is dropped and the drop disclosed. Claimed absences ("no source addresses X") must be mechanically provable against the catalogue. Every correction propagates to every carrier with a dated note; history is preserved, never rewritten; archives and snapshots are untouchable.

S3 — Provenance. Every source is classed — Indigenous-authored (Indigenous voice about itself), co-produced (co-produced), non-Indigenous-authored (about Indigenous people) (about, not by) — and the class travels with every use. non-Indigenous-authored (about Indigenous people) never carries a position, only flagged contextual fact. Grey areas travel forever with their entries: collection-vehicle testimony stays labeled as testimony, identity-scrutiny caveats stay attached to the work, co-production stays disclosed. Anonymity chosen by a source's own convention is preserved, never resolved.

S4 — Conduct. No contact of any kind by any AI or any lane — no email, no form, no DM, no "just verifying a detail." No mass instruments ever touch Indigenous organizations: no invite batches, no ladders, no blast lists. No named-individual targeting; institutional framing always. Ceremonial and spiritual life is not enumerated beyond public-facing service organizations. And no money: nothing offered, nothing implied, the honoraria model held as a drafted contingency for a future only they can choose. Content concerning the dead, the sacred, or community grief routes to a future, Indigenous-governed review seat — never resolved internally. That seat's emptiness does not transfer its authority to us; it holds the question open.

S5 — Data. OCAP® named precisely: First Nations-specific, trademarked, governed by FNIGC — never a generic label for "Indigenous data." CARE alongside, distinctly. Métis and Inuit data governance named as their own processes. Our own self-assessment against these standards is "partial" and is stated wherever it matters — it cannot become "compliant" without the real engagement we refuse to simulate, and we say exactly that.

S6 — Publication. Same status gate as every other row and page — visibility of public information is not the risk; conduct is — so no exclusion-by-othering and no lowered bar either. Every public surface links the Indigenous source itself: the nation's own site over the city's summary, the org's own report over coverage of it. Every public surface carries the honest-limitation caveat verbatim: no Indigenous person or organization has reviewed, endorsed, or participated in this work, and it says so.

S7 — Quality. The bar is external: benchmarked against the world's best Indigenous- accountability research — Indigenous-led work we do not compete with on voice and must match on rigor. We beat it only on the one axis available to a guest: fidelity and completeness of listening. Any deliverable that would embarrass that benchmark is a failure, not a draft.

S8 — Review. Two layers, always: the builder's own fabrication guard, then an independent reviewer who re-fetches primary sources and never reviewed their own build. Seals are recorded with sample tables. Reviewer findings are fixed same-sitting or carried as named cards — never silently absorbed.

S9 — Memory. Every instruction that shapes this project is banked verbatim, zero truncation, before any processing. Every lesson is wired into a file, a checklist, or a failing check; a lesson that lives only in prose did not happen.

S10 — The epigraph. "This is not our land" is not a land acknowledgement; it is an operating constraint. When any rule above is ambiguous in a live case, resolve toward the reading that takes less and points more. Decoration is forbidden: the sentence appears where it governs, not where it flatters.

§5 — What success is not

Not endorsement — we will never claim it, and its absence is stated on every surface. Not partnership — the seat stays empty until filled from the other side. Not completion — the record is living or it is dead. Not gratitude — nothing here earns or expects it; respect that invoices is rent-seeking. Not our story — if this project succeeds completely, the trail it leaves is other people reading Indigenous words at Indigenous doors, and barely noticing who built the signage.
