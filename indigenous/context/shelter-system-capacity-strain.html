Indigenous context: Shelter System Capacity Strain

No Indigenous person or organization has reviewed, endorsed, or participated in this work, and this page says so.

What the general capacity-strain finding misses

The promoted finding on this page documents a 423.5% rise in assaults against shelter staff (2011-2021) and a scheduled capacity reduction (Better Living Centre, tied to FIFA World Cup venue-booking). Neither figure is broken out by Indigenous status, and neither document currently cites any Indigenous-led shelter provider or Indigenous governance body with standing over Toronto's shelter/Coordinated Access system.

What is documented, in Indigenous organizations' own words

  • TICAB (Toronto Indigenous Community Advisory Board) holds final-decision-making authority over Coordinated Access policy specifically (distinct from the non-binding Aboriginal Affairs Advisory Committee) — already documented in depth in `that page's own internal recordsthis issue's own promoted research document, not re-derived here. TICAB's own quoted position — "the most ideal way to eliminate Indigenous homelessness is to ensure there are affordable housing opportunities for Indigenous people in Toronto" — is reproduced in an earlier internal research file.
  • Anduhyaun Inc., Toronto's oldest Indigenous women's shelter (founded 1973), provides emergency shelter and second-stage housing specifically for Indigenous women and their children — a distinct shelter-system node the general capacity-strain finding does not name. https://anduhyaun.org/ (Indigenous-authored)
  • Ontario Aboriginal Housing Services, the largest Indigenous non-profit housing provider in Ontario, frames the housing-system pressure this page documents as a jurisdiction-and-partnership problem in its own words — see the Cora McGuire-Cyrette quote in an earlier internal research file. https://www.ontarioaboriginalhousing.ca/ (Indigenous-authored)

CARE check (this library's Indigenous-sources framework, applied to this addition)

  • Collective benefit: points readers of this page toward Indigenous-governed shelter/housing bodies with actual decision authority (TICAB) or direct service delivery (Anduhyaun, OAHS), rather than letting a general capacity-strain narrative implicitly stand in for their situation.
  • Authority to control: every claim above is a direct, attributed pointer to the named organization's own published statement; nothing here reinterprets or aggregates their words.
  • Responsibility: no relationship exists between this project and any organization named above — the honest-limitation disclosure in an earlier internal research file §7 applies in full.
  • Ethics: this block does not claim TICAB, Anduhyaun, or OAHS have reviewed or endorsed this page; it cites only what each has already published.
