Indigenous context: AI Public Good Adoption

No Indigenous person or organization has reviewed, endorsed, or participated in this work, and this page says so.

What the general finding misses

The FLAGSHIP's own guardrails — bias auditing, human-in-the-loop, transparency, consultation before deployment — are exactly the terrain Chiefs of Ontario has already published a full research paper on, addressed to "the current draft framework being proposed for the Ontario public services use of AI." A named First Nations political body has already stated what that governance must look like to be real — a bar (rights-holder consultation, not advisory-table box-ticking) the flagship's own "human-in-the-loop" language doesn't reach.

Chiefs of Ontario: what real AI consultation requires

"It is crucial that any AI system that might affect First Nations be developed in consultation with First Nations... Consultation should take place with the rights holders—the First Nations themselves. Consulting with advisory tables or community groups is no substitute for consultation with the leadership of the affected First Nations." — Chiefs of Ontario, Research and Data Management Sector (author: Dr. Benjamin Wald), First Nations and Artificial Intelligence Research Paper, 2024, §4.4.2, https://chiefs-of-ontario.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024_08_16_RP_First_Nations_and-_AI_Paper_BW_final_Ack.pdf (Indigenous-authored)

"It is crucial that First Nations individuals and First Nations communities have avenues to object to AI decision making that could be the result of algorithmic bias... a right to collectively object to AI decision making is also required to head off the risk of algorithmic bias." — same source, §4.4.3. Ellipsis omits one clause on individual-level proof burden. (The paper's OCAP material — Bonnie Healey's "Four Directions" quote, the AFN brief — is already carried in full in the sealed F2 overlay; not restated here.)

The Indigenous Protocol and Artificial Intelligence position paper

A distinct, non-Ontario complement: a multi-nation collection (Aotearoa, Australia, North America, the Pacific) of Indigenous-centred AI design guidelines — flagged as broader than this page’s Toronto/Ontario scope, not a local position:

"We are entering a new phase of language revitalization where technology can assist Indigenous people in organizing data in ways that allow us to synthesize ancestral knowledge and rebuild systems of knowledge keeping and transmission." — Dr. Noelani Arista (Kanaka Maoli), quoted in Indigenous Protocol and Artificial Intelligence Position Paper, ed. Jason Edward Lewis, Initiative for Indigenous Futures, 2020, https://www.indigenous-ai.net/position-paper/ (Indigenous-authored)

Distinctness, kept exact

  • Chiefs of Ontario — a provincial political body addressing Ontario's own draft public-sector AI framework directly.
  • Animikii and the Indigenous Mapping Collective — already treated in full in the F2 overlay; pointed-to, not re-quoted, per this lane's non-duplication discipline.
  • Initiative for Indigenous Futures — a global, multi-nation collective (Hawaiian, Māori, Lakota, Coquille, Anishinaabe, Basque); not an Ontario voice, not folded into it as though it were.

Honest gaps this overlay does not close

  1. No catalogued source addresses broadband access, affordability, or connectivity — digital-equity-connectivity's own scope. Grep-checked against this library's Indigenous-sources catalogue and all twelve in_their_own_words/ files for "broadband," "internet access," "digital divide," "connectivity": zero matches.
  2. No named position on ConnectTO, Toronto's own broadband/Wi-Fi program, found catalogued.
  3. No Toronto-specific or municipal (vs. provincial) AI position was located — the Chiefs of Ontario paper's framework reference is to Ontario's draft, not a City instrument.

CARE check (this library's Indigenous-sources framework

  • Collective benefit: surfaces a named First Nations body's own detailed AI-governance standard the flagship's own guardrail language doesn't reach.
  • Authority to control: every claim is a direct, attributed quotation, verified live.
  • Responsibility: no relationship exists between this project and Chiefs of Ontario or the Initiative for Indigenous Futures — this library's Indigenous-sources framework's disclosure applies in full.
  • Ethics: the global position paper's authorship is kept explicit and distinct from Ontario-specific material; Animikii/IMC pointed to, not duplicated, honouring F2's own work.
