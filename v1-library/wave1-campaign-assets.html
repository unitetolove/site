TIER 1: THE FIVE INDEFENSIBLE NUMBERS

These five statistics are primary-sourced, independently verifiable, and survive legal scrutiny. Use them as your opening salvo on any platform.

NUMBER 1 — THE SYSTEM VERDICT

"In the City of Toronto's own 2025 strategic-plan consultation, a survey of 176 stakeholders — residents' associations, health and homelessness organizations, and people with lived experience — found only 6% believe homelessness services have improved in five years; 66% say worse. (What We Heard, Apr 2025, App. A, p.58.)"

Source: City of Toronto's own What We Heard consultation report, April 2025 (SN Management for TSSS). This is not an advocacy survey. This is a cross-sector stakeholder survey — residents' associations, health orgs, providers, and people with lived experience, in a document produced for the City. URL: toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/979d-Strategic-Plan-Stakeholder-Engagement-Report-FULL.pdf ✅

NUMBER 2 — THE COST THAT SAVES NOTHING

"Toronto spends $49,640 per person per year keeping someone in emergency shelter. Housing First costs $6,311. For every person housed instead of sheltered, taxpayers save $43,329 per year."

Sources: $49,640 = $136/night × 365 (Toronto Auditor General, February 2025) ✅; $6,311 = net Housing First cost after 69% offset (Latimer et al. 2020, Psychiatric Services, DOI:10.1176/appi.ps.202000029) ✅

NUMBER 3 — THE PROVINCIAL STRATEGY THAT DOESN'T EXIST

"Ontario's Auditor General told the Ford government in 2021 to build a provincial homelessness strategy with specific targets and timelines. A 2023 follow-up found 40% of the AG's recommendations — including the strategy — showed 'little or no progress.' It's now 2026. No strategy exists."

Sources: AG 2021 (auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en21/AR_Homelessness_en21.pdf) ✅; AG 2023 follow-up (auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en23/1-07FU_homelessness_en23.pdf) ✅

NUMBER 4 — THE BENEFIT CUT FOR TENT DWELLERS

"Ontario Works has been frozen at $733/month since 2018 — about a quarter of full-time minimum-wage pay today, down from roughly 70% in 1990. A person in a tent who can't prove rent gets only the ~$343 basic-needs portion. People sleeping in tents on public land pay no rent. The Province's income support system charges a penalty for being homeless."

Source: Ontario Regulation 134/98 (Ontario Works Act) — the law itself ✅

NUMBER 5 — THE POLICY THAT COST 1,583 PEOPLE THEIR HOUSING

"Ontario suspended the Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit for 7 months in 2024. That year, Toronto's shelter-to-housing exits fell 26.7% — 1,583 fewer people housed (5,927 → 4,344). The suspension is the most visible policy change in that window, and the cost of lost throughput runs into the tens of millions. (TSSS 2026 Budget Notes.)"

Source: TSSS 2026 Budget Notes, backgroundfile-261532.pdf ✅

THE COUNTER-ARGUMENT THEY WILL USE — AND HOW TO ANSWER IT

"Toronto's homeless count dropped 21% in 2025 — proof the approach is working."

This is true. The October 2025 count was approximately 12,180 — down 21% from 15,418 in October 2024. Here is the honest answer:

"Toronto's homeless count dropped 21% in 2025 — but the City's own outreach staff say much of that is people becoming less visible, not housed, and Ontario's total still rose 7.8%. The Toronto decline is driven mainly by fewer refugee claimants in the shelter system — a federal immigration shift, not a provincial housing policy success. Fewer tents you can see is not the same as fewer people without homes." (City SNA, Apr 2026; AMO/HelpSeeker, Jan 2026.)

The campaign's response is not to dispute the 21% figure — it is real. The response is to explain what it means and what it doesn't. Chronic homelessness in Toronto was not resolved. The shelter system remains at near-capacity. Ontario's provincial total rose 7.8% in the same period.

TIER 2: THE FIVE MOST POWERFUL ACCOUNTABILITY QUESTIONS

For candidate town halls, social media, and deputation submissions. Each question has a primary-sourced predicate.

Q1 — The AG Question "In 2021, Ontario's Auditor General said the Province needs a homelessness strategy with specific actions, targets, and timelines. That was five years ago. There is still no strategy. Will you commit to implementing AG Recommendation 1? Yes or no?"

Q2 — The OW Question "Ontario Works pays tent dwellers approximately $343/month — not $733 — because the shelter allowance requires proof of paid rent. Will you end the shelter allowance deduction for people without fixed addresses and raise OW to a living rate?"

Q3 — The COHB Question "The Province suspended COHB for seven months in 2024. Toronto's housing exits fell 26.7%. We have documented 1,583 people who should have been housed but weren't. Will you commit to permanent, full COHB funding at 2023 levels?"

Q4 — The HART Hub Question "The Province says HART Hubs are a homelessness solution. The Association of Municipalities of Ontario's own commissioned research says the ~900 supportive housing units (as of April 2026, across 29 hubs; original 2025 commitment was 375 homes across 19 hubs) (range: 375 per original AMO/2025 plan; up to 540 per current government figure) created alongside $378M investment 'are insufficient.' HART Hubs don't guarantee permanent housing. Will you require housing exit guarantees in HART Hub contracts?"

Q5 — The HSCIS Question "The City adopted a plan for 20 new permanent shelters in November 2023. It is now June 2026. Zero are open. The first opens in 2027 — four years after the commitment. The Province has not committed to funding its $166.9M share. Will you personally commit to securing that federal and provincial funding?"

TIER 3: THREE FRAMES FOR THREE AUDIENCES

Frame A — For Fiscal Conservatives: "This Is a Terrible Investment"

The shelter system costs $897.957M gross in 2025 (Toronto AG / Budget Committee 2025 ✅). Every night someone spends in shelter instead of housing costs $43,329 more per year than Housing First would. A shelter bed in 2024 served half as many people as it used to — because people have nowhere to exit to (TSSS 2024 Annual Report ✅). Ontario's own Financial Accountability Office found provincial subsidized housing spending decreased 0.1% annually in real terms from 2004 to 2025 (FAO March 2026 ✅). The system is expensive because it's structured wrong. The cheap option — Housing First — is the one the Province refuses to fund.

Frame B — For Social Justice Advocates: "This Is a Race and Rights Crisis"

The 2024 Street Needs Assessment found Black people are 58% of shelter users while making up approximately 10% of Toronto's population (2024 SNA ✅). Indigenous people are 31% of outdoor homeless while making up approximately 3% of Toronto's population (2024 SNA ✅). The 2SLGBTQ+ share of the homeless population doubled between 2021 and 2024. Ontario courts have found that Charter s.7 protects the right to shelter oneself in public spaces where no adequate alternative exists. Ontario's Safer Municipalities Act (S.O. 2025, c.5) defines a tent used as a dwelling on public land as a 'public place,' and lets police fine people up to $10,000 — or jail them up to six months — for trespass or for refusing an order to leave. It pairs criminal penalties for encampment residents with no guaranteed housing alternative — and, critically, the government did not invoke the notwithstanding clause, leaving the Act open to Charter s.7 challenge (Safer Municipalities Act, S.O. 2025, c.5 ✅). This is criminalization of survival.

Frame C — For Municipal Voters: "Your Ward. Your Council Candidate. Their Record."

The City of Toronto has committed to 20 new permanent shelters. Seven confirmed addresses span Wards 2, 3, 6, 8, 9, 18, and 20. Zero are open. First openings: 2027. All other wards: no confirmed site yet. Every sitting councillor voted on HSCIS in November 2023 (EC7.7). Find their vote. Find their public statements on their ward's site — or absence of one. Ask the question: "What have you done to move this from paper to shelter?"
