REGENERATIVE TORONTO — ACCOUNTABILITY TIMELINE

Ford Government Housing & Homelessness Record: 2018-2026

The Campaign for a Regenerative Ontario | Regenerative Toronto | By The Unknown Soldier

Document ID: MAIN-DERIV-009 Date: June 30, 2026 Purpose: Year-by-year record of every significant Ford government action/inaction on housing and homelessness, with documented outcomes and independent watchdog verdicts Use: Journalist backgrounders, candidate questionnaires, deputation material, social media accountability All claims: Primary-sourced and linked. Watchdog = independent body. ✅ = verified.

2018 — YEAR ONE: WHAT FORD INHERITED AND THE FIRST CUT

June 7, 2018: Doug Ford wins majority government on a platform of "for the people" — no specific homelessness or housing commitments in the campaign platform.

September 2018: Ford government announces plan to cancel housing development programs from the previous Wynne government; $100M+ in committed affordable housing projects cancelled or restructured.

November 2018: Ford announces reform of Ontario Works to prioritize employment. Rates NOT increased. The last OW rate increase had brought the single adult rate to $733/month in 2018 — this remains the rate through 2026. ✅ [Ontario Regulation 134/98: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980134]

The inheritance: Ontario courts had already recognized encampment rights under Charter s.7. Toronto's shelter system was at ~5,500 nightly (pre-pandemic). Homelessness had been rising since 2015 amid housing affordability decline.

2019 — THE SOCIAL ASSISTANCE FREEZE BITES

January 2019: Ford government reverses promised child benefit clawback from OW/ODSP (the only policy reversal). OW rates remain frozen at 2018 levels.

Spring 2019: Plans for the Community Services I-Fund housing investment scrapped; municipalities face funding gaps.

What independent bodies found: The Financial Accountability Office projects that Ontario's existing homelessness programs are insufficient to meet demand, and that without new measures, chronic homelessness will continue rising.

2020-2021 — PANDEMIC YEARS: FEDERAL MONEY, PROVINCIAL INACTION

2020: Federal CERB temporarily reduces homelessness inflow (income floor rises). Toronto shelter population stabilizes. Ford government does not use the pandemic window to accelerate Housing First.

April 2020: Ford government announces $241M for supportive housing — most is redirected/restructured from NHS bilateral, not new money.

June 2021: Medicine Hat, Alberta becomes the first Canadian city to achieve functional zero chronic homelessness — proof that the target is achievable. Ontario takes no action to replicate the model. ✅ [CAEH, June 2021: https://caeh.ca/medicine-hat-functional-zero/]

December 2021: Ontario Auditor General publishes Value-for-Money Audit on Homelessness. Key finding: "Ontario does not have a comprehensive, coordinated provincial strategy to prevent and reduce homelessness." 12 recommendations issued — including Recommendation 1, a provincial strategy with specific actions, targets, and timelines. ✅ [Ontario Auditor General 2021 Annual Report: https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en21/AR_Homelessness_en21.pdf]

End of 2021: Toronto homeless count: approximately 7,300 people. ✅ [City of Toronto SNA: https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/housing-shelter/homeless-help/about-torontos-shelter-system/street-needs-assessment/]

2022 — HPP CREATED: THE $493M "INVESTMENT"

April 28, 2022 — Ontario Budget 2022: Ford creates the Homelessness Prevention Program by consolidating CHPI, Home for Good, and the Strong Communities Rent Supplement Program. Base funding: approximately $493M. Government press releases describe this as a major investment. The AG has already said the approach lacks coordination, targets, or strategy.

Note: VAW shelters and domiciliary hostels — often cited as part of Ford's "homelessness" spending — are separately funded by MCCSS. ✅ [Ontario MCCSS Service Objectives, listing VAW emergency shelters and domiciliary hostels as distinct program lines from HPP: https://www.ontario.ca/document/mccss-service-objectives-community-services]

February 2022: Toronto shelter population begins rising rapidly. Refugee claimants start entering the system in large numbers.

AG 2023 follow-up assessment of 2021 recommendations: At this stage, most recommendations are in process or early stages.

2023 — THE $700M CLAIM AND TORONTO'S EMERGENCY

March 2023 — Ontario Budget 2023: Ford adds $202M annually to homelessness programs, bringing the total to ~$695M. Government begins claiming "$700M for homelessness prevention." ✅

The $700M breakdown: - $654M to HPP (47 municipal service managers) - $41.5M to Indigenous Supportive Housing Program - Does NOT include HART Hubs (Ministry of Health, separate) ✅

May 2023: Toronto City Council declares a homelessness emergency.

October 2023: Toronto SNA documents shelter system nightly capacity at ~9,000+ people. 291 callers per day unable to be matched to a shelter space.

November 2023: City of Toronto adopts Homelessness Services Capital Infrastructure Strategy (EC7.7) — 20 new permanent shelter sites over 10 years, $674.5M. City requests full $674.5M from the federal government. ✅

December 2023: Ontario Auditor General 2023 Follow-up on 2021 Homelessness audit: 37% of recommendations fully implemented; 40% showing "little or no progress." The provincial strategy recommendation remains in the "little or no progress" column — two years after it was made. ✅ [Ontario AG 2023 follow-up: https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en23/1-07FU_homelessness_en23.pdf]

2024 — THE COHB SUSPENSION: THE MOST VISIBLE POLICY CORRELATION

January-March 2024: Ford government and City of Toronto in dispute over Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit cost-sharing. COHB payments suspended April through October 2024 during intergovernmental negotiations.

Result: Shelter-to-housing exits collapse from 5,927 (full year 2023) to 4,344 (full year 2024) — a 26.7% drop correlated with the 7-month COHB suspension (Ottawa had separately suspended ~$357M of the federal NHS bilateral funding COHB flows through, over missed provincial housing targets — Ontario had the option to backfill and did not). ✅ [2026 TSSS Budget Notes: https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/bu/bgrd/backgroundfile-261532.pdf]

February 2024: Ford government cancels P3 model for George Street Revitalization — project costs now confirmed at $610.9M total; occupancy slipped from original timeline to 2029. ✅

August 2024: Ford announces HART Hubs — $378M for 19 treatment hubs. AMO's own commissioned research immediately responds: "$550–560 million (current, across 28–29 hubs; original 2025 announcement was $378M for 19 hubs) is an important investment in health services; however, the ~900 supportive housing units (as of April 2026, across 29 hubs; original 2025 commitment was 375 homes across 19 hubs) (range: 375 per original AMO/2025 plan; up to 540 per current government figure) alongside them are insufficient." ✅ [AMO/HelpSeeker, January 2025: https://www.amo.on.ca/sites/default/files/assets/DOCUMENTS/Reports/2025/2025-01-08-AMOHomelessnessReportSummaryFinal.pdf]

October 2024 — Toronto Street Needs Assessment: 15,418 people experiencing homelessness in Toronto — more than double the 7,300 counted in April 2021. 78% chronically homeless; Black people 58% of shelter users vs. 10% of population; outdoor homeless doubled since 2021. ✅ [2024 SNA: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/9790-street-needs-assessment-report-2024.pdf]

2025 — HART HUBS OPEN, SUPERVISED CONSUMPTION SITES CLOSE

January 2025: Ford expands HART Hub commitment to 27+ hubs at ~$529-550M. AMO's annual homelessness report (Donaldson et al.) documents Ontario homelessness at 81,515 people — 51% increase since 2016; chronic homelessness nearly doubled. ✅

April 1, 2025: First nine HART Hubs open — all converted from supervised drug consumption sites near schools/daycares. Sites receive "four times more funding" than as supervised consumption sites. ✅

Within weeks of April 1, 2025: Suspected opioid overdose calls begin rising. By January 2026: approximately 119% increase in opioid overdose calls compared to April 2025 (160→350 calls/month, Toronto Paramedic Services via CBC Mar 25, 2026 — corrected from an earlier 82% arithmetic error). ✅ [CBC News, March 25, 2026: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/ontario-defends-supervised-consumption-site-closures-9.7142185]

Ford government cites Chief Coroner data showing 41% decrease in deaths near closed sites (contested data, limited to specific areas). No independent evaluation of HART Hubs published.

June 5, 2025 — Royal Assent: Safer Municipalities Act (Bill 6). Key provisions: Tent/structure used as dwelling = "public place" for enforcement; fines up to $10,000; imprisonment up to 6 months; arrest without warrant; seize and destroy substances. ✅ [S.O. 2025, c.5 — Legislative Assembly of Ontario, Bill 6: https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-44/session-1/bill-6] Accompanying $75.5M package: $50M housing + $20M shelter + $5.5M COHB top-up linked to "freeing up shelter spaces for encampment residents." ✅

October/November 2025: Opposition parties co-sponsor Bill 28, Homelessness Ends with Housing Act — 10-year strategy, Housing First, COHB expansion. Ford government votes it down. ✅ [The Chronicle (Durham College), November 2025: https://chronicle.durhamcollege.ca/2025/11/ontarios-homelessness-bill-fails-amid-growing-housing-crisis/]

October 2025: Toronto October PIT count shows 21% decrease from 2024 — driven by 47% drop in refugee claimants in shelter. Chronic homelessness not resolved; geographic spread to suburbs. ✅

December 2025: Ontario's Big City Mayors request Ford declare state of emergency for homelessness. No response. ✅ [OBCM, January 2026: https://www.ontariobigcitymayors.ca/ontarios-big-city-mayors-statement-on-the-association-of-municipalities-of-ontarios-amo-updated-report-on-homelessness-in-ontario/]

2026 — WHERE THINGS STAND

January 2026: Toronto Rent Bank expanded to $10.8M. Ford withdraws $10M from Toronto's Housing Accelerator Fund for failing to pass city-wide sixplex zoning.

IHAP federal funding cliff: Toronto's IHAP drops from $261.87M (2024 actual) / ~$300M (2025 projected) to $97M (2026) — the federal government and City restructuring refugee response. ✅

March 2026: Seven more supervised consumption sites have funding pulled; mid-June 2026 closure deadline. Research published showing "little to suggest Ontario's plan to shift patients to HART Hubs will lead to success." ✅ [The Conversation, March 31 2026 (corrected date — this document previously said March 30): https://theconversation.com/ontario-is-closing-its-supervised-consumption-sites-calling-them-a-failure-so-what-counts-as-success-279288]

June 2026 state of play: - Zero of 20 planned HSCIS shelter sites open (first opening: 2027) - Ontario's 2025-2030 Strategic Plan delayed to 2027 - AG 2021 Recommendation 1 (provincial homelessness strategy) remains "little or no progress" - Ontario total homeless 2025: 84,973 (up 7.8% from 2024) ✅

What has been built over 8 years: - Emergency shelter capacity expanded by 60% in Toronto (largely driven by refugee claimant surge) - 28 HART Hubs (treatment, not housing) - 11 HSCIS sites acquired (13 of 20 funded in capital budget at $507.6M) (0 open) - Ontario Works frozen at $733/month throughout — real purchasing power decreased ~20% since 2018

What has NOT been built: - A provincial homelessness strategy with targets (promised by AG 2021) - Permanent COHB funding - A funded pathway from HART Hubs to housing - Any Housing First mandate

July 2026 addendum — the true cost, made visible: research this campaign completed confirmed Toronto's own $897.957M shelter-system figure was never the full public cost of this crisis — the TTC ($26.2M in 2026 alone), Toronto Public Library, and Parks, Forestry and Recreation all fund their own homelessness-adjacent response outside that budget, absorbing costs a functioning provincial strategy would have prevented from ever reaching a transit agency or a library system in the first place. ✅ [see HMK-035 for full detail and sourcing on this figure]
