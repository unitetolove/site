If not Toronto, then where?

Vision

Toronto belongs to the people who show up for it. We’re neighbours, not politicians — building open research, plain-language tools, and real receipts so all of us can decide our city’s future together, out loud.

What this is (and isn’t)

This is not an organization. There is nothing to join, no staff, no membership, no dues. This begins as one person and a set of ideas, published behind a library of verified civic information that anyone can check. Everything else already belongs to the city: the organizations, the halls, the facilitators, the methods, the talent, and the unhappiness — the deep, city-wide sense that we were built for something more than this.

The bet is simple: everyone is unhappy, the infrastructure already exists, and the only missing ingredient is the trigger that gets the crowd and the infrastructure into the same conversation. This site, the library beneath it, and the election ahead of us are that trigger. Nothing more — and if it works, nothing less.

The welcome count

One link, everywhere: uniteTOlove.ca. Not ten different sign-up forms, not a dozen group chats that never meet each other — one door. Voters and non-voters. Residents and the excluded. Toronto first, then the GTA, then the world — counted publicly, sliced, with the Toronto number always the headline.

Count: not yet live — the sign-up and public counter ship in a later iteration.

Who is The Unknown Soldier?

The Unknown Soldier isn’t a mystery for its own sake. It’s a neighbour, not a name — because the point is the work, not the person doing it. Judge the ideas, not the byline. Every claim we publish carries its receipts: sources, links, and the reasoning behind it, in the open, so you never have to take our word for it.
