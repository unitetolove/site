We can do this, Toronto, together.

Toronto will decide its future over the next few years — with us or without us. Everything on this site exists to make it with us.

The stakes are plain. This fall is Toronto’s last municipal election before 2030 — and by then, the biggest decisions of our lifetimes, about AI, housing, climate, and how we govern ourselves, will largely be made. If we don’t decide the future we want for ourselves, it will be decided for us. So this project asks for exactly one thing — not your money, never your money: your time and your attention.

The way there is simple to say. Help the whole city learn how it actually works — that is the Library. Then talk — everywhere — until ten thousand small conversations grow into one big one. Agree on a plan. Put it to every candidate, identically. And spend the year after the election building it, together. The whole plan, honestly told.

The idea

Unite Toronto behind becoming the world’s most abundant, regenerative city — a city of enough, for everyone, forever — leading Ontario and the world.

A city deciding its own future is just work — and every problem this city has, someone somewhere has already solved. The answer has never been left or right. The answer is down, then up: neighbours first, then blocks, then the whole city pointing at the same goals at the same time. We overcome fear the only way anyone ever has — by seeking understanding, and by doing together.

One citizen started this and stays out of the story on purpose: the work is signed The Unknown Soldier so the ideas have to stand on their own. What one person can honestly do is this: inspire · inform · connect · equip · guide · hand off · exit. That last word is a promise, not a flourish. If you want the why anyway, read the letter of introduction — then forget the author and keep the tools.

The first ask — thirty seconds

Sign up for the conversation. A name and a way to reach you — that’s all. No membership, no party, no meeting you must attend, no donation asked anywhere, ever. Anyone may sign: voters and residents, the permanent resident who can’t vote, the student, the commuter, the teenager with a parent’s blessing, and the diaspora watching home. The ballot has a citizenship requirement; the conversation does not. Your signature moves a public number the whole city can watch — proof we’re showing up. Every step past that is optional, and every one returns a receipt you can check.

The elections at our door

First: Beaches–East York elects a Member of Parliament on Monday, August 31, 2026 — two weeks from now, and the closest thing this project has to home ground. We took the riding apart at street level: the full riding portrait — every candidate, the geography, the census, the 311 record, the media landscape — plus six data stories and an interactive atlas, every figure carrying its source, its date, and a download of the data behind it. If you live there, start with the mutual-aid directory — the help map we built because nobody else had.

Then Scarborough Southwest elects a Member of Provincial Parliament on Thursday, September 3, 2026 — separate riding, separate ballot, separate rules, same treatment: its own riding portrait, and its own chapter in the community data — the same depth, three days later. The other 24 wards each have a profile of their own, growing toward October.

Then the big one. In October 2026, Ontario elects every municipal council in the province — every mayor, every councillor, every school trustee, all on one day. SOTE 2026 — the State of the Election is the public scoreboard: who’s running, what they promise, and the receipts — serving every candidate in every race on identical terms, endorsing nobody, ever. We keep it at two scales:

Toronto 2026 25 wards The city where this record began: the field, the platforms, and the raw math of every council race. Ontario 2026 2,305 races The same instrument, applied province-wide — 3,396 seats to fill across 415 municipalities and school-board seats.

If the empty ballot lines bother you too: you could run — and this site equips any candidate, of any politics, for free. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. Either way, here’s how to vote.

The plan

What could Toronto do in one year, all-out? What future would we pick if we picked one on purpose? The Plan holds the whole answer in one place — the vision, the strategy, the mobilization, and how the pieces fit — written to be checked, argued with, improved, and forked. It is a working draft on purpose: the city finishes it, or it isn’t finished.

The proof

Before this project asked anyone for a minute of their time, it did the homework. The Library is the receipts room: flagship investigations — ending homelessness, why Toronto can’t move, surveillance pricing — alongside issue backgrounders across the city’s hardest questions, the Government Atlas, futures research, and Permanent Culture 3.0, the regenerative-systems research underneath it all. Every claim carries a receipt. When we’re wrong, we say so in public and leave the correction visible.

Something you can do today

No committees, no waiting. Act is the list of things a person can actually do — starting with thirty seconds, scaling up as far as you want to go. The fastest one: The Harvest — bring us what your corner of the city knows, and it becomes part of the commons.

Find your people

Toronto is a city of tribes — hundreds of communities, congregations, clubs, diasporas, fandoms and crews, each already organized, each already caring about something. Connect maps them, and holds our research on how cities actually talk: assemblies, consensus tools, and the platforms where the conversation already lives. Find your tribe, or put yours on the map.

End homelessness first

A movement that claims a city can do anything should prove it on the thing everyone calls impossible. Ours is ending homelessness in Toronto: the full book — the problem, the history, the costs, who has actually done it, and what it would take here — with the evidence shown. This is the first flagship. The method that built it is the method for everything else.
