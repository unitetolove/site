Ministry of Energy and Mines - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-energy-mines (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Ministry's current enabling legislation is the Ministry of Energy Act, 2011, S.O. 2011, c. 9, Sched. 25 (WebSearch/Wikipedia-sourced citation; Ontario Wikipedia entry on the Ministry, cross-referenced with the confirmed repeal record below). ⚠️ Still being checked: the specific ontario.ca/laws e-Laws page for this Act could not be located/resolved via WebFetch this review. What is directly verified is that the predecessor statute, the Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.23, was repealed effective June 6, 2011 per 2011, c. 9, Sched. 27, ss. 32, 42 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m23, verified), consistent with the 2011 recreation of the Ministry under a standalone Act.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Ministry sets the regulatory framework for electricity pricing and price-mitigation policy, supports energy efficiency/conservation and clean-technology programs, works with Indigenous partners and sector stakeholders on energy policy, and oversees mineral-resource development, geoscience/mining information systems, and Ring of Fire mineral-development support (ontario.ca Ministry overview, https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-energy-and-mines; registry row on-ministry-energy-mines).

Governance & reporting line

Headed by a Minister with a Deputy Minister as deputy head of the public-service ministry, reporting through Cabinet to the Legislative Assembly in the usual Westminster-model fashion; the Ministry oversees energy-sector agencies including the Independent Electricity System Operator and Ontario Energy Board via statute and ministerial directive (ontario.ca Ministry overview, https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-energy-and-mines; registry row on-ministry-energy-mines).

Budget scale

$8,943.0M total planned expenditures for 2025-26 ($7,142.8M operating + $1,800.1M capital), of which $6,463.0M is Electricity Price Mitigation spending; down 3.3% from $9,247.6M in 2024-25 estimates, largely reflecting reduced price-mitigation costs (ontario.ca Ministry of Energy and Mines published plans and annual reports, 2025-2026, https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-energy-and-mines).

Institutional history

Traces to the 1959 Department of Energy Resources; renamed and reorganized repeatedly — Department of Energy and Resources Management (1964), folded into Department of the Environment (1971), re-established as Ministry of Energy (1973), merged as Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure (2008-2010, repealed enabling Act 2011), reverted to Ministry of Energy (2010-2024, per the Ministry of Energy Act, 2011), styled Ministry of Energy and Electrification (June 2024-March 2025), and currently Ministry of Energy and Mines (March 2025-present) (WebSearch/Wikipedia summary of Ministry history, cross-checked against the confirmed 2011 repeal of R.S.O. 1990, c. M.23, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m23; registry url_status flagged ⚠️ still being checked, notes "no current plan located").

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is the entire mines and minerals mandate, absent from the two earlier annual reports and appearing fully only after the ministry's March 2025 rename to "Ministry of Energy and Mines," bringing a new Mines and Minerals Program, Ring of Fire development, and a Critical Minerals Processing Fund ($500M). Backgrounder summary

The Ministry (current name Ministry of Energy and Mines, "MEM") sets Ontario's legislative and policy framework for electricity pricing, price-mitigation, energy efficiency/conservation, clean-technology programs, and — since the March 2025 rename — mineral-resource development, geoscience/mining information, and Ring of Fire mineral-development support. It oversees the Independent Electricity System Operator (IESO) and Ontario Energy Board (OEB) and reports Government Business Enterprise income from Hydro One and Ontario Power Generation (OPG). Backgrounder-stated 2025-26 planned expenditure is $8,943.0M total ($7,142.8M operating + $1,800.1M capital), down 3.3% from $9,247.6M in 2024-25 estimates. The Ministry's name and mandate scope have changed repeatedly since a 1959 origin, most recently: Ministry of Energy (2010-2024) → Ministry of Energy and Electrification (June 2024-March 2025) → Ministry of Energy and Mines (March 2025-present).

Priority evolution

1998-99 (bp-1999, 701357a0085e): Unreadable — see Residuals & gaps. No priority content recoverable from this review.

2023-24 plan / 2022-23 results (ar-2024, 5ffa0ebe8a2e), Ministry of Energy: Framed around a single "affordable, reliable energy system" strategic plan with no separately labelled strategic-priority list (that tabulated format appears starting ar-2025). Headline 2022-23 results theme is nuclear refurbishment progress (Darlington and Bruce units) plus continued Pickering operation through September 2026, alongside the first Ontario SMR construction-licence application (OPG to CNSC, Oct 2022) and a Dec 2022 groundbreaking for the Darlington SMR with Premier Ford and Minister Smith. The IESO's Dec 15, 2022 "Pathways to Decarbonization" study is reported, projecting 69,000 MW of new non-emitting supply and $375-425B in capital cost to meet 2050 demand. The Electrification and Energy Transition Panel (EETP) is disclosed as newly established, expected to report by end of 2023.

2024-25 plan / 2023-24 results (ar-2025, 23d253fb9f94), Ministry of Energy: First appearance of the tabulated "Strategic priority / Description / Intended outcomes / Discussion / Key programs and initiatives" reporting structure, organized under five headings: Generation and Procurement, Transmission and Distribution, Energy Efficiency, Integrated Energy Planning, Electricity price mitigation, and Reducing Red Tape. New organizing document named: "Powering Ontario's Growth: Ontario's Plan for a Clean Energy Future" (released July 2023), described as the Ministry's pragmatic long-term plan to 2050. The EETP's final report, "Ontario's Clean Energy Opportunity," is reported delivered in January 2024 with 29 recommendations. Bill 165, the "Keeping Energy Costs Down Act, 2024," is introduced (Feb 2024). Darlington Unit 3 refurbishment completed 169 days ahead of schedule (July 2023); Bruce Unit 6 completed ahead of schedule (Sept 2023).

2025-26 plan / 2024-25 results (ar-2026, 24cb3491e36c), Ministry of Energy and Mines: Organizational rename to "Ministry of Energy and Mines" (MEM) reflected throughout; a new "Mines and Minerals Program" and "Supporting a strong and sustainable mineral development sector" priority stream is added, absent from both prior reports. The Ministry's core planning document is superseded: "Ontario's Affordable Energy Future: The Pressing Case for More Power" (Oct 2024) launches consultation toward "Energy for Generations: Ontario's Integrated Plan to Power the Strongest Economy in the G7" — Ontario's first Integrated Energy Plan (IEP), enabled by the Affordable Energy Act, 2024 (Royal Assent Dec 2024), with a 25-year planning horizon and a 5-year plan-refresh cycle. A new "Broadband and Cellular Infrastructure Program" is also present as a distinct program area (traceable to at least ar-2026; not present as a named program in ar-2024/ar-2025 text read this review — ⚠️ still being checked whether it existed under a different label earlier). Electricity demand growth framing sharpens: "for the first time since 2005" demand is rising (ar-2025 framing) to a forecast 75% growth by 2050 (ar-2026 framing). New large-scale nuclear predevelopment work is disclosed at OPG's Wesleyville site (up to 10,000 MW). A new Critical Minerals Processing Fund ($500M) and continuing Critical Minerals Innovation Fund ($25M, plus $5M over two years starting 2025-26) are reported under the new mines mandate, alongside continuing Ring of Fire all-season-road and Resource Revenue Sharing programs.

  • Renamed — reporting structure: ar-2024 (5ffa0ebe8a2e) uses free-form section narrative ("2023-24 Strategic plan," lettered subsections A/B/C); ar-2025 (23d253fb9f94) and ar-2026 (24cb3491e36c) both adopt the tabulated "Strategic priority / Description / Intended outcomes / Discussion / Key programs and initiatives" format, with ar-2026 additionally adding a formal "ENERGY-related KPIs" and "MINES-related KPIs" indicator table (target value vs. current value) not present in either prior report.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is an Ontario provincial ministry; its full mandate is province-wide and its programs are described throughout in explicitly Ontario-scoped terms. Toronto-specific references found in the archived text: the Ministry's 12-year Energy Efficiency Framework is forecast to deliver "3,000 MW of peak demand savings, equivalent to 70% of the summer peak electricity demand for the entire City of Toronto" (ar-2026, 24cb3491e36c). Toronto Hydro is named as one of the utilities required to offer the Ultra-Low Overnight price plan from its May 2023 launch (ar-2024, 5ffa0ebe8a2e). On the mines side, ar-2026 states Toronto is "the mining finance capital of the world," with 37% of global mining equity capital raised in 2024 coming from the Toronto Stock Exchange (TSX) and TSX Venture Exchange, and approximately 1,100 mining companies listed on those two exchanges (24cb3491e36c) — this is a substantive, Toronto-specific claim tied to the new mines mandate.
