City of Markham - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: mark-city-of-markham (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Markham is a lower-tier municipality incorporated as a body corporate under s. 4(1) of the Municipal Act, 2001, S.O. 2001, c. 25 (consolidated text verified at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/01m25, fetched 2026-07-30, HTTP 200). Its change from Town to City status was effected by municipal By-law 2012- under s. 187(1) of that Act (authority to change name), with s. 187(4)-(5) confirming the name change does not alter its status as a lower-tier municipality or affect its rights/obligations (By-law 2012-, "A by-law to change the name of The Corporation of the Town of Markham to The Corporation of the City of Markham," fetched and confirmed directly 2026-07-30, https://www2.markham.ca/markham/ccbs/indexfile/Agendas/2012/Council/cl120529/by%20-%20City%20Status%20for%20Markham.pdf).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Markham is a lower-tier city within upper-tier York Region (registry note, mark-city-of-markham, this library's government-document registry), delivering local services under the general municipal-purposes provisions of the Municipal Act, 2001. City status took effect July 1, 2012, with the same council members continuing as city councillors (Markham By-law 2012-, fetched 2026-07-30).

Governance & reporting line

Markham is led by an elected Mayor and Council; its current strategic direction is "Building Markham's Future Together 2020-2026" (document id mark-city-of-markham-sp-2026, https://www.markham.ca/sites/default/files/about-city-markham/BMFT+2020-2026+Strategic+Plan+Summary+AODA.pdf). Council and committee meetings are the primary decision-making venue (https://www.markham.ca/about-the-city-of-markham/city-hall/council-and-committee-meetings, registry source authority).

Budget scale

~$296.2M 2025 tax-levy-supported Operating Budget, excluding Waterworks and Building (City of Markham 2025 Budget document, confirmed at multiple points including "The 2025 Operating Budget for City services totals $296.2M excluding, Waterworks, Building", document id mark-city-of-markham-bud-2025, https://www.markham.ca/sites/default/files/2025-06/City-of-Markham-2025-Budget-GFOA.pdf, fetched and extracted 2026-07-30). Total Capital Budget requests for 2025 were $178,989,400 (same document).

Institutional history

Incorporated as the Town of Markham in 1971 and changed to City of Markham status effective July 1, 2012 via municipal by-law under s. 187 of the Municipal Act, 2001 (Markham By-law 2012-, fetched 2026-07-30; 1971 incorporation date per search context, ⚠️ still being checked 1971 date against a primary source not confirmed this review).

City of Markham - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 / registry: mark-city-of-markham / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest structural addition is Ontario's Strong Mayors, Building Homes Act, 2022, extended to Markham's mayor June 16, 2023, which replaced the Council Budget Committee process with mayoral budget authority — the 2025 budget was tabled by the Mayor and deemed adopted with no Council amendments. The most consequential quiet change is the complete displacement, sometime between 2015 and 2020, of the original six/seven-item issue-based priority list (Growth Management, Transportation, etc.) by a four-goal corporate framework created in 2013 — no document states the older list's retirement. The most load-bearing number is the 2025 total budget of $675.2 million (Operating $296.2M, Capital $178.9M), up from $316.2M in 2010. One open question the record cannot resolve: the exact year the four-goal framework became the sole organizing structure, since no budget documents exist for 2016-2019 or 2021-2024 to pin it down.

Backgrounder summary

Markham is a lower-tier municipality in York Region (GTA), incorporated as a body corporate under the Municipal Act, 2001. It changed from Town to City status effective July 1, 2012 by municipal by-law under s. 187 of the Act. Its current strategic direction is "Building Markham's Future Together 2020-2026" (BMFT), and its 2025 tax-levy-supported Operating Budget was ~$296.2M (excluding Waterworks and Building), with a 2025 Capital Budget of $178,989,400. The backgrounder flags the 1971 incorporation date as unverified against a primary source.

Priority evolution

2010 (bud-2010, a3300fad03d9; fs-2010, 1dde6c74a356), Town of Markham: Both documents' cover pages read "Town of Markham," confirming pre-city-status framing. Council and senior staff had identified six strategic priorities in 2007, confirmed through the "Click with Markham" consultation (over 5,000 respondents): Growth Management, Transportation/Transit, Environment, Municipal Services, Parks/Recreation/Culture/Library Master Plan and Public Safety, and Diversity — all organized under the "Building Markham's Future Together" (BMFT) strategic direction, alongside a separately named "Greenprint" Community Sustainability Plan (four pillars: Healthy Environment, Economic Vitality, Social Equity, Vibrant Culture), then in draft and slated for Council adoption in 2011 (a3300fad03d9; 1dde6c74a356). The Town delivered a zero percent tax increase for three consecutive years (2009-2011) (1dde6c74a356). The "Excellence through Efficiency and Effectiveness" (E3) continuous-improvement program was already operating, with $8.5M in cumulative expenditure savings/revenue enhancements captured in 2009-2010 (1dde6c74a356). The Town approved a total 2010 budget of $316.2 million (Operating $153.8M; Capital $77.9M; Building Standards $7.2M; Engineering $5.1M; Planning & Design $4.9M; Waterworks $67.3M) (a3300fad03d9). The Town had been debt-free since 2002 and operated a "Pay As You Go" fiscal policy (1dde6c74a356).

2015 (bud-2015, 977a9f1f7dbf; fs-2015, 0fa1a808c846), City of Markham: Both documents' cover pages now read "The Corporation of the City of Markham," internally corroborating the Town-to-City status change the backgrounder dates to July 1, 2012. Population was reported as approximately 336,000 in the budget's executive summary and "more than 350,000" in the annual report's mayor's message — two contemporaneous 2015 documents giving different rounded figures (977a9f1f7dbf; 0fa1a808c846). The old issue-based priority list had grown to seven strategic priorities, still keyed to the "2007-2010 Council term": Growth Management, Transportation, Environment and Sustainability, Municipal Services, Integrated Leisure Master Plan/Public Safety, Diversity, and — newly formalized as a distinct, named priority — "Markham 2020 – Economic Development Strategy" (977a9f1f7dbf). "Parks, Recreation, Culture, Library Master Plan and Public Safety" (2010's phrasing) had been renamed "Integrated Leisure Master Plan/Public Safety" by 2015 (a3300fad03d9; 977a9f1f7dbf). Separately and significantly, in the third quarter of 2013 the Executive Leadership Team created a new, higher-level framework of four corporate goals — Exceptional Services by Exceptional People; Engaged, Diverse and Thriving City; Safe and Sustainable Community; Stewardship of Money and Resources — piloted in the 2014 business-planning cycle and rolled out corporate-wide for 2015, running in parallel with the older seven-priority list within this same document (977a9f1f7dbf). The City approved a total 2015 budget of $426.5 million (Operating $185.9M; Library $13.4M; Capital $102.2M; Waterworks $101.8M; Planning & Design $8.2M; Engineering $6.6M; Building Standards $8.5M), with a 2.50% tax increase (2.34% for operations, 0.16% as year 2 of a 3-year phased recovery charge tied to the December 2013 ice storm) (977a9f1f7dbf). E3 cumulative savings reached $22 million since 2009 (0fa1a808c846). Markham hosted the 2015 Pan Am and Parapan Am Games (badminton, table tennis, para table tennis, water polo) at the new Markham Pan Am Centre, and in May 2015 the Ontario government approved a new York University campus in Markham Centre (0fa1a808c846; 977a9f1f7dbf). Growth mandate: the Province and York Region required Markham to plan for a population of approximately 422,000 by 2031, a further 90,000 people over twenty years (977a9f1f7dbf).

2020 (bud-2020, f5854f8c9c3f; fs-2020, 896e6a1458c1), BMFT 2020-2023: The four-goal framework created in 2013 is now the sole organizing strategic architecture — the older six/seven-item issue-based priority list is absent from both 2020 documents. The four goals are restated with revised wording: Exceptional Services by Exceptional People; Engaged, Diverse, Thriving and Vibrant City (adds "Vibrant" relative to the 2013/2015 wording); Safe, Sustainable and Complete Community (adds "Complete," drops "City"); Stewardship of Money and Resources (f5854f8c9c3f). Population exceeded 350,000, making Markham the fourth-most-populous GTA municipality after Toronto, Mississauga and Brampton, with population growth of over 50,000 since 2009 (f5854f8c9c3f). 2019 marked the 225th anniversary of Markham's 1794 founding (225 bur oak trees planted to mark it) (f5854f8c9c3f). The Markham Innovation Exchange (MiX), announced in 2019, designated roughly 1,900 acres of private and city-owned land for an innovation district (f5854f8c9c3f). The City approved a total 2020 budget of $522.0 million (Operating $240.9M; Capital $111.5M; Waterworks $140.7M; Planning & Design $10.6M; Engineering $8.8M; Building Standards $9.5M), a 1.95% tax increase (f5854f8c9c3f). Provincial Bill 108 (More Homes, More Choice Act, royal assent June 6, 2019) began reshaping development-charge and community-benefit-charge structures; a 2019 Regional Government Review of eight regional municipalities including York Region concluded with no significant 2020-budget impact; Markham's new Official Plan (adopted by Council December 2013, approved by York Region June 2014) had its city-wide policies substantially confirmed by the Ontario Municipal Board in 2018 (f5854f8c9c3f). E3 cumulative savings reached $29.4M-$31.0M since 2009 across the two 2020 documents (f5854f8c9c3f; 896e6a1458c1). The COVID-19 pandemic is documented only in the annual report (fs-2020): a comprehensive relief package including property-tax payment deferral, a 2021 property-tax freeze, and Markham's "second digital-only" annual report (896e6a1458c1). fs-2020 also states, "In 2021, we are marking our 50th anniversary since incorporation as a municipal government" — this corroborates, from within the archived series itself, the 1971 incorporation date the backgrounder flagged as unverified (896e6a1458c1).

2025-2026 (bud-2025, e0fe3c697450; fs-2025, aabffa621bed; sp-2026, b7f227fe7c30), BMFT 2020-2026: The Strategic Plan was revised and extended from "2020-2023" to "2020-2026" and officially adopted by Council in April 2024, following a 2023 community survey (2,000+ combined responses across 2019 and 2023 rounds) (b7f227fe7c30). The same four goals persist with 2020-era wording (Exceptional Services by Exceptional People; Engaged, Diverse, Thriving & Vibrant City; Safe, Sustainable & Complete Community; Stewardship of Money & Resources), now with net-zero-by-2050 GHG reduction made an explicit, named target under Goal 3, and Truth and Reconciliation Commission Calls to Action and Indigenous partnerships named explicitly under Goal 2 — neither is present in the 2010/2015-era documents read (b7f227fe7c30). Population exceeded 370,000, the largest of nine York Region municipalities; 2024 year-end population was 363,622 (e0fe3c697450). The most consequential mandate change in the series: Ontario's Strong Mayors, Building Homes Act, 2022 (enacted November 23, 2022, tied to the province's goal of 1.5 million homes by 2031) was extended to Markham's mayor as of June 16, 2023, first applied to the FY2024 budget and continued for FY2025 — replacing the formal Budget Committee process with a Mayor-tables/Council-this library's internal records sequence within a 55-day statutory window (e0fe3c697450; aabffa621bed). The 2025 Budget was tabled by the Mayor October 28, 2024 and deemed adopted November 27, 2024 with no Council amendments (e0fe3c697450; aabffa621bed). The City approved a total 2025 budget of $675.2 million (Operating $296.2M; Capital $178.9M; Waterworks $154.3M; Planning & Design $17.3M; Engineering $12.7M; Building Standards $15.8M) — matching the backgrounder's Operating and Capital figures exactly — with a 3.88% tax increase (e0fe3c697450; aabffa621bed). Provincial housing mandate: build 44,000 homes in Markham over the next 8 years; Bill 23 (More Homes Built Faster Act, November 28, 2022) restructured the Development Charges Act to limit developer funding of growth, and Bill 185 (2024) further amended DC phase-in rules (e0fe3c697450). The Yonge North Subway Extension (YNSE) — an 8-kilometre, five-station extension — appears in this series for the first time as a named, actively-coordinated shared Markham/Province transportation priority; it is absent from the 2020 budget document (e0fe3c697450; b7f227fe7c30). E3 cumulative savings reached $37.2 million since 2009 (aabffa621bed). Markham launched "Millie the butterfly," described as Canada's first municipal mascot, in 2025 (aabffa621bed).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Every sampled document frames Markham as "centrally located within the Greater Toronto Area" and situates it within York Region, directly adjacent to Toronto (a3300fad03d9; 977a9f1f7dbf; f5854f8c9c3f; e0fe3c697450). Beyond that general geographic framing, the archived series documents several concrete Toronto ties:

  • Direct fiscal/operational dependency: Markham purchases water from the City of Toronto via York Region, and this cost was the majority driver of Markham's 2025 water-rate increase (3.05%, an annual increase of $31.51 for the average home) (e0fe3c697450).
  • Transit infrastructure integration: the Yonge North Subway Extension — an 8-kilometre, 5-station extension connecting into Toronto's Yonge subway network — is a named, actively-coordinated shared Markham/Province transportation priority in the two most recent documents (e0fe3c697450; b7f227fe7c30); sp-2026 additionally names GO Train service improvements and the Highway 7 / 407 Transitway corridor as transit priorities requiring coordination beyond Markham's borders (b7f227fe7c30).
  • Regional utility integration: the 2015 annual report documents the merger of Enersource, Horizon Utilities and PowerStream (with the joint purchase of Hydro One Brampton) into what became Alectra Inc. — a GTA-wide electricity-distribution consolidation Markham's municipally-owned enterprise (Markham Enterprises Corporation) participated in as a shareholder (0fa1a808c846; also referenced fs-2020, 896e6a1458c1; fs-2025, aabffa621bed).
  • Comparative provincial policy positioning: fs-2025 cites a 2024 C.D. Howe Institute fiscal-transparency report ranking Markham "the highest ranked municipality in the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area, and the second-ranked in the Province of Ontario" — a direct third-party comparison against Toronto and other GTHA peers reported within Markham's own document (aabffa621bed).
  • Shared provincial governance instrument: the Strong Mayors, Building Homes Act, 2022 was extended to a designated list of twenty-six Ontario municipalities including both Markham and Toronto — the same legislative mechanism applies to both, reflecting shared provincial oversight rather than a direct municipal-to-municipal relationship (e0fe3c697450).
  • Historical rail linkage: the 1871 Toronto and Nipissing Railway (with a station in Markham) is credited in bud-2025's historical background section with reorienting Markham's economy from agricultural production toward commuting and supply relationships with Toronto — legacy context, not a current-operations claim (e0fe3c697450).

⚠️ Still being checked: the archived series contains no evidence of Markham operating any facility, office, or service delivery point located within the City of Toronto's own municipal boundary. Toronto relevance documented here is regional/adjacent — a shared labour market, a shared regional government (York Region), shared provincial mandates, and a physical utility dependency on Toronto's water system — not direct territorial overlap.
