Bank of Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-bank-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Bank of Canada is established by the Bank of Canada Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2, confirmed as current to 2026-06-14 on Justice Laws (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/B-2/). Its strategic mandate spans monetary policy (price stability), financial-system oversight, currency issuance and payments infrastructure, and economic research (The Bank of Canada's 2025–27 Strategic Plan Framework, https://www.bankofcanada.ca/about/governance-documents/the-bank-of-canadas-2025-27-strategic-plan/the-bank-of-canadas-2025-27-strategic-plan-framework/).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Bank conducts monetary policy to maintain price stability, promotes a stable and efficient financial system, issues and manages Canada's bank notes, provides funds-management and payments infrastructure, and produces economic research and analysis informing policy (https://www.bankofcanada.ca/about/governance-documents/the-bank-of-canadas-2025-27-strategic-plan/the-bank-of-canadas-2025-27-strategic-plan-framework/; registry id: fed-bank-canada).

Governance & reporting line

The Bank is a special federal Crown corporation led by the Governor (currently Tiff Macklem, appointed June 3, 2020 for a seven-year term), who serves as CEO and chairs both the Board of Directors and the Governing Council; the Senior Deputy Governor (Carolyn Rogers, appointed December 15, 2021) shares monetary-policy responsibility and sits on the Board (https://www.bankofcanada.ca/about/). It is structurally independent: the Deputy Minister of Finance attends Board discussions but cannot vote, and the Bank sets policy within a jointly agreed monetary-policy framework with the Government of Canada (https://www.bankofcanada.ca/about/).

Budget scale

Total operating expenditures of $740 million for fiscal year 2025; total assets of approximately $240.5 billion (~$240,522 million) as of December 31, 2025 (Bank of Canada Annual Report 2025, https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2026/04/annual-report-2025/).

Institutional history

Established in 1935 as a privately held institution and converted into a special federal Crown corporation in 1938 (https://www.bankofcanada.ca/about/).

Bank of Canada - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 / registry: fed-bank-canada / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is retail payments supervision, signalled in 2019, given statutory basis by the Retail Payment Activities Act (2021), and formalized as the Bank's fifth core function by 2023, becoming operational in September 2025 with roughly 1,600 payment service providers registered. The biggest and most notable new element is a 15% core-operating-expense reduction commitment by end-2028, tied to the federal government's Comprehensive Expenditure Review — the first austerity commitment named anywhere in this thirty-year series, appearing only in the final (2025) report. The most load-bearing number is FY2025 total assets of approximately $240.5 billion against $740 million in operating expenditures, with the policy rate cut four times (100 basis points) during 2025 in response to US tariffs and trade volatility. One open question: whether central bank digital currency (Digital Canadian Dollar) research continued, paused, or was shelved after a November 2023 consultation report — it's absent from the 2024 and 2025 text, but both captures are too thin or narrowly scoped to confirm discontinuation.

Backgrounder summary

The Bank of Canada operates under the Bank of Canada Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2, with a mandate spanning monetary policy (price stability), financial-system oversight, currency issuance and payments infrastructure, and economic research. It is a special federal Crown corporation, established in 1935 as a privately held institution and converted to Crown-corporation status in 1938. It is led by the Governor as CEO and chair of both the Board of Directors and the Governing Council (currently Tiff Macklem, appointed 2020) and a Senior Deputy Governor (Carolyn Rogers, appointed 2021); the Deputy Minister of Finance sits on the Board without a vote, and the Bank sets policy within a jointly agreed framework with the Government of Canada. Fiscal year 2025 operating expenditures were $740 million, with total assets of approximately $240.5 billion as of December 31, 2025.

Priority evolution

1996-2006 (386a63ce52fe through da4fab24d6c7, 11 documents): No institutional content is recoverable — see Residuals & gaps.

2007 (bcc49c25ceb2): "Sharply increased volatility in financial and foreign exchange markets" and widening credit spreads through 2007, with inflation still close to the 2 percent target. The text is framed as the outgoing Governor's "final Annual Report message" and reviews seven ongoing challenges from his term, though the departing Governor is not named in the extracted text.

2008 (7c834c59bd51): "The financial turmoil that began mid-2007 deteriorated into a full-blown global financial crisis through 2008," with an "increasing impact by year's end" on the financial system and economy despite the "resilience and soundness" of Canadian institutions.

2009 (c5f6f5f2ded6): "A year of financial market strains and economic disruption," with initial signs of progress by mid-2010 (the report covers 2009 but was released in the Bank's 75th-anniversary year).

2010 (f0fc234e8142): "The economic recovery became firmly entrenched."

2011 (372f1898556e): "A challenging year for the global economy. Amid global turbulence, Canada's macroeconomic policy framework has stood us well."

2012 (c5552075a262): "Tentative recovery for the world economy"; the Bank frames its role as working "to promote the economic and financial well-being of Canadians."

2013 (0d57aa2d0b17): "A challenging year... Inflation continued to drift below target, and the economy failed to move onto a more sustainable track."

2014 (0111576387cd): "Inflation remained very close to target, the economy grew at a reasonable pace." First explicit naming of "Governor Stephen S. Poloz" in the extracted series.

2015 (cb3818e3d5a7): Minimal content beyond confirming Governor Poloz's continued tenure and a routine account of 2015 management and activities.

2016 (be3aee58d495): First year with a structured "Bank of Canada Mandate and Planning Framework" section in the extracted text. 2016 marked "the 25th anniversary of the monetary policy framework"; the inflation-targeting agreement with the Government of Canada was renewed, maintaining the 2 percent total-CPI-inflation target "until 2021." The Bank names a recurring "medium-term plan (MTP)," set every three years, as its planning instrument. The Bank ran a public consultation to select an "iconic Canadian woman" for the next bank-note series (the resulting $10 note, released 2018, featured Viola Desmond per later reports). Financial-system work is framed around "ongoing reforms to address weaknesses identified during the 2007-09 global financial crisis."

2017 (b22cd5df6be1): Growth of "some 3 per cent, the highest in the G7," and "more than 400,000 new jobs." The Bank's mandate is quoted directly from the Bank of Canada Act for the first time in this series: "to promote the economic and financial welfare of Canada." The Bank "kicked off its five-year research agenda leading up to the next renewal of the inflation-targeting agreement... in 2021," including a 50-participant external workshop. The Financial System Review (FSR) is described as biannual, newly using loan-level microdata.

2018 (e085afcb81ae): The policy rate was raised three times during the year as monetary stimulus was reduced. New mortgage-finance policies and continued regulatory reform are cited as strengthening financial-system resilience. 2018 was "the first full year employees were back" in the renovated Wellington Street head office, and it closed out the 2016-18 MTP cycle.

2019 (e56ab6275ce3): The richest single document in the series (Governor Poloz's final Annual Report before his seven-year term ended). The Bank opened a new Calgary Operational Site in 2019 "to enhance the resilience of its market and banking operations." "In focus" items introduced for the first time: digitalization and the Bank, modernizing Canada's payment systems, enhancing operational resilience, researching a central bank digital currency (CBDC), communicating with Canadians, and the economics of climate change. The 2019-21 MTP, titled "Leading in the New Era," is described in detail: the Bank's resource plan splits into "core operations, investments, and new mandates," with a stable operational envelope of "1,500 people (plus or minus 1 percent)" and a "2 percent cap on annual expenditure growth." Three new priorities are explicitly named as driving that MTP: (1) a next-generation macroeconomic forecasting model, (2) a concrete CBDC contingency plan, and (3) advancing climate-change analysis — with "more than 30 positions" reallocated within the 1,500-person cap to fund them. Retail-payments-system oversight is flagged as "expected to happen in 2020," the first explicit signal of what became the Retail Payment Activities Act mandate. The Bank published its first fully digital Financial System Review in 2019.

2020 (053442271a47): Only a thin navigation/table-of-contents capture survives. Section headers confirm "In focus" themes of "Extraordinary operations," "Diversity and inclusion," "Digitalization and data," and "Climate change," and the tagline "DYNAMIC | ENGAGED | TRUSTED" appears for the first time in this exact form. The Governor's foreword opens: "The year 2020 was like no other," noting the year began with unemployment near an all-time low, inflation near 2 percent, and the policy rate at 1¾ percent, before the Bank found itself "far from home" a year later — the only substantive content recovered for this year.

2021 (c6aa43709b7c, annual report; 0da9ea8dc724, Monetary Policy Framework Renewal, December 2021): The annual report's "In focus" items: "Renewing the Bank's monetary policy framework" (renewed for 2022-2026, on the regular five-year cycle); "Supervising retail payments in Canada" under the newly assented Retail Payment Activities Act (Royal Assent June 2021); "Preparing for a central bank digital currency"; and "Advancing the Bank's climate change work." The companion Monetary Policy Framework Renewal background paper is the most detailed policy document in the series: it confirms the 2 percent inflation target and 1-to-3 percent control range were retained, but the renewed agreement explicitly commits the Bank to "continue to support maximum sustainable employment" alongside price stability, describing well-anchored inflation expectations as "critical to achieving both." The paper documents that the Bank tested a "horse race" of alternative frameworks — average inflation targeting, a dual mandate, price-level targeting, and NGDP targeting — against the status quo, informed by public consultations conducted in 2019-2020, and concluded that flexible inflation targeting, augmented by explicit employment-sensitivity language, outperformed the alternatives. The renewed framework also commits the Bank to "develop the modelling tools needed to take into account the important implications of climate change."

2022 (72f59f7e3dd1): "Inflation started the year at just under 5% and rose to 8% in June, before falling to just over 6% by the end of the year." The Bank "responded forcefully... by undertaking the most rapid tightening cycle in Canada's history." "Retail payments supervision" now appears as its own standing report section (alongside the four legacy functions), and "Enhancing stakeholder engagement" and "Advancing the Bank of Canada's climate change work" continue as In Focus items.

2023 (daa8be58f9d1): The fullest document in the series. Inflation fell from a 2022 peak of 8.1% to 3.4% by December 2023; the policy rate rose to 5.00% by July before holding steady. The Bank now states its mandate is delivered through five core functions rather than four: monetary policy, financial system, currency, funds management, and (newly formalized) retail payments supervision. The Bank was completing the second year of its 2022-24 strategic plan, "Delivering on Our Promise," organized around "five overarching themes" — the terminology "strategic plan" replaces the earlier "medium-term plan (MTP)" language used through 2019, though the exact year of that renaming is not pinned down by this series (see Residuals & gaps). Governance changes: a new, external non-executive Deputy Governor role was created and filled (economics professor Nicolas Vincent, two-year term); the Bank began publishing written summaries of Governing Council deliberations after each rate decision, a new transparency practice. In March 2023, US and Swiss bank failures tested global financial stability; the Bank states the Canadian system "proved resilient." In October 2023 the Bank designated four payment systems (Visa's VisaNet, two Mastercard systems, and Interac's Inter-Member Network) as "prominent" under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act, bringing them under formal Bank oversight — a concrete exercise of the newly expanded financial-market-infrastructure oversight role. The Bank advanced benchmark reform (CARR) to retire the Canadian Dollar Offered Rate (CDOR) in favour of CORRA, with bankers' acceptances also being wound down. Nearly 90,000 Canadians responded to the Bank's Digital Canadian Dollar consultation, summarized in a November 2023 report; work also began on a new $20 note featuring King Charles III following the change of monarch. The Bank began developing an Indigenous Reconciliation Action Plan, engaging directly with Indigenous employees, alongside standing Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (2022-24), Cyber Security (2022-24), and Accessibility (2023-25) strategies. Governance structure: a maximum-12-member independent Board plus Governor, Senior Deputy Governor, and non-voting Deputy Minister of Finance, per the Bank of Canada Act; four new independent directors joined in 2023.

2024 (574f6f1c2ccd): Thin capture. "As 2024 ends, our economy is in much better shape," with 2024 "heralded... as the year we returned inflation to target." In Focus items: "Driving decisions with data" (evolving use of diverse data sources, risk reduction in data/technology) and "Making connections" (Governing Council members travelling to communities across the country). 2024 is separately confirmed (via the 2023 report's forward-looking budget table) as the final year of the 2022-24 "Delivering on Our Promise" strategic plan.

2025 (6979863c0c2f): The Bank marked its 90th anniversary in 2025 ("born in a time of deep economic upheaval... created to restore stability and confidence"). The policy rate was cut four times (100 basis points total) in response to US tariffs and trade-policy volatility; inflation stayed near 2 percent and Canada avoided recession. Canada held the G7 presidency in 2025, and the Bank contributed to G7 discussions on cross-border payments, artificial intelligence, financial stability, cyber security, and non-bank financial institution risk. Work began on the 2026 monetary policy framework renewal (the next five-year cycle), with named research questions on supply-shock responses, core-inflation measurement, and the housing-affordability/inflation link — the report explicitly states "the 2% inflation target is not being reviewed." The retail-payment-service-provider (PSP) supervisory mandate came into effect in September 2025, with about 1,600 PSPs registered. The November 2025 federal budget announced two entirely new prospective Bank responsibilities: oversight of consumer-driven (open) banking and regulation of stablecoins — both undeveloped in this report beyond the announcement itself. The Bank "built on" a system-wide financial-stability surveillance framework "introduced in 2024," adding an expanded financial-stability-indicators dashboard. New-note work continued: a vertical $20 note is due in early 2027, with a new $5 note and security-feature work on future $50/$100 notes also underway. The 2025-27 strategic plan, "Canadians Count on Us," succeeds "Delivering on Our Promise." Notably, the Bank committed to reducing core operating expenses by 15% by the end of 2028, explicitly tied to "the federal government's Comprehensive Expenditure Review" — the first austerity/cost-reduction commitment named anywhere in the series. The balance sheet returned to "normal" management in 2025 after post-pandemic quantitative tightening, resuming routine term-repo and treasury-bill operations (GoC bond purchases not expected to resume until 2027). Board turnover was significant: three directors resigned (Aug-Nov 2025) and five new directors were appointed November 27, 2025.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • February 1991 — Canada's original inflation target announced, the founding act of the current monetary-policy framework (0da9ea8dc724, retrospective).
  • 2007-2009 — Global financial crisis; "full-blown global financial crisis through 2008" and subsequent recession (ar-2008, 7c834c59bd51), with financial-system reform work described in later reports as a multi-year response (ar-2016, be3aee58d495).
  • 2016 — 25th anniversary of the inflation-targeting framework; agreement renewed, 2% target maintained "until 2021" (ar-2016, be3aee58d495).
  • June 2021 — Retail Payment Activities Act receives Royal Assent, creating the Bank's new PSP-supervision mandate (ar-2021, c6aa43709b7c; detailed in ar-2023, daa8be58f9d1).
  • December 2021 — Monetary policy framework renewed for 2022-2026; 2% target and 1-3% control range retained, but "maximum sustainable employment" support language formally added (0da9ea8dc724).
  • 2022 — Most rapid policy-tightening cycle in Canada's history; inflation peaks near 8% (ar-2022, 72f59f7e3dd1).
  • 2023 — External non-executive Deputy Governor role created; Governing Council deliberation summaries begin publishing; four payment systems designated "prominent" under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act (October 2023); CDOR-to-CORRA benchmark transition advanced (daa8be58f9d1).
  • September 2025 — Retail-payment-service-provider supervision mandate becomes operational; ~1,600 PSPs registered (ar-2025, 6979863c0c2f).
  • November 2025 — Federal budget announces two new prospective Bank mandates: consumer-driven banking oversight and stablecoin regulation (ar-2025, 6979863c0c2f) — outcome and implementation not covered within the archived series.
  • 2025-2028 — Bank commits to a 15% reduction in core operating expenses, explicitly tied to the federal government's Comprehensive Expenditure Review; 2026 is described as the first budget year in which cuts take effect (ar-2025, 6979863c0c2f).
  • 2026 (forward-looking, named in ar-2025) — Next monetary-policy-framework renewal cycle begins; the 2% inflation target is explicitly stated as not under review, distinguishing this renewal from earlier ones where alternative-framework options were tested (ar-2025, 6979863c0c2f, contrast with 0da9ea8dc724's "horse race" review process in 2021).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Every dated press-release/media-relations line recovered in this series places the Bank's operations in "Ottawa, Ontario" (e.g., ar-2007 through ar-2018, bcc49c25ceb2 through e085afcb81ae) — the Bank's head office has been in Ottawa, Ontario throughout the archived period, including a renovated Wellington Street headquarters reoccupied in 2018 (ar-2018, e085afcb81ae). The archived series does not establish any Bank of Canada office, operational site, or institutional footprint in the City of Toronto specifically.
