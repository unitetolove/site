Municipal Property Assessment Corporation - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-municipal-property-assessment-corporation (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

MPAC is a statutory corporation created and continued by the Municipal Property Assessment Corporation Act, 1997, S.O. 1997, c. 43, Sched. G (consolidated text verified at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97o43 — note the e-Laws URL slug retains the corporation's original 1997 short name, "Ontario Property Assessment Corporation," even though the Act's title was updated). Its assessment function is exercised in compliance with the separate Assessment Act (cited on mpac.ca/en/AboutUs, fetched, but not independently verified this review — ⚠️ still being checked the Assessment Act citation).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

MPAC's role, per its own site, is "to accurately assess and classify all properties in Ontario" (mpac.ca/en/AboutUs, fetched); it maintains an inventory of nearly 5.7 million properties with an estimated total assessed value of $3.17 trillion and describes itself as "the largest assessment jurisdiction in the world" (MPAC 2024 Annual Report, mpac.ca, fetched). Assessment rolls are delivered annually to Ontario municipalities, which use them to calculate property and education tax bills (mpac.ca/en/AboutUs, fetched).

Governance & reporting line

Governance runs through a 13-member board of directors appointed by the Minister of Finance: seven municipal representatives, four taxpayer representatives, and two provincial representatives, per the Act's s.3 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97o43, verified). The board elects its chair from the municipal representatives and the Minister appoints a vice-chair from the provincial representatives (same source). Every Ontario municipality is a statutory member of the Corporation (Act s.3, as cited on mpac.ca). MPAC is described as "an independent, not-for-profit corporation funded by all Ontario municipalities" rather than a ministry-line-department (mpac.ca/en/AboutUs, fetched).

Budget scale

~$219.4M total 2024 municipal levy — the sum billed to all Ontario municipalities to cover MPAC's costs of operations, capital spending and reserve requirements net of other revenue (MPAC, "How MPAC calculated the 2024 Municipal Levy," mpac.ca/sites/default/files/docs/pdf/2024-MunicipalLevy-Infographic_WEB.pdf, fetched and extracted).

Institutional history

The Corporation was created effective December 31, 1997 under the 1997 Act as the Ontario Property Assessment Corporation and continued/renamed the Municipal Property Assessment Corporation; it became operational December 31, 1998 when Ontario transferred property-assessment responsibility from the Ministry of Finance to the new arm's-length body (Wikipedia summary of MPAC's founding, cross-referenced against the Act's continuation language at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97o43; ⚠️ still being checked exact operational date against a primary MPAC source).

Priority evolution

2018 (ar-2018, 5d3744a0392a): MPAC operates under the 2017–2020 Strategic Plan, its "five strategic outcomes": Invest in Our People, Focus on Operational Excellence/Efficiency and Operations, Build Our Customer and Stakeholder Relationships, Deliver Quality Traceable Assessments, Expand Business Development. Nicole McNeill serves her first full year as President and CAO; Dan Mathieson (Mayor of Stratford) chairs the Board. The Service Level Agreement (SLA) with municipalities, implemented in 2017, completes its first full year of measurement — 91% of service levels met. MPAC describes itself as "the largest assessment jurisdiction in North America" (not "the world"). Total assessed value $2.78 trillion across 5,293,723 properties; municipal levy revenue $206,573K. A first-for-Ontario First Nations assessment partnership (Chippewas of Kettle and Stony Point, with the First Nations Tax Commission, begun 2016) expands in 2018 to Wasauksing First Nation and Chippewas of Georgina Island First Nation.

2019 (ar-2019, 1c2b63b1d48b): MPAC marks its "20th anniversary," stating explicitly "MPAC was created in 1999" — a claim from the archived text itself, at odds with the backgrounder's 1997/1998 dating (see Residuals). Alan Spacek becomes Board Chair in September 2019, succeeding Dan Mathieson, who had chaired since 2010. A new Data Governance Office is established (first mention). MPAC earns ISO/IEC 27001 certification for the first time in the archived series. MPAC is named one of "Greater Toronto's Top Employers for 2020" — the first appearance of this recurring employer-branding recognition, repeated in every subsequent annual report through 2024. Total value $2.96 trillion, 5.36 million properties; municipal levy revenue $210,911K.

2020 (ar-2020, f2a1b822545f): The COVID-19 pandemic is the year's dominant event. The provincial government postpones the 2020 province-wide Assessment Update — a major disruption to MPAC's traditional four-year reassessment cycle, with 2021-taxation-year assessments continuing to use the January 1, 2016 valuation date. Three guiding principles are adopted for crisis decision-making: employee health/safety/wellness; keeping the business running; slowing COVID-19 spread. An employee-led Equity, Diversity and Inclusion Committee launches (precursor to the later formal EDIA program). MPAC earns ISO/IEC 27017 (cloud security) certification for the first time and re-earns ISO/IEC 27001. Municipal levy revenue reaches $214,919K, a figure that then holds flat through 2023 (see Budget & mandate inflection points).

2021 (ar-2021, ec83e39a677f; sp-2021, 559d1813ed22): A new 2021–2025 Strategic Plan ("We Value Ontario") replaces the 2017–2020 plan, organized around 4 pillars, each with 3–4 named initiatives: Putting our people and culture first; Delivering continuous operational excellence; Elevating the property owner and stakeholder experience; Unlocking opportunities to drive value and additional revenue (sp-2021). The Chair's message explicitly reframes MPAC's identity: "Traditionally, our identity centred around the province-wide update of property values we complete every four years. With that update on continued pause... we have had an opportunity to step back and reflect on the other ways we provide value" — the tagline "Ontario's property market experts" appears for the first time and recurs through the rest of the series (ar-2021). The municipal levy holds at a 0% increase "for the second year in a row." MPAC formally launches its Equity, Diversity, Inclusion and Anti-Racism (EDIA) initiative, completing its first of three phases (assessment and capacity building) by year-end; Canada's first National Day for Truth and Reconciliation is observed with a paid day off. MPAC begins building the capability to deliver "real-time values" for every Ontario property — the first mention of an initiative that recurs through 2024. MPAC forms its first international partnership, with the Valuation Office of Ireland, to commercialize its assessment technology. MPAC publishes its first-ever Corporate Performance Report and Municipal Partnerships Report (new transparency products).

2022 (ar-2022, ab22117bd12b), "Leading the Way Forward": The municipal levy holds at 0% increase "for a third year." MPAC founds a dedicated EDIA Office led by a new Executive Director (Rupa Aggarwal), implementing the first of 43 recommendations from a third-party equity assessment. A three-phase Flexible Work pilot launches — one of the first in Ontario's public sector. Business Development revenue reaches a record $26M+ (13% ahead of target), driven by the insourced propertyline™ e-store. The October 24, 2022 municipal and school board elections mark MPAC's final delivery of the province-wide Preliminary List of Electors (PLE) — this statutory responsibility transfers to Elections Ontario effective January 1, 2024, with MPAC retaining only school-support-designation changes beyond that date. Financially, 2022 is the only year in the archived series with a net deficiency of revenue over expenses (–$6,517K), driven by a $(13,711)K swing in fair value of investments. A new Appendix introduces a formal "List of measures and performance" — target/baseline/actual KPI tables across four categories (Assessment Excellence, Assessment Accuracy & Equity, Customer Service & Stakeholder Engagement, Operational Efficiency) — the first appearance of this structured performance-measurement regime in the series.

2023 (ar-2023, 890d431d3f96), "Pursuing Excellence": MPAC states it "began laying the foundation for our new strategic plan, which aligns with and supports the Government of Ontario's review of the assessment and taxation system in this province" — the first mention in the series of a provincial system-wide review MPAC is contributing to. Artificial intelligence/machine learning becomes an explicit strategic direction for the first time ("we will use that experience to guide us through the rapid expansion of uses for artificial intelligence"), with an AI Policy under development. The Board approves a new Corporate Data Strategy following 12 weeks of municipal/taxpayer discovery sessions in partnership with AMO. A record $42 billion in new assessment is captured. ISO/IEC 27018 (cloud data-privacy) certification is achieved for the first time. The Flexible Work pilot is extended through 2023 "with plans to make it permanent in 2024." A new Data Sharing and Services Agreement (DSSA) is introduced, merging the SLA, licensing agreements, and data-use terms into one framework. Tailte Éireann (the Valuation Office of Ireland) formally launches MPAC's commercial CAMA software product for a 5-year term — the maturation of the 2021 partnership into MPAC's first international commercial deployment. Business Development marks its 20th anniversary (since 2003), having generated $270M cumulative revenue. Financially, MPAC returns to a positive excess of revenue over expenses ($9,822K) after 2022's deficiency.

2024 (ar-2024, fcc1a914086c): The President's message states MPAC is "clos[ing] the chapter on our 2021-2024 Strategic Plan" — note the plan is here dated as ending in 2024, one year earlier than the "2021-2025" title on the archived sp-2021 document itself (see Residuals, and the registry's own pre-existing "⚠️ Still being checked: plan-horizon-2025" flag). A successor 2025–2028 Strategic Plan is developed, introducing MPAC's first fully articulated Vision ("To be the global leader transforming property assessment, data, and services"), Mission, Values, and Commitment statement. MPAC reports contributing to the Government of Ontario's assessment/taxation system review with priorities on "affordable rental housing, student housing, and enhanced information sharing." "MPAC's Data Strategy" is formally initiated with AMO input, building on 2023's Corporate Data Strategy; 99% of Ontario municipalities have now signed the DSSA introduced in 2023. MPAC onboards a second commercial technology client, the City of Winnipeg's Assessment & Taxation Department, alongside the continuing Tailte Éireann (Ireland) relationship. MPAC becomes "the first assessment body in North America and the largest Canadian organization" to obtain RESO Common Format certification. A new long-term real-estate target is announced: reduce the footprint of all Ontario workplaces by an average of 50% by 2029 (six offices already reduced by an average of 69% at year-end 2024). A new Social Impact and Inclusion Department consolidates EDIA and employee-led community committees. Record new assessment reaches $42.7 billion; total inventory nearly 5.7 million properties valued at $3.17 trillion (matching the backgrounder's headline figures).
