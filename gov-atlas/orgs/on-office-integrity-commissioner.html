Office of the Integrity Commissioner (Ontario) - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-office-integrity-commissioner (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Integrity Commissioner is established under the Members' Integrity Act, 1994, S.O. 1994, c. 38, consolidated text fetched and verified at e-Laws (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/94m38). Section 23(1): "There shall be an Integrity Commissioner who is an officer of the Assembly." The Office additionally administers duties under four further statutes — the Lobbyists' Registration Act, 1998, the Public Service of Ontario Act, 2006, the Cabinet Ministers' and Opposition Leaders' Expenses Review and Accountability Act, 2002, and the Public Sector Expenses Review Act — covering seven distinct mandates in total (https://www.oico.on.ca/en/about-us-overview).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

MPP integrity (disclosure statements, confidential advice, contravention inquiries); lobbyist registration compliance; ministers'-staff and public-sector ethics guidance; receiving disclosures of wrongdoing; and reviewing cabinet-minister, opposition-leader and agency expenses (Members' Integrity Act, ss.20-21, 28, 30-34, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/94m38; https://www.oico.on.ca/en/about-us-overview). The Commissioner "cannot investigate matters outside the jurisdiction of the Office," which excludes municipalities (https://www.oico.on.ca/en/about-us-overview).

Governance & reporting line

An independent Officer of the Legislative Assembly; appointed by Assembly order following unanimous agreement of an all-party panel chaired by the Speaker, for a five-year term renewable once (Members' Integrity Act, ss.23(2)-23(3), 23.1(1), https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/94m38); reports annually to the Speaker (s.24, same source).

Budget scale

Budget scale: ⚠️ not yet confirmed against an original source. (Two annual-report pages were fetched but the Financial Statement section content was not retrievable via automated fetch this review.)

Institutional history

The Office states it "was established...in 1988" (https://www.oico.on.ca/en/about-us-overview); it continues today as an officer of the Assembly under the Members' Integrity Act, 1994 (verified, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/94m38), and its mandate has since broadened through additional statutes (Lobbyists' Registration Act 1998, Public Service of Ontario Act 2006, and the two expenses-review Acts) rather than a formal renaming of the Office ⚠️ still being checked — the 1988 predecessor statute's exact title was not independently located/confirmed this review.

Office of the Integrity Commissioner (Ontario) - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: on-office-integrity-commissioner / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition across the readable record is the May 2019 merger with the Office of the Conflict of Interest Commissioner, which added a seventh mandate (Public Sector Ethics) and, per the Office's own account, effectively doubled its staff complement from 13 to nearly 30 over the following years (ar-2019, d0a675521bd5; ar-2024, 822c214c2357). The biggest quietly-dropped-then-reversed element is enforcement leniency under the Lobbyists' Registration Act: the Commissioner explicitly held back from penalties through the late 2010s while lobbyists learned the new investigative regime, then reversed that posture in 2024-25 with the first-ever penalties naming lobbyists (ar-2025, ecfcec9e0441). The most load-bearing number is the office's total annual budget, which rose from $3,436,880 in 2022-23 to $3,642,704 in 2023-24 (salaries and benefits alone reaching $3,547,000 in 2024-25) (ar-2023, 9e9b15d1f744; ar-2024, 822c214c2357; ar-2025, ecfcec9e0441). One open question: the record is silent on why the six-piece annual-report structure used 2002-2013 cannot be verified in any detail, because all twelve of those years' archived captures are Internet Archive catalog wrapper pages rather than the reports themselves — a genuine 11-year blind spot in an otherwise 23-year documented series (2002-2025).

Backgrounder summary

The Integrity Commissioner is an Officer of the Legislative Assembly of Ontario established under the Members' Integrity Act, 1994, appointed by unanimous all-party agreement for a five-year term (renewable once) and reporting annually to the Speaker (Members' Integrity Act ss.23-24, per backgrounder). Beyond the founding MPP-integrity mandate, the Office administers duties under four further statutes — the Lobbyists' Registration Act, 1998; the Public Service of Ontario Act, 2006; the Cabinet Ministers' and Opposition Leaders' Expenses Review and Accountability Act, 2002; and the Public Sector Expenses Review Act — for seven mandates in total (backgrounder). The Office states it traces to 1988 (backgrounder, citing oico.on.ca), though the readable annual-report record independently corroborates only that the Office marked its "20th anniversary" as of the 2009-10 report (ar-2010, stub metadata, 329cbad9f7f8) and its "35th anniversary" as of the 2022-23 report (ar-2023, 9e9b15d1f744) — both consistent with an approximately 1988 founding date. Budget scale was unverified in the backgrounder; this brief grounds it directly: total office expenditures were $3,436,880 in FY2022-23, rising to $3,642,704 in FY2023-24, with salaries and benefits alone at $3,547,000 in FY2024-25 (ar-2023, 9e9b15d1f744; ar-2024, 822c214c2357; ar-2025, ecfcec9e0441).

Priority evolution

2002-2013 (12 stub-suspected years): No priority evolution can be reconstructed for this span from the archived captures; see Residuals & gaps. The only durable facts recoverable from IA catalog metadata are: the Office's "principal work" was already described consistently across these years as MPP-inquiry response, complaint investigation, financial-disclosure review, and lobbyist registration (metadata description, multiple years e.g. ar-2008 3062f285d9c6, ar-2010 329cbad9f7f8, ar-2011 61beecd18457, ar-2012 f2dbb5622188, ar-2013 0fd72497da93) — i.e., a four-mandate office, before the two 2019-era additions. The ar-2009 catalog record notes the report itself included "a brief history of the office" for its 20th anniversary (32780cddc9bd), consistent with an approximately-1989/1990 founding reference distinct from the "1988" figure in the backgrounder — flagged ⚠️ still being checked.

2014-2018 (five-mandate era, news-release-only captures): Every year in this span is captured only as an OICO website news-release announcing that year's filing, not the report itself, but each release carries real figures. The consistent "five key responsibilities" framing (Members' Integrity; Disclosure of Wrongdoing; Expenses Review; Ministers' Staff Ethical Conduct; Lobbyists Registration) is stated identically in ar-2014 through ar-2018 (74208c9384eb; 57a2fcb1d6f6; 1163dbe3ed57; 20c09818a58f; 8f1c8db5b815). A structural inflection lands mid-span: amendments to the Lobbyists Registration Act, 1998, in force July 1, 2016, gave the Commissioner (as Lobbyists Registrar) investigative and penalty powers for the first time (ar-2016, 1163dbe3ed57); ar-2017 reports the "first investigation concluded" under that new power (20c09818a58f). Commissioner Lynn Morrison, in office since 1988 per her own account, announced her retirement in the ar-2015 release (57a2fcb1d6f6); J. David Wake is named as Commissioner starting with the ar-2016 release (1163dbe3ed57), with his own later account dating his start to February 1, 2016 (ar-2024, 822c214c2357).

2019 (d0a675521bd5) — mandate-expansion inflection: The Office's May 1, 2019 merger with the Office of the Conflict of Interest Commissioner is first reported here, adding "Public Sector Ethics" as a named mandate and bringing the total to seven mandates under five statutes (ar-2019, d0a675521bd5). The same release notes the mandate absorbed a record MPP-inquiry workload (533 inquiries, up from 292 the year before) attributed to the 2018 provincial election's 73 new MPPs (d0a675521bd5).

2020-2022 (pandemic-era, seven-mandate steady state): All three releases report the same seven-mandate structure and describe pandemic-driven operating adjustments — remote work, remote training delivery, and a multi-year dip in Disclosure of Wrongdoing filings and expense-claim volume that each report separately links to reduced office attendance and travel (ar-2020, 77d65c374011; ar-2021, 8647baa7ae18; ar-2022, f6c229985c27). ar-2022 flags the run-up to the 2022 provincial election as driving an increase in political-activity-rule questions across mandates (f6c229985c27).

2022-23 (9e9b15d1f744) — full-text era begins; election-year + legislative-review framing: The first fully-captured report. Commissioner Wake, in his eighth annual report, pivots the message format away from a single-mandate deep dive (used the two preceding years, per his own account) toward a consolidated list of proposed legislative amendments across every mandate — the first time this framing appears at this depth (9e9b15d1f744). The report is also the first to disclose, at the institutional level, an active inquiry into a minister's conduct relating to Greenbelt land-removal decisions, opened following a request under s.30 of the Members' Integrity Act, 1994 (9e9b15d1f744) — the inquiry itself concluded the following year (see below). The financial statement first appears as a distinct itemized line-item table in this era's captures ($3,436,880 total, 2022-23).

2023-24 (822c214c2357) — the Greenbelt inquiry, Wake's valedictory report, and lobbyist-registry anniversary: This is the pivotal document of the series. The Commissioner's report on the minister's conduct in the Greenbelt matter (filed August 30, 2023, per oth-2023, 103628fd35d7) is described institutionally: following review of evidence from 62 individuals and thousands of pages of documents, the inquiry found the minister had breached ss.2 and 3 of the Members' Integrity Act, 1994 by failing to supervise a chief-of-staff-led selection process, and recommended a reprimand by the Legislative Assembly (ar-2024, 822c214c2357; oth-2023, 103628fd35d7, para. 220 recommending reprimand). A companion report on a related request concerning the Premier found insufficient grounds to open an inquiry into his conduct with respect to two family social events (oth-2023-2, 8b1b29664d55) — an outcome distinct from, and less severe than, the minister's-conduct finding, and it should not be conflated with it. The Auditor General's parallel special report on the same matter is referenced institutionally as recommending a legislative review of three governing statutes (ar-2024, 822c214c2357). Separately, the Ontario Lobbyists Registry marked its 25th anniversary this year, having launched January 15, 1999 as the first registry of its kind in Canada (822c214c2357). Commissioner Wake announces his intended retirement in this, his ninth and what he expects to be his final, report, and reflects that the Office grew from 13 employees in six mandates (2016) to 29 people in seven mandates by 2024 (822c214c2357).

2024-25 (ecfcec9e0441) — leadership transition and lobbyist-enforcement hardening: Commissioner Wake retired February 28, 2025; Deputy Commissioner Cathryn Motherwell authored this report as Commissioner and was formally appointed April 16, 2025 (ecfcec9e0441). The report records two enforcement "firsts" under the Lobbyists' Registration Act, 1998: the first penalties naming lobbyists found to have placed public office holders in a real or perceived conflict of interest, and the first penalty naming a lobbyist and prohibiting him from lobbying the Ontario government for two years (six penalties total across 17 concluded investigations) (ecfcec9e0441). The report explicitly frames this as a deliberate policy evolution, quoting Commissioner Wake's own 2017-18 report describing an initial reluctance to penalize while the post-2016 investigative regime was new, and stating that reluctance would diminish over time (ecfcec9e0441, quoting ar-2017-18). The Disclosure of Wrongdoing mandate received what the report itself calls "a record 40 disclosures from current or former members of the Ontario Public Service," which it attributes to public servants returning to in-person work after a pandemic-era dip — a superlative the Office asserts about its own institutional history, not one this brief's readable 2014-2025 span can independently establish (ecfcec9e0441). Separately, the Office describes engaging with the province's Bill 241/Bill 9 Municipal Accountability Act process — a proposal for municipal integrity-commissioner standards on which this provincially-focused Office has no statutory role but was asked by the Premier's Office to advise (ecfcec9e0441).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — Public Sector Ethics mandate (7th mandate): created by the May 1, 2019 merger with the Office of the Conflict of Interest Commissioner; first reported ar-2019 (d0a675521bd5), operationalized as a distinct reported mandate from ar-2020 onward (77d65c374011).
  • Added — Lobbyist Registrar investigative and penalty powers: LRA amendments in force July 1, 2016, first reported ar-2016 (1163dbe3ed57); first investigation concluded ar-2017 (20c09818a58f); first penalties actually imposed on named lobbyists only in ar-2025 (ecfcec9e0441) — a nine-year gap between power granted and first punitive use, which the Office itself frames as a deliberate phased-enforcement posture rather than an oversight (ecfcec9e0441).
  • Added — consolidated cross-mandate legislative-amendment agenda: first appears as an organizing device in the Commissioner's message ar-2023 (9e9b15d1f744), replacing the single-mandate deep-dive framing used in the two prior years' messages per the Commissioner's own account (9e9b15d1f744).
  • Quietly dropped — single-mandate "deep dive" annual message framing: used in at least the two annual reports preceding ar-2023 per Wake's own retrospective statement (9e9b15d1f744); not used again in ar-2024 or ar-2025, both of which use valedictory/transition framing instead.
  • Renamed/expanded — "five key responsibilities" (2014-2018) become "seven mandates under five pieces of legislation" (from ar-2019, d0a675521bd5, restated identically through ar-2022, f6c229985c27) — the same underlying scope of MPP integrity, expenses review, and lobbyist registration, with Ministers' Staff Ethical Conduct and the new Public Sector Ethics/Disclosure-of-Wrongdoing-framework mandates now enumerated separately.
  • Leadership change, not a priority change but load-bearing for interpreting all of the above: J. David Wake begins February 1, 2016 (per his own ar-2024 retrospective, 822c214c2357), succeeding Lynn Morrison, who announced retirement in ar-2015 (57a2fcb1d6f6); Wake retires February 28, 2025, succeeded by Cathryn Motherwell, appointed April 16, 2025 (ecfcec9e0441).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is a direct, GTA-adjacent provincial oversight body: the Office is physically located in Toronto (Commissioner's reports are dated "Toronto, Ontario," e.g. oth-2023, 103628fd35d7; oth-2023-2, 8b1b29664d55) and its jurisdiction — MPP conduct, ministers'-staff ethics, and Queen's Park lobbying — is centred on the seat of Ontario's government in downtown Toronto. Its lobbyist-registry data names specific GTA-relevant lobbying targets (e.g., Metrolinx and the Independent Electricity System Operator appear among top-five lobbied agencies in ar-2023 and ar-2024, both bodies with direct Toronto-region transit/infrastructure remits: 9e9b15d1f744; 822c214c2357). The Office's jurisdiction explicitly excludes municipalities (backgrounder) — meaning it has no authority over the City of Toronto's own integrity-commissioner function — but its 2024-25 report describes the Office as having been asked by the Premier's Office to advise on a proposed province-wide municipal-integrity-commissioner accountability framework (Bill 241/Bill 9), which if enacted would touch Toronto's municipal ethics regime indirectly (ar-2025, ecfcec9e0441).
