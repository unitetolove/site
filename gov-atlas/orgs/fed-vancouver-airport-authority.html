Vancouver Airport Authority - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-vancouver-airport-authority (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Vancouver Airport Authority (YVR) is one of Canada's Airport Authorities: a "private, not-for-profit, non-share capital corporation" that is "not a Crown corporation, nor an agent of the Crown," incorporated under the Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act (Transport Canada, Airport Authorities briefing, https://tc.canada.ca/en/corporate-services/transparency/briefing-documents-transport-canada/2023-dm/transport-canada-structure-portfolio/shared-governance-organizations/airport-authorities, fetched this review; YVR is named among the 21 designated authorities on this page). The Authority operates Vancouver International Airport "pursuant to a lease of most of Sea Island, Richmond, British Columbia, from the Government of Canada [the 'Ground Lease']" (Vancouver Airport Authority 2023 Consolidated Financial Statements, note 1, https://www.yvr.ca/-/media/files/2023-annual-reports/2023-12-31-yvraa-consolidated-financial-statements.pdf, registry doc fed-vancouver-airport-authority-fs-2023). A Library of Parliament background paper records that "in 1992, the government concluded negotiations and established four private, not-for-profit local airport authorities in Vancouver, Calgary, Edmonton and Montréal" as part of the shift away from direct federal operation of airports (Airports in Transition, PRB 02-41E, https://publications.gc.ca/Collection-R/LoPBdP/BP/prb0241-e.htm, fetched this review).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

YVR manages, operates and develops Vancouver International Airport under the Ground Lease, "solely responsible for operating, maintaining, and developing the airport(s) in accordance with the terms of the lease," and is financially self-sufficient - funding operations through aircraft operator charges, passenger fees (including the Airport Improvement Fee) and commercial revenues rather than government appropriations (Transport Canada, Airport Authorities briefing, cited above). The Authority also holds interests in commercial subsidiaries tied to the airport lands, including a retail designer outlet centre developed through a 50%-owned partnership on Sea Island (2023 Consolidated Financial Statements, note 1, cited above). Its stated mission is "Connecting British Columbia proudly to the world" (https://www.yvr.ca/en/about-yvr, fetched via cache this review).

Governance & reporting line

YVR is governed by a community-based Board of Directors of 15: nine directors nominated by designated entities - the City of Vancouver, City of Richmond, Government of Canada, Metro Vancouver, Greater Vancouver Board of Trade, Association of Professional Engineers and Geoscientists of British Columbia, Law Society of British Columbia and Chartered Professional Accountants of British Columbia - plus up to five additional community-appointed directors and the President & CEO (ex officio) (https://www.yvr.ca/en/about-yvr/leadership/board-of-directors, fetched this review). "The Board of Directors is responsible for the overall stewardship of the Airport Authority, overseeing the Airport Authority's strategic direction, governance, financials and alignment to its values" (same source). As a non-share capital corporation rather than a Crown corporation, the Authority does not report through a federal Minister in the way AECL or NAV CANADA do; Transport Canada's oversight runs through the terms of the Ground Lease rather than a statutory reporting line (Transport Canada, Airport Authorities briefing, cited above).

Budget scale

For the year ended December 31, 2023, the Authority recorded an "excess of revenue over expenses" of $55.7 million, reversing a $26.0 million deficiency the prior year, and closed the year with total assets of approximately $3.09 billion (up from $3.01 billion in 2022) (Vancouver Airport Authority 2023 Consolidated Financial Statements, Statement of Changes in Net Assets and Statement of Financial Position, https://www.yvr.ca/-/media/files/2023-annual-reports/2023-12-31-yvraa-consolidated-financial-statements.pdf, registry doc fed-vancouver-airport-authority-fs-2023). ⚠️ still being checked exact total revenue/total expense line-item figures for FY2023 - the Statement of Operations table in the archived PDF did not extract to text this review; only the net excess/deficiency and balance-sheet totals were confirmed.

Institutional history

Vancouver was one of the first four Canadian airports transferred to a not-for-profit local airport authority in 1992, alongside Calgary, Edmonton and Montreal, ahead of the federal government's formal 1994 National Airports Policy (Airports in Transition, PRB 02-41E, https://publications.gc.ca/Collection-R/LoPBdP/BP/prb0241-e.htm, fetched this review). The Authority's Ground Lease with the Government of Canada dates from this transfer; ⚠️ still being checked the exact ground-lease commencement date (commonly cited elsewhere as July 1, 1992, but not confirmed this review against a document in the org's own registered doc shelf).
