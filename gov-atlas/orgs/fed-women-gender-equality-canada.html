Women and Gender Equality Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-women-gender-equality-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

WAGE is established under the Department for Women and Gender Equality Act, S.C. 2018, c. 27, s. 661 (chapter designation W-11.3), consolidated text verified at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/W-11.3/. Section 2 provides "there is established a department of the Government of Canada called the Department for Women and Gender Equality over which the Minister for Women and Gender Equality...presides," and s.4(1) extends the Minister's powers to "all matters...relating to women and gender equality" not assigned to another department (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

WAGE describes its role as "convener, knowledge broker, and capacity builder," developing policy, conducting research and funding community initiatives addressing systemic barriers facing women, girls and 2SLGBTQI+ people (2025-26 Departmental Plan, document id fed-women-gender-equality-canada-dp-2026, https://www.canada.ca/en/women-gender-equality/transparency/departmental-plans/2025-2026.html, r.jina.ai text-rendering fallback used for retrieval). It organizes work under a single core responsibility, "Advancing gender equality" (same source; statute s.4(1)).

Governance & reporting line

WAGE reports to the Minister of Women and Gender Equality per s.2 of the Act (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/W-11.3/; 2025-26 Departmental Plan, document id fed-women-gender-equality-canada-dp-2026).

Budget scale

~$407.1M total planned spending for 2025-26 ($383.0M advancing gender equality, $24.1M internal services; 444 planned FTEs), per the 2025-26 Departmental Plan (document id fed-women-gender-equality-canada-dp-2026, https://www.canada.ca/en/women-gender-equality/transparency/departmental-plans/2025-2026.html).

Institutional history

WAGE was established by statute in 2018 as a full department under the Department for Women and Gender Equality Act, S.C. 2018, c. 27, s. 661 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/W-11.3/). ⚠️ still being checked - its commonly cited predecessor lineage from Status of Women Canada was not confirmed against a primary source this review.

Women and Gender Equality Canada - strategy evolution

TL;DR: The archived series is a single-year planned/actual pairing — the 2025-26 Departmental Plan and the 2024-25 Departmental Results Report — both naming the same four core commitments (advance gender equality, lead GBA Plus government-wide, prevent/address gender-based violence, advance 2SLGBTQI+ equality), so no multi-year evolution is visible, only what changed between one year's plan and the prior year's results. The clearest concrete addition across the pair is the Menstrual Equity Fund, newly funded via the 2024 Fall Economic Statement and reported as offsetting an otherwise sharp, multi-program funding cliff. The most load-bearing number is the drop in planned core-responsibility spending from $383.0M (2025-26) to $265.9M (2026-27) to $60.6M (2027-28), driven by the simultaneous, stated expiry of Budget 2021, 2022, and 2023 time-limited funding streams (National Action Plan to End Gender-Based Violence, 2SLGBTQI+ Action Plan renewal, general gender-equality funding). One open question the two-document window cannot resolve: whether any of this expiring funding will be renewed in a future budget, since neither document commits to or rules out renewal.

Priority evolution

A two-document window one year apart cannot establish a multi-year evolution narrative. What the two documents do establish: drr-2025 (reporting on 2024-25) and dp-2026 (planning 2025-26) name substantially the same commitment structure — "promote and advance women and gender equality," "lead the systematic application of GBA Plus throughout the Government of Canada," "prevent and address gender-based violence (GBV)," and "promote and advance 2SLGBTQI+ equality," plus an internal "strengthen organizational well-being and enhance WAGE capacities" commitment (1c0d4cf92b57; ebe4691c0343) — indicating this four-commitment structure was stable across the one-year window this archived pair covers. Both documents also organize substantive work under the identical single core responsibility, "Advancing gender equality," with the same two named programs beneath it (Expertise and Outreach; Community Action and Innovation) in both documents (1c0d4cf92b57; ebe4691c0343).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

Two documents one year apart cannot show additions, drops, or renames in the sense a multi-year series would — no earlier Departmental Plan or Results Report is readable in this archived set to compare against. One concrete addition is visible directly between the two documents: the Menstrual Equity Fund pilot, already running in 2024-25 in partnership with Food Banks Canada (over 72.7 million products distributed since 2023 per drr-2025, ebe4691c0343), is named in dp-2026 as receiving new, distinctly sourced funding from the 2024 Fall Economic Statement that is explicitly credited with offsetting the broader 2026-27 funding decline (1c0d4cf92b57) — a shift from pilot-phase to a named, separately-funded ongoing commitment across the two documents. No priority is named as dropped between the two documents; both retain all four commitments and the internal-services strand.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2024-25 actual total spending: $368,962,159 (including internal services), against 2024-25 planned/forecast figures in the $378.6M range reported in dp-2026's own three-year comparison table (ebe4691c0343; 1c0d4cf92b57) — independently reconfirming the backgrounder's separately-sourced 2025-26 figure is in the same order of magnitude and continuous with actuals.
  • 2025-26 planned spending: $407,142,146 total ($383,042,861 advancing gender equality + $24,099,285 internal services), matching the backgrounder's figure exactly (1c0d4cf92b57).
  • Sharp planned decline from 2025-26 to 2027-28: total planned spending falls from $407,142,146 (2025-26) to $284,695,249 (2026-27) to $76,266,802 (2027-28) — both documents attribute this to the simultaneous, stated expiry of Budget 2021 funding (National Action Plan to End Gender-Based Violence), Budget 2022 funding (2SLGBTQI+ Community Capacity Fund renewal, first Federal 2SLGBTQI+ Action Plan implementation, GBV provincial/territorial work), and Budget 2023 funding ("to advance gender equality in Canada"), offset only partly by new Fall Economic Statement 2024 Menstrual Equity Fund money (1c0d4cf92b57; ebe4691c0343).
  • Corresponding FTE decline: planned FTEs fall from 444 (2025-26) to 314 (2026-27) to 254 (2027-28), a reduction of 130 FTEs in 2026-27 alone attributed to the same time-limited-funding expiry (1c0d4cf92b57; ebe4691c0343).
  • Refocusing Government Spending reductions disclosed in drr-2025: WAGE identified spending reductions under the Budget 2023 government-wide $14.1B/5-year exercise — $1,000,000 (2024-25), $1,500,000 (2025-26), $2,000,000 (2026-27 and after) — achieved via reduced professional-services and travel spending (ebe4691c0343). This is a materially smaller, differently-scoped reduction than the funding-cliff described above, which stems from time-limited program funding expiring on its own schedule rather than an announced austerity target.
  • GBA Plus 30th anniversary (2025) — both documents mark 2025 as the 30th anniversary of the Government of Canada's Gender-Based Analysis Plus tool, with dp-2026 describing plans to "update enhancements and improve understanding and use of disaggregated data" and optimize GBA Plus governance structure (1c0d4cf92b57; ebe4691c0343).
  • New GBA Plus Resource Centre and governance structure launched in 2024-25 — drr-2025 reports WAGE "launched a new governance structure" and "developed a new GBA Plus Resource Centre" during the reporting year; dp-2026 carries this forward as continued work rather than a new initiative (ebe4691c0343; 1c0d4cf92b57).
  • First-cycle Disaggregated Data Action Plan with Statistics Canada — drr-2025 reports WAGE partnered with Statistics Canada on "Canada's first Disaggregated Data Action Plan," securing inclusion of gender-identity and sexual-orientation questions in national surveys during 2024-25 (ebe4691c0343); dp-2026 continues this partnership without naming it as newly launched, describing ongoing support for Statistics Canada's "disaggregated data action plan" (1c0d4cf92b57).
  • Canada's first 2SLGBTQI+ national survey — named in dp-2026 as planned for deployment in 2025-26, with results reporting expected in 2026-27 (1c0d4cf92b57); not yet deployed as of the drr-2025 reporting period.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Neither document names Toronto or Ontario as a specific program site or subject; WAGE's mailing address is in Ottawa (P.O. Box 8097, Station T CSC, Ottawa, ON K1G 3H6) (1c0d4cf92b57; ebe4691c0343). The one Ontario-specific mention in either document is a funded-project example, not a WAGE program: drr-2025 cites "Leveraging Public Sector Procurement Policies to Expand Opportunities for Women in Engineering" (Ontario Society of Professional Engineers), under which "several Ontario municipalities had committed to adopting parts of the OSPE's recommendations in their own social procurement policies" (ebe4691c0343) — a third-party grant recipient's project outcome, not a WAGE-run Toronto/Ontario initiative. Beyond this single funded-project mention, relevance to this registry is indirect: WAGE's national mandate (GBA Plus, GBV prevention funding, 2SLGBTQI+ equality programs) applies uniformly across Canada including Ontario and Toronto/GTA-based grant recipients and stakeholders, but no Toronto-specific WAGE office, program, or initiative is named in either document.
