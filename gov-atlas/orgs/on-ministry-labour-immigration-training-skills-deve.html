Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Wave 1 / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-labour-immigration-training-skills-deve (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Ministry is constituted by order-in-council under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.25, s. 2(1)-(2) (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e25, fetched and verified), like other Ontario ministries, rather than by its own founding act. It administers 39 acts, with foundational legislation including the Labour Relations Act, 1995, the Occupational Health and Safety Act, the Employment Standards Act, 2000, and the Workplace Safety and Insurance Act, 1997 (Published plans and annual reports 2025-2026: Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development, https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-labour-immigration-training-and-skills-development, fetched via text-render).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Ministry states its vision is to lead "Ontario's effort to be the best jurisdiction in North America to recruit, train, retain, protect, and reward workers," organized around "dynamic and equitable labour markets, safe and harmonious workplaces, and competitive labour and employment regulations" (same source, fetched). Functions include workplace safety/health prevention and enforcement (partnering with WSIB), employment-standards inspection and enforcement (minimum wage, vacation pay, hours of work), labour relations mediation/conciliation and arbitrator appointment, delivery of employment and training services and apprenticeship/skilled-trades promotion, and facilitation of skilled-immigration pathways (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-labour-immigration-training-skills-development, fetched via text-render; registry row on-ministry-labour-immigration-training-skills-deve).

Governance & reporting line

Led by a Minister of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development, a member of Executive Council appointed under the Executive Council Act (ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e25, fetched); ⚠️ VERIFY current Minister's name not confirmed via fetched source this pass.

Budget scale

~$2.10B total planned operating and capital spending for 2025-26 ($2,102,746,014: $1,913,403,314 operating + $189,342,700 capital); 2024-25 interim actuals were ~$1,801.6M operating + $20.6M capital (Published plans and annual reports 2025-2026: Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development, https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-labour-immigration-training-and-skills-development, fetched via text-render).

Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development - strategy evolution

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is explicit tariff-response framing, appearing only in the final report (ar-2026) as "Maximizing and protecting the supply of skilled workers while reducing the tariff impact," backed by new training/support centres and Skills Development Fund tariff-mitigation funding. The biggest quietly-dropped item is COVID-19 response as an organizing frame — prominent through ar-2023, then fading from the narrative structure with no report declaring the pandemic response concluded. The most load-bearing number is the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program's federal nomination allocation being cut roughly in half to 10,750 for 2025, explicitly attributed in the ministry's own KPI table to "the federal government's reduction of provincial nominee program allocations by 50 percent." One open question the record cannot resolve: the exact date/mechanism of the ministry's own name change to add "Immigration," occurring sometime between the ar-2022 and ar-2023 reports with no stated effective date or authorizing order-in-council.

Backgrounder summary

The Ministry is constituted by order-in-council under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.25, and administers 39 acts, with foundational legislation including the Labour Relations Act, 1995, the Occupational Health and Safety Act, the Employment Standards Act, 2000, and the Workplace Safety and Insurance Act, 1997 (backgrounder). It states its vision as leading "Ontario's effort to be the best jurisdiction in North America to recruit, train, retain, protect, and reward workers," delivered through workplace safety/health enforcement (with WSIB), employment-standards enforcement, labour relations mediation/arbitration, employment and training services/apprenticeship promotion, and skilled-immigration nomination (backgrounder). Budget scale per the backgrounder is ~$2.10B total planned 2025-26 spending; this pass confirms that figure directly from the archived ar-2026 document itself (see Budget & mandate inflection points).

Priority evolution

ar-2021 (96c226086730), "Published Plans and Annual Reports 2020-2021: Ministry of Labour, Training and Skills Development": Ministry name is "Ministry of Labour, Training and Skills Development" (MLTSD) — no "Immigration" in the title yet. Four "primary areas of responsibility": Employment Ontario, Occupational Health and Safety, Employment Standards, Labour Relations. Dominated by COVID-19 response: a dedicated "COVID-19 response" section describes WSIB payment deferrals ($1.9B in employer relief), doubled Health and Safety Call Centre capacity (25 to 50 lines), and a newly announced Workforce Development and Training Review and Action Plan (96c226086730). 2020-21 base operating spend $1,596.9M (96c226086730).

ar-2022 (7263b4f1a66f), "...2021-2022...": Still "Ministry of Labour, Training and Skills Development." A fifth primary area is added — "Global Talent and Adult Language Training" — folding in the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP) and adult-language settlement services, previously described only as a subordinate program under Employment Ontario. An "Anti-Racism Action Plan" appears for the first time as an internal ministry initiative under the OPS Anti-Racism Policy. An "Ontario Workforce Recovery Advisory Committee" is announced. 2021-22 base operating spend $2,077.9M (7263b4f1a66f).

ar-2023 (fe0cebb84725), "...2022-2023...": Ministry name changes in this document's own title and body text to "Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development" (MLITSD) — the current name, with "Immigration" now formally incorporated. The fifth program area is renamed "Global Talent and Settlement Services" (GTSS). A new government-priority line is added explicitly: "Supporting a harmonious and stable labour relations climate that minimizes the number and duration of work stoppages." The Ontario College of Trades (OCOT) wind-down culminates in the creation of Skilled Trades Ontario (STO) as a new Crown agency via the Building Opportunities in the Skilled Trades Act, 2021 (BOSTA), proclaimed January 1, 2022 (fe0cebb84725). Skills Development Fund (SDF) named for the first time as a named program vehicle. 2022-23 base operating spend $2,081.6M (fe0cebb84725).

ar-2024 (9766ae564903), "...2023-2024...": Three explicit named priorities for Employment Ontario appear for the first time in this tabulated form: "Providing better training," "Transforming Employment Services," and "Growing the Skilled Trades" (9766ae564903). Service System Managers (SSMs) begin rolling out across 15 catchment areas as the vehicle for "Employment Services Transformation" (EST). Two formal Indigenous engagement structures are named: the Urban Indigenous Engagement Table (UIET) and a Chiefs of Ontario (COO) partnership/Employment Table Working Group. OINP's federal nomination allocation rises to 16,500 (a stated 70% increase over the prior year), with a path to 18,000 by 2025 (9766ae564903). 2023-24 base operating spend $1,890.5M (9766ae564903) — a fall from the prior year's $2,081.6M.

ar-2025 (6050603e101a), "...2024-2025...": "Integrated Employment Services (IES)" supersedes "Employment Services Transformation" as the named service-delivery-model term, folding social-assistance employment services into Employment Ontario. New: an "Integrated Compliance Project" is announced under Employment Standards, explicitly extending the multi-ministry COVID-19 enforcement-coordination model into peacetime regulatory compliance work (6050603e101a). BOSTA enforcement is for the first time folded explicitly into the OHS program's own stated scope ("OHS Enforcement activities are focused on...supporting skilled trades and apprenticeship system modernization through the...BOSTA program"), with registration required for 23 compulsory trades. 2024-25 base operating spend $1,894.1M (6050603e101a).

ar-2026 (46694ee44337), "...2025-2026...": First appearance of formal, tabulated "Relevant Ministry Level KPIs" with baseline/trend/target values for each of the five program areas (EO, OHS, ES, LR, GTSS/OINP, Immigration and Settlement) — a materially new reporting structure not present in any prior year in this series (46694ee44337). Economic framing shifts sharply to tariff response: a new stated priority is "Maximizing and protecting the supply of skilled workers while reducing the tariff impact on the Ontario labour market," including new "training and support centres" for tariff-affected laid-off workers and additional SDF funding explicitly tied to "Mitigating Economic Impacts of Tariffs" (46694ee44337). The Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act (DPWRA) enters into force July 1, 2025, extending ESA-style protections to gig/platform workers (rideshare, food delivery, courier). OINP's federal nomination allocation is reported cut roughly in half to 10,750 for 2025, with the ar-2026 KPI table explicitly attributing this to "the federal government's reduction of provincial nominee program allocations by 50 percent" (46694ee44337) — the clearest instance in the series of an external federal policy shift directly constraining a named Ontario ministry priority. 2025-26 base operating spend $2,348.3M / total planned spending (operating + capital) $2,102.7M (46694ee44337) — matches the backgrounder's figure.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — "Immigration" formally incorporated into ministry name and mandate: ministry is "Ministry of Labour, Training and Skills Development" through ar-2022 (7263b4f1a66f); becomes "Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development" starting ar-2023 (fe0cebb84725) — the current name.
  • Added — Global Talent / immigration as a formal fifth program area: absent as a standalone pillar in ar-2021 (96c226086730, where OINP appears only as a subordinate Employment Ontario program); becomes a named fifth area of responsibility ("Global Talent and Adult Language Training") from ar-2022 (7263b4f1a66f) onward, renamed "Global Talent and Settlement Services" (GTSS) from ar-2023 (fe0cebb84725).
  • Added — Anti-Racism Action Plan: first appears ar-2022 (7263b4f1a66f) as an internal-facing OPS Anti-Racism Policy commitment; continues in every subsequent report through ar-2026.
  • Added — Skilled Trades Ontario (STO) as successor to the Ontario College of Trades (OCOT): OCOT wind-down is discussed as in-progress in ar-2021/ar-2022 (96c226086730; 7263b4f1a66f); STO is established via BOSTA, proclaimed January 1, 2022, and first reported as operational in ar-2023 (fe0cebb84725).
  • Added — tabulated "Relevant Ministry Level KPIs" reporting structure: absent from ar-2021 through ar-2025; first appears ar-2026 (46694ee44337), applied across all five program areas with explicit baseline/trend/target values.
  • Added — tariff-response framing as an explicit priority: absent from all prior years; first appears ar-2026 (46694ee44337), tied to new SDF funding and new "training and support centres" for affected workers.
  • Added — Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act (DPWRA) enforcement: named as newly in force (July 1, 2025) in ar-2026 (46694ee44337) only; absent from all prior reports in the series.
  • Renamed — "Employment Services Transformation" (EST) becomes "Integrated Employment Services" (IES): EST is the operative term ar-2023 through ar-2024 (fe0cebb84725; 9766ae564903); IES supersedes it as the named model from ar-2025 (6050603e101a) onward, reported as fully implemented province-wide (all 15 catchments) by March 2025 per ar-2026 (46694ee44337).
  • Quietly dropped — explicit COVID-19 response as an organizing frame: a dedicated, extensive "COVID-19 response" section is present and prominent in ar-2021, ar-2022, and ar-2023 (96c226086730; 7263b4f1a66f; fe0cebb84725); COVID-related content shrinks to occasional legacy references (e.g., WIPB program wind-down) by ar-2024 (9766ae564903) and is essentially absent as an organizing theme by ar-2025/ar-2026 — no report explicitly declares the pandemic response concluded; it simply fades from the narrative structure.
  • Quietly dropped — "Skilled Trades Panel": active and reporting through Phase One and Phase Two mandates in ar-2022/ar-2023 (7263b4f1a66f; fe0cebb84725, submitting its final report Fall 2021 per ar-2023); not mentioned in ar-2024 onward — presumed wound down following its advisory mandate's completion and BOSTA's passage, but no report states this explicitly.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • April 29, 2021 — COVID-19 Putting Workers First Act, 2021 passed, creating the Worker Income Protection Benefit (WIPB), an employer-reimbursement program for COVID-related paid leave, administered by WSIB (7263b4f1a66f).
  • January 1, 2022 — Building Opportunities in the Skilled Trades Act, 2021 (BOSTA) proclaimed into force; Skilled Trades Ontario (STO) established as successor to the Ontario College of Trades, with CEO and 11-member board appointed same date (fe0cebb84725).
  • 2022 Ontario Budget — $205M reinvested to extend/enhance the Ontario Jobs Training Tax Credit (fe0cebb84725).
  • Early 2023 — government announces $224M investment in a new SDF Capital Stream, launched June 30, 2023 in partnership with Infrastructure Ontario (9766ae564903).
  • October 26, 2023 / March 21, 2024 — Working for Workers Act, 2023 and Working for Workers Four Act, 2023 receive Royal Assent, amending ESA, WSIA, and FARPACTA (9766ae564903).
  • 2023-24 to 2024-25 — OINP federal nomination allocation rises from 16,500 (2023) to a reported 21,500 nominations issued in 2024-25 (9766ae564903; 46694ee44337).
  • March 2024 / May 2025 — new Working at Heights training standards implemented April 1, 2024; Occupational Illness Leadership Table established to implement 41 recommendations from the Occupational Disease Landscape Review Report (6050603e101a; 46694ee44337).
  • 2025 Ontario Budget, "A Plan to Protect Ontario" — 2025-26 total planned spending confirmed at $2,102.7M ($1,913.4M operating + $189.3M capital), a 20.9% year-over-year increase attributed in the report itself to "increase in demand driven programming within Employment Ontario, through programs such as SDF as a result of the existing economic climate" (46694ee44337) — matches the backgrounder's figure exactly.
  • July 1, 2025 — Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act (DPWRA) comes into force (46694ee44337).
  • 2025 (calendar year) — federal government cuts Ontario's Provincial Nominee Program allocation by 50%, reducing OINP's 2025 nomination target from a prior trend of over 10,000 to 10,750/8,850 depending on the cited table row — the ar-2026 KPI table states the target value "decreased as a result of the federal government's reduction of provincial nominee program allocations by 50 percent" (46694ee44337) — the clearest documented federal-provincial funding/mandate friction point in this series.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is an Ontario provincial ministry; its entire mandate and operations are Ontario-wide by construction, with Toronto explicitly named as one of the ministry's own operating geographies and as host to numerous entities it regulates or interacts with. Specific Toronto/GTA references found directly in the archived text: the Toronto Transit Commission and the Toronto Transit Commission Labour Disputes Resolution Act, 2011 are named among "Back to work" Acts the ministry administers in every report in the series (e.g., 96c226086730; 46694ee44337); the ar-2021 report names the University of Toronto and York University among institutions with major collective agreements expiring that fiscal year (96c226086730); the ar-2021 report describes a four-month audit of tower cranes across Ontario launched after "two tower crane failures in Toronto during the summer of 2020" (96c226086730); the ar-2022 report names the ministry's early Service System Manager (SSM) engagement work in "the complex catchment areas of Toronto, Northeast, and Northwest" as the final phase of Employment Services Transformation rollout (fe0cebb84725), with ar-2026 confirming Toronto, Northeast and Northwest were the last three catchments to receive full IES implementation, completed March 2025 (46694ee44337); the 2022-23 report names the Toronto Business Development Centre (TBDC) as the selected administrator of the OINP Entrepreneur Pilot (fe0cebb84725). No report identifies a ministry headquarters address or states Toronto as the ministry's primary office location, though as an Ontario ministry headquartered within the provincial government's Queen's Park complex this is a near-certainty; that specific fact is not grounded in the archived text this pass. ⚠️ VERIFY: ministry headquarters address/location, not confirmed via archived text this pass.
