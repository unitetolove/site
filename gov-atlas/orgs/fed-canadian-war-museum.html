Canadian War Museum - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-canadian-war-museum (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canadian War Museum is one of two national museums (alongside the Canadian Museum of History) operated by a single Crown corporation continued under the Museums Act, S.C. 1990, c. 3 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/m-13.4/page-1.html, confirmed this pass). The corporation was established under the Act effective July 1, 1990, and renamed the Canadian Museum of History Corporation by an amendment (the Canadian Museum of History Act) on December 12, 2013 (https://www.warmuseum.ca/about/our-purpose, fetched this pass). The Museum's mandate is "to enhance Canadians' knowledge, understanding and appreciation of events, experiences, people and objects that reflect and have shaped Canada's history and identity" (same source), with the War Museum's specific focus on Canada's military history.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Delivers exhibitions, education programming, and collections care focused on Canadian military history, holding more than 3 million artifacts including the roughly 14,000-work Beaverbrook Collection of War Art and extensive archival holdings (https://www.warmuseum.ca/about/our-purpose, fetched this pass). In fiscal 2024-2025 the War Museum recorded 320,000 on-site visits (of 999,000 combined with the Museum of History) and 219,000 paid admissions, against a 2024-2025 target of 390,000/220,000 respectively (Canadian Museum of History Annual Report 2024-2025, pp.30-31, https://www.warmuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/annual-report-2024-2025.pdf, fetched and parsed this pass).

Governance & reporting line

Governed by an 11-member Board of Trustees (a Chair, a Vice-Chair, and nine further Trustees) of the parent Canadian Museum of History Corporation - there is no separate, War-Museum-specific governing board (Annual Report 2024-2025, "Board of Trustees" section, same source). Trustees are appointed by the Minister of Canadian Heritage, with the approval of the Governor-in-Council, for terms not to exceed four years, with a maximum of three terms (https://www.warmuseum.ca/about/governance, fetched this pass). The corporation reports to Parliament through the Minister of Canadian Heritage and is governed by the Crown-corporation control and accountability regime under Part X of the Financial Administration Act, alongside the Museums Act and its own by-laws (Annual Report 2024-2025, same source).

Budget scale

The parent corporation - covering both the Canadian Museum of History and the Canadian War Museum together - recognized $96.6 million ($96,579 thousand) in Parliamentary appropriations as revenue in fiscal 2024-2025, against a net result of operations before appropriations of a $95.6 million deficit (Annual Report 2024-2025, financial statements, https://www.warmuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/annual-report-2024-2025.pdf, fetched and parsed this pass). ⚠️ VERIFY - a War Museum-only budget or appropriation breakout is not separately published in the corporation's public reporting; the $96.6 million figure is corporate-wide.

Institutional history

The Museum traces its origins to 1880, beginning as a collection of militia artifacts, and opened its current LeBreton Flats building in downtown Ottawa on May 8, 2005 - coinciding with both the 60th anniversary of V-E Day and the Museum's own 125th anniversary (https://www.warmuseum.ca/about/our-purpose, fetched this pass). The registry's document shelf flags a genuine publication gap in the corporate-plan/annual-report series for fiscal 2016-2017, the Vimy Ridge centenary year - never crawled by Wayback, and absent from the live warmuseum.ca corporate-reports listing (research/atlas/government_documents.csv, row fed-canadian-war-museum-ar-2017).
