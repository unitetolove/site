Library of Parliament - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Wave 1 / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-library-parliament (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Library of Parliament is established under the Parliament of Canada Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. P-1, Part V, ss. 73-76, consolidated text at laws-lois.justice.gc.ca (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-1/, fetched and verified directly; section text at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-1/page-8.html, also fetched). Section 73 vests the collection "for the use of both Houses of Parliament"; s.74 vests direction and control in the two Speakers; s.75 provides for a Governor-in-Council-appointed Parliamentary Librarian holding deputy-head rank (same source). The Library's own stated mission is "to contribute to Canadian parliamentary democracy by creating, managing and delivering authoritative, reliable and relevant information and knowledge for and about Parliament" (Annual Report 2024-2025, https://lop.parl.ca/staticfiles/PublicWebsite/Home/About/CoporateDocuments/PDF/AnnualReport_2024-2025-e.pdf, doc-shelf: annual-report).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Library provides non-partisan research, analysis, and information services to both Houses of Parliament and their members, and maintains the parliamentary collection (registry row fed-library-parliament; s.73, Parliament of Canada Act). Until 2017 the Parliamentary Budget Officer operated as an office within the Library under the same Act before becoming a fully independent Agent of Parliament (Parliament of Canada Act historical amendment notes, ss.79.01/79.1, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-1/page-8.html).

Governance & reporting line

"The Speaker of the Senate and the Speaker of the House of Commons are jointly responsible for the direction and control of the Library of Parliament in accordance with the Parliament of Canada Act" (Annual Report 2024-2025, cited above), assisted during each session by a joint committee appointed by the two Houses (s.74(1), Act). The Parliamentary Librarian, appointed by the Governor in Council to hold office during pleasure, has deputy-head rank and day-to-day control and management of the Library subject to the Speakers (s.75, Act). A Standing Joint Committee on the Library of Parliament, comprising Senators and MPs, assists the Speakers in oversight (Annual Report 2024-2025).

Budget scale

~$62.1 million in actual spending for 2024-2025, against total authorities of $63,045,262 (Annual Report 2024-2025, https://lop.parl.ca/staticfiles/PublicWebsite/Home/About/CoporateDocuments/PDF/AnnualReport_2024-2025-e.pdf, doc-shelf: annual-report).

Institutional history

The Library traces to the joint parliamentary library provisions consolidated in the Parliament of Canada Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. P-1; the modern governance structure (joint Speaker direction, GIC-appointed Parliamentary Librarian) is set out in the currently consolidated ss.73-76 of that Act (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-1/); the Parliamentary Budget Officer, originally created within the Library's structure by the Federal Accountability Act (2006, c.9, s.116 amending the Act), was reorganized into a fully separate independent Agent of Parliament by 2017 amendments (2017, c.20, s.128) and further amended in 2022 (2022, c.10, s.243) — amendment notes fetched directly from the consolidated Act text (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-1/page-8.html). ⚠️ VERIFY: exact year the joint Library institution itself was first created (pre-Confederation/1867-era origin) not independently confirmed this pass.
