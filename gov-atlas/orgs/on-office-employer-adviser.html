Office of the Employer Adviser - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-office-employer-adviser (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Office of the Employer Adviser (OEA) is continued by s.176(2) of the Workplace Safety and Insurance Act, 1997, S.O. 1997, c. 16, Sched. A: "The Office of the Employer Adviser is continued. Its functions are to educate, advise and represent primarily those employers that have fewer than 100 employees" (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97w16, fetched and verified, s.176(2); registry row on-office-employer-adviser). It also carries duties under s.50 of the Occupational Health and Safety Act for unlawful-reprisal matters (OEA "About OEA" page, https://www.employeradviser.ca/about-oea/, fetched).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The OEA provides free, confidential legal advice, representation and education to Ontario employers - mainly those with fewer than 100 workers - on workers' compensation claims and costs under the WSIA, and on unjust-reprisal complaints under the OHSA; paralegals and lawyers represent employers before the Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) and the Workplace Safety and Insurance Appeals Tribunal (WSIAT) (https://www.employeradviser.ca/about-oea/, fetched).

Governance & reporting line

The OEA describes itself as "an independent agency of the Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development (MLITSD)" (https://www.employeradviser.ca/about-oea/, fetched). Under WSIA s.176(3), "The Minister shall determine the amount of the costs that may be incurred by each office in performing its functions and the Board shall pay them" - i.e., the Minister of Labour sets cost allocations and the WSIB funds the office through employer premiums/administrative fees (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97w16, fetched, s.176(3); site funding statement, https://www.employeradviser.ca/about-oea/). FTE staffing complement (28.0 FTE as of Q3 2024-25) is assigned directly by MLITSD (Office of the Employer Adviser Business Plan 2025-2026 to 2027-2028, registry strategy_current_url).

Budget scale

⚠️ not yet confirmed against an original source. The Business Plan 2025-28 (registry strategy_current_url) confirms 28.0 FTE positions assigned by MLITSD but does not itemize a dollar operating budget in the sections reviewed; costs are drawn from WSIB premium revenue per WSIA s.176(3) rather than direct legislative appropriation.

Institutional history

The Office of the Employer Adviser was "continued" (not newly created) by the Workplace Safety and Insurance Act, 1997, indicating an earlier origin under the predecessor Workers' Compensation Act framework; the OEA's own site states it has served Ontario employers "for more than 35 years" as of 2026, placing establishment in the early 1990s (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97w16, s.176(2); https://www.employeradviser.ca/about-oea/, fetched).

Backgrounder summary

The Office of the Employer Adviser (OEA) is continued by s.176(2) of the Workplace Safety and Insurance Act, 1997, "to educate, advise and represent primarily those employers that have fewer than 100 employees" (backgrounder, citing ontario.ca/laws/statute/97w16), with a parallel mandate under s.50 of the Occupational Health and Safety Act for unlawful-reprisal matters involving employers with fewer than 50 employees. It provides free legal advice, representation and education to Ontario employers on workers' compensation claims/costs under the WSIA and on OHSA reprisal complaints, with paralegals and lawyers representing employers before the WSIB and WSIAT (backgrounder). It describes itself as "an independent agency of the Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development"; costs are set by the Minister of Labour under WSIA s.176(3) and paid by the WSIB from employer premiums, not direct appropriation (backgrounder).
