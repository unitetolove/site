Mississauga Library - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: miss-mississauga-library (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Mississauga Library is a statutory public library board operating under the Public Libraries Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.44 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p44 — fetched via text-render fallback and verified 2026-07-30, title confirmed as "Public Libraries Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.44"). The Act requires council-established boards to have at least five council-appointed members (s. 9(1)), bars council appointees from forming a majority (s. 10(2)), and directs boards to provide "comprehensive and efficient public library service" (s. 20).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Library is an agency of the City of Mississauga (registry row: miss-mississauga-library, parent_org field), delivering public library service including the Hazel McCallion Central Library and branch locations across the city (mississauga.ca/library/library-board/, fetched; document id: miss-mississauga-library-ar-2025). Its Board's role is to "discuss, set and govern policy intended to improve the City's libraries" (mississauga.ca/library/library-board/, fetched), and the system publishes a "Library Future Directions Plan" (2024) referenced on the board page.

Governance & reporting line

The 2022-2026 Board has nine members: two City Councillors (from Wards 4 and 8) and seven citizen appointees representing other wards, each serving four-year terms; the Director of Library Services (Rona O'Banion per the fetched page) is the primary staff contact for the Board (mississauga.ca/library/library-board/, fetched; registry row notes: "statutory library board 2 councillors plus 7 citizens"). The Board meets seven times annually and hears public delegations under set notice rules (mississauga.ca/library/library-board/, fetched).

Priority evolution

A 13-year, two-document gap cannot show a continuous evolution. What the two documents do establish: in 2012, the Library organized its work around the Board's four-year plan "Moving the Library Forward," with seven named strategies (Promoting Awareness, Celebrating our Community, Innovating Through Technology, Ensuring Library Space that Works, Providing Life-Long Learning Support, Offering Superior Service at a Reasonable Cost, Developing Board Effectiveness) and five named "key markets" (Youth, Older adults, New Canadians, Families and Caregivers, Small Business) (8fb8dc7d7351). The 2025 document contains no equivalent named strategic-plan structure — it is organized entirely around SROI value categories (Education, Economic Development, Civic Engagement, Space, Inclusion & Well-being, Culture, Entertainment & Leisure) rather than board-set strategic priorities, making direct like-for-like priority comparison impossible from this document alone (5f3b81e1a4b7).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed
