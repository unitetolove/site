Judicial Appointments Advisory Committee - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-judicial-appointments-advisory-committee (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Judicial Appointments Advisory Committee (JAAC) operates under the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43; its own Annual Report is submitted "pursuant to section 43 of the Courts of Justice Act" (JAAC Annual Report 2024-25, Letter of Transmittal, p.iv, https://www.ontariocourts.ca/ocj/files/open/JAAC-2024-2025-Ann-Rep.pdf, fetched this review). The Committee's original mandate, set out when it was announced as a pilot project on December 15, 1988 by then-Attorney General Ian Scott, was "First, to develop and recommend comprehensive, sound and useful criteria for selection of appointments to the judiciary, ensuring that the best candidates are considered; and second, to interview applicants selected by it or referred to it by the Attorney General and make recommendations" (same source, Introduction, p.vii).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

"All judges of the Ontario Court of Justice are appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council on the recommendation of the Attorney General from amongst a list of applicants recommended to him or her by the Committee" (JAAC Annual Report 2024-25, p.vii). In the 1 April 2024 - 31 March 2025 reporting period the Committee held 15 sessions over 28 meeting days, conducted 157 interviews for 142 candidates, made 175 recommendations (94 total candidates), and 32 judges were appointed from its recommendations (same source, Executive Summary, p.v). Since the Committee's inception in 1989 through March 2025, 589 judges have been appointed based on its recommendations (same source, Part I, p.1). The Committee also develops and reviews its own selection criteria, policies and outreach procedures (same source, Parts IV-VI).

Governance & reporting line

The Public Appointments Secretariat lists the Committee's website as its agency page (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/448, fetched this review). Per that source, the 14-member committee comprises two provincial judges (appointed by the Chief Justice of the Ontario Court of Justice), three lawyers (appointed by the Attorney General from lists submitted by legal organizations), seven non-judge, non-lawyer public members (appointed by the Attorney General), and one Judicial Council representative; the Attorney General designates the chair, who serves terms of up to three years and may be reappointed. On April 19, 2021, the Courts of Justice Act was amended to increase the minimum number of candidates recommended per vacancy from two to six, allow the Attorney General to appoint the three lawyer members directly from law-association-submitted lists, and require diversity statistics in annual reports (JAAC Annual Report 2024-25, Introduction, p.vii). The Committee reports/advises the Attorney General for Ontario (2024-25 transmittal addressed to the Honourable Doug Downey, Attorney General, p.iv).

Budget scale

⚠️ still being checked - no separately published annual operating budget was located this review; JAAC is a small advisory committee administratively supported within the Ministry of the Attorney General rather than an independent spending body. Its 2024-25 Annual Report lists per-member "Appointee Remuneration" (a standard per-diem disclosure for Ontario public appointees) but does not provide a consolidated committee operating-cost total (JAAC Annual Report 2024-25, Table of Contents entry "Appointee Remuneration," p.iii, https://www.ontariocourts.ca/ocj/files/open/JAAC-2024-2025-Ann-Rep.pdf).

Institutional history

The Committee began as a pilot project announced in the Ontario Legislature on December 15, 1988 by then-Attorney General Ian Scott, and "On February 28, 1995, the Courts of Justice Act established the Committee by legislation" (JAAC Annual Report 2024-25, Introduction, p.vii). It was substantially amended on April 19, 2021, expanding the minimum candidate slate per vacancy and adjusting lawyer-member appointment and diversity-reporting requirements (same source). The total number of applicants from the Committee's inception through March 31, 2025 was 4,564, of whom 589 judges have ultimately been appointed to the Ontario Court of Justice based on Committee recommendations (same source, p.vii and p.1).

Judicial Appointments Advisory Committee - strategy evolution

2026-08-05 / registry: on-judicial-appointments-advisory-committee / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: JAAC's archived record is unusually complete for a small advisory body — 25 annual reports covering calendar/fiscal years 2000 through 2025, missing only a standalone 2019 report because the Committee itself switched from calendar-year to fiscal-year reporting that year and bridged the gap with a 15-month report (ar-2020) rather than skipping a year of activity. The biggest priority addition across the quarter-century is formal diversity-statistics reporting, which evolved through three distinct steps: voluntary applicant self-identification added to the intake form in 2017, made a legal requirement by an April 19, 2021 statutory amendment, then re-scoped again in 2024 when "cultural identity" was legislatively removed as a category. The biggest quietly dropped element is the pre-2021 "at least two candidates per vacancy" recommendation floor, replaced by a six-candidate minimum with no fanfare beyond the statute text itself. The most load-bearing number is the Committee's own cumulative count: 589 judges appointed from its recommendations since 1989 inception, as of the newest report (ar-2025) — a monotonic, uninterrupted climb across all 25 documents with no discontinuities. One open question the record raises but cannot fully resolve: the backgrounder's stated 14-member committee composition does not match the "13 members" figure the Committee itself states verbatim in every single annual report from 2000 to 2025 — see Residuals.

Backgrounder summary

JAAC operates under the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43, submitting its Annual Report under s.43 (backgrounder, citing JAAC Annual Report 2024-25). It recommends candidates for Ontario Court of Justice judicial appointments to the Attorney General; as of the 2024-25 report, 589 judges had been appointed from its recommendations since inception, from 4,564 total applicants — both figures independently re-confirmed against ar-2025's own text (dad86befea9e). The backgrounder states the Committee began as a December 15, 1988 pilot project (Attorney General Ian Scott) and was established by legislation February 28, 1995, both re-confirmed. One correction to the backgrounder: it describes a "14-member committee" (2 provincial judges + 3 lawyers + 7 lay members + 1 Judicial Council representative, sourced from the Public Appointments Secretariat's agency page). That breakdown sums to 13, not 14, and every single annual report read for this brief — all 25, 2000 through 2025 — states verbatim "the Committee is to have 13 members of which the majority shall be lay persons" (e.g. eb82c682338e, dad86befea9e). This brief treats 13 as the correct, self-reported figure and flags the backgrounder's "14" as an apparent arithmetic error in its source, not a change over time.

The missing 2019 report is explained, not a data or capture gap. ar-2020 ("JAAC Annual Report 2019-2020," e207bb465909) states explicitly: "While the Committee's previous annual reports were by calendar year, the Committee will now be reporting by fiscal year (April 1 to March 31). In order to transition to fiscal year reporting, this annual report covers a 15-month period" (Jan 1, 2019 – Mar 31, 2020). ar-2021 onward reports clean fiscal years. This is a reporting-cadence change the Committee made itself, not a document that was never captured — the addendum's characterization of this series as a clean "2000-2025 full run" is correct in substance (no year of Committee activity is undocumented) even though 2019 has no standalone filing.

Priority evolution

2000-2005 (eb82c682338e through 3f0d0b4cc697): A formative, steady-state period. Minimum recommendation floor is "at least two" candidates per vacancy throughout. Criteria terminology shifts from "division" to "jurisdiction" (ar-2001). Outreach is modest and growing (~160 to ~205 associations notified by 2005). The Committee repeatedly expresses concern about declining new-applicant counts (ar-2004, ar-2005) and begins explicitly encouraging "qualified members of under-represented groups to apply" (ar-2005) — an early, general diversity-outreach statement, years before any formal data collection exists. Cumulative appointments climb from 178 (2000) to 240 (2005).

2006-2012 (c3a12c2316e4 through 7073c3759532): Outreach becomes geographically active for the first time — named regional interview trips begin (North Bay 2006, Thunder Bay 2007, Chatham/Sault Ste. Marie/Ottawa 2008), and Toronto starts being described explicitly as the default interview base with travel "outside of Toronto" as the marked exception. Criteria language shifts from "minority groups" to "equality-seeking groups" (ar-2008), alongside the first open-forum diversity event (with the Ontario Bar Association, Nov 2008) and a student-focused "Diversifying the Judiciary" event (ar-2009). Application/appointment volume fluctuates sharply year to year (6 appointments in 2007, 20 in 2006, 3 in 2010) with no superlative language used by the Committee itself despite the swings. Cumulative appointments reach 322 by 2012.

2013-2017 (08345351389a through c33415f4a978): A period of structural and leadership change. Long-serving Committee Secretary Priscilla Chu (since 1999) retires (ar-2013); a major leadership turnover follows in 2016, explicitly flagged by the Committee as "significant" — new Chair Fareed Amin replaces Hanny Hassan (12+ years as Chair), alongside several 12-year lay members rotating out (ar-2016, 458616dc3fd9). Terminology changes from "First Nations" to "Indigenous," applied retroactively to historical tables (ar-2016). A first legislative amendment package lands in 2017 (distinct from the better-known 2021 amendment): the Courts of Justice Act is amended to require tabling the annual report in the Legislative Assembly, let the Attorney General direct report content, and grant Committee members personal-liability immunity (ar-2017, c33415f4a978). The same year, July 6, 2017, the Committee adds a voluntary "Self-Identification Regarding Diversity" section to its application form — the first applicant-level diversity data collection in the series, four years before any legal requirement to do so. Appointments swing from 17 (2016) to 47 (2017), still without superlative framing.

2018-2021 (7a5f890f1dd8 through cc84b53e2ad3): ar-2018 introduces the first per-vacancy applications-received table. The 15-month bridge report (ar-2020, covering Jan 2019–Mar 2020) adds a dedicated "Diversity Statistics" section with a full applicant demographic table — but contains no mention of COVID-19 despite ending March 31, 2020. ar-2021 (covering Apr 2020–Mar 2021) is the pandemic-response document: "in response to the circumstances created by the COVID pandemic, the Committee transitioned from a paper-based to an electronic process for submission of applications... began to conduct interviews and hold meetings by videoconference." Advertising in the Ontario Reports print publication is discontinued (April 2020), moving to website-only. This period ends three weeks before the next major legislative change: the April 19, 2021 amendment to the Courts of Justice Act (s.43.1), which raises the minimum recommended-candidates floor from two to six, changes how the three lawyer members are appointed, and makes diversity-statistics reporting a legal requirement — none of which appears in ar-2021 itself, whose reporting period ends just before the amendment took effect.

2022-2025 (20f82dd41972 through dad86befea9e): ar-2022 (covering Apr 2021–Mar 2022, but published March 2024 after a roughly two-year publication lag) is the first report to quote the amended statute's "at least six candidates" language throughout, and its footnotes confirm diversity reporting is "now a legal requirement." Individual vacancies occasionally show fewer than six recommended candidates via a statutory shortfall exception (s.43.1(9)), not a residual old rule. March 6, 2024: a further statutory amendment removes "cultural identity" as a reportable diversity category, and the application form is revised again (Feb 2024) with modernized terminology ("2SLGBTQI+," "racial and ethnic minorities" replacing "LGBTQ2"/"visible and ethnic-cultural minorities"). A mandatory judicial-education undertaking is added to the Act (June 8, 2023), and standardized French-language proficiency testing is introduced (May 17, 2023). ar-2024 and ar-2025 both state that "all interviews and meetings have been taking place virtually since March 2020," with no reversion to in-person practice recorded through the newest report. ar-2025 documents the clearest expansion in the series: 32 judges appointed in one year (vs. 18-22 in each of the three prior years) following a May 30, 2024 Ontario government commitment to appoint at least 25 additional Ontario Court of Justice judges to address a criminal-case backlog — the Committee's own Executive Summary calls this "approximately 50% more appointments compared to each of the previous three reporting periods," the clearest superlative claim anywhere in the 25-year record. ar-2025 also reports two diversity-statistics tables running in parallel (legacy May-2023 form and new Feb-2024 form) due to a mid-year form transition, with the new form benchmarked against 2021 Census Ontario population data for the first time, plus a new social-media outreach channel (LinkedIn/X, from May 2024).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — voluntary applicant self-identification: first appears July 6, 2017 (ar-2017, c33415f4a978), a Committee-initiated practice, four years ahead of any legal requirement.
  • Added — mandatory diversity-statistics reporting: made a legal requirement by the April 19, 2021 amendment (first reflected in ar-2022's footnotes, 20f82dd41972); re-scoped again March 6, 2024 when "cultural identity" is legislatively dropped as a category (ar-2024, a05c462a483f).
  • Added — French-language proficiency testing: introduced May 17, 2023 (ar-2024, a05c462a483f), not present in any earlier report.
  • Added — mandatory judicial-education undertaking: added to the Act June 8, 2023 (ar-2024, a05c462a483f).
  • Added — social-media outreach (LinkedIn/X): first appears May 2024 (ar-2025, dad86befea9e).
  • Added — per-vacancy interview/recommendation reporting (not just applications received): first appears ar-2021 (cc84b53e2ad3); a bare applications-only table existed from ar-2018 (7a5f890f1dd8).
  • Quietly dropped/replaced — the "at least two" candidates-per-vacancy floor, used verbatim in every report from ar-2000 (eb82c682338e) through ar-2021 (cc84b53e2ad3, whose reporting period ends just before the change), replaced by a six-candidate minimum under the April 19, 2021 amendment with no narrative fanfare — the change is visible only in the statute-quoting language of ar-2022 onward (20f82dd41972).
  • Quietly dropped — in-person interviews and meetings, universal through ar-2020 (e207bb465909), replaced by videoconference from ar-2021 (cc84b53e2ad3) onward with no stated reversion through the newest report, ar-2025 (dad86befea9e) — "the Committee may review this practice in future" is the only forward-looking language offered.
  • Renamed — demographic terminology, in three separate steps: "minority groups" → "equality-seeking groups" (ar-2008, 7012ef9c449f); "First Nations" → "Indigenous" (ar-2016, 458616dc3fd9, applied retroactively to historical tables); "LGBTQ2" → "2SLGBTQI+" and "visible/ethnic-cultural minorities" → "racial and ethnic minorities" (ar-2024, a05c462a483f).
  • Renamed/legislated — the 2017 amendment package (tabling requirement, AG content-direction power, member liability immunity) is distinct from and precedes the better-known 2021 amendment (minimum-candidates increase, lawyer-appointment process, diversity-reporting requirement) — the two are easily conflated but are separate legislative events three-plus years apart (ar-2017, c33415f4a978; ar-2022, 20f82dd41972).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • February 28, 1995 — Committee established by legislation (backgrounder; re-confirmed across the series' repeated institutional-history boilerplate).
  • 2017 legislative amendment — tabling requirement, AG content-direction power, member liability immunity (ar-2017, c33415f4a978).
  • July 6, 2017 — voluntary applicant self-identification added to intake form (ar-2017, c33415f4a978).
  • April 19, 2021 — minimum recommended-candidates floor raised from two to six; lawyer-member appointment process changed; diversity-statistics reporting made a legal requirement (backgrounder; ar-2022, 20f82dd41972).
  • March 2020-March 2021 — full pivot to electronic applications and videoconference interviews/meetings in response to COVID-19 (ar-2021, cc84b53e2ad3); no stated reversion through ar-2025.
  • June 8, 2023 — mandatory judicial-education undertaking added to the Act (ar-2024, a05c462a483f).
  • March 6, 2024 — "cultural identity" removed as a legislated diversity-reporting category; application form revised (ar-2024, a05c462a483f).
  • May 30, 2024 — Ontario government commits to appointing at least 25 additional Ontario Court of Justice judges to address a case backlog, driving the ~50% single-year appointment surge reported in ar-2025 (dad86befea9e).
  • Budget/operating cost: the backgrounder found no consolidated committee operating-cost total, and nothing in the 25 annual reports read for this brief discloses one either — JAAC discloses per-member appointee remuneration (a standard Ontario public-appointee disclosure) but never a consolidated committee budget figure across the entire series.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Direct: JAAC recommends judicial candidates for the Ontario Court of Justice province-wide, and Toronto is consistently the single largest-volume location in the record by every measure available. A per-vacancy applications-received table begins ar-2018 (7a5f890f1dd8); interview and recommendation columns are added starting ar-2021 (cc84b53e2ad3), and from that year forward, individual Toronto-area vacancies (most often multiple concurrent "Toronto (Criminal)" competitions, plus Family and Bilingual variants) are consistently the highest-volume rows in the province by applications, interviews, and recommendations — e.g. ar-2025's Toronto (Criminal)(4) vacancy drew 176 applications, 49 interviewed, 29 recommended (dad86befea9e), the largest single-vacancy figures in that year's table. The Committee never publishes a rolled-up "Toronto region" or "GTA" aggregate, however — each vacancy is reported as a discrete row, so no single Toronto-wide total exists anywhere in the series. Toronto is also the Committee's default interview base throughout (first made explicit ar-2006, c3a12c2316e4, "outside of Toronto" framing for regional travel), and new Family Law Service Centre-adjacent GTA courthouse developments (e.g. the SCJ virtual practice court at 361 University Ave.) appear incidentally in later reports' operational detail.
