Ministry for Seniors and Accessibility - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-seniors-accessibility (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

No dedicated "Ministry for Seniors and Accessibility Act" was found on e-Laws (searches for "Ministry of Seniors Affairs Act" and "Ministry for Seniors and Accessibility Act" returned only unrelated Ministry statutes) ⚠️ still being checked — absence not exhaustively proven. Ontario ministries generally exist by order-in-council under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.25, s.2(1)-(2), which empowers the Lieutenant Governor to "appoint...ministers of the Crown" and "prescribe the duties of the ministers...and the duties of any ministries" (fetched and verified, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e25). The Ministry directly administers five statutes: Retirement Homes Act, 2010, S.O. 2010, c. 11 (current, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/10r11); Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, 2005, S.O. 2005, c. 11 (current, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/05a11); Ontarians with Disabilities Act, 2001; Seniors Active Living Centres Act, 2017; and Senior Volunteer Appreciation Week Act, 2021 (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-seniors-accessibility).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

For seniors: disseminating healthy-aging/program information, recognizing community contributions, promoting social inclusion and age-friendly communities, and overseeing the Retirement Homes Regulatory Authority. For accessibility: embedding accessibility into government policy, developing standards and educational resources, and enforcing AODA compliance (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-seniors-accessibility; registry row on-ministry-seniors-accessibility).

Governance & reporting line

Headed by a Minister with a Deputy Minister as deputy head, in the standard Westminster ministry structure; oversees the Retirement Homes Regulatory Authority (a delegated administrative authority) and the Accessibility Standards Advisory Council (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-seniors-accessibility).

Budget scale

$181.11 million total planned operating expenditure for 2025-26 (no capital), down 2.6% from $185.95 million in 2024-25 estimates (ontario.ca Ministry for Seniors and Accessibility published plans and annual reports 2025-2026, https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-seniors-and-accessibility).

Ministry for Seniors and Accessibility - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: on-ministry-seniors-accessibility / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: Across three consecutive published-plans-and-annual-reports (FY2023-24 through FY2025-26), the Ministry's four-part strategic-priority framework quietly dropped "organizational and operational excellence" after the 2023-24 edition, leaving three priorities from 2024-25 onward. The standout growth story is the Seniors Active Living Centres (SALC) Program, which nearly tripled from 299 to 413 programs on a newly announced $17 million/3-year investment, while reported "program participants served" jumped from 353,654 (2022-23) to 997,475 (2023-24) — a discontinuity the ministry itself attributes partly to improved SALC reporting mechanics rather than pure growth. Planned operating expenditure declined modestly across the series: $184.9M (2023-24 est.) → $185.65M (2024-25 est.) → $181.11M (2025-26 est., down 2.6%). One open question: whether the "organizational and operational excellence" priority was formally retired or simply folded into the other three, since no report states a reason for its disappearance.

Backgrounder summary

The Ministry for Seniors and Accessibility (MSAA) has no dedicated founding statute located on e-Laws; it exists via order-in-council under the Executive Council Act. It directly administers five statutes: the Retirement Homes Act, 2010; the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, 2005 (AODA); the Ontarians with Disabilities Act, 2001; the Seniors Active Living Centres Act, 2017; and the Senior Volunteer Appreciation Week Act, 2021. It oversees the Retirement Homes Regulatory Authority (RHRA, a delegated administrative authority) and the Accessibility Standards Advisory Council. Planned 2025-26 operating expenditure was $181.11 million, down 2.6% from $185.95 million in 2024-25 — figures confirmed independently in the archived annual reports below. No authoritative source for the Ministry's founding year or predecessor-portfolio lineage was located in Wave 1.

Priority evolution

2023-24 plan / 2022-23 results (ar-2024, 41877dd8a865): Vision framed around removing/preventing accessibility barriers and supporting older Ontarians' safety, independence, and engagement. Four named key priorities: "Fostering an inclusive and accessible Ontario," "Supporting quality of life and well-being of seniors and people with disabilities," "Promoting modern regulation," and "Achieving organizational and operational excellence including user centric experience." Ministry organized into two divisions (Policy/Program/Strategic Partnerships; Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities). Named 2023 budget additions: $1.5M for the Seniors Community Grant program and ~$1.0M over three years for the Seniors Safety Line. 2022-23 results cited: 299 SALC programs, $14.1M invested; $5.8M through Seniors Community Grants (299 projects); Ontario Community Support Program deliveries reached 2.3M cumulative since April 2020.

2024-25 plan / 2023-24 results (ar-2025, a42c8cd5d3ea): The fourth priority ("organizational and operational excellence") is absent — the strategic plan now names only three priorities: "Fostering an inclusive and accessible Ontario," "Supporting quality of life and well-being of seniors and people with disabilities," and "Promoting modern regulation." Programs are newly framed as delivered through two Transfer Payment Agreement (TPA) streams (annual grants; strategic initiative/partnership programs), with a fuller named-program list than the prior report (SCG, SALC, ECP, ICG grant streams; HVMP, EAPO, FARFO, Alzheimer Society's Finding Your Way, Queen's University's Age Friendly Communities Outreach, OACAO, and the Assaulted Women's Helpline-operated Seniors Safety Line as strategic-partnership programs). 2023-24 results: SALC expanded from 299 to 316 programs (17 new, $14.5M total); "program participants served by MSAA programs and funding" jumped to 997,475 from 353,654 the prior year, against a 2024-25 target of 785,000 (already exceeded) — the report flags this discontinuity is due partly to the SALC program's data-collection standardization taking effect this year (metrics previously varied inconsistently across ~300 SALC operators).

2025-26 plan / 2024-25 results (ar-2026, c9c23dfab425): Same three-priority structure retained, restated with slightly different verbs ("developing, implementing and enforcing the AODA" folded into priority one). SALC expansion continues sharply: 316 → 413 programs (97 new in 2024-25), funded by a newly disclosed $17 million/three-year investment beginning 2024-25, plus a 10% increase to per-program Maintenance and Operating funding caps (to $55,000/program) and an expanded eligible-operator list (registered charities, Legions, Lions clubs added alongside municipalities/non-profits). MSAA "program participants" metric reaches 1,000,000 as the new 2025-26 target (2023-24 actual: 997,475). Compliance-audit volume rose sharply: 1,285 audits (2023-24) → 1,855 audits (2024-25).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Dropped — "Achieving organizational and operational excellence including user centric experience": present as a fourth named key priority in ar-2024 (41877dd8a865, FY2023-24 plan); absent from ar-2025 (a42c8cd5d3ea) and ar-2026 (c9c23dfab425), which name only three priorities. No report states a reason for the drop; ⚠️ still being checked whether it was formally retired, subsumed into the remaining three, or simply not restated as boilerplate. This is the one 3-document series's clearest "quietly dropped" case.
  • Added — Transfer Payment Agreement (TPA) program-delivery framing: absent from ar-2024's "Ministry programs" section (which lists divisional roles, not a TPA taxonomy); introduced in ar-2025 (a42c8cd5d3ea) as "two different streams of Transfer Payment Agreements — annual grant programs and on-going strategic initiative and partnership programs," with a full named-program roster; retained in ar-2026 (c9c23dfab425).
  • Added — explicit "modern regulatory principles" framing for AODA compliance: ar-2026 (c9c23dfab425) is the first of the three reports to explicitly name the five IASR (Integrated Accessibility Standards Regulation) compliance areas (information and communications; employment; transportation; design of public spaces; customer service) and to describe compliance activity as guided by "modern regulatory principles" prioritizing support-before-enforcement; ar-2024 and ar-2025 describe compliance activity in less structured terms.
  • Renamed — SALC expanded-operator eligibility: ar-2026 (c9c23dfab425) discloses that eligible SALC-funding recipients were expanded beyond "incorporated, not-for-profit organizations and municipalities" (the framing used in ar-2024 and ar-2025) to explicitly include registered charities, Legions, and Lions clubs — tied to the $17M/3-year SALC expansion.
  • Growing — SALC Program scale: 299 programs (ar-2024, results FY2022-23) → 316 programs (ar-2025, results FY2023-24) → 413 programs (ar-2026, results FY2024-25). This is the single largest program-scale change visible across the series.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2023 provincial budget — additional $1.5M for Seniors Community Grant program and ~$1.0M over three years for Seniors Safety Line, first disclosed in ar-2024 (41877dd8a865).
  • FY2023-24 — SALC data-collection standardization implemented, materially inflating the reported "program participants served" metric from 353,654 to 997,475 in the same year (ar-2025, a42c8cd5d3ea) — the ministry attributes this explicitly to improved measurement, not solely to program growth.
  • FY2024-25, beginning year — $17 million/three-year SALC Program expansion investment announced, funding 97 new centres in year one alone and a 10% per-program funding-cap increase (ar-2026, c9c23dfab425).
  • Planned operating expenditure: $184.9M (2023-24 est.) → $185.65M (2024-25 est., +0.4%) → $181.11M (2025-26 est., -2.6%) — a essentially flat-to-declining budget trend even as the SALC program scale nearly grew by 40% in the same window, and the "program participants" KPI target grew from 785,000 to 1,000,000.
  • Deputy Minister turnover across the series: Martha Greenberg (ar-2024, April 2023 org chart) → Melissa Thomson (ar-2025, April 2024 org chart) → Peter Kaftarian, Interim Deputy Minister (ar-2026, June 2025 org chart). The Minister (Raymond Sung Joon Cho) is constant across all three reports; ⚠️ still being checked reason for the Deputy Minister's "interim" status as of the most recent report.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

All three reports describe a province-wide mandate (Ontario-wide SALC, SCG, HVMP, EAPO, and AODA-compliance programs) rather than city-specific Toronto programming; no Toronto-specific office, program, or funding line is named in any of the three documents. The ministry works with the Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO) on Age-Friendly Communities sessions "for municipalities" generally (ar-2025, a42c8cd5d3ea; ar-2026, c9c23dfab425) — Toronto is not named as a distinct participant. Toronto/GTA relevance is therefore indirect: as Ontario's largest municipality it is a de facto major recipient of province-wide SALC/SCG/HVMP/AODA-compliance activity, and its many obligated organizations (businesses, non-profits, public-sector bodies) fall within AODA compliance-audit scope, but the archived series contains no Toronto-specific figures, sites, or program carve-outs to cite.
