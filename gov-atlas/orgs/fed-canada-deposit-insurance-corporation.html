Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-canada-deposit-insurance-corporation (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) was established in 1967 by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation Act (the CDIC Act) and is a Crown corporation without share capital named in Part I of Schedule III to the Financial Administration Act (CDIC 2025 Annual Report, Notes to the financial statements, note 1, https://www.cdic.ca/wp-content/uploads/cdic-2025-annual-report.pdf). Its statutory objects are "to provide insurance against the loss of part or all of deposits in member institutions; to promote and otherwise contribute to the stability of the financial system in Canada; to pursue these objects for the benefit of depositors of member institutions and in such manner as will minimize the exposure of the Corporation to loss; and to act as the resolution authority for its member institutions" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CDIC insures deposits at member financial institutions (banks, federally regulated credit unions, trust and loan companies) and has the power to acquire assets from and provide guarantees or loans to member institutions, inspect member institutions, act as liquidator/receiver/inspector, establish a bridge institution, and convert debt of a failing domestic systemically important bank into common shares to recapitalize it (CDIC 2025 Annual Report, Notes, note 1, https://www.cdic.ca/wp-content/uploads/cdic-2025-annual-report.pdf). As of April 30, 2024, deposits insured by CDIC totalled $1,241 billion, up 4.9% year-over-year, and CDIC has resolved 43 member failures to date, protecting $26 billion in insured deposits (CDIC 2025 Annual Report, pp.1, 17, same URL). It is funded by member-institution premiums, not taxpayer dollars (https://www.cdic.ca/about-us/).

Governance & reporting line

CDIC's Board of Directors comprises a chairperson, six other private-sector directors, and six ex officio directors (the CDIC CEO, the Governor of the Bank of Canada, the Deputy Minister of Finance, the Commissioner of the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and a Deputy Superintendent of Financial Institutions or another OSFI officer) (CDIC 2025 Annual Report, Part 3 - Corporate Governance, "Board of Directors", https://www.cdic.ca/wp-content/uploads/cdic-2025-annual-report.pdf). CDIC sits within the federal financial-safety-net institutional structure alongside OSFI, the Bank of Canada and the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, under the Minister of Finance and National Revenue and ultimately Parliament (same source, "Institutional Structure" diagram).

Budget scale

Premium revenue (CDIC's primary funding source, assessed against member institutions) was $986 million for fiscal 2024-25, up $95 million (11%) from the prior year; total revenue for fiscal 2024-25 was $1,080 million (CDIC 2025 Annual Report, Part 1 MD&A, https://www.cdic.ca/wp-content/uploads/cdic-2025-annual-report.pdf). The ex ante fund (CDIC's accumulated deposit-insurance reserve) stood at $10.2 billion, or 82 basis points of insured deposits, as at March 31, 2025 (same source).

Institutional history

CDIC was established as a federal Crown corporation in 1967 by the CDIC Act (CDIC 2025 Annual Report, p.1 and Notes note 1, https://www.cdic.ca/wp-content/uploads/cdic-2025-annual-report.pdf; https://www.cdic.ca/about-us/).

Backgrounder summary

CDIC was established in 1967 by the CDIC Act as a Crown corporation without share capital (Financial Administration Act Schedule III, Part I). Its statutory objects are to insure eligible deposits at member institutions, contribute to financial-system stability, minimize the Corporation's own loss exposure, and act as the resolution authority for its members. CDIC insured $1,241B in deposits as of April 30, 2024 (up 4.9% year-over-year) and has resolved 43 member failures since 1967, protecting $26B in insured deposits; it is funded entirely by member-institution premiums, not taxpayer dollars (backgrounder, citing CDIC 2025 Annual Report).

Priority evolution

2018 (bp-2018, d4da6a8cd438): Four strategic objectives: (1) Preparedness — advancing resolution readiness, including a multi-year plan toward providing insured-deposit access within seven days of a failure; (2) Deposit insurance program — modernizing by-laws and legislation; (3) Stakeholders — public awareness (target 60-65%, then at 49-55%) and member-relationship strengthening; (4) Organization — evolving the workplace. Bail-in resolution powers for domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs) were coming into force in 2018 following June 2017 legislative amendments; the ex ante deposit-insurance fund stood at 55 basis points of insured deposits against a 100-basis-point target set by a 2011 funding strategy.

2019 (ar-2019, 49dfd0dc14ff): Leadership renewal (new President and CEO Peter D. Routledge, five new private-sector directors). A new, more ambitious reimbursement goal is named: "T+0" — building, within five years, a system capable of reimbursing depositors on the day of member failure. A new Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) function is created (April 1, 2019) with CDIC's first Chief Risk Officer (appointed late 2018) and a new Board Risk Committee (March 2019), reframed as a "second line of defence."

2020-2021 (ar-2021, d46285c62a8f): The first phase of a legislated deposit-insurance-coverage modernization (Royal Assent June 2018, from a review announced in Budget 2014) came into force April 30, 2020: coverage extended to eligible deposits held in foreign currency and to deposits with terms greater than five years. The Payout Modernization Project was launched in 2020 as a multi-year technology initiative described as "the largest financial investment in CDIC's history," aimed at delivering the T+0/rapid-reimbursement goal. The second coverage-modernization phase (separate RDSP/RESP coverage categories, removal of separate mortgage-tax-account coverage, enhanced trust-deposit disclosure rules) was delayed by the pandemic from its original April 30, 2021 date to April 30, 2022. The ex ante fund stood at 67 basis points as at March 31, 2021, with a funding plan projecting the 100-basis-point target reached by 2027/2028.

2023 (bp-2023, 359e892b2f54): Strategic objectives are condensed from four to three: "Be resolution ready," "Reinforce trust in depositor protection," "Strengthen organizational resilience." An Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) strategy is formally launched, including work toward Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) compliance. A new First Home Savings Account (FHSA) deposit category is added (effective April 1, 2023, following Budget 2022/Fall Economic Statement legislation). The ex ante fund is at 73 basis points (Dec 2022).

2025 (ar-2025, e50f164e7f08), Chair's nine-year retrospective (2016-2025): The most consequential reversal in the readable series: in late 2024, following a comprehensive review, CDIC cancelled the Payout Modernization Project — the multi-year, "largest financial investment in CDIC's history" initiative from 2020 — after determining "the solution was not meeting our requirements," reverting to bolstering the existing legacy payout system instead. The same report records the Differential Premiums By-law expanded from four to five risk-based premium categories (effective April 1, 2025), the conclusion of a Deposit Insurance Study (launched 2023) with findings submitted to Finance, and a full ESG framework (Environmental/Social/Governance triangle) now embedded in reporting. Strategic objectives are now framed as three: Depositor trust and confidence / Resolution readiness / Organizational strength. Ex ante fund at 82 basis points (March 2025).

2026-27 (bp-2026, f8e071c1ec0d): Strategic priorities expand back to four, reworded around a "refocusing on core activities, capabilities, and disciplines" theme: Advance crisis readiness (with a much less ambitious "48-hour open-bank resolution readiness" target replacing the earlier T+0/7-day goals), Preserve public confidence, Elevate the use of data (new — explicit AI-and-analytics-era objective, paired with a "Data Platform Modernization" initiative and a requirement to "seek ministry approval prior to any use of artificial intelligence"), and Evolve the deposit insurance framework (following an August 2025 Finance-department review launched off CDIC's own 2024 Deposit Insurance Study findings). The plan explicitly incorporates government-wide "Comprehensive Expenditure Review" spending cuts: the 2026-27 operating budget of $73.6M is down from an $85M 2025-26 forecast. The ex ante fund's near-term target is now 85 basis points (expected reached H2 2025-26), explicitly framed as a milestone en route to a to-be-reviewed "updated fund size target" — the original 100-basis-point target's status after this review is not established from documents read.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — Enterprise Risk Management function: created April 2019 (49dfd0dc14ff) with a dedicated Chief Risk Officer and Board Risk Committee, "transforming" through the pandemic years into a "three lines of defence" model.
  • Added — ESG / climate-disclosure strategy: absent from bp-2018; formally launched by bp-2023 (359e892b2f54), fully embedded (dedicated Environmental/Social/Governance framework diagram, TCFD appendix) by ar-2025 (e50f164e7f08).
  • Added — "Elevate the use of data" strategic priority: new for bp-2026 (f8e071c1ec0d) — no equivalent named objective in any earlier document read this review; tied explicitly to AI-era data-platform modernization.
  • Added, then cancelled — Payout Modernization Project / "T+0" same-day reimbursement: goal first named ar-2019 (five-year "audacious goal"); project launched 2020 as CDIC's largest-ever financial investment (per ar-2021, d46285c62a8f); cancelled late 2024 after determining the solution didn't meet requirements (ar-2025, e50f164e7f08); replaced in bp-2026 by a markedly less ambitious 48-hour open-bank-resolution readiness target.
  • Renamed/restructured — strategic-objective count: four (2018-2019) → three (2023-2025, "Be resolution ready / Reinforce trust in depositor protection / Strengthen organizational resilience," later "Depositor trust and confidence / Resolution readiness / Organizational strength") → four again (2026-27, "Advance crisis readiness / Preserve public confidence / Elevate the use of data / Evolve the deposit insurance framework"). The underlying scope (resolution preparedness, public trust/awareness, organizational capacity) persists throughout; the count and exact wording do not.
  • Quietly under revision — ex ante fund target: a fixed 100-basis-point target has governed funding strategy since 2011 (bp-2018, d4da6a8cd438); by bp-2026 the plan speaks only of an 85-basis-point "near-term" target with a formal review to follow (f8e071c1ec0d). ⚠️ still being checked whether 100bps remains the eventual target or is being revised downward — not resolved by the documents read.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • June 2017 / 2018 — CDIC formally designated resolution authority for all members including D-SIBs; bail-in regime brought into force (bp-2018, d4da6a8cd438).
  • June 21, 2018 — Royal Assent for CDIC Act amendments modernizing the deposit-insurance-coverage framework (from a review announced in Budget 2014), phased in April 30, 2020 and (delayed by COVID from 2021 to) April 30, 2022 (ar-2021, d46285c62a8f).
  • 2020 — Payout Modernization Project launched as CDIC's largest-ever financial investment (ar-2021, d46285c62a8f).
  • April 1, 2023 — First Home Savings Account (FHSA) added as a new separate-coverage deposit category (bp-2023, 359e892b2f54).
  • Late 2024 — Payout Modernization Project cancelled after a comprehensive review found the solution inadequate (ar-2025, e50f164e7f08).
  • April 1, 2025 — Differential Premiums System expanded from four to five risk-based premium categories (ar-2025, e50f164e7f08).
  • August 2025 — Department of Finance launched a federal review of the deposit-insurance framework, building on CDIC's own concluded Deposit Insurance Study (bp-2026, f8e071c1ec0d).
  • 2026-27 — Government-wide Comprehensive Expenditure Review reduces CDIC's operating budget ($85M forecast 2025-26 → $73.6M budgeted 2026-27); ex ante fund near-term target set at 85bps pending a formal review of the ultimate fund-size target (bp-2026, f8e071c1ec0d).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Indirect but structurally present: CDIC is a federal Crown corporation with dual head/Toronto offices (head office in Ottawa; a standing Toronto office at 1200-79 Wellington Street West, named in every document read this review) and insures deposits at Ontario-headquartered D-SIBs and other member institutions operating across the GTA. Ontario-specific policy content (e.g., Ontario housing-market risk) appears only as one input among national economic-risk factors in the Membership/risk-assessment sections, not as an Ontario-targeted program. This is a national deposit-insurance mandate with a physical but administrative Toronto presence, not a GTA-focused institution.
