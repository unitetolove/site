Council of the College of Registered Psychotherapists and Registered Mental Health Therapists of Ontario - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Wave 1 / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-council-college-registered-psychotherapists-regi (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The College (operating publicly as "CRPO") is established under the Psychotherapy Act, 2007, S.O. 2007, c. 10, Sched. R (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/07p10, fetched and verified — Schedule R confirmed as the Psychotherapy Act, 2007), which operates within the framework of the Regulated Health Professions Act, 1991 (https://crpo.ca/about-us/, fetched, confirming "CRPO was established under the Psychotherapy Act, 2007"). Its mandate is to regulate registrants "in the interest of protecting the public" (https://crpo.ca/about-us/governance/, fetched). The registry's full legal name includes "Registered Mental Health Therapists," but material fetched this pass describes CRPO's regulatory scope as covering Registered Psychotherapists only (⚠️ VERIFY current RMHT scope/status).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CRPO regulates Registered Psychotherapists, who provide talk-based therapy to help individuals, couples and families with mental health and well-being, and administers the "Controlled Act of Psychotherapy" under the RHPA framework (https://www.crpo.ca/, fetched). As of its most recent annual report, CRPO oversees "fifteen thousand and counting registrants" (CRPO Annual Report 2024-2025, doc_id on-council-college-registered-psychotherapists-regi-ar-2025).

Governance & reporting line

CRPO is governed by a Council of nine elected registrant members plus additional public members appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council, per the Psychotherapy Act, 2007 (https://crpo.ca/about-us/governance/, fetched). As a health regulatory college, CRPO does not report to a ministry parent in the ordinary departmental sense; registry row shows no parent_org (registry row on-council-college-registered-psychotherapists-regi), consistent with self-regulating college status under the RHPA/Psychotherapy Act framework.

Budget scale

~$9.5M total revenue ($9,482,861) against ~$7.4M total expenses ($7,430,630) for the year ended March 31, 2025, yielding an excess of revenue over expenses of $2,052,231; registrant fees were the largest revenue line at $7,537,479 (CRPO Annual Report 2024-2025, Statement of Financial Position/financial statements, doc_id on-council-college-registered-psychotherapists-regi-ar-2025, fetched).

Institutional history

Established by the Psychotherapy Act, 2007 (S.O. 2007, c. 10, Sched. R) within the Regulated Health Professions Act, 1991 co-regulatory scheme (statute, fetched); the exact year the College began active regulation/proclamation of the Act was not confirmed this pass (⚠️ VERIFY). The homepage still displays the legacy name "CRPO" (College of Registered Psychotherapists of Ontario) despite the registry's updated legal name (registry row notes field).

Council of the College of Registered Psychotherapists and Registered Mental Health Therapists of Ontario - strategy evolution

TL;DR: The biggest addition is independent discipline adjudication: piloted as the "Health Professions Discipline Tribunal Pilot" in April 2023 and formalized by 2024-25 as the "Ontario Registered Psychotherapists Discipline Tribunal," moving hearings away from an all-Council-member model toward non-Council adjudicators. Backgrounder summary

CRPO (operating publicly under the legacy brand "College of Registered Psychotherapists of Ontario," though its registry legal name adds "and Registered Mental Health Therapists of Ontario") is established under the Psychotherapy Act, 2007 (S.O. 2007, c. 10, Sched. R), operating within the Regulated Health Professions Act, 1991 (RHPA) co-regulatory framework. Its mandate is to regulate registrants "in the interest of protecting the public." It is governed by a Council of elected registrant members plus publicly appointed members and, as a self-regulating health college, has no ministry parent in the ordinary departmental sense, though it is statutorily accountable to the Ministry of Health (later Ministry of Health and Long-Term Care references appear in the archived series). As of its most recent annual report (FY2024-25), CRPO reported "fifteen thousand and counting registrants" and total revenue of ~$9.5M against ~$7.4M expenses.

Priority evolution

2016-17 (ar-2017, 9f69fea7bc93), "CRPO's second year following proclamation": Framing centres on foundational build-out — first registration renewal cycle, launch of the Quality Assurance (QA) Program's Peer and Practice Review (PPR) tools, and the College's first three Discipline Committee hearings. Report structure is a straightforward Mission/Vision/Values front matter followed by a "Dashboard" of registration/QA/complaints statistics and per-committee (statutory and non-statutory) narrative reports. CRPO frames itself explicitly as "a relatively new regulatory body," one of "26 health regulatory bodies in the province of Ontario" under the RHPA, accountable to the Ministry of Health and Long-Term Care and to the Fairness Commissioner of Ontario (9f69fea7bc93).

2019-20 (ar-2020, c29455f600b8), themed around "right touch regulation": A marked strategic-framework shift. This is the first report in the archived set to name a formal, dated strategic plan: Council's first-ever strategic planning session (September 2019) produced the three-year plan "Fostering Excellence, Trustworthiness and Accessibility" (2020-2023), informed by an independent governance review (June 2019, "Situational Analysis on the State of Governance"). The same report introduces a formal five-point "Regulatory Objectives" framework (serve/protect the public interest; promote confidence in professional regulation; regulate transparently/proportionately/unbiasedly/proactively; promote equity, diversity and inclusion; promote access to mental health services) — a structure that persists, essentially unchanged, through every later report in this set. The report also documents CRPO's initial COVID-19 response (payment-deadline extensions, remote-proctored exams, pandemic practice guidance) as an emergent, late-in-year priority (c29455f600b8).

2020-21 (ar-2021, 1184adc8a57f), "Modernizing Governance Practices": Governance modernization becomes the full report theme. Council approved competency matrices for Council/committee composition, began Council-effectiveness evaluation with an external consultant, and undertook early equity-diversity-inclusion (EDI) steps, including a targeted call for BIPOC candidates for committee appointments. COVID-19 response continues as a named highlight section (12 bulletins, remote exam sittings, live-streamed Council meetings). Report also documents completion of Ontario's first sector-wide College Performance Measurement Framework (CPMF) report, a Ministry-mandated cross-college accountability instrument (1184adc8a57f).

2022-23 (ar-2023, fdcb3d48616f): DEI work formalizes into a standing structure: a 15-member Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Working Group (DEI WG) is established following a "proto-group"-led recruitment process. The report names a second strategic planning session (September 2022; the first was 2019, confirming the ar-2020 dating). A significant discipline-process reform is piloted: the "Health Professions Discipline Tribunal Pilot," beginning April 2023, introducing non-Council, experienced adjudicators as Discipline Committee hearing-panel chairs, explicitly aimed at increasing separation between Council and discipline adjudication. The Registrar's message also discloses a new external, third-party review of the College's complaints/reports processes, framed around a "trauma-informed approach" (fdcb3d48616f).

2024-25 (ar-2025, 6b22091c30be), CRPO's tenth-anniversary-of-regulation report: The Discipline Tribunal reform (piloted 2022-23) is completed and formalized: the "Ontario Registered Psychotherapists Discipline Tribunal" (ORPDT) is named as CRPO's new, adopted discipline-hearing body, described as part of the province-wide Health Professions Discipline Tribunals system, accompanied by By-Law amendments. The DEI Working Group's earlier ad hoc footing gives way to a structured "DEI Framework" (seven strategic "Roots"), developed by a dedicated DEI Task Group explicitly in preparation for a 2026-2029 strategic plan cycle, plus a new internal staff-led "IDEA" (Inclusion, Diversity & Equity Alliance) committee. A new, unplanned regulatory-adjacent workstream appears: CRPO's response to a 2024 Canada Revenue Agency HST-exemption change for psychotherapy services, requiring registrant-facing billing guidance. The report also documents an Indigenous-registration-pathway consultation launched this year and continued implementation of recommendations from the "Trauma Informed Review" of complaints/reports handling first disclosed in ar-2023 (6b22091c30be).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • January 1, 2020 — the "controlled act of psychotherapy" comes into full legal effect under the RHPA framework, restricting performance of psychotherapy as a controlled act to CRPO registrants and members of five other named Ontario health colleges (c29455f600b8) — the single largest scope-of-practice inflection point in the series, though it lands just before/at the edge of the ar-2020 reporting year.
  • March 2020 — COVID-19 pandemic onset disclosed at the close of the ar-2020 fiscal year (year end March 31, 2020), triggering emergency payment-deadline and process flexibilities that carry into and dominate parts of ar-2021 (c29455f600b8; 1184adc8a57f).
  • September 2019 — Council's first-ever strategic planning session, producing the 2020-2023 strategic plan (retrospectively named in c29455f600b8).
  • September 2022 — second Council strategic planning session (fdcb3d48616f).
  • April 2023 — Health Professions Discipline Tribunal Pilot begins (fdcb3d48616f).
  • 2024, ongoing into 2025 — By-law amendments passed formalizing the Ontario Registered Psychotherapists Discipline Tribunal (ORPDT) as CRPO's adopted discipline-hearing model (6b22091c30be).
  • 2024 — Canada Revenue Agency HST-exemption change for psychotherapy services triggers new registrant-guidance workstream (6b22091c30be).
  • Registrant-fee revenue growth: Membership/registrant fee revenue rose from $2,582,555 total revenue (ar-2017, of which membership fees were $1,966,654) to $9,482,861 total revenue (ar-2025, of which registrant fees were $7,537,479) — reflecting the registrant base growing from roughly 4,004 (as at March 31, 2017, per ar-2017 dashboard) to approximately 15,314 (as at Dec. 31, 2024, per ar-2025) over the archived series (9f69fea7bc93; 6b22091c30be). Ontario/Toronto relevance

CRPO's operations are Ontario-wide by statutory design — the Psychotherapy Act, 2007 is Ontario provincial legislation, and every annual report in the series reports registrant counts broken out by Ontario region/district (Western, Northern, Eastern, Central East, Southwestern, Central West, Central, and Out-of-Province) (1184adc8a57f; fdcb3d48616f; 6b22091c30be). CRPO's registered office/headquarters address, given consistently across the series' financial statements and contact pages, is 375 University Avenue, Toronto, ON M5G 2J5 (1184adc8a57f; fdcb3d48616f; 6b22091c30be) — placing CRPO's physical institutional presence squarely in downtown Toronto. The "Central" district (District 7), which multiple reports' regional breakdowns suggest corresponds to the Greater Toronto Area, is consistently the largest single registrant region by count and share (e.g., 4,016 registrants / 26.22% of the total in ar-2025, up from 3,122 / 27.4% in ar-2023) (fdcb3d48616f; 6b22091c30be) — though the reports do not further break out Toronto proper from the wider Central district. CRPO's auditor (Crowe Soberman LLP) is also Toronto-based per the ar-2017 audit opinion signature block (9f69fea7bc93).
