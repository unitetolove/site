Canadian Heritage - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canadian-heritage (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Canadian Heritage is established as a federal department under the Department of Canadian Heritage Act, S.C. 1995, c. 11 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-17.3/index.html — fetched and verified 2026-07-30). Section 2(1) provides: "There is hereby established a department of the Government of Canada called the Department of Canadian Heritage over which the Minister of Canadian Heritage, appointed by commission under the Great Seal, shall preside" (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-17.3/page-1.html, fetched).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Minister's statutory powers, duties and functions cover "initiating, recommending, coordinating, implementing and promoting national policies, projects and programs with respect to Canadian identity and values, cultural development and heritage" (Department of Canadian Heritage Act, s. 4), as well as organizing, sponsoring and promoting public activities and events in the National Capital Region, and funding test cases of national significance related to human rights and fundamental freedoms (same section). Current program areas span creativity/arts and culture, heritage and celebration, sport, diversity and inclusion, and official languages (Departmental Plan 2026-27, document id: fed-canadian-heritage-dp-2027).

Governance & reporting line

The Minister of Canadian Heritage presides over the Department and "holds office during pleasure" (Act, s. 2(2)); a Deputy Minister is appointed as deputy head (Act, s. 3). As a federal department, Canadian Heritage reports to Parliament through the Minister and tables an annual Departmental Plan and Departmental Results Report (registry row: fed-canadian-heritage, doc_types field: departmental-plan, results-report).

Budget scale

~$1.86B total planned spending for 2026-27 — the Departmental Plan 2026-27 states "total planned spending (including internal services) for Canadian Heritage is $1,857,662,321" with 1,872 planned full-time-equivalent staff (document id: fed-canadian-heritage-dp-2027).

Institutional history

Established as a federal department by the Department of Canadian Heritage Act, S.C. 1995, c. 11, assented to June 15, 1995 and brought into force July 12, 1996 by order-in-council SI/96-68 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-17.3/page-1.html, fetched); any predecessor-department history (e.g., functions inherited from the former Department of Communications/Secretary of State) is ⚠️ not yet confirmed against an original source.

Canadian Heritage - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 / registry: fed-canadian-heritage / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is FIFA World Cup 2026 co-hosting, essentially absent before FY2024-25 and then becoming a concrete $220M Budget 2024 commitment with signed Toronto ($104.34M) and British Columbia ($115.66M) contribution agreements. The biggest quietly dropped element is the nine-season "Northern Lights" Parliament Hill multimedia show, which ended in summer 2024 with no named successor. The most load-bearing number is the FY2026-27 Comprehensive Expenditure Review, cutting planned spending by $34.67M rising to $93.10M by 2028-29 (roughly 172 FTEs), concentrated in Creativity/Arts/Culture grant programs — bringing total planned spending back to roughly FY2020-21 nominal levels, about 19% below the FY2022-23 peak. One open question: the final legislative disposition of Bill C-63 (the Online Harms Act), introduced February 2024, still "proposed" through FY2025-26, and then entirely absent from the FY2026-27 plan with no report stating whether it received Royal Assent, was withdrawn, or died on the order paper.

Priority evolution

The archived series only yields substantive, readable text starting with the FY2019-20 results report; every departmental plan for FY2004-05 and FY2012-13 through FY2020-21 failed to extract (see Residuals & gaps). The narrative below therefore starts in FY2019-20 and is continuous from there through the FY2026-27 plan.

FY2019-20 (drr-2020, a7a1fecd3ee5): Baseline structure is five core responsibilities — Creativity/arts and culture, Heritage and celebration, Sport, Diversity and inclusion, Official languages. The Indigenous Languages Act received royal assent June 21, 2019, backed by Budget 2019 funding of "$333.7 million over five years, and $115.7 million per year thereafter." Canada's Anti-Racism Strategy and a Federal Anti-Racism Secretariat were established that year; the LGBTQ2 Secretariat (relocated into Canadian Heritage from the Privy Council Office in fall 2019) launched the LGBTQ2 Community Capacity Fund; the Local Journalism Initiative launched with $50M over five years; the Canadian High Performance Sport Strategy launched May 2019 alongside early work on a Universal Code of Conduct to Prevent and Address Maltreatment in Sport. Ministers named: Guilbeault, Chagger and Joly post-2019 election, with Rodriguez and Duncan named as having held the portfolios earlier the same fiscal year. Total actual spending $1,550,574,951; FTEs 1,843.3.

FY2020-21 (drr-2021, aa2e861bc45e): Dominated by pandemic response — the Emergency Support Fund for Cultural, Heritage and Sport Organizations (ESF) is introduced as a standing acronym in this report's glossary, alongside continued LGBTQ2 Action Plan community engagement (a national online survey fielded Nov 2020-Feb 2021 drew "over 25,000 survey responses"). The ministerial roster has grown to five: Rodriguez (Canadian Heritage), Petitpas Taylor (Official Languages), Hussen (Housing and Diversity and Inclusion), Ien (Women and Gender Equality and Youth), St-Onge (Sport) — a structural expansion from the three-minister FY2019-20 configuration. Total actual spending $1,961,542,247 (up ~26% year over year); FTEs 1,844.4.

FY2021-22 (dp-2022, 63211df78379; drr-2022, 35e13e885f22): The plan frames the year around pandemic recovery, an additional $181.5M for arts/culture COVID-safe programming, the "first-ever federal LGBTQ2 action plan," and Indigenous Languages Act implementation. Results confirm total actual spending of $2,006,534,179 (FTEs 1,991.6). Two organizational changes land this year: in July 2021 the Minister of Canadian Heritage announced the Sport Dispute Resolution Centre of Canada (SDRCC) would establish and deliver an independent Office of the Sport Integrity Commissioner; and effective October 26, 2021 the LGBTQ2 Secretariat was transferred out of Canadian Heritage to Women and Gender Equality Canada (confirmed retrospectively in dp-2023). In November 2021 the Université de l'Ontario français was inaugurated in downtown Toronto, with the federal government funding 50% of project cost, "over $63 million" over the first four years.

FY2022-23 (dp-2023, 749de439f9b5; drr-2023, c908a4eb1934): The plan describes a $500M Recovery Fund/Reopening Fund for arts, culture, heritage and sport (2021-22 and 2022-23, spread across 14 programs) and an "ambitious legislative agenda," including reintroducing Broadcasting Act reform. Results confirm passage: Bill C-11 became the Online Streaming Act (royal assent April 2023); Bill C-18 became the Online News Act (royal assent June 2023); Bill C-13 amending the Official Languages Act passed as the Act for the Substantive Equality of Canada's Official Languages (royal assent June 20, 2023, confirmed in drr-2024). The first Special Representative on Combatting Islamophobia was appointed January 2023. The department organized commemorations for the Platinum Jubilee, the 2022 Royal Tour, and Queen Elizabeth II's death (Sept 8, 2022). Total actual spending peaked this year at $2,300,555,037 (FTEs 2,132.1) — the highest figure in the readable series.

FY2023-24 (dp-2024, 2095f7a03b93; drr-2024, 48013763d4f4): The plan signals a shift "from supporting culture organizations through short-term emergency and recovery measures to more strategic and long-term supports." Results describe two state funerals organized by the department (Brian Mulroney, Feb 2024; Ed Broadbent, Jan 2024), a coronation celebration for King Charles III, and continued Residential Schools National Monument development. Bill C-63, the Online Harms Act, was introduced February 26, 2024. The Action Plan for Official Languages 2023-2028 was unveiled — "$4.1 billion in funding over five years" with roughly 20 partner institutions. Total actual spending fell to approximately $2.15 billion (report states "$2.1 billion... a decrease of $148 million compared to the previous year"; the five-core-responsibility-plus-internal-services breakdown sums to $2,152,461,302). By core responsibility that year: Creativity/arts/culture $680,764,756; Heritage/celebration $170,609,666; Sport $282,740,470; Diversity/inclusion $308,781,339; Official languages $605,631,238; Internal services $103,933,833.

FY2024-25 (dp-2025, e24ed820a604; drr-2025, 31f3acb3d615): The plan cites the 2023 Fall Economic Statement and explicitly names Budget 2023 government-wide spending reductions ("$14.1 billion over five years... and $4.1 billion every year after that") as now landing on the department's own cost-saving measures. Planned initiatives include groundbreaking for a 2SLGBTQI+ National Monument and a Global Affairs Canada Commemorative Artwork, the end of the nine-season "Northern Lights" Parliament Hill show, and a renewed Canadian Sport Policy. Results confirm: total actual spending $2,140,649,974 (FTEs 1,964); the new Anti-Racism Strategy — "Changing Systems, Transforming Lives: Canada's Anti-Racism Strategy 2024-2028" — launched June 8, 2024; the first-ever "Canada's Action Plan on Combatting Hate" launched September 24, 2024; Bill C-63 (Online Harms Act) is still described as "the proposed bill to address Online Harms" — not reported as enacted. FIFA World Cup 2026 hosting preparation intensifies: an MOU process with the municipal governments of Toronto and Vancouver and the provincial governments of Ontario and British Columbia, and signed contribution agreements of $104.34M with the City of Toronto and $115.66M with British Columbia (summing to the $220M Budget 2024 commitment cited in dp-2027). This report is the first to be signed by a minister under the new title "Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages" (Steven Guilbeault), replacing the "Minister of Canadian Heritage" title used in every prior report in this series, and the first signed by a single minister rather than a five-minister slate.

FY2025-26 (dp-2026, ca1c32ed218a): "From the Minister" is now a single-signatory section (Guilbeault), naming two Secretaries of State as colleagues: Adam van Koeverden (Sport) and Nathalie Provost (Nature). Priorities: 60th anniversary of the Canadian flag; continued Anti-Racism Strategy 2024-2028 and Action Plan on Combatting Hate implementation; Bill C-63 (Online Harms Act) still described as "proposed," with the department planning to "advance the legislative process" and "lead pre-implementation to establish a Digital Safety Commission"; Copyright Act modernization work (artist's resale right, generative AI); continued FIFA World Cup 2026 co-hosting work.

FY2026-27 (dp-2027, b9333b50ee0d): Framed explicitly around a "Comprehensive Expenditure Review": planned departmental spending reductions of $34,670,817 (2026-27), $49,234,226 (2027-28) and $93,095,243 (2028-29), with an estimated 172 FTE reduction by 2028-29. Cuts are concentrated in Creativity, Arts and Culture programming — Canada Cultural Space Fund, Canada Media Fund, Canada Periodical Fund, Local Journalism Initiative, Digital Citizen program ($75.9M / 44 FTEs by 2028-29) — plus $17.2M / 128 FTEs in operating efficiencies. Bill C-63 / Online Harms Act is entirely absent from this plan (last mentioned in dp-2026). FIFA World Cup 2026 co-hosting (June 11-July 19, 2026) and a renewed Canada Strong Pass (free/discounted museum access, June 19-Sept 7, 2026) are named highlights. Total planned spending (including internal services): $1,857,662,321; total planned FTEs: 1,872 — matching the backgrounder figure exactly. Planned spending for Core Responsibility 1 (Creativity, arts and culture) alone: $471,115,556 / 371 FTEs, down sharply from the $694,269,526 actual spent under that same core responsibility just two years earlier (FY2024-25, drr-2025).

  • Added — Official Languages Act modernization.   • Added — FIFA World Cup 2026 co-hosting. Essentially absent before FY2024-25; becomes a named budget line (Budget 2024, $220M over four years) and a concrete municipal funding relationship (Toronto and Vancouver contribution agreements) by drr-2025 (31f3acb3d615), continuing through dp-2027 (b9333b50ee0d).

"Minister of Canadian Heritage" (used in every report from drr-2020 through drr-2024) gives way to "Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages" starting with the drr-2025 signature line (31f3acb3d615), continuing through dp-2026 and dp-2027.   • Stable throughout — the five-core-responsibility structure itself. Creativity/Arts and Culture, Heritage and Celebration, Sport, Diversity and Inclusion, Official Languages, plus Internal Services, is unchanged in name and count from dp-2022 (63211df78379) through dp-2027 (b9333b50ee0d) — in contrast to the ministerial/political layer above it, the Departmental Results Framework itself shows no renaming or restructuring in the readable window.
  • Quietly dropped — "Northern Lights" Parliament Hill multimedia show. Ran nine seasons; ended summer 2024 per dp-2025 (e24ed820a604), which states only that the department "will work towards finding new ways to continue to animate the downtown core" — no named successor in any later document read.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Real but concentrated in a small number of identifiable funding lines rather than a broad Toronto-specific mandate; most Canadian Heritage programming is nationally structured (open grant/contribution programs, provincial official-languages transfers) with no Toronto carve-out. Documented Toronto/Ontario-specific commitments in the readable series: - FIFA World Cup 2026 co-hosting — Toronto is one of two Canadian host cities (with Vancouver); a $104.34 million contribution agreement was signed with the City of Toronto in FY2024-25, part of the $220M Budget 2024 commitment, with essential federal services (safety/security, border, public health, immigration) coordinated across roughly 20 federal organizations (drr-2025, 31f3acb3d615; dp-2027, b9333b50ee0d, on Toronto and Vancouver as the two host cities and Ontario/BC as host provinces). - Université de l'Ontario français — inaugurated November 2021 in downtown Toronto; federal government funded 50% of total project cost, committing over $63 million over the institution's first four years (drr-2022, 35e13e885f22). - University of Toronto — host institution for the federally supported Gender Equity in Sport Research Hub, launched December 1, 2020 in partnership with Université Laval (dp-2022, 63211df78379); also named as a partner in an official-languages-adjacent Canada History Fund initiative alongside Université Laval (drr-2023, c908a4eb1934). - Smaller program-level examples: the Legacy Fund supported commemoration of the 175th anniversary of Toronto's medical response to the Irish famine (dp-2022, 63211df78379); the Building Communities through Arts and Heritage program's Local Festivals component funded Toronto's "Queer Pride Festival" (dp-2025, e24ed820a604). No document in this series establishes a Canadian Heritage regional office, standing institutional presence, or Toronto-specific policy carve-out beyond these funded initiatives and the FIFA World Cup hosting relationship.
