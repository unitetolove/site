Peel Regional Police - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: peel-peel-regional-police (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Peel Regional Police (PRP) is a municipal police service maintained under the Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019, S.O. 2019, c. 1, Sched. 1 (CSPA), which governs municipal policing in Ontario and replaced the Police Services Act (fetch-verified https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c01). Under CSPA Part IV, the police service board (the Peel Police Service Board) maintains the service and appoints its Chief of Police; PRP's own conduct materials still reference the predecessor Police Services Act code of conduct (https://www.peelpolice.ca/who-we-are/; https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p15).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

PRP delivers front-line policing — call response, investigations, enforcement, and police visibility — to the Cities of Brampton and Mississauga (population over 1.5 million) and to Toronto Pearson International Airport (registry row, registry id: peel-peel-regional-police; https://www.peelpolice.ca/who-we-are/). It describes itself as the second-largest municipal police service in Ontario and third-largest in Canada, with roughly 2,421 police officers and over 1,000 civilian employees (https://www.peelpolice.ca/who-we-are/).

Governance & reporting line

PRP reports to the Peel Police Service Board (registry parent_org: peel-peel-police-services-board), which sets objectives, policy, budget and Chief appointments under CSPA ss.37-39, while the Chief retains authority over day-to-day operational decisions (https://www.peelpoliceboard.ca/about-us/board-mandate/). Current governing document: 2024-2027 Strategic Plan, developed with the Board and community partners around three goals — community, people, and work (registry row strategy_current_url: https://www.peelpolice.ca/who-we-are/reports-documents-and-plans/2024-2027-strategic-plan/).

Budget scale

~$835.7M operating budget for 2026 (within the Board-approved $837.27M total 2026 Operating Budget for Peel policing), a 9.9% increase over 2025, funding an additional 175 officers and 25 civilian professionals (2026 Peel Regional Police Operating & Capital Budget Report, https://www.peelpoliceboard.ca/media/f5hdpur3/report-2026-police-budget.pdf).

Institutional history

PRP was formed January 1, 1974 through the amalgamation of five police services — Mississauga, Port Credit, Streetsville, Brampton and Chinguacousy — coinciding with the creation of the Region of Peel (https://www.peelpolice.ca/who-we-are/history/).

Backgrounder summary

Peel Regional Police (PRP) is a municipal police service maintained under the Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019 (CSPA), which replaced the Police Services Act; the Peel Police Service Board sets objectives, policy, budget and Chief appointments, while the Chief holds day-to-day operational authority. PRP polices Brampton, Mississauga and Toronto Pearson International Airport (population over 1.5 million), describing itself as the second-largest municipal police service in Ontario and third-largest in Canada. Its current governing document is the 2024-2027 Strategic Plan, organized around three goals — community, people and work. The 2026 operating budget is approved at $837.27 million (9.9% over 2025), funding 175 additional officers and 25 civilian professionals. PRP was formed January 1, 1974 through the amalgamation of five predecessor services coinciding with the creation of the Region of Peel.

Priority evolution

2019 (ar-2019, 76ad4442a15c): Report closes out the 2017-2019 Strategic Plan, organized under three "Areas of Focus" — Community Safety Together; Professional, Diverse and Supportive Workplace; Quality Service and Fiscal Responsibility. Chief Nishan Duraiappah was installed October 1, 2019, succeeding an interim chief, and formed a "Chief's Transition Team" of frontline/civilian members to guide his onboarding. A cross-functional Diversity and Inclusion Committee (17 volunteer members) was formed in 2019, informed by an external assessment from the Canadian Centre for Diversity and Inclusion (CCDI). Force size: 2,128 officers, 896 civilians, 30 cadets. The 2020 Budget was approved at $445.8 million, funding 35 additional officers and 18 civilians.

2020 (ar-2020, af8f2453692c): Dominated by COVID-19 response; PRP frames itself as an essential service and documents infection-control adaptations. A new organizational structure creates two commands not present in 2019 — Community Safety and Well-Being Operations and Innovation & Technology Command — redeploying over 180 officers and civilians to priority areas. The Mobile Crisis Rapid Response Team (MCRRT) launches February 2020 with the Canadian Mental Health Association Peel Dufferin. A Diversity, Equity and Inclusion (DEI) bureau and a five-year DEI Action Plan are established with CCDI. In October 2020, PRP, the Police Services Board and the Ontario Human Rights Commission (OHRC) sign an MOU committing to "legally binding remedies" to identify and eliminate systemic racism in policing — a commitment that recurs and deepens in every later document in this series. Also in October 2020, Peel Regional Council formally adopts the Community Safety and Well-Being (CSWB) Plan, framed under the province's Safer Ontario Act (Bill 175). Body Worn Cameras begin deployment to all frontline officers; a Digital Evidence Management System (DEMS) and a Real Time Operations Centre (RTOC) are introduced as new technology priorities. The 2021 Budget is approved at $462.5 million, a 3.8% increase over 2020.

2020-2023 Strategic Plan (sp-2023, d62a1a726c31): Formal successor to the 2017-2019 plan. The three "Areas of Focus" are reframed as three standing "Goals" — Community Safety & Well-Being Together (Our Community), Inclusive, Engaged & Progressive Workplace (Our People), Accountability, Equity & Service Excellence (Our Work) — establishing the Community/People/Work tripartite structure that persists through the most recent 2026 business plan. The Mission statement narrows from "protect the lives, property and rights of all" (2019) to "protect the rights and well-being of all." New named objectives absent from the 2019 report: a centralized Intimate Partner Violence unit, a formal review of mental-health call response, a "Vision Zero" road-safety goal, an open-data policy commitment, and sustainable environmental practices as a discrete objective.

2021-2023 (ar-2021 491e7471c454, ar-2022 732ef9f1f017, ar-2023 2d18ffd4d976): All three archived captures are landing-page stubs, not the underlying annual reports (see Residuals & gaps). What can be recovered for this window comes only retrospectively, via the "2020-2023 Strategic Plan Accomplishments" section of the 2024-2027 Strategic Plan (sp-2027, 7ccc5fdd1d65): during this period PRP established a dedicated Intimate Partner Violence Unit in partnership with the Safe Centre of Peel; developed and began implementing the Mental Health and Addiction Strategy; launched an Autism Strategy described as "believed to be the first of its kind in Canada"; formed an Anti-Racism Advisory Committee; created the Safer Roads Team; launched the Peel Region Situation Table; hosted the first Auto Theft Summit (2023); implemented a new Computer Aided Dispatch system; and was awarded Silver Level Certification in the Healthy Workplace Standard by Excellence Canada. ⚠️ Still being checked: none of this can be dated to a specific year within 2021-2023, or traced to primary annual-report text, because the three annual reports for those years did not extract.

2024-2027 Strategic Plan (sp-2027, 7ccc5fdd1d65): Retains the three-goal Community/People/Work structure but rewrites the Mission entirely — "Committed to community safety and well-being through progressive, innovative and inclusive service excellence" — dropping the earlier "protect the rights of all" formulation. New or reworded elements versus the 2020-2023 plan: an explicit "human rights lens" applied across all operations; a formal "Human Rights Project" in partnership with OHRC (successor to the 2020 MOU); a target employee-engagement index of 70%; a Crime Severity Index (CSI) benchmark replacing the earlier raw crime-rate-per-100,000 benchmark; and specific public-trust survey targets (80% agreement). Consultation feedback records resident concern about a perceived increase in crime despite continued personal-safety satisfaction.

2024 (ar-2024, 3698a758085d): Only a short landing-page teaser survives extraction (see Residuals & gaps); it confirms the report is organized under the same three 2024-2027 pillars (Our Community/Our People/Our Work) but contains no further substantive detail.

2024, statistical (sr-2024, 96fc33136caa): A five-year (2020-2024) divisional crime-statistics compendium. Its content is operational/tactical crime data (offence counts, solvency rates, collision rates by division) rather than institutional strategy, and is out of scope for detailed citation under this brief's operational-detail exclusion. It is retained in the inventory as a genuine, non-stub document and is used here only to corroborate the growth-driven "regional demand" narrative repeated in the 2026 Business Plan: region-wide Crimes Against Property rose from 23,325 (2020) to 37,972 (2024) and total Motor Vehicle Collisions rose from 16,576 to 42,733 over the same span (96fc33136caa).

2025-2026 Business Plan (bp-2026, 48575f907072): The most recent and most detailed archived document, headlined by "historical underinvestment in staffing" relative to regional growth. Under "Project 300," the plan commits to hiring 300 new frontline officers in 2025; the 2026 budget adds a further 175 officers and 25 civilian professionals. Priorities appearing for the first time in this series: Next Generation 9-1-1 (NG 9-1-1) — PRP's communications centre became "the first major Public Safety Answering Point in Canada" to transition, cutting average 9-1-1 call-wait times from 64 seconds (2023) to 12 seconds (2025); a Covert Online Digital Enforcement Section (CODES) pilot targeting dark-web criminal activity; an expanded Public Order Unit (2024) for demonstrations/protests tied to international events; a centralized Hate Crime Unit; a Youth Engagement Strategy (March 2025); a new internal-facing Equity, Diversity and Inclusion Strategy (successor framing to the 2020 DEI Action Plan); the Peel Police Foundation, a new charitable non-profit (April 2025); and a "30x30" pledge to reach 30% female recruit classes by 2030. The Service Delivery Model is restated across six units, including a renamed "Strategy, Innovation and Technology" command (successor to 2020's "Innovation & Technology Command"). The OHRC partnership is reported as having produced "over 260" adopted recommendations and a 79% reduction in the rate of force-related injury since 2020, with a new "Disparity Analysis and Well-Being Plan" superseding earlier human-rights reporting. Major capital priorities: a new 23 Division (first divisional expansion in over 40 years, targeted to open late 2026) and redevelopment of the 1981-built Sir Robert Peel Centre (groundbreaking September 8, 2025).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Peel Region is directly adjacent to Toronto and part of the Greater Toronto Area (GTA); PRP's own documents repeatedly frame its operations within that regional footprint rather than in isolation. PRP polices Toronto Pearson International Airport directly through its Airport Division, describing it as "Canada's largest and busiest international airport" with over 46 million passengers in 2024 (bp-2026, 48575f907072; also ar-2019, 76ad4442a15c; ar-2020, af8f2453692c). Multiple joint-forces investigations in the archived series explicitly span Peel and Toronto jurisdictions: a 2019 firearms/narcotics investigation conducted "with the assistance of London Police and Toronto Police Services" (ar-2019, 76ad4442a15c); a 2020 street-gang dismantling operation conducted jointly with "the Toronto Police Service and other provincial partners," with search warrants executed "across the Greater Toronto Area" (ar-2020, af8f2453692c); and 2025 projects (Pelican, Ghost, Night Train) explicitly described as addressing criminal networks operating "across the Greater Toronto Area" (bp-2026, 48575f907072). Project ERASE, PRP's recurring street-racing enforcement campaign, is run as "a multijurisdictional enforcement campaign... across the Greater Toronto Area (GTA)" in both 2019 and 2025 (ar-2019, 76ad4442a15c; bp-2026, 48575f907072). The Auto Theft Summit convened by PRP and the Police Service Board addressed a trend explicitly framed as regional/GTA-wide, not Peel-specific (bp-2026, 48575f907072). No document in the archived series indicates PRP has an operational presence inside the City of Toronto itself; Toronto/GTA relevance in this series is through cross-jurisdictional operations, the Pearson Airport mandate, and shared regional crime trends (organized auto theft, street racing, gang/gun violence) rather than through territorial jurisdiction.
