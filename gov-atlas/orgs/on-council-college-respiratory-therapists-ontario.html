Council of the College of Respiratory Therapists of Ontario - backgrounder

Mandate & statutory basis

The Council of the College of Respiratory Therapists of Ontario (CRTO) is established under the Respiratory Therapy Act, S.O. 1991, c. 39, s. 6, and operates within the framework of the Regulated Health Professions Act, S.O. 1991, c. 18, Sched. 2 (Public Appointments Secretariat agency profile, https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/286, fetched this review). Its statutory role is "to regulate the practice of Respiratory Therapy and govern the Members of the College in the public interest" (https://www.crto.on.ca/, fetched this review), ensuring the public has access to services from competent, respectful health professionals (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/286).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The College sets entry-to-practice requirements, develops standards of practice, administers continuing-competence programs, promotes interprofessional collaboration, and manages professional discipline and complaints processes for respiratory therapists in Ontario (https://www.crto.on.ca/, fetched this review). Council committees perform quasi-judicial functions regarding registration-certificate suspensions and revocations, operating through six statutory committees covering executive, registration, complaints, quality assurance, patient relations, and financial matters (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/286).

Governance & reporting line

The Council comprises seven to ten members elected by College members in accordance with by-laws, plus five to eight public (non-member) appointees selected by the Lieutenant Governor in Council; the Council annually elects a president and vice-president from among its members (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/286). The College reports to the Ministry of Health as its regulatory oversight ministry (same source).

Budget scale

For the fiscal year ended February 28, 2026, the College reported total revenue of $3,103,580 (2025: $3,029,774), comprising registration and renewal fees of $2,755,288, application fees of $20,700, investment income of $260,092, and competency-assessment revenue of $67,500 (CRTO Financial Statements, year ended February 28, 2026, p.5, https://crto.on.ca/pdf/AnualRpts/anulrptFA2526.pdf, fetched this review; registry doc id on-council-college-respiratory-therapists-ontario-fs-2026). Salaries and benefits were the largest expense line at $1,790,862 for the year (same source).

Institutional history

The respiratory therapy profession in North America traces its development to World War II-era advances in oxygen-therapy technology, but the College itself "was officially born" in 1994, coinciding with the coming into force of Ontario's Regulated Health Professions Act framework for the profession, per the College's own history page (https://www.crto.on.ca/public/about-us/crto-history/, fetched this review). The College marked its 20th anniversary in 2014 (same source). ⚠️ still being checked - the precise proclamation date distinguishing the 1991 enabling statutes from the 1994 operational start is not further broken down in the source consulted this review.

Priority evolution

This is a continuous 20-year annual-report series (fiscal years ending Feb 28/29, 2007 through 2026) plus a standalone financial-statements document for the final year.

2007-2012 (earliest era, ar-2007 through ar-2012). The College's earliest archived material frames its work against a 2003 Strategic Plan's six initiatives: needs-based entry-to-practice requirements, adequate RT supply, positive College-member relations, public education, legislative influence, and external partnerships (ar-2007, 8c0f1c015684). Registrar and CEO Gord Hyland died in office in October 2006; Christine Robinson was appointed the new Registrar and CEO, effective March 2007 (ar-2007). In February 2007, Council voted unanimously to pursue changing entry-to-practice from diploma to baccalaureate degree, the product of a three-year, four-phase study (ar-2008, 839ac510ba37). A new 2008-2011 Strategic Plan (seven strategic initiatives plus a formal "Guiding Principle") was approved November 2008 (ar-2009, 834ac08fd21a). Following Bill 171 (Health Systems Improvement Act) amendments to the RHPA, effective June 4, 2009, the Complaints Committee was renamed and restructured as the Inquiries, Complaints and Reports Committee (ICRC), and the Respiratory Therapy Act was amended to add a fifth controlled act, "administering a prescribed substance by inhalation" (ar-2010, 6ac2775e5a13). A Scope of Practice Summit (May 2011, nearly 100 profession-wide "thought leaders") set the direction for a new strategic plan; the multi-year baccalaureate-entry-to-practice project was wound down in 2011 after the Ministries of Health and of Training, Colleges & Universities declined to refer the request to a pan-Canadian process (ar-2011, 1e931695f01c) — a full reversal of a priority pursued since at least 2007. A new Strategic Plan 2011-2016 (seven strategic directions) was adopted December 2011; Registrar Christine Robinson retired December 1, 2011, succeeded by outgoing Council President Kevin Taylor, who became Registrar and CEO (ar-2012, b0d36672772c) — the same fiscal year posted the College's first deficit in this series, $(40,525), coinciding with the end of multi-year HealthForceOntario project funding and an office-renovation cost spike.

2013-2018 (continued 2011-2016 plan, then a new 2016-2020 plan, ar-2013 through ar-2018). Deficits persisted through most of this window: $(46,833) (FY2013), $(4,433) (FY2014, narrowing sharply), $(78,537) (FY2015, the largest to that point). New President David Jones took office in 2014/2015, introducing four areas of focus: Engagement, Supporting Practice Excellence, Professionalism, Stewardship of the Public Trust (ar-2015, 71d579a0fabb). A new CRTO Strategic Directions and Key Priorities 2016-2020 framework (five pillars: Member Engagement, Governance & Accountability, Enhancing Professionalism, Healthcare Community, Core Business Practices) was introduced in 2016, the same year the College posted its first surplus in several years, $97,184 (ar-2016, 01f9dafc1255) — helped by a one-time E-Health Ontario projects-funding line that appears only this year. The deficit returned sharply in FY2017, $(129,030) (ar-2017, 92f2b3464d6a), and widened further to $(287,744) in FY2018 — the largest deficit anywhere in this 20-year record, coinciding with a roughly 22% one-year decline in net assets (ar-2018, 625d77c5a7a8). New President Christina Sperling took office in 2017/2018; the Quality Assurance Program was formally renamed the Professional Development Program (PDP).

2019-2022 (pandemic response, then a leadership loss, ar-2019 through ar-2022). FY2019 posted a large deficit, $(200,822), on higher revenue (ar-2019, 077283375208). FY2020 (year ended Feb 29, 2020, just before Ontario's pandemic onset) shows no COVID-19 language at all — confirming this fiscal year predates the pandemic — and returned a surplus, $279,579 (ar-2020, 0c8efbf03cef). FY2021, the first full pandemic year, is extensively pandemic-themed: an Emergency Registration Policy "enabled us to register over 200 additional RTs in a condensed timeline" amid workforce-shortage fears, all Council/committee meetings moved virtual, and the College posted another surplus, $257,269 (ar-2021, 8a95d2adceee); new President Allison Chadwick took office this year. The most significant single event in the recent record: Registrar and CEO Kevin Taylor, who had served nine years, became ill in April 2021 and died in early August 2021; Deputy Registrar Carole Hamp subsequently became Registrar and CEO (ar-2022, 520c7aaba163). The same year saw the College begin developing a 2021-2025 Strategic Direction & Key Priorities (successor to 2016-2020) and create a new, non-statutory Finance and Audit Committee (FAC) — explicitly described as a response to government signals that health-college Executive Committees might be eliminated.

2023-2026 (DEI, risk management, and the FAC's reversal, ar-2023 through ar-2026 plus fs-2026). FY2023 saw the release of a Diversity, Equity and Inclusion (DEI) Strategy, completion of an external review of professional-conduct processes, and a new Risk Management Framework, alongside the College's first deficit since FY2018/19, $(15,778) (ar-2023, 648f73d24c61). FY2024's organizing theme was "Risk Management" — new cybersecurity investment, a new financial-advisory/audit team, and an auditor change (Hilborn LLP → Grewal Guyatt LLP); this year also saw the College's first Discipline Committee referral in the archived record's recent stretch (no hearing took place) (ar-2024, 4932f8a2757a). FY2025's theme, "Building Trust Through Inclusive Participation," introduced the year's most forward-looking initiative: CRTO and the Respiratory Therapy Society of Ontario (RTSO) launched a joint working group to develop a submission to the Ontario Ministry of Health to expand respiratory therapy's scope of practice (ar-2025, fbc340209e41) — the archived record's last substantive priority addition. FY2026, themed "Accountability You Can See," records the Finance and Audit Committee's demotion to ad hoc status — its original 2022 rationale (pre-empting a feared Executive Committee elimination) explicitly abandoned as never having materialized — alongside the year's single Discipline Committee hearing (case-numbered only, no individual named) and the highest complaints volume in the whole 20-year record, 80 new matters (ar-2026, d57b2981029b; fs-2026, 459eeaa0e6ff).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Pursued for years, then abandoned — baccalaureate-degree entry-to-practice: unanimous Council intent February 2007, backed by a three-year study, formally wound down in 2011 after the responsible Ministries declined to advance it (ar-2008, 839ac510ba37; ar-2011, 1e931695f01c).
  • Renamed — Complaints Committee → Inquiries, Complaints and Reports Committee (ICRC), effective June 4, 2009, per Bill 171/RHPA amendments (ar-2010, 6ac2775e5a13) — persists under this name through 2026.
  • Renamed — Quality Assurance Program → Professional Development Program (PDP), completed by FY2017/18 (ar-2018, 625d77c5a7a8).
  • Added, then quietly discontinued — the non-statutory Professional Practice Committee: added 2010, active through at least 2013-2016 (ar-2010; ar-2013, 9677583fda11), no longer appears in the committee list by FY2017/18 — its disappearance is not explained in any document read.
  • Added, then explicitly reversed within four years — the Finance and Audit Committee (FAC): created 2022 "in response to indications from the Ontario government that Executive Committees of health regulatory colleges might be eliminated"; by the 2026 report, "no action has been taken over the past four years to eliminate Executive Committees... the FAC's original purpose is no longer required," and it is demoted to an ad hoc committee effective the 2026-2027 fiscal year (ar-2022, 520c7aaba163; ar-2026, d57b2981029b) — the clearest self-contained "added then dropped" story in this brief.
  • Strategic-plan succession, three generations across the record: 2003 Plan (six initiatives) → 2008-2011 Plan (seven initiatives) → 2011-2016 Plan (seven strategic directions) → 2016-2020 Strategic Directions (five pillars) → 2021-2025 Strategic Direction & Key Priorities (initiated 2022, same five pillar names retained but reframed from program-specific language toward principles-based framing, e.g. "Right-Touch Regulation," per ar-2025's synthesis of the plan).
  • Added — a formal Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Strategy (2023, ar-2023, 648f73d24c61) — no prior document in this series names DEI as a distinct workstream.
  • Added, most recently — a joint scope-of-practice-expansion initiative with the Respiratory Therapy Society of Ontario (2025, ar-2025) — the record's newest priority, unresolved as of the last document.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • The Respiratory Therapy Act was amended (~2009-2010) to add a fifth controlled/authorized act — administering a prescribed substance by inhalation — a direct legislative-mandate expansion (ar-2010, 6ac2775e5a13).
  • Registration Regulation amendments under the Respiratory Therapy Act passed February 17, 2012: added a statutory "good character" requirement for registration, and replaced "Inactive Status" with a formal "Inactive Class of Registration" — visible directly in that year's membership table (ar-2012, b0d36672772c).
  • Financial trajectory, two multi-year deficit clusters bookending a pandemic-era recovery: revenue grew from $1,178,155 (FY2007) to $3,103,580 (FY2026, per fs-2026) — roughly 2.6x over the full run — but surpluses and deficits alternated repeatedly rather than trending smoothly. A first deficit cluster ran FY2012-2015 (four consecutive deficit years, $(40,525) to $(78,537)), followed by a brief FY2016 surplus, then a second, deeper deficit cluster FY2017-2019 (culminating in FY2018's $(287,744), the largest in the whole series). Pandemic-era cost reductions (travel, in-person meetings, occupancy) drove strong surpluses FY2020-2022 ($279,579, $257,269, $65,711), before a small FY2023 deficit ($(15,778)) and a return to strong surpluses by FY2025-2026.
  • A registration-education/insurance requirement was confirmed via a comprehensive by-law review (FY2014): mandatory professional-liability insurance minimum of $2 million per occurrence for practising RTs, alongside a new Conflict of Interest Regulation (O.Reg. 250/13) coming into force (ar-2014, 550a8cb4b4ce).
  • COVID-19 Emergency Registration Policy (2021) registered "over 200 additional RTs in a condensed timeline" amid pandemic workforce-shortage concerns — the single largest one-time registration-volume intervention in the record (ar-2021, 8a95d2adceee).
  • Registrar succession chain: Gord Hyland (d. in office, Oct 2006) → Christine Robinson (Acting Mar 2007, confirmed, served to Dec 2011) → Kevin Taylor (Dec 2011, previously Council President; served until his death in office, early August 2021, after nine years) → Carole Hamp (previously Deputy Registrar, became Registrar and CEO from 2021-2022) — two of the four Registrars in this 20-year record left office through death or serious illness while serving, a notable leadership-continuity pattern.
  • A steep, recent divergence between complaints volume and registrant growth: new matters received climbed from 24 (FY2018/19) to 38 (FY2019/20) to 37 (FY2021/22) to 46 (FY2022/23) to 65 (FY2023/24) to 57 (FY2024/25) to 80 (FY2025/26, the highest in the record) (ar-2019 through ar-2026), while the registrant base grew only about 18% over the same span (3,559 to 4,206). The College attributes part of this to process improvements enabling more matters to be tracked and logged, and to post-pandemic workforce strain, rather than to any change in registrant conduct.
  • Vulnerable Sector Checks (VSC) policy drafted 2024, took effect September 1, 2024 — a new mandatory registration-screening requirement (ar-2024, 4932f8a2757a; ar-2025, fbc340209e41).
  • New Emergency Class of Registration added to the Registration Regulation per an Ontario Ministry of Health mandate applicable to all health regulatory colleges, alongside a new "As of Right" exemption (O.Reg. 199/23) letting eligible out-of-province RTs work in specified Ontario settings without CRTO registration (ar-2024).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

CRTO's head office has been in Toronto throughout the archived series, relocating within the city at some point between the FY2022/23 and FY2024/25 reports: 180 Dundas Street West, Suite 2103 (confirmed through ar-2023, 648f73d24c61) → 90 Adelaide Street West, Suite 300 (confirmed from ar-2025, fbc340209e41, onward); the exact transition date is not pinned down within this document set. Unlike the other three orgs in this batch, CRTO's own statistics show a genuine, consistently quantified GTA concentration: the College's largest electoral/statistical district — named "Metro Toronto" in early reports, later "Greater Toronto" and then "Greater Toronto, Haliburton, Northumberland, Simcoe" (District 4) — has been the single largest of the College's seven districts in every year read, growing from 559 of 2,099 members with district data (2007) to 1,762 members (2026, the district's geographic description by then explicitly listing "Haliburton, Kawartha Lakes, Peterborough, Northumberland, Simcoe, Durham, York, Peel and Toronto"). The Michener Institute for Applied Health Sciences, Toronto, is named as the sole RT training program in that district throughout the series. The 2026 annual report is the first to include a land acknowledgment explicitly naming Toronto: "Our office is in Toronto... covered by Treaty 13... and the Williams Treaties" (ar-2026, d57b2981029b). Relevance here is more direct than for a purely province-wide regulator: while CRTO's statutory mandate and complaints/discipline processes apply uniformly across Ontario, its registrant base is genuinely concentrated in the Toronto/GTA region to a documented, quantified degree not found in this batch's other three orgs.
