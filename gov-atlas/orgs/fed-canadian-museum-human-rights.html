Canadian Museum for Human Rights - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-canadian-museum-human-rights (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canadian Museum for Human Rights (CMHR) "is a Crown corporation established by the Museums Act and governed by the control and accountability regime established under Part X of the Financial Administration Act" (CMHR 2024-25 Annual Report, "Together in Human Rights," p.7, https://humanrights.ca/media/4300/download?attachment). Its stated mandate is to "explore the subject of human rights, with special but not exclusive reference to Canada, in order to enhance the public's understanding of human rights, to promote respect for others, and to encourage reflection and dialogue" (https://humanrights.ca/about).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Located in Winnipeg, the Museum describes itself as "the first museum solely dedicated to the evolution, celebration and future of human rights" (https://humanrights.ca/about). It is also required to comply with the Federal Accountability Act, the Access to Information Act, the Privacy Act, the Criminal Code, the Official Languages Act and Regulations, the Canadian Human Rights Act, and the Canada Labour Code (CMHR 2024-25 Annual Report, p.7, https://humanrights.ca/media/4300/download?attachment).

Governance & reporting line

The Board of Trustees - a chairperson, a vice-chairperson and up to nine other trustees - is appointed by the Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages, with the approval of the Governor in Council, for terms of up to four years; the Board is accountable to Parliament through that Minister, and the Museums Act vests day-to-day management in the CEO and management team (CMHR 2024-25 Annual Report, p.7, https://humanrights.ca/media/4300/download?attachment).

Budget scale

For 2025-26, the CMHR will receive $26.9 million in base parliamentary appropriations to fund operations, supplemented by $1.7 million in Support for National Museums funding (pending parliamentary approval); the operating budget on a cash basis is $31.7 million, allocated across museum content/programs/engagement ($14.2M), accommodations ($7.8M, including $2.8M PILT), and internal services ($9.7M) (CMHR Corporate Plan Summary 2025-2026, pp.13-14, https://humanrights.ca/media/4321/download?attachment).

Institutional history

The Museum opened to the public in September 2014; its 2024-25 Annual Report notes "September 2024 marked 10 years since we opened our doors" (https://humanrights.ca/media/4300/download?attachment). ⚠️ still being checked - the specific year the Museums Act was amended to add the CMHR as a named national museum was not directly confirmed by a source read this review.

Canadian Museum for Human Rights - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: fed-canadian-museum-human-rights / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is equity/anti-racism as an organizing institutional priority — triggered when former and current employees came forward in June 2020 with accounts of systemic racism and discrimination, producing a two-phase independent review, a November 2020 "Creating an equitable museum" framework, a new Diversity and Inclusion Board Committee, and — as one specific downstream project — an LGBT Purge Project Advisory Council (2021-22) to develop content on Canada's historic purge of LGBTQ2+ military/RCMP/civil-service employees. The biggest quietly-changed element is the strategic-plan cadence itself: a five-year plan (2017-18 to 2021-22) gives way to an unnamed "transitional strategic plan" (2022-23) before a fully new framework, "Our Path Forward," is approved by the Board in Q3 2023-24 — with none of the reports explicitly narrating why the transition took an extra "transitional" year. The most load-bearing number is the $35 million in Budget 2018 funding that offset a planned appropriations reduction stemming from the Museum having front-loaded base operating funds into 2012-13 to complete its capital construction project — without which the Museum's own 2017-18 Annual Report says it faced "serious financial shortfall and substantial cuts." One open question: what the Museum's next Office of the Auditor General special examination (scheduled to begin in 2025, per the FY2025-26 Corporate Plan) will find, given the Museum's most recent special examination (reported in FY2017-18) had already flagged financial uncertainty as a serious risk.

Backgrounder summary

Per our research file for that body: the CMHR is a Crown corporation established by the Museums Act, governed under Part X of the Financial Administration Act, mandated to "explore the subject of human rights, with special but not exclusive reference to Canada... to promote respect for others, and to encourage reflection and dialogue." Located in Winnipeg, it describes itself as the first museum solely dedicated to human rights. Governance is an 11-member Board of Trustees appointed by the Minister (through the Governor in Council) for terms up to four years. For FY2025-26 it will receive $26.9M in base parliamentary appropriations plus $1.7M in Support for National Museums funding, against a $31.7M cash operating budget. It opened to the public in September 2014; the backgrounder could not confirm the exact year the Museums Act was amended to add the CMHR as a named national museum (this brief establishes it: March 13, 2008, Royal Assent).

Priority evolution

2008-2009 (ar-2009, d5f3bca7976e), "First Annual Report": CMHR created by the Act to Amend the Museums Act (Royal Assent March 13, 2008) — the first national cultural institution created since the late 1960s and the first located outside the National Capital Region. Legislation came into force August 10, 2008; first Board of Trustees appointed August 26, 2008. First Corporate Plan (approved by government September 2008) named three strategic priorities: manage the Building Construction Project soundly/transparently/on time/on budget; develop content/programming through consultation; adopt sound governance practices. Reports to Parliament through the Minister of Canadian Heritage and Official Languages.

2013-2014 (ar-2014, dc9e4f213bbf), "Open... 2013-2014 Annual Report": Pre-opening year, themed around "Open minds / Open arms" programming (e.g., a Filipino-Canadian "comfort women" survivor speaking tour; an Aboriginal-youth "spirit panels" project for the Indigenous Perspectives gallery). Care and control of the new building transferred January 6, 2014. Net Parliamentary appropriations $19.9M (up from $14.5M).

September 2014: Museum opens to the public (per backgrounder and confirmed institutional milestone referenced throughout later reports, e.g. ar-2025 96a76743cbc0).

2016 (Board strategic-plan revision, referenced in ar-2019 0f8a209fdf42): In 2016 the Board and senior leadership revised the strategic plan for the five fiscal years beginning 2017-18, "to refine the focus of Museum operations and incorporate the lessons learned since opening in September 2014." The Board updated the plan again in 2017 for its FY2018-19 second year.

2017-2018 (ar-2018, 078b6553d40a): The Office of the Auditor General of Canada completed a special examination of the Museum, flagging financial uncertainty as a serious risk factor. That risk was resolved when Budget 2018 provided an additional $35 million over six years to offset a planned reduction in appropriations — the reduction stemmed from the Museum having moved a portion of its base operating appropriations forward to 2012-13 to complete the capital construction project. The Museum welcomed its millionth visitor in 2017 and restructured its relationship with the Friends of the CMHR (its philanthropic foundation), welcoming a new Friends CEO.

2018-2019 (ar-2019, 0f8a209fdf42): Second year of the five-year strategic plan (2017-18 to 2021-22); third consecutive fiscal year of a moderate operating surplus. A new Strategic Planning and Programs Committee (formerly the Museum Programs Committee) of the Board was formed to oversee the current strategic plan's implementation.

2020-2021 (ar-2021, 6dff1b0bd6c8), "Room for Everyone": COVID-19 closed the Museum twice (Mar 14-Jun 16, 2020; Oct 31, 2020-Feb 23, 2021); revenue from operations fell to $0.4M against a normal ~300,000 annual visitor base; the Government of Canada provided $2.2M in COVID-19 relief funding to the CMHR specifically (part of relief for all national museums). Separately and more significantly: in June 2020, former and current Museum employees came forward to share experiences of systemic racism and discrimination, prompting an external review commissioned by the Museum. Isha Khan was appointed the Museum's new CEO in August 2020 (succeeding John Young, who is named in the 2017-18 report as President and CEO); in November 2020 she released "Creating an equitable museum: A framework to create a safe, respectful and healthy workplace." A new Diversity and Inclusion Committee of the Board was also formed.

2021-2022 (ar-2022, ff2fda287fdd), "Facing Truth": The Museum received the report from the second phase of the independent systemic-racism/discrimination review in June 2021; all recommendations from both phases were reported "actioned or completed" by year-end, embedded in a broader operational framework. Separately, an LGBT Purge Project Advisory Council was established to guide a major exhibition/content project on the historic purge of LGBTQ2+ employees from the Canadian military, RCMP, and federal civil service (1950s-1990s) — a content-development initiative distinct from the Museum's internal-workplace review. The Museum's "People, Culture and Growth" division was renamed and reorganized, with five new leadership-team members welcomed. COVID-19 relief funding to the CMHR rose to $3.9M this year; the Museum closed for a further ~4 months (May-July 2021; Dec 2021-Feb 2022).

2022-2023 and transition (per grep-sampled ar-2023, ar-2024; not read in full): References to a "transitional strategic plan" and ongoing "refine our strategic plan" work suggest the five-year plan's FY2021-22 endpoint was followed by an interim planning period rather than an immediate new multi-year plan.

Q3 2023-24 / 2024-2025 (bp-2026, 20f0529b4f9b; ar-2025, 96a76743cbc0): The Board approved a new strategic framework, "Our Path Forward," in Q3 2023-24, developed through consultation with Elders, employees, volunteers, contractors, peers, educators, and the public. It centres relationships, transformational experiences, storytelling, and modeling/promoting human rights principles, organized around three strategic priorities for the FY2026 planning period: national reach, sustainability, and equity. September 2024 marked the Museum's 10th anniversary (2.25 million onsite visitors over the decade); Isha Khan accepted a second five-year term as CEO. The reporting Minister is now named "Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages" in ar-2025 — a rename from "Minister of Canadian Heritage," which bp-2026 (referencing a September 2024 mandate letter) still uses, bracketing the rename to sometime between September 2024 and the FY2024-25 annual report's publication.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — equity/anti-racism as an organizing institutional priority: absent from the founding strategic priorities (2008-2019); triggered by employee accounts in June 2020 (ar-2021, 6dff1b0bd6c8), formalized via the November 2020 "Creating an equitable museum" framework and a new Diversity and Inclusion Board Committee; deepened via the second-phase review report (June 2021) and the LGBT Purge Project Advisory Council (ar-2022, ff2fda287fdd).
  • Added — CANDU-style capital-to-operating funding fix: the $35M Budget 2018 offset for the appropriations reduction tied to the 2012-13 capital-funding shift is the first explicit "budget inflection" tied to a stated funding-model problem in the series (ar-2018, 078b6553d40a).
  • Renamed/restructured — strategic-plan cadence: the founding Corporate Plan framework (2008-2009, three priorities) gives way to a five-year strategic plan (2017-18 to 2021-22, revised by the Board in 2016 and updated in 2017, per ar-2019 0f8a209fdf42), which is itself succeeded by a "transitional strategic plan" (per grep-sampled ar-2022/ar-2023 references) and then by "Our Path Forward" (approved Q3 2023-24, per bp-2026 20f0529b4f9b) — no report explicitly narrates why an interim "transitional" plan was needed between the two named frameworks.
  • Renamed — reporting Minister: "Minister of Canadian Heritage" (2009 through at least Sept 2024, per bp-2026) → "Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages" (ar-2025, 96a76743cbc0) — a federal machinery-of-government rename, not CMHR-specific. This is the same rename independently confirmed in the fed-canadian-museum-history and fed-canadian-museum-nature briefs this batch, suggesting a single Canadian Heritage portfolio-wide ministerial rename in 2025.
  • Renamed — "People, Culture and Growth" division: reported as "renamed and re-organized" in ar-2022 (ff2fda287fdd) with five new leadership hires; the prior name is not given in the excerpt read.
  • Quietly changing — CEO leadership: John Young (President and CEO, named through ar-2018) → interim CEO (the Board Chair, summer 2020) → Isha Khan (appointed August 2020; second five-year term confirmed 2024-25, ar-2025 96a76743cbc0) — no report frames the John Young→interim transition explicitly as a departure-and-succession event within the read excerpts.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Indirect — the CMHR is a federal national museum headquartered in Winnipeg, Manitoba, with no documented Ontario or Toronto physical presence in the read material. The series does reference Ontario/Toronto-adjacent programming and partnerships (e.g., events in Toronto and Ottawa around the millionth-visitor milestone, per ar-2018 078b6553d40a; consultations with the Royal Ontario Museum, per ar-2019 0f8a209fdf42), but these are national-outreach touchpoints, not operational footprint. Ontario/Toronto relevance here runs through the CMHR's role in the same federal Canadian Heritage cultural-institution policy space (parliamentary appropriations, Museums Act governance, the 2025 Minister rename) that also governs Ontario-based national museums and the AGO's provincial funding relationship, rather than through any presence in the GTA.
