Canadian Museum of History - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-canadian-museum-history (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canadian Museum of History (formerly the Canadian Museum of Civilization) was established by the Museums Act on July 1, 1990, and renamed under the Canadian Museum of History Act on December 12, 2013 (CMH Annual Report 2024-2025, "Mandate" and Notes to financial statements note 1, https://www.historymuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/annual-report-2024-2025.pdf). Its statutory mission, as stated in the Museums Act, is "to enhance Canadians' knowledge, understanding and appreciation of events, experiences, people and objects that reflect and have shaped Canada's history and identity, and also to enhance their awareness of world history and cultures" (same source, note 1).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Corporation is an agent Crown corporation named in Part I of Schedule III to the Financial Administration Act and also operates the Canadian War Museum, fulfilling its mandate through core responsibilities in exhibitions/education/communication related to Canadian history, collections holding roughly 4 million items, and Digital Museums Canada, a digital-capacity investment program for heritage organizations (CMH Annual Report 2024-2025, Notes note 1 and "Mandate" section, https://www.historymuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/annual-report-2024-2025.pdf; http://www.historymuseum.ca/about/our-purpose).

Governance & reporting line

The Corporation is governed by an 11-member Board of Trustees (a Chair, a Vice-Chair, and up to nine other Trustees), appointed by the Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages with Governor in Council approval, for terms not exceeding four years; the Board is accountable to Parliament through that Minister (CMH Annual Report 2024-2025, "Board of Trustees", https://www.historymuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/annual-report-2024-2025.pdf). The Museum functions at arm's length from the Government of Canada in daily operations, as a Crown corporation within the Canadian Heritage Portfolio (same source).

Budget scale

The Corporation recognized $96.6 million in parliamentary appropriations in fiscal 2024-2025, an increase of $4.8 million over 2023-2024, against a net result of operations surplus of $1 million for the year (CMH Annual Report 2024-2025, "Parliamentary Appropriations" and note 15, https://www.historymuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/annual-report-2024-2025.pdf).

Institutional history

Established by the Museums Act on July 1, 1990 as the Canadian Museum of Civilization, the institution traces roots back to 1856; it was renamed the Canadian Museum of History on December 12, 2013 through an amendment to the Museums Act (CMH Annual Report 2024-2025, "Mandate" and Notes note 1, https://www.historymuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/annual-report-2024-2025.pdf; http://www.historymuseum.ca/about/our-purpose).

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is the institution's own 2013-2014 rename and remandate — from the Canadian Museum of Civilization to the Canadian Museum of History, effective December 12, 2013 under a new Canadian Museum of History Act, which the organization's own FY2014-15 report calls "our new name and mandate." Priority evolution

December 12, 2013 / FY2014-15 (ar-2015, c5faf3cc383d), the rename year: "Our national museum of human history set a revised course at the end of 2013, navigating towards a new destination established by Parliament" — the Canadian Museum of Civilization is renamed the Canadian Museum of History under the Canadian Museum of History Act. In April 2014 the Board approved five new Strategic Directions to guide the institution for five years: (1) establish the Museum as a hub of Canadian history for Canada and the world; (2) connect Canadians to their history; (3) develop a collection reflecting Canada's history and distinctiveness; (4) engage partnerships/networks; (5) ensure financial resources to carry out the mandate. The centrepiece deliverable named for this new direction is the Canadian History Hall, not due to open until 2017. This same year, LGen (ret'd) Bill Leach, the Board's Chairman, is reported to have passed away (institutional/officeholder context only, no adverse-finding pairing).

2015-2017 (grep-sampled, ar-2016 19ec44f3bb4b; not independently verified by full read): Continued transition-era reporting "against these new Strategic Directions"; Canadian War Museum marks its 10th anniversary in its current building (2016). No archived AR exists for FY2016-17 (registry gap between ar-2016 and ar-2018 — see Residuals).

July 1, 2017 (grep-sampled, ar-2018 01f6f7ddd4d7): The Canadian History Hall opens, timed to the 150th anniversary of Confederation — described as "the crowning achievement of the transformation" begun with the 2013-14 rename. The Museum raised nearly $9 million in gifts/pledges toward the Hall and the First World War Centenary commemoration (a joint effort with the Canadian War Museum, whose own centenary commemoration ran four years, closing out in FY2018-19 per grep-sampled ar-2019 0dca7dad099b).

FY2019-20 (grep-sampled, ar-2020 6e33bbe5a3fc): The Museums surpass one million visitors since the Canadian History Hall's 2017 opening. Parliamentary appropriations recognized: $80.4M, described as a decrease that year (part of a five-year approval schedule for additional funding, in its fourth year). COVID-19 is first mentioned as a forward risk (World Health Organization declaration March 11, 2020, right at fiscal year-end).

FY2020-2023 (grep-sampled, ar-2021 a53e242f5066; ar-2022 a13b1a323285; ar-2023 a4e37bf7d4ce): Pandemic-dominated years — closures, virtual pivots, 75th-anniversary VE Day programming shifted online (2021), continuing pandemic effects into 2022; a new Director confirmed in FY2022-23 (Caroline Dromaguet). Grep hits reference "the establishment of new strategic directions" and a "collaborative and forward-thinking strategic planning process" underway in FY2022-23, foreshadowing the October 2022 Board action captured in the newest report.

October 2022 / January 2023 (ar-2025, 10d30be0eea3): The Board of Trustees establishes a new set of five Strategic Directions (superseding the April 2014 set, without an explicit retirement statement): (1) engage people from across Canada in diverse histories and stories; (2) foster an innovative workforce culture promoting equity, respect, and belonging; (3) advance reconciliation and strengthen commitment to Indigenous Peoples' rights; (4) build organizational resilience through sustainable practices; (5) leverage technology to drive innovation and access. A companion vision/values exercise, presented to staff in January 2023, produced the vision statement "Dare to inspire tomorrow's history," governing FY2023-24 through FY2027-28.

FY2024-2025 (ar-2025, 10d30be0eea3): $96.6M in parliamentary appropriations recognized (up $4.8M over FY2023-24), $1M net surplus. The reporting Minister is named "Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages" — the same rename independently confirmed in the CMHR and CMN briefs this batch.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Indirect — the Canadian Museum of History and the Canadian War Museum are both located in the National Capital Region (Gatineau, Quebec and Ottawa, Ontario respectively — the War Museum's Ottawa, Ontario location is the org's one direct physical Ontario footprint, per grep-sampled ar-2023 a4e37bf7d4ce, "The Canadian War Museum is located in Ottawa, Ontario"). Neither institution is documented as having a Toronto or GTA physical presence in the material read this review. Relevance to Ontario/Toronto runs through the same federal Canadian Heritage cultural-institution policy space (Museums Act governance, parliamentary appropriations, the 2025 Minister rename) shared with the other three orgs in this batch, and through Digital Museums Canada's national digital-capacity investment program, which funds heritage organizations across Canada including in Ontario, rather than through any Toronto-area operational footprint.
