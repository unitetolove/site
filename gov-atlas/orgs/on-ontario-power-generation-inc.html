Ontario Power Generation Inc. - backgrounder

Mandate & statutory basis

OPG is a business corporation, not a body created directly by statute: the Electricity Act, 1998, S.O. 1998, c. 15, Sched. A (consolidated text fetched and verified at e-Laws, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98e15) defines "Ontario Power Generation Inc." in its Interpretation section as "the corporation incorporated as Ontario Power Generation Inc. under the Business Corporations Act on December 1, 1998" (Business Corporations Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. B.16, current text also fetched and verified, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90b16). The Electricity Act's Part IV.1 governs OPG's provincial obligations but its detailed provisions were not retrieved this pass ⚠️ VERIFY.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Ontario's largest clean-electricity generator, producing roughly half the province's electricity from 66 hydroelectric stations, 2 nuclear stations, 1 solar facility, 1 biomass station, 1 oil/gas station, and 4 combined-cycle gas turbine stations operated through subsidiary Atura Power; OPG also owns two further nuclear stations leased to Bruce Power L.P.; over 11,000 employees (opg.com "Who We Are," https://www.opg.com/who-we-are/).

Governance & reporting line

A Crown corporation/government business enterprise wholly owned by the Province of Ontario as sole shareholder, governed by a Board of Directors (Chair Wendy Kei, appointed June 27, 2019) with President and CEO Nicolle Butcher (appointed December 19, 2024) (WebSearch/Wikipedia summary, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Power_Generation; opg.com "Who We Are," https://www.opg.com/who-we-are/) ⚠️ VERIFY — the statutory basis for sole-shareholder status was not directly quoted from the Electricity Act's Part IV.1 this pass.

Budget scale

~$5.53 billion total revenue / ~$1.20 billion net income (2018 figures, WebSearch/Wikipedia infobox, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Power_Generation). More current (2024, matching OPG's own cited "2024 Annual Report") figures: ⚠️ VERIFY - not grounded this pass — opg.com blocked automated document fetch (HTTP 403) this pass.

Institutional history

Established in April 1999 as one of five successor corporations from the breakup of the former Ontario Hydro under the Progressive Conservative Harris government's electricity-market restructuring (Electricity Act, 1998), assuming ownership of Ontario Hydro's generating stations; incorporated under the Business Corporations Act on December 1, 1998, ahead of the April 1, 1999 restructuring date (Electricity Act, 1998, Interpretation section, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98e15; WebSearch/Wikipedia summary, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Power_Generation).

Backgrounder summary

OPG is a business corporation — not a body created directly by statute — incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (Ontario) on December 1, 1998, and defined in the Electricity Act, 1998's Interpretation section; it is wholly owned by the Province of Ontario as sole shareholder. It is Ontario's largest clean-electricity generator, operating 66 hydroelectric stations, 2 nuclear stations, 1 solar facility, 1 biomass station, 1 oil/gas station, and 4 combined-cycle gas turbine stations (through subsidiary Atura Power), plus two further nuclear stations leased to Bruce Power L.P., with over 11,000 employees. It was established in April 1999 as one of five successor corporations from the breakup of Ontario Hydro. The backgrounder's 2018-vintage budget figures (~$5.53B revenue / ~$1.20B net income) are superseded below by figures read directly from the one usable archived document.
