Council of the College of Traditional Chinese Medicine Practitioners and Acupuncturists of Ontario - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-council-college-traditional-chinese-medicine-pra (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The College of Traditional Chinese Medicine Practitioners and Acupuncturists of Ontario (CTCMPAO) regulates the practice of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and acupuncture under the Traditional Chinese Medicine Act, 2006, S.O. 2006, c. 27, and the Regulated Health Professions Act, 1991, S.O. 1991, c. 18 (RHPA), which was proclaimed in force December 31, 1993 (CTCMPAO Annual Report 2021-2022, p.3, https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/resources/forms-and-documents/2021-2022_CTCMPAO_AnnualReport.pdf; https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/regulation/). Its stated mandate is "protecting the public through a transparent and effective regulatory environment while guiding the Traditional Chinese Medicine profession," summarized on its homepage as "To Serve and Protect the Public" (https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/; CTCMPAO Annual Report 2021-2022, p.3). The TCM Act, 2006 received Legislative Assembly approval in December 2006, authorizing appointment of a Transitional Council and Registrar (https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/about-us/road-to-regulation/); self-regulation of TCM practitioners and acupuncturists in Ontario began in 2013, making Ontario the second Canadian province to regulate the profession (⚠️ VERIFY exact proclamation date - CTCMPAO's own "Road to Regulation" and "Regulations" pages did not state the specific month/day this pass, though secondary sources place it in April 2013).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CTCMPAO informs practitioners of their professional and ethical responsibilities and informs the public of the standard of care to expect; sets requirements for becoming a registered TCM Practitioner and/or Acupuncturist in Ontario; runs a continuing quality-improvement/quality-assurance program; and investigates and responds to concerns about registrants' conduct, competence, or quality of care (CTCMPAO Annual Report 2021-2022, p.3, https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/resources/forms-and-documents/2021-2022_CTCMPAO_AnnualReport.pdf). Registrants are organized for Council-election purposes into five electoral districts spanning the Counties of Kenora, Rainy River, Thunder Bay, Algoma and Cochrane through to the City of Toronto and the Regional Municipality of York (same source, p.4).

Governance & reporting line

CTCMPAO's governing body is its Council, described in the College's own reporting as functioning as "its Board of Directors," responsible for policy-setting and oversight (CTCMPAO Annual Report 2021-2022, p.3, https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/resources/forms-and-documents/2021-2022_CTCMPAO_AnnualReport.pdf). Under the TCM Act, Council must include a minimum of five and no more than eight public members, appointed by the Ontario government (Lieutenant Governor in Council), alongside professional members elected by registrants in the five electoral districts; each statutory committee (Executive, Inquiries Complaints and Reports, Patient Relations, etc.) likewise combines public and professional members (same source, p.3-4). Between the government's 2021 move to repeal the TCM Act (subsequently withdrawn) and March 2022, Council operated without its full statutory complement of publicly appointed members; the government appointed additional public members in March 2022, restoring Council to full constitution (same source, p.2, p.4). As a health-professions regulatory college under the RHPA framework, CTCMPAO is listed on Ontario's central Public Appointments Secretariat agencies roster and is accountable to the Ontario Minister of Health for its legislated mandate (registry row on-council-college-traditional-chinese-medicine-pra, source_authority https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agencies-list).

Budget scale

For the fiscal year ended March 31, 2022 (the most recent registered annual report), CTCMPAO reported total revenue of $3,154,626 (membership dues $2,693,444; examination fees $385,630; other $75,552) against total expenses of $3,240,718, producing a deficiency of revenue over expenses from operations, before depreciation, of $86,092; after $98,962 in depreciation, the College recorded a net deficiency of revenue over expenses of $185,054 for the year. Net assets stood at $5,438,741 at year-end (down from $5,623,795 the prior year), split across an Operating Fund ($2,938,741) and reserve funds for Investigations and Hearings ($1,000,000), Sexual Abuse Therapy ($200,000), Strategic Initiatives ($500,000), Contingency ($500,000) and Property and Technology ($300,000) (CTCMPAO Annual Report 2021-2022, Summary Statement of Financial Position and Statement of Operations, p.47-48, https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/resources/forms-and-documents/2021-2022_CTCMPAO_AnnualReport.pdf). Institutional history

The TCM Act, 2006 received Legislative Assembly approval in December 2006, enabling appointment of a Transitional Council and Registrar to build the College's organizational structure ahead of self-regulation (https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/about-us/road-to-regulation/); a run of Transitional Council annual reports from 2008-2011 is registered for this org_id (research/atlas/government_documents.csv rows -ar-2009, -ar-2010, -ar-2011). Self-regulation of TCM practitioners and acupuncturists formally began in 2013 (⚠️ VERIFY exact date - not confirmed directly on CTCMPAO's own site this pass). In 2021-22 the Council briefly lost its full complement of government-appointed public members amid a provincial move to repeal the TCM Act, which the government subsequently reversed; public members were restored to Council in March 2022, allowing the College to function as a fully constituted regulator (CTCMPAO Annual Report 2021-2022, p.2, https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/resources/forms-and-documents/2021-2022_CTCMPAO_AnnualReport.pdf). CTCMPAO's current strategic plan covers 2023-2028 (https://www.ctcmpao.on.ca/about-us/strategic-plan/).
