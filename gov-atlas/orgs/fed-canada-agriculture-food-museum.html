Canada Agriculture and Food Museum (Ingenium) - backgrounder

Backgrounder / this library's government-strategy research Batch 12 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-canada-agriculture-food-museum (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canada Agriculture and Food Museum (CAFM) is not itself a separate legal entity. It is one of three national museum sites operated by a single federal Crown corporation whose legal name under the Museums Act, S.C. 1990, c. 3 is "National Museum of Science and Technology," commonly branded "Ingenium – Canada's Museums of Science and Innovation" since 2017 (Ingenium Annual Report 2024-25, sha256-12 f282fa612d43, p.4, note 1; Ingenium Corporate Plan Summary 2026-27 to 2030-31, sha256-12 2ec815d9d4f8, p.4). The Corporation's Museums Act mandate: "To foster scientific and technological literacy throughout Canada by establishing, maintaining, and developing a collection of scientific and technological objects, with special but not exclusive reference to Canada, and by demonstrating the products and processes of science and technology and their economic, social, and cultural relationships with society" (Annual Report, sha256-12 f282fa612d43, p.8; Corporate Plan Summary, sha256-12 2ec815d9d4f8, p.5). The Corporation is named in Part I of Schedule III to the Financial Administration Act and is not subject to income tax (Annual Report, sha256-12 f282fa612d43, p.39, Note 1).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CAFM operates as "a unique, demonstrative working farm featuring hundreds of animals including heritage breeds and a dairy operation," showcasing "the relationships between the environment, science, technology, agriculture and food, through the lens of sustainable agriculture" (Corporate Plan Summary, sha256-12 2ec815d9d4f8, p.5). It is administered alongside two sister sites — the Canada Aviation and Space Museum (CASM) and the Canada Science and Technology Museum (CSTM) — under Ingenium's shared collection, governance, and financial structure; there is no CAFM-specific board, statute, or standalone budget (Annual Report, sha256-12 f282fa612d43, p.24). This is a federal Crown institution entirely distinct from, and administratively unconnected to, the City of Toronto orgs in this batch.

Governance & reporting line

Ingenium is governed by a Board of Trustees of up to 11 members (currently one vacancy), appointed by the Governor in Council on the recommendation of the Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages, to whom the Board is accountable to Parliament (Annual Report, sha256-12 f282fa612d43, p.4). Day-to-day operations are led by the President and CEO (Christina Tessier, reappointed to a second five-year term June 11, 2023), supported by an executive team that includes a site-level Director General, Canada Agriculture and Food Museum (Kerry-Leigh Burchill as of March 31, 2025) (Annual Report, sha256-12 f282fa612d43, p.6; Corporate Plan Summary, sha256-12 2ec815d9d4f8, p.7). The Corporation operates at arm's length from government under Part X of the Financial Administration Act (Corporate Plan Summary, sha256-12 2ec815d9d4f8, p.4).

Budget scale

Corporation-wide (not CAFM-specific — no site-level budget is separately published): 2026-27 operating and capital budgets total $56.601M (cash basis): $41.03M from parliamentary appropriations, $12.3M from revenues/contributions/interest, $2M from deferred appropriations, and $1.271M drawn from unrestricted net assets (Corporate Plan Summary, sha256-12 2ec815d9d4f8, p.3). CAFM-specific figures reported within the corporate totals: $920K in budgeted 2026-27 admission/program revenue (of $9.3M corporate operating revenue) and a $2.0M "CAFM Lifecycle repairs" capital line for 2026-27, the single largest site-specific capital item that year (same source, p.21, 27).

Institutional history

Established as a Crown corporation by the Museums Act, 1990 (in force July 1, 1990) (Annual Report, sha256-12 f282fa612d43, p.39, Note 1). Ingenium completed its 2020-2025 Strategic Plan in fiscal 2024-25 and launched a new Strategic Plan for 2025-2030 on April 1, 2025 (Annual Report, sha256-12 f282fa612d43, p.2; Corporate Plan Summary, sha256-12 2ec815d9d4f8, p.9) — see the brief's Priority evolution section for how the two plans' named strategic goals differ.

Canada Agriculture and Food Museum - strategy evolution

TL;DR: This is a federal Crown institution, not a City of Toronto org — CAFM is one of three museum sites operated by Ingenium (legal name "National Museum of Science and Technology") under the Museums Act, with no CAFM-specific budget, board, or strategic plan of its own. The biggest priority addition is Ingenium's new 2025-2030 Strategic Plan (launched April 1, 2025), whose three goals were renamed from the prior 2020-2025 plan's goals, though the corporate plan itself describes the new plan as "an evolution of," not a break from, the old one. The load-bearing number is CAFM-specific: a $2.0 million "CAFM Lifecycle repairs" capital allocation for 2026-27, the single largest site-specific capital line that year. The biggest quietly changed element is Ingenium's financial trajectory: from a modest $0.8M operating loss in 2024-25 to a projected structural deficit reaching -$14.9M by 2030-31 as temporary federal top-up funding is assumed to expire, even though — unlike Statistics Canada, this batch's format-reference org — Ingenium states explicitly it "was not subject to reductions from the Comprehensive Expenditure Review." One open question the two-document window cannot resolve: whether Parliament renews the temporary funding beyond 2026-27, which the corporate plan's own deficit projection assumes will not happen.

Backgrounder summary

Ingenium ("National Museum of Science and Technology" under the Museums Act, 1990) is a federal Crown corporation named in Part I of Schedule III to the Financial Administration Act, mandated "to foster scientific and technological literacy throughout Canada by establishing, maintaining, and developing a collection of scientific and technological objects... and by demonstrating the products and processes of science and technology." It operates three sites — the Canada Agriculture and Food Museum (CAFM), Canada Aviation and Space Museum (CASM), and Canada Science and Technology Museum (CSTM) — under a single Board of Trustees (Governor-in-Council appointees) accountable to Parliament through the Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture. CAFM itself has no separate legal, governance, or budgetary structure; it is administered by a site-level Director General reporting to Ingenium's President and CEO. This org's enabling basis and governance are entirely separate from this batch's four City of Toronto orgs (tor-communications, tor-people-equity, tor-toronto-cyber-security, tor-technology-services) and no shared-document relationship exists between them.

Priority evolution

2024-25 (f282fa612d43): Reports the final year of Ingenium's 2020-2025 Strategic Plan, organized under three strategic goals: "Collaborate to Innovate," "Access for All," "Strengthen our Foundations." Corporate performance: "met or surpassed 89% of its reported metrics." CAFM-specific activity cited: a new Sustainable Agriculture Gallery ("funded in part by Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada"), accessibility improvements following a Rick Hansen Foundation audit, a Canadian Museums Association honourable mention for an Aquaculture travelling exhibition, and a CHEO summer-camp accessibility partnership (p.10-14).

2026-27 (2ec815d9d4f8): Reports Ingenium's new 2025-2030 Strategic Plan (launched April 1, 2025), reorganized under three renamed goals: "Connect Society with Science, Technology, and Innovation," "Cultivate a Sense of Belonging," "Strengthen Ingenium's Foundations." CAFM-specific activity named for the plan period: continued Sustainable Agriculture Gallery/exhibition renewal work and a long-term site-plan process (p.13-14), and — carried over as a named risk mitigation from the prior year's risk register — a review of the "CAFM Master Site Plan to... reduce biosecurity risks" (ar-2025, p.27; corporate plan does not repeat this specific item verbatim, ⚠️ still being checked whether it remains active).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Renamed — all three corporate strategic goals: "Collaborate to Innovate" → "Connect Society with Science, Technology, and Innovation"; "Access for All" → "Cultivate a Sense of Belonging"; "Strengthen our Foundations" → "Strengthen Ingenium's Foundations" (f282fa612d43, p.2; 2ec815d9d4f8, p.9-14). The underlying substance overlaps heavily (both plans name access/inclusion, financial sustainability, and public STEAM engagement as goals); the corporate plan itself describes the new plan as "an evolution of work achieved in the 2020-2025 Strategic Plan" (f282fa612d43, p.28) rather than a break from it.
  • Added — a dated governance change: the Sustainability and Outreach Committee, active through 2024-25 (3 meetings) (f282fa612d43, p.5), "did not meet in 2025-26 and was dissolved by the Board of Trustees in September 2025" (2ec815d9d4f8, p.7).
  • Added — explicit fiscal-restraint framing absent from ar-2025: the corporate plan states "While Ingenium was not subject to reductions from the Comprehensive Expenditure Review, it continues to exercise fiscal restraint and find efficiencies, in line with Government of Canada objectives" (2ec815d9d4f8, p.3) — this federal government-wide CER context (also named for a different org, fed-statistics-canada, in this batch's format-reference brief) does not appear at all in ar-2025.
  • No priority is stated as dropped outright; CAFM's Sustainable Agriculture Gallery and site-plan work continue across both documents.

CAFM is an Ontario institution but not GTA/Toronto-specific; neither document names any Toronto-specific program, partnership, or presence.
