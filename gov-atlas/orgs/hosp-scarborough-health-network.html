Scarborough Health Network - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: hosp-scarborough-health-network (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

SHN operates as an approved public hospital under the Public Hospitals Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.40, which requires an approved governing board and empowers the Minister of Health to issue binding directives and set funding conditions (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p40, verified 2026-07-30). Its own audited financial statements confirm the corporate lineage directly: "On December 1, 2016, the Birchmount and General sites of The Scarborough Hospital and the Centenary site of Rouge Valley Health System amalgamated forming Scarborough and Rouge Hospital. In December 2018, the legal name was changed from Scarborough and Rouge Hospital to Scarborough Health Network" (Scarborough Health Network Consolidated Audited Financial Statements, year ended March 31, 2025, document id hosp-scarborough-health-network-fs-2025, https://www.shn.ca/wp-content/uploads/SHN_2024-25-Audited-Financial-Statements.pdf).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

SHN is "a multi-location acute care community hospital" operating three sites — General, Birchmount, and Centenary (document id hosp-scarborough-health-network-fs-2025; registry row hosp-scarborough-health-network, notes field). It signed a 2023-24 Hospital Service Accountability Agreement with Ontario Health that included a balanced total-margin target, later extended by OH (document id hosp-scarborough-health-network-fs-2025).

Governance & reporting line

SHN is governed by a Board of Directors; its independent auditor's report is addressed "To the Board of Directors of Scarborough Health Network" (document id hosp-scarborough-health-network-fs-2025). The consolidated statements exclude the separately governed Scarborough Health Network Research Institute and the Scarborough Health Network Foundation (document id hosp-scarborough-health-network-fs-2025). Per the registry, Ontario Health (on-ontario-health) is the funding/oversight parent for this independent, non-profit hospital corporation.

Budget scale

~$1.055B total consolidated revenue against ~$1.066B total consolidated expenses for the year ended March 31, 2025 (a deficiency of $10.8M), of which $848.3M came from the Ministry of Health and Cancer Care Ontario (Scarborough Health Network Consolidated Audited Financial Statements FY2025, document id hosp-scarborough-health-network-fs-2025).

Institutional history

Predecessor sites trace to Scarborough General Hospital (opened 1956), Centenary Hospital (opened 1967, named for Confederation's centenary), and Scarborough Grace/Birchmount Hospital (opened 1984 by The Salvation Army); a 1998 amalgamation created The Scarborough Hospital (General + Grace/Birchmount) while Centenary joined Ajax and Pickering to form Rouge Valley Health System (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough_Health_Network, fetched via r.jina.ai 2026-07-30). The province dissolved Rouge Valley in 2016, moving Centenary to The Scarborough Hospital to form the current three-site network (renamed "Scarborough and Rouge Hospital" effective December 1, 2016, then "Scarborough Health Network" in December 2018) — this final stage independently confirmed in the organization's own audited financial statements (document id hosp-scarborough-health-network-fs-2025).

Scarborough Health Network - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 (rolling continuation) / registry: hosp-scarborough-health-network / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest addition is a new five-year plan, "We Are Reimagining Care" (2024-2029), replacing the predecessor "Transforming Care" era with five named Strategic Directions and its own People, Health Literacy, and Education strategies. The most notable quiet drop is SHN's role as host hospital for the seven-hospital regional Epic clinical information system, which ended April 1, 2025 when operations transferred to a new joint corporation, "Enhance Ontario" — alongside the Health Equity Certificate Program (prominent in 2020-21) disappearing without explanation by the 2024 strategic plan. The most load-bearing number is FY2024-25 revenue of $1,055,008 thousand against $1,065,847 thousand in expenses, crossing $1B for the first time but posting a $(10,839) thousand deficiency — the second-largest deficit in the series after FY2022-23's $(24,485) thousand, which coincided with the Bill 124 wage-reopener liability. One open question the record cannot resolve: the exact date SHN's Ontario Health regional designation changed from "Ontario Health - East" to "Ontario Health - Toronto," since no FY2024 financial statement exists to narrow the window.

Backgrounder summary

Scarborough Health Network (SHN) is an approved public hospital under Ontario's Public Hospitals Act, formed December 1, 2016 as "Scarborough and Rouge Hospital" from the amalgamation of the Birchmount and General sites of The Scarborough Hospital with the Centenary site of the (province-dissolved) Rouge Valley Health System, then renamed Scarborough Health Network in December 2018. It is a multi-location acute care community hospital operating three sites (General, Birchmount, Centenary), governed by an independent Board of Directors, with Ontario Health as its funding/oversight parent. Its FY2025 consolidated financials show ~$1.055B revenue against ~$1.066B expenses (a $10.8M deficiency), with $848.3M of that revenue from the Ministry of Health and Cancer Care Ontario. The consolidated statements exclude the separately governed SHN Research Institute and SHN Foundation. This brief independently verifies these figures against the archived series below and extends the picture across FY2020-FY2025.

Priority evolution

FY2019-20 (fs-2020, abcd2ac73000; ar-2020, 8d6b929c6a27): The Hospital reports a small consolidated deficiency of $541 thousand on revenue of $686.4M (fs-2020), funded primarily through the "2019-2020 Hospital Service Accountability Agreement" with the Central East Local Health Integration Network ("LHIN"), which included a balanced total-margin target (fs-2020, note 1). On December 17, 2019 the Hospital issued $120M in series-A unsecured debentures (2.894%, 40-year term, maturing December 17, 2059) to finance current and future capital projects (fs-2020, note 11). The annual report frames the year around a "Transforming Care" clinical-services-planning initiative launched the prior year, producing two new women's-and-children's centres of excellence at Centenary and General (2019) and an Integrated Stroke Unit at Birchmount (2020), alongside the founding of the Scarborough Ontario Health Team (SOHT), a 30-plus-organization local network (ar-2020, 8d6b929c6a27). The report separately notes $2,220 thousand in COVID-19 pandemic-response spending to March 31, 2020, with $1,801 thousand of anticipated recovery revenue recognized (fs-2020, note 21).

FY2020-21 (fs-2021, 12eb30c99a13; ar-2021, 25e7277d82ba): Revenue rose to $762.2M against $757.7M expenses; a one-time $26,833 thousand Ministry of Health working-capital grant, explicitly intended "to address systemic working fund deficits," pushed the reported excess of revenue over expenses to $31,421 thousand (fs-2021, note 23; statement of operations). Effective April 1, 2021, Ontario Health ("OH"), a Crown agency established June 6, 2019, "assumed all responsibilities of LHIN as it relates to the Hospital," with all LHIN agreements transferred to OH (fs-2021, note 1). The annual report documents an intense COVID-19 operating year — 3,091 COVID-19 positive patients treated, 578,000+ vaccine doses administered, a new VaxFacts clinic, and a Team-Based Model of Care shift — alongside new service launches (SCOPE hotline, SHN@Home, Virtual Urgent Care Clinic, a Long COVID Service, and an Orthopedic Trauma Centre of Excellence) (ar-2021, 25e7277d82ba). SHN joined the Toronto Academic Health Science Network (TAHSN) in June of this reporting year, and was preparing to go live December 3 with a shared Epic clinical information system spanning seven hospitals in Ontario's central-east region (ar-2021, 25e7277d82ba).

FY2021-22 (fs-2022, 1dd4dc15cf31): Revenue reached $798.4M against $795.6M expenses, an excess of $2,822 thousand — no comparable one-time grant this year (statement of operations). The Hospital's accountability agreement is now described as a "2019-2021 Hospital Service Accountability Agreement" with "Ontario Health - East," extended by OH's own discretion to cover 2021-2022 as well (fs-2022, note 1). A new "Other long-term liabilities" note discloses upfront licensing fees for the Hospital's clinical information system, payable monthly through December 31, 2025, plus a $3,941 thousand annual software-maintenance commitment (fs-2022, note 13) — the financial-statement footprint of the Epic go-live described in ar-2021. Pandemic-related MOH funding fell from $132.3M (2021) to $83.5M (2022) across categories including incremental COVID-19 operating expenses, temporary physician funding, and pandemic wage-enhancement programs (fs-2022, note 24).

FY2022-23 (fs-2023, 13f98c61168e): The Hospital reports its largest deficiency in the archived series: $(24,485) thousand, on revenue of $851.5M against $876.0M expenses (statement of operations). Two new structural items appear this year: first, the consolidated statements now also exclude "The Scarborough Health Network Research Institute (the 'SHNRI')" in addition to the Foundation (fs-2023, description of business) — the first appearance of SHNRI as a named, separately governed entity in this series; second, the Hospital adopted Canadian public sector accounting standard PS 3280 (Asset Retirement Obligations) effective April 1, 2021 on a modified retroactive basis, restating opening FY2022 net assets down by $10,335 thousand for asbestos-abatement and equipment-removal obligations (fs-2023, note 2). The accountability agreement is now described as extended to cover both 2021-2022 and 2022-2023 (fs-2023, description of business). A new note discloses that the Hospital "has recorded a liability at March 31, 2023 for the estimated impact of retroactive salary increases related to the repeal of Bill 124" (the Protecting a Sustainable Public Sector for Future Generations Act, 2019), following a November 29, 2022 Ontario Superior Court ruling voiding the wage-cap legislation; as of this filing it remained "uncertain whether the Ministry of Health will directly fund this expense" (fs-2023, note 22(d) and note 26).

Strategic Plan 2024-2029 (sp-2024, 715053eea403): SHN adopted a new five-year plan titled "We Are Reimagining Care," built on a 2023 engagement process named "Reimagining Care: Strategic Planning Process" that included community forums, a Community Advisory Council, and interviews with Ontario Health, the Ministry of Health, and Ontario Health Teams (sp-2024, 715053eea403). The plan states a vision of becoming "Canada's leading community teaching health network" and a mission of "improving lives through exceptional care," underpinned by four values — Compassionate, Inclusive, Innovative, Courageous — that echo the same four values (Compassion, Inclusion, Innovation, Courage) named in ar-2020 (sp-2024, 715053eea403; ar-2020, 8d6b929c6a27). Five "Strategic Directions" are named: People and Culture; System Integration and Transformation; Population Health and Equity; Research and Education; and Quality and Patient Safety, each carrying its own named Year-1 and Year-2 accomplishments and Year-3 priorities (sp-2024, 715053eea403). Documented Year-1/Year-2 accomplishments include a new People Strategy 2024-2029, a new Health Literacy Strategy 2024-2029, a new Education Strategy 2024-2029, an Interprofessional Primary Care Team (IPCT) at General Hospital (600+ patients attached to a primary-care provider in its first six months, 2,000+ by Year 2), an Urgent Care Centre opened March 2024, Indigenous self-identification signage, a specialized sickle-cell-disease care pathway, and a 21-station dialysis-unit expansion at Centenary plus six new dialysis rooms at General (sp-2024, 715053eea403).

FY2024-25 (fs-2025, 028709150f1a; ar-2025, 259d67e9c62c): Revenue reached $1,055,008 thousand against $1,065,847 thousand expenses, a deficiency of $(10,839) thousand — the series' first year crossing $1B in revenue, and the second-largest deficit in the archived series (fs-2025, statement of operations). Ministry of Health and Cancer Care Ontario funding was $848,295 thousand, matching the backgrounder's cited figure (fs-2025, statement of operations). The Hospital's regional partner is now named "Ontario Health - Toronto," a change from "Ontario Health - East" used in fs-2022/fs-2023 (no fs-2024 document is archived to date this transition precisely) (fs-2025, description of business). A new "2023-24 Hospital Service Accountability Agreement" replaced the multi-year-extended 2019-2021 agreement, and was itself extended to include 2024-25 (fs-2025, description of business). Grants received from the Foundation nearly doubled, from $16,032 thousand (FY2024) to $29,859 thousand (FY2025) (fs-2025, note 19) — corroborating the annual report's claim that "SHN Foundation granted its highest-ever funding to SHN," funding two new donor-supported facilities: a Community Mental Health Centre and the Northpine Diagnostic Imaging Department at General Hospital (ar-2025, 259d67e9c62c). The Bill 124 liability first booked in fs-2023 is now resolved on the funding side: "The MOH has provided the Hospital with funding to address the cost of the retroactive wage adjustments" (fs-2025, note 24). As a subsequent event, effective April 1, 2025, the Hospital's role as host hospital for the seven-hospital regional Epic clinical information system ended: operations, assets ($59,157 thousand long-term carrying value) and liabilities transferred to "Enhance Ontario," a new corporation jointly controlled by the participating hospitals (fs-2025, note 25). The annual report itself is rebranded from "SHN 365 - Year in Review" (ar-2020, ar-2021) to "Impact Unlocked" / "SHN 365 (2024-2025)," and names a new President and CEO, David Graham, alongside continuing Board Chair Matt Ainley (ar-2025, 259d67e9c62c) — Elizabeth Buller held the CEO role as of ar-2020 and ar-2021.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This organization's Toronto/Scarborough footprint is not inferred — it is the subject of the archived series itself. SHN operates three hospital sites (General, Birchmount, Centenary) serving "more than 850,000 people from the Scarborough community and beyond," with 8 designated City of Toronto neighbourhood-improvement areas, 60% newcomers to Canada, and 75% visible minorities among its service population (ar-2025, 259d67e9c62c). Named Toronto-specific partnerships and affiliations documented across the series include: membership in the Toronto Academic Health Science Network, joined in FY2020-21 (ar-2021, 25e7277d82ba); "Scarborough Anchor Institutions" partnership with the Toronto Zoo, Centennial College, and the University of Toronto Scarborough (ar-2021, 25e7277d82ba); a University of Toronto Scarborough Academy of Medicine and Integrated Health clinical-teaching partnership, cited as driving record medical-student intake for 2025-26 (sp-2024, 715053eea403; ar-2025, 259d67e9c62c); recognition in 2021 as "the first community-based teaching hospital in Toronto to join an elite group of Ontario hospitals that have achieved Stage 6" HIMSS EMRAM status (sp-2024, 715053eea403); the 2021 Scarborough Hero Awards, presented with then-Toronto Mayor John Tory and the Ontario Poet Laureate in attendance (ar-2021, 25e7277d82ba); and a 2024 Toronto Women in Emergency Medicine Lifetime Achievement Award to an SHN emergency physician (ar-2025, 259d67e9c62c). The Hospital's own financial statements name its Ontario Health regional-oversight partner as "Ontario Health - Toronto" as of FY2025, a change from "Ontario Health - East" in FY2022/FY2023 (fs-2025, 028709150f1a; fs-2022, 1dd4dc15cf31; fs-2023, 13f98c61168e) — see Residuals & gaps for the undated nature of that relabeling. No document in this series describes SHN operating, or seeking to operate, outside the Scarborough/Toronto-East service area.
