Toronto Police Service - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: tor-toronto-police-service (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

TPS sits under a dual statutory framework: the City of Toronto Act, 2006, S.O. 2006, c. 11, Sched. A, and the Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019, S.O. 2019, c. 1, Sched. 1 (CSPA) (both fetch-verified: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11; https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c01). COTA s.402(1) continues the former "Toronto Police Services Board" as the "Toronto Police Service Board" as of CSPA's coming into force, and s.403 authorizes TPS to perform policing services beyond the CSPA baseline within the City (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11). CSPA itself is the general provincial statute governing municipal police services and boards (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c01).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

TPS provides municipal policing for the City of Toronto from headquarters at 40 College Street, organized into 16 patrol divisions plus specialized units such as the Emergency Task Force, Transit Bureau and Organized Crime Enforcement (registry row, registry id: tor-toronto-police-service; https://www.tps.ca/mission-vision-values/). Approved staffing complement for 2026 is 8,419 positions (2026 Operating Budget and 2026-2035 Capital Budget and Plan: Toronto Police Service, https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/967f-2026-Public-Book-TPS-V1.pdf).

Governance & reporting line

TPS reports to the Toronto Police Service Board (registry parent_org: tor-toronto-police-services-board), continued under COTA s.402(1) and exercising the policy-setting, budget-approval and Chief-appointment duties assigned to police service boards under CSPA Part IV (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11; https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c01). Registry notes flag that a dedicated, currently-posted strategic plan could not be confirmed this review; the current lens document on file is the 2026 Operating Budget and 2026-2035 Capital Budget and Plan (registry row strategy_current_url, notes field).

Budget scale

~$1.70B gross 2026 operating budget ($1,432.9M net, after $271.4M in revenues) (2026 Operating Budget and 2026-2035 Capital Budget and Plan: Toronto Police Service, https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/967f-2026-Public-Book-TPS-V1.pdf).

Institutional history

Tracing to the Toronto Police force established in 1834, the service merged with 12 other municipal forces in 1957 to form the Metropolitan Toronto Police, and was renamed Toronto Police Service in 1998 upon the amalgamation of the City of Toronto (fetched secondary source, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Police_Service; ⚠️ still being checked against a City of Toronto/TPS primary source).

2026-08-02 / registry: tor-toronto-police-service / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

Priority evolution

This is a two-point series with a nine-to-ten-year gap between the only two Service-authored strategy statements in the archive (bp-2014/2016, covering the 2014-2016 planning cycle, and ar-2024, covering calendar 2024). No document in this archive covers 2017-2023 from the Service's own strategic voice. Evolution claims below are scoped strictly to those two documents; nothing here should be read as continuous coverage of the intervening years.

2013-14 to 2016 (bp-2014/bp-2016, 6309000ebb28), "2014-2016 Business Plan," Board Chair Alok Mukherjee and Chief William Blair: The Service's stated priorities were three: Safe Communities and Neighbourhoods, Economic Sustainability & Operational Excellence, and High Quality Professional Service to the Community. Under the third priority, explicit goals included "implement organizational and operational changes that address concerns of bias, discrimination, and profiling, including racial profiling, to improve public trust and confidence," with performance indicators tracking community perception of officer trustworthiness and fair treatment (6309000ebb28). The plan's own environmental-scan data reported that the proportion of Toronto residents who believed police targeted members of minority or ethnic groups for enforcement had risen from 17% (2011) to 34% (2012), and that community-reported satisfaction with the Service had fallen from 97% (2011) to 81% (2012) (6309000ebb28) — the plan does not characterize these as crises, only as scan findings informing priority-setting.

2024 (ar-2024, a0fee0562e99), Chief Myron Demkiw, Board Chair Councillor Shelley Carroll: The report frames the year around "Chief's Priorities" (unnamed as a fixed list in the text extracted, displayed per-article) and around two organization-wide themes: "delivery of core services" (with an explicit focus on reducing 9-1-1 response times) and "accelerating police reform." The Board's own message states the Board was, as of this report, still "in the process of developing its Strategic Plan," guided by a draft vision statement, meaning no completed, board-approved strategic plan is confirmed anywhere in this archive (a0fee0562e99). The Equity Strategy — released December 2023, per the report "for the first time in the organization's history" — is presented as the current organizing document for equity-related priorities, structured around four named priorities: Confronting Anti-Black Racism, Supporting Indigenous Cultural Safety, Fostering 2SLGBTQI+ Inclusion, and Cultivating a Respectful Workplace (a0fee0562e99).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — a named, structured Equity Strategy with four sub-priorities (Anti-Black Racism / Indigenous Cultural Safety / 2SLGBTQI+ Inclusion / Respectful Workplace): absent as a named document from bp-2014/2016, which addressed bias/discrimination as goals under the broader "High Quality Professional Service" priority without a dedicated equity framework; first published December 2023 per ar-2024 (a0fee0562e99).
  • Added — SafeTO, a named 10-year Community Safety & Wellbeing Plan with 26 Actions across 7 Strategic Goals, co-designed with the City of Toronto: not present in bp-2014/2016's priority structure; described in ar-2024 as an established, multi-year framework already organized into "Priority Zones," implying adoption sometime in the unread 2017-2023 interval, though this archive cannot date its origin more precisely (a0fee0562e99). ⚠️ still being checked exact adoption date — not stated in either archived document.
  • Added — a formal external police-reform implementation program tied to the 2020 "Police Reform in Toronto" report (Chair Jim Hart), tracked as 81 Directions, 71 of 81 reported implemented as of ar-2024: no analog exists in bp-2014/2016, which predates the report by six years; the reform report itself is not in this archive (a0fee0562e99).
  • Renamed/reframed — "bias-free service" goal language (2014-2016) becomes formal Equity Strategy priorities (2023-24): the underlying subject (racial profiling, discrimination) persists across both documents, but the 2014-2016 framing treated it as one goal among several under a general service-quality priority, while the 2024 framing gives it a dedicated, named, four-part strategy document — the reports do not explain the intervening steps of that structural change.
  • Continuity — response-time reduction as a named priority in both documents, though the two reports do not use comparable measurement bases (see Residuals); the persistence of "reduce response times" as a stated priority across a decade-plus gap is itself the one evolution claim this thin record can support with confidence.
  • Cannot assess — economic sustainability / operational excellence priority (2014-2016) and its 2024 status: ar-2024 does not use this phrase or an evident successor framing; whether it was dropped, renamed, or simply not restated in a differently-organized report cannot be determined from two documents alone. ⚠️ still being checked.
