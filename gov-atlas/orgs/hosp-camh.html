Centre for Addiction and Mental Health - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: hosp-camh (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

CAMH operates as an approved public hospital under the Public Hospitals Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.40, which requires an approved governing board and empowers the Minister of Health to issue binding operational directives and set funding conditions (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p40, verified 2026-07-30). CAMH's own audited financial statements confirm this directly: "CAMH was incorporated by Letters Patent of Amalgamation under the Corporations Act (Ontario) without share capital on January 23, 1998... The operations of CAMH are subject to the provisions of the Public Hospitals Act (Ontario)" (CAMH Audited Financial Statements, year ended March 31, 2025, document id hosp-camh-fs-2025, https://www.camh.ca/-/media/driving-change-files/annual-reports/centre-for-addiction-and-mental-health_financial-statement-2025_final-pdf.pdf).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CAMH is a specialty psychiatric hospital on two primary Toronto sites, providing clinical care and recovery services, research, and education in addiction and mental health, and playing "a significant role in policy development and support in Ontario's mental health and addictions system" (document id hosp-camh-fs-2025). It operates the province's only stand-alone psychiatric emergency department and describes itself as Canada's largest mental health teaching hospital, fully affiliated with the University of Toronto (https://www.camh.ca/en/driving-change/about-camh, fetched 2026-07-30).

Governance & reporting line

CAMH is governed by a Board of Trustees: its audited financial statements are addressed "To the Board of Trustees of the Centre for Addiction and Mental Health" and approved for release by the "Chair, Board of Trustees" and the "Chair, Audit, Finance and Resource Committee" (document id hosp-camh-fs-2025). Operationally, CAMH is funded and held to account through Multi-Sector and Hospital Service Accountability Agreements with Ontario Health (https://www.camh.ca/en/driving-change/about-camh/performance-and-accountability, fetched 2026-07-30), consistent with the registry's parent_org listing of on-ontario-health as the funding/oversight parent for this independent, non-profit hospital corporation (registry row hosp-camh).

Budget scale

~$740.6M total revenue against ~$709.8M total expenses for the year ended March 31, 2025, yielding an excess of revenue over expenses of $30.9M; of total revenue, $558.8M came from the Ministry of Health and federal government (CAMH Audited Financial Statements FY2025, document id hosp-camh-fs-2025).

Institutional history

CAMH was formed on January 23, 1998 through the amalgamation of four predecessor institutions — the Queen Street Mental Health Centre (tracing to the 1850 Provincial Lunatic Asylum), the Clarke Institute of Psychiatry (1966), the Addiction Research Foundation (1949, originally the Alcoholism Research Foundation), and the Donwood Institute (1967) (document id hosp-camh-fs-2025 for the 1998 amalgamation date; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Addiction_and_Mental_Health, fetched via r.jina.ai 2026-07-30, for the four founding institutions and their individual dates — ⚠️ still being checked against a CAMH-authored history page, not confirmed against a primary CAMH source this review).

Backgrounder summary

CAMH is an approved public hospital under Ontario's Public Hospitals Act, incorporated by Letters Patent of Amalgamation under the Corporations Act (Ontario) on January 23, 1998, from the merger of four predecessor institutions. It operates as a specialty psychiatric hospital on two Toronto sites, providing clinical care, research and education in addiction and mental health, and plays a stated role in Ontario mental health and addictions policy. It is governed by a Board of Trustees and funded and held to account primarily through the Ministry of Health, with Ontario Health as the registry's listed funding/oversight parent. For the year ended March 31, 2025, CAMH reported total revenue of approximately $740.6M against $709.8M in expenses, an excess of $30.9M, with $558.8M of revenue from the Ministry of Health and federal government combined.

Priority evolution

FY2019-20 (ar-2020, 58b598a8c018; fs-2020, bda120221a90): The report opens mid-pandemic ("difficult times... our hospital focuses on keeping our patients and community safe"), noting virtual-care visits rose from ~350/month to ~3,000/month between March and April 2020, and describes community consultation underway on a new strategic plan, "One CAMH," to "guide the organization into 2023" (58b598a8c018). CAMH released a new Alcohol Policy Framework and disseminated health-equity initiatives led by teams including Shkaabe Makwa and a Horizontal Violence, Anti-Racism, Anti-Oppression Working Group (58b598a8c018). Financially, Ministry of Health revenue was $357.0M (2019: $345.4M) of total revenue $475.0M against expenses of $474.9M, an excess of $151K (fs-2020, bda120221a90).

FY2020-21 (ar-2021, 7c7568ac8e81; fs-2021, 791829e57de2): CAMH states it "embarked on the first year of our new strategic plan, One CAMH, with a vision of redefining health," organized in the report under "Inspire / Include / Impact" (7c7568ac8e81). In November 2020 CAMH "completed the largest phase of its bold redevelopment" with the opening of the McCain Complex Care & Recovery Building and Crisis & Critical Care Building. In February 2021 CAMH launched "Dismantling Anti-Black Racism," a strategy naming 22 actions to be completed by 2022; the report states Black people in the GTA are more likely to report poor mental health and more likely to access care through emergency or police services. CAMH also formally launched Shkaabe Makwa as an integrated Indigenous-health centre, twenty years after the founding of its predecessor team (7c7568ac8e81). Financially, the revenue line was relabeled "Ministry of Health and federal government" (from "Ministry of Health" alone) and a new "COVID-19 revenue" line of $17.8M appeared; total revenue was $498.6M against expenses of $498.6M — a nil excess (791829e57de2).

FY2021-22 (ar-2022, fe002a72d8cd; fs-2022, 29df280ac18a): Reporting structure shifts to "Research / Virtual Care and Digital Health / System Leadership / Health Equity / Mental Health Movement." In May 2021 CAMH launched a three-year CAMH Truth and Reconciliation Action Plan; in November 2021 the Substance Abuse Program for African Canadian and Caribbean Youth (SAPACCY) secured $2.9M in annual provincial funding to build a wider network, adding new community sites in Toronto, Peel, Hamilton, Ottawa and Windsor. Youth Wellness Hubs Ontario (YWHO) received a $1M RBC Foundation gift to expand its virtual platform (fe002a72d8cd). A separate financial-statement note records that on June 29, 2020 CAMH entered a five-year, up-to-$21M contribution agreement with the Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC) to help implement a Canadian Suicide Prevention Service with CMHA-National and Crisis Services Canada — the funding root of what becomes the 9-8-8 helpline in FY2023-24 (29df280ac18a). Ministry of Health and federal government revenue rose to $382.5M (COVID-19 funding $13.5M) of total revenue $530.7M against equal expenses — again nil excess (29df280ac18a).

FY2022-23 (ar-2023, eb14ac91991d; fs-2023, 887061d38402): CAMH's 25th-anniversary report states in its own words that CAMH "was created through a merger between what was then called the Queen Street Mental Health Centre, the Addiction Research Foundation, the Clarke Institute of Psychiatry and the Donwood Institute" — a CAMH-authored primary-source confirmation of the four founding institutions the backgrounder had flagged for verification against a non-Wikipedia source (eb14ac91991d). In February 2023 CAMH launched "No One Left Behind," described as "the world's largest fundraising campaign in support of hospital-based mental health research," with a $500M goal to fund a new Research & Discovery Centre; Orlando Corporation's $50M gift is named as the largest corporate donation in CAMH's history. The report states Emergency Department visits exceeded 16,000 (a 10% increase year over year) and that Canada would get a three-digit suicide-prevention number in November 2023, to be coordinated and delivered by CAMH (eb14ac91991d). YWHO reports Government of Ontario funding for eight new hub sites, reaching 22 communities province-wide. Financially, Ministry of Health and federal government revenue rose to $423.4M (COVID-19 funding down to $10.2M) of total revenue $585.7M against equal expenses — a third consecutive year of nil excess (887061d38402).

FY2023-24 (ar-2024, 8e6ef1ad1ecf; fs-2024, 734b7a921d39; sp-2024, 669781d52807): CAMH launches Connected CAMH 2024-2030, its successor strategic plan to One CAMH, built on more than 700 consultations and organized around three "Strategic Directions" — Advance Care, Get Upstream, Lift Societal Health — under a vision of "Health redefined" and mission language on "integrated care," "the most difficult questions about mental illness," and removing "barriers to belonging" (669781d52807). The annual report names a list of "is Health" pillars — Mental Health, Housing, Equity, Truth and Reconciliation, Climate Action, Education, Employment, Income Equity, Family — as framing for the new plan (8e6ef1ad1ecf). In November 2023, with Government of Canada funding, CAMH "led and coordinated the launch of 9-8-8, Canada's first nationwide Suicide Crisis Helpline." On February 29, 2024 CAMH broke ground on the final redevelopment phase — the Temerty Discovery Centre and the Secure Care and Recovery Building — with Ontario Premier Doug Ford and provincial officials attending (8e6ef1ad1ecf). Financially, Ministry of Health and federal government revenue jumped to $486.3M (+14.8% year over year; COVID-19 funding fell to $1.8M), total revenue reached $649.0M against expenses of $648.9M, producing a $136K excess — the first non-nil surplus since FY2019-20 (734b7a921d39). The financial statements record, for the first time in the series, that the redevelopment project is funded by the Ministry of Health, the CAMH Foundation, "and by CAMH through internally generated funds and debt" — the "and debt" language is new to this year's note (734b7a921d39).

FY2024-25 (ar-2025, 1e126e1382da; fs-2025, c0dd312dba92): On May 1, 2024 CAMH issued $120M of 5.246% Senior Unsecured Series A Debentures — its first bond-style long-term debt in the archived series — to finance the Phase 1D research building, with principal repayment beginning November 2027 and full maturity in 2064 (c0dd312dba92). The Temerty Discovery Centre (named for a $75M lead gift from the Temerty Foundation) and the Waverley House Secure Care & Recovery Building (named for a $203M gift, described as funding "compassionate, modernized forensic care for a patient population long overlooked by the system") were named this year, with opening targeted for 2027 (1e126e1382da). The Foundation's Phase 1D Research Building grant commitment rose from $240M (fs-2024) to $289M, with cumulative receipts jumping from $19.0M to $100.0M during the year (c0dd312dba92). No One Left Behind is reported to have raised over $600M from nearly 200,000 donors, exceeding its original $500M goal, and CAMH reports research spending surpassed $100M for the first time. New system-integration initiatives named include a formal CAMH–University Health Network partnership on physical/mental-health care integration, a CAMH–Lakeridge Health partnership extending CAMH's model to Durham Region, and AMANI — described as "a reimagining of the long-standing SAPACCY program into a province-wide network" for Black youth mental health and substance-use services, backed by $11M in federal and provincial funding (1e126e1382da). Financially, Ministry of Health and federal government revenue rose again to $558.8M (+14.9%; the COVID-19 funding line is nil for the first time in the series), total revenue reached $740.6M against expenses of $709.8M, for an excess of $30.9M — by far the largest surplus recorded in the archived series (c0dd312dba92).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

CAMH's relevance to Toronto is direct rather than inferred: every document in this series describes CAMH as based on "two primary sites in Toronto" (bda120221a90) and centred on its Queen Street West campus, explicitly named as being "in the heart of West Queen West" (eb14ac91991d). The FY2024-25 report describes a new virtual urgent-care clinic explicitly "for people in Toronto" (1e126e1382da) and names food insecurity as "a growing crisis in Toronto" in describing its Gifts of Light Food Pantry expansion (1e126e1382da). CAMH's research partnerships are also Toronto-anchored, including the Toronto Adolescent & Youth (TAY) Cohort Study, a SickKids–CAMH collaboration, and a new CAMH–University Health Network partnership (fe002a72d8cd; eb14ac91991d; 1e126e1382da). Beyond the city, CAMH's programs extend across Ontario — Youth Wellness Hubs Ontario, NAVIGATE (expanded to more than 50 Ontario sites with $19M in provincial funding in 2024), the Ontario Student Drug Use and Health Survey, and a new CAMH–Lakeridge Health partnership serving Durham Region — consistent with the backgrounder's description of CAMH's stated role in Ontario mental-health and addictions policy (8e6ef1ad1ecf; 1e126e1382da). CAMH's capital redevelopment is funded in material part by the Ontario Ministry of Health (bda120221a90 through c0dd312dba92 consistently), reinforcing the direct provincial and municipal stake in the institution's physical footprint in Toronto.
