National Museum of Science and Technology - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-national-museum-science-technology (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

"The National Museum of Science and Technology (the Corporation) was established by the Museums Act on July 1, 1990, and is a Crown corporation named in Part I of Schedule III to the Financial Administration Act" (Ingenium Annual Report 24-25, Note 1 "Authority, mandate and operations", https://ingenium.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Ingenium-Annual-report-24-25_FINAL.pdf, fetched this review). Per the Museums Act, its mandate is "to foster scientific and technological literacy throughout Canada by establishing, maintaining and developing a collection of scientific and technical objects...and by demonstrating the products and processes of science and technology and their economic, social, and cultural relationships with society" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Corporation operates publicly under the trade name "Ingenium - Canada's Museums of Science and Innovation" and manages three national museum sites: the Canada Science and Technology Museum, the Canada Aviation and Space Museum, and the Canada Agriculture and Food Museum (same source).

Governance & reporting line

Ingenium "is a Crown corporation governed by a Board of Trustees, who are appointed by the Governor in Council on the recommendation of the Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages" and "is accountable to Parliament, through [that Minister], for Ingenium's business affairs" (same source). The Board has up to 11 trustees and operates subject to Part X of the Financial Administration Act; its financial statements are audited by the Auditor General of Canada, who "reports to the Minister responsible for the Corporation" (same source).

Budget scale

Parliamentary appropriations recognized on an accrual basis rose from $47.5 million in 2023-24 to $49.1 million in 2024-25; total revenues were $14.4 million (up from $13.3 million) and total expenses were $64.3 million (up from $58.5 million), an increase driven by capital projects and new exhibitions (Ingenium Annual Report 24-25, "Sources of funds"/"Expenditures" sections, same URL).

Institutional history

Established July 1, 1990 under the Museums Act as the National Museum of Science and Technology, the Corporation now operates publicly as Ingenium (same source, Note 1).

National Museum of Science and Technology - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: fed-national-museum-science-technology / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is the Ingenium Centre, a $150.6-million purpose-built collections storage and research facility (base construction alone) built 2016-2020, which absorbed the bulk of the corporation's capital budget for half a decade and is credited with a Gold-Level Rick Hansen Foundation accessibility certification. The biggest quietly dropped element is not a program but a financial cushion: the corporation's unrestricted net assets, positive every year through 2025-26 (forecast $8.1 million), are projected to turn negative by 2027-28 (-$7.2 million) and keep falling to -$51.0 million by 2030-31 as temporary federal top-up funding is assumed to lapse after 2026-27 — a structural deficit the corporation itself frames as deliberate ("was not subject to reductions from the Comprehensive Expenditure Review" but "continues to exercise fiscal restraint"). The most load-bearing number specific to this org's own flagship site, the Canada Science and Technology Museum (CSTM): attendance of 367,000 in 2019-20 collapsed to 279,000 by 2022-23 and is budgeted flat at 325,000 through 2026-27 — a recovery that stalls below the pre-pandemic baseline even as the corporation's other two sites (CASM, CAFM) receive comparatively larger shares of the 2024-2031 capital plan. One open question the record cannot resolve: what becomes of the corporation's Sustainability and Outreach Committee, established August 2020 and dissolved by the Board in September 2025 with no stated reason in either document read.

Backgrounder summary

The National Museum of Science and Technology was established by the Museums Act on July 1, 1990, and is a Crown corporation named in Part I of Schedule III to the Financial Administration Act, mandated "to foster scientific and technological literacy throughout Canada by establishing, maintaining and developing a collection of scientific and technical objects... and by demonstrating the products and processes of science and technology and their economic, social, and cultural relationships with society." It operates publicly under the trade name "Ingenium — Canada's Museums of Science and Innovation" and manages three national museum sites: the Canada Science and Technology Museum (CSTM), the Canada Aviation and Space Museum (CASM), and the Canada Agriculture and Food Museum (CAFM). It is governed by a Board of Trustees (up to 11 members, Governor-in-Council appointees on the recommendation of the Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages), operates under Part X of the Financial Administration Act, and is audited by the Auditor General of Canada. Parliamentary appropriations recognized on an accrual basis rose from $47.5 million (2023-24) to $49.1 million (2024-25); total revenues $14.4 million and total expenses $64.3 million in 2024-25, an increase driven by capital projects and new exhibitions.

Priority evolution

2020-21 (ar-2021, 9a64032c1a0a): First-year implementation of Ingenium's five-year 2020-2025 Strategic Plan (three goals: Collaborate to Innovate; Access for All; Strengthen our Foundations) coincides directly with COVID-19 onset — "the greatest challenge Ingenium has ever faced," per Chair Neil Russon and Director/CEO Christina Tessier's joint message. Museum and site closures, suspended travelling exhibitions, and a pivot to virtual programming (Curating Under Quarantine crowd-sourcing project; participation in the Connected North distance-learning program reaching 40+ remote schools, "many located in Indigenous communities"). Construction/consolidation of the new Ingenium Centre continues through the pandemic; the facility receives Gold-Level Accessibility Certification from the Rick Hansen Foundation. First two Indigenous Sky Symposium sessions held (September equinox, December solstice), reaching 680 participants. A new Sustainability and Outreach Committee is established at the Board level, August 2020.

Undated background, established in bp-2025 (af0205ee75ce): The Canada Science and Technology Museum "reopened in 2017 to a surge of popularity and very positive reviews" — the only direct textual confirmation, in the documents read this review, of the widely known multi-year closure and renewal that preceded the corporation's current 11-permanent-exhibition CSTM configuration; the cause and exact closure dates are not stated in the passage read and are not otherwise sourced in this brief.

2024-25 → 2026-2031 (ar-2025, f282fa612d43, sampled; bp-2031, 2ec815d9d4f8): Governance continuity confirmed directly: Christina Tessier remains Director and CEO and Neil Russon remains Board Chair through ar-2025, i.e., no leadership change across the 2021-2025 window read — a contrast with this batch's ship-rail brief, where the equivalent office saw three leadership transitions in the same years. The Sustainability and Outreach Committee, active through 2024-25, "did not meet in 2025-26 and was dissolved by the Board of Trustees in September 2025" (per the CAFM sibling brief's fuller read of this same ar-2025 document; not independently re-verified in this org's own copy this review — see Residuals). The 2025-2030 Strategic Plan (launched April 1, 2025) renames all three prior strategic goals while the corporate plan itself frames the new plan as "an evolution of" the 2020-2025 plan, not a break from it. CSTM-specific findings from bp-2031's own tables (read directly under this org's document id): attendance 367,000 (2019-20 actual) → 279,000 (2022-23) → 270,782 (2023-24) → 331,504 (2024-25 actual, a real rebound) → budgeted flat at 325,000 for both 2025-26 and 2026-27 — landing just under, not at, the pre-pandemic 2019-20 level. CSTM's own capital allocation in the same plan is comparatively thin: $370K (2024-25) then $0 for three straight years (2025-26 through 2027-28) before resuming at $200K/year from 2028-29, versus CASM's $719K rising to a sustained $2.5M/year by 2027-28 and CAFM's one-time $2.0M in 2026-27 — CSTM, this brief's subject org and the corporation's only "comprehensive science and technology museum," receives the smallest and most back-loaded capital commitment of the three sites in this plan.
