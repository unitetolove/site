Independent Electricity System Operator - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-independent-electricity-system-operator (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The IESO is a not-for-profit corporation without share capital continued under Part II of the Electricity Act, 1998, S.O. 1998, c. 15, Sched. A (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98e15, verified). Section 5 provides for the 2014-2015 amalgamation of the predecessor IESO and the Ontario Power Authority into the single continuing corporation; section 6 sets out its statutory objects, including directing operation of the IESO-controlled grid, maintaining system reliability, and operating IESO-administered electricity markets (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98e15, s.4-6).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The IESO's stated core purpose is "to ensure the reliability of the province's power system on behalf of all Ontarians," encompassing wholesale electricity market operation, system planning/procurement, and grid reliability standards enforcement (IESO corporate overview, https://www.ieso.ca/en/Corporate-IESO; registry row on-independent-electricity-system-operator). Its current corporate strategy document is titled "Preparing for the Electricity System of Tomorrow: IESO Corporate Strategy (2022-2027)" (registry strategy_current_url, https://www.ieso.ca/-/media/Files/IESO/Document-Library/corporate/strategy/IESO-Corporate-Strategy-2022-2027.pdf).

Governance & reporting line

The IESO is under the jurisdiction of the Ontario Minister of Energy, with a President and CEO and Leadership Team implementing strategy under oversight of a Board of Directors; the Ontario Energy Board sets the IESO's fees/usage charges and operating licence conditions, and it is subject to FIPPA and public this library's internal records disclosure requirements (IESO corporate overview, https://www.ieso.ca/en/Corporate-IESO; https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98e15).

Budget scale

The IESO's OEB-approved 2024 revenue requirement (funded via usage fees charged to market participants, not general provincial appropriation) was reported at $218.4M as part of a 2023-2025 settlement agreement (Energy Regulation Quarterly case comment on IESO's OEB revenue-requirement application, https://energyregulationquarterly.ca/case-comments/ieso-application-to-the-ontario-energy-board-for-2024-and-2025-incremental-revenue-requirement-expenditures-and-usage-fees). ⚠️ Still being checked: this figure comes from a secondary legal-commentary source rather than the IESO's own audited financial statements, which were not directly extractable this review (registry doc-shelf lists 19 "financials" documents, doc_years 2013-2028, not individually reviewed here).

Institutional history

Continued as a corporation under Part II of the Electricity Act, 1998 (in force 2000, following the 1998 market-restructuring "Bill 35" reforms); amalgamated with the Ontario Power Authority in 2014-2015 (effective per 2014, c.7, Sched.7, s.3(1)) to form the single IESO entity that continues today (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98e15, s.5).

Independent Electricity System Operator - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 (rolling continuation) / registry: on-independent-electricity-system-operator / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest addition is a statutory mandate expansion: the 2025 "Protect Ontario by Securing Affordable Energy for Generations Act" formally added "supporting economic growth" to the IESO's mandate, alongside Ontario's first Integrated Energy Plan ("Energy for Generations"). The most consequential quiet change is the repeal of Bill 124 creating an unbudgeted $39.4 million compensation cost pressure across the 2023-2025 cycle, driving the IESO to request a 40% Revenue Requirement increase for 2026 (to $328.8 million) explicitly framed as a "one-time correction," not an ongoing scope increase. The most load-bearing number is that 2026 increase itself, projected to add 48 cents/month to the average residential bill. One open question the record cannot resolve: the outcome of the seventh and final Integrated Energy Plan report-back, described as "on track" for Q1 2026 delivery but falling after the archive's most recent entry.

Priority evolution

2016-2020 (sp-2016, d7f3e98ecc0f): Under President & CEO Bruce Campbell and Chairman Tim O'Neill, the IESO's five-year Strategic Plan sets a vision — "Powering a reliable and sustainable energy future for Ontario" — organized around 3 strategic themes (providing public value; respecting and valuing communities, customers and stakeholders; building corporate resilience), 4 corporate goals and 11 supporting strategic objectives. The plan's environmental scan names climate-change policy, emerging technologies (microgrids, storage, smart grids), regional planning and workforce-retirement risk as the coming five years' key drivers, following on the prior five years' achievement of coal phase-out and initial renewable integration.

2017 (ar-2017, 885dcab66bc7): Market Renewal is formally launched, targeting up to $5.2 billion in efficiency improvements over 10 years; a corporate reorganization is initiated; a public engagement on the Meter Data Management/Repository (MDM/R) is launched; and the Feed-in Tariff/microFIT renewable procurements conclude, ending that acquisition model.

2018 (ar-2018, a29eb24dd6ef): Under President & CEO Peter Gregg, the IESO develops a multi-year Innovation Roadmap looking out to 2030, and holds its operating (revenue-requirement) budget flat for a third consecutive year "in keeping with efforts to reduce the cost of electricity for Ontarians."

2019 (ar-2019, 54379bbe4264): The revenue requirement remains flat for a fourth year (Statement of Operations shows $190.8M budgeted core-operations system fees for 2019 against $194.3M actual in 2018); the IESO's first Annual Planning Outlook (APO), a 20-year forecast, is developed; and the first integrated regional resource-planning cycle is launched explicitly covering the Windsor-Essex, York and Toronto regions plus Hamilton and Ottawa sub-regions. Distributed energy resources (DERs) are flagged, for the first time, as a potential major system contingency by 2025.

2020 (ar-2020, f812670e205f): Terry Young becomes Interim President and CEO (succeeding Peter Gregg). In response to COVID-19, the IESO reduces its 2020 revenue requirement (breaking the prior flat-fee streak) while deferring lower-priority projects. A Resource Adequacy Framework is established and a Capacity Auction implemented; a new conservation framework (launched January 2021, up to $692 million over four years) is finalized to replace the outgoing Conservation First Framework.

2021 (ar-2021, 2cfc5ebd3527): Lesley Gallinger joins as President & CEO mid-year. The IESO releases its first Annual Acquisition Report under the new Resource Adequacy Framework and advances the Enabling Resources Program. A feasibility study on a municipally-requested 2030 natural-gas phase-out (called for by more than 30 Ontario municipalities) finds that deadline infeasible; the Minister of Energy separately asks the IESO to evaluate a gas-generation moratorium and develop a decarbonization pathway, due November 2022.

2022 (ar-2022, f73cb80f8d45; oth-2022, 90d272f93f80): The IESO launches its refreshed five-year Corporate Strategy, "Preparing for the Electricity System of Tomorrow" (2022-2027), replacing the 2016-2020 Strategic Plan's 3-theme/4-goal/11-objective architecture with 3 pillars: "Drive and Guide the Sector's Future," "Ensure System Reliability while Supporting Cost-Effectiveness," and "Drive Business Transformation." In December 2022 the IESO delivers Pathways to Decarbonization to the Minister of Energy, finding a moratorium on new gas generation feasible starting 2027 (at ~$26 billion in new infrastructure investment) while explicitly noting that gas generation would still be needed "to... meet local needs in the Greater Toronto Area (GTA)" — an issue the report says the IESO is "actively evaluating further."

2023 (ar-2023, 2323a1f9a000): Execution of the 2022-launched Corporate Strategy continues; Pathways to Decarbonization's recommendations feed directly into the government's "Powering Ontario's Growth" plan. The Long-Term 1 (LT1) procurement RFP window closes; an Advanced Technology Research Plan is developed; and the IESO provides formal input to the province's Electrification and Energy Transition Panel and leads Ontario's response to the federal government's draft Clean Electricity Regulations.

2024 (ar-2024, a7e6bf55e723; bp-2027, 4f86c665902c): The LT1 RFP concludes with 1,784 MW of storage — described as Canada's largest energy-storage procurement to date. "The Big Build" emerges as a governing frame for planning/procurement work (bp-2027). Regional/bulk planning engagements are launched explicitly naming a "Toronto" regional electricity plan and a separate "GTA West" bulk-transmission plan, alongside Greater Ottawa, East Lake Superior, York Region and Kitchener-Waterloo-Cambridge-Guelph. External Relations and Indigenous Engagement Frameworks are released (November 2024). The 2021-2024 Conservation and Demand Management Framework concludes and is replaced by a new enduring demand-side-management framework. The repeal of Bill 124 (the provincial wage-restraint law) creates a material unbudgeted cost pressure absorbed within the 2023-2025 Business Plan cycle.

2025 (ar-2025, f48980b9c491; bp-2028, 14a017fdd4ca): The Market Renewal Program launches in May 2025, roughly eight years after its 2017 launch (ar-2017, 885dcab66bc7). The "Protect Ontario by Securing Affordable Energy for Generations Act" amends the IESO's statutory mandate to explicitly add supporting economic growth, reinforcing Ontario's first Integrated Energy Plan, "Energy for Generations" (released summer 2025). The IESO conducts a formal "mid-cycle review" of the 2022-2027 Corporate Strategy, described as intended "to sharpen, not overhaul" the strategy; as part of this refinement, pillar 2 is renamed from "Ensure System Reliability while Supporting Cost-Effectiveness" (used unchanged in ar-2022, ar-2023 and ar-2024) to "Advance Reliability and Cost-Effectiveness in an Evolving System," while pillars 1 and 3 ("Drive and Guide the Sector's Future"; "Drive Business Transformation") are retained unchanged. The subsequent 2026-2028 Business Plan (bp-2028) organizes its own priorities under three newly named headings — "Accelerating the Big Build," "Enabling Economic Growth in the Short Term," "Delivering on Our Core Mandate" — nested beneath, but not identical in wording to, the three Corporate Strategy pillars.

  • Added — "Enabling Economic Growth in the Short Term" and "Delivering on Our Core Mandate": new named Business Plan priorities introduced in bp-2028 (14a017fdd4ca), alongside "Accelerating the Big Build" — not present as headings in bp-2027 (4f86c665902c).

  • Added — Enabling Resources Program (ERP): first named in ar-2021 (2cfc5ebd3527); continues as an active market-evolution program through bp-2028 (14a017fdd4ca), which frames it as "the next step in the IESO's planned market evolution."
  • Added — economic-growth mandate element: the "Protect Ontario by Securing Affordable Energy for Generations Act," per ar-2025 (f48980b9c491), "added supporting economic growth to our mandate" — a statutory-level addition rather than a mere strategic re-emphasis.
  • Added — External Relations Engagement Framework and Indigenous Engagement Framework: both released November 2024 (ar-2024, a7e6bf55e723), replacing the IESO's prior ad hoc/informal engagement approach described in earlier Annual Reports (e.g., ar-2017, 885dcab66bc7; ar-2018, a29eb24dd6ef).
  • Dropped/replaced — 2021-2024 Conservation and Demand Management Framework: concludes in 2024 and is replaced by a new 2025-2036 electricity Demand-Side Management (eDSM) Framework, a 12-year, $10.9-billion commitment (bp-2027, 4f86c665902c; bp-2028, 14a017fdd4ca; ar-2024, a7e6bf55e723; ar-2025, f48980b9c491).
  • Dropped — standalone Annual Acquisition Report as a distinct publication, superseded by integration into the Annual Planning Outlook effective 2024 (bp-2027, 4f86c665902c).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2017-2019 — revenue requirement/operating budget held flat for three consecutive years "in keeping with efforts to reduce the cost of electricity for Ontarians" (ar-2018, a29eb24dd6ef); ar-2019's own Statement of Operations shows $190.8M budgeted core-operations system fees for 2019 against $194.3M actual in 2018 (ar-2019, 54379bbe4264).
  • 2020 — the IESO reduces its revenue requirement in direct response to COVID-19, breaking the three-year flat-fee run (ar-2020, f812670e205f).
  • Mid-2021 — CEO transition: Lesley Gallinger becomes President & CEO, succeeding Interim President & CEO Terry Young, who had succeeded Peter Gregg (ar-2021, 2cfc5ebd3527; ar-2020, f812670e205f).
  • 2022 — launch of the "Preparing for the Electricity System of Tomorrow" Corporate Strategy (2022-2027) (ar-2022, f73cb80f8d45).
  • 2023 (retroactive to 2023-2025 Business Plan cycle) — repeal of Bill 124 (the provincial public-sector wage-restraint law) creates an unbudgeted $39.4 million compensation/benefits cost pressure across the 2023-2025 cycle; combined with a $5.7 million cloud-computing accounting-treatment shift, this drives a projected $26.6 million operating deficit by end of 2025, exceeding the IESO's approved operating reserve (bp-2027, 4f86c665902c).
  • May 2025 — the Market Renewal Program launches, roughly eight years after its 2017 formal launch (bp-2027, 4f86c665902c; ar-2025, f48980b9c491; ar-2017, 885dcab66bc7).
  • 2025 — the "Protect Ontario by Securing Affordable Energy for Generations Act" expands the IESO's statutory mandate to explicitly add supporting economic growth (ar-2025, f48980b9c491).
  • 2025 — Ontario's first Integrated Energy Plan, "Energy for Generations," is released; its Implementation Directive tasks the IESO with seven new comprehensive reports to government, six delivered by year-end with the seventh on track for Q1 2026 (ar-2025, f48980b9c491).
  • 2026 Business Plan cycle — the IESO requests a 40 per cent Revenue Requirement increase for 2026 (to $328.8 million total revenue), followed by 9 per cent (to $357.2 million, 2027) and 2 per cent (to $364.4 million, 2028); the 2026 step-up is explicitly framed as "largely... a one-time correction" to resolve the Bill 124-driven structural deficit rather than an ongoing scope increase, projected to add 48 cents/month (44 cents of it in 2026 alone) to the average residential bill (bp-2028, 14a017fdd4ca).
  • 2026-2028 — average FTE staffing is budgeted to grow from 930 (2025 revised budget) to 1,068 by 2028, a roughly 15 per cent three-year increase (bp-2028, 14a017fdd4ca), continuing growth already seen from 861 actual FTEs in 2023 to a 971 outlook for 2025 (bp-2027, 4f86c665902c).
  • 2026-2028 — the IESO's target operating reserve is raised from $10 million to $30 million, explicitly because "the original $10-million reserve was set under the assumption of a stable, one-year planning horizon" that the organization judges no longer adequate given the pace of sector change (bp-2028, 14a017fdd4ca).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Unlike a federally headquartered body with no Ontario footprint, the IESO is an Ontario body, and its Toronto/GTA relevance is direct rather than incidental. Its registered head office across every document that states an address is 1600-120 Adelaide Street West, Toronto, Ontario M5H 1T1 (sp-2016, d7f3e98ecc0f; bp-2027, 4f86c665902c; bp-2028, 14a017fdd4ca), with multiple Annual Reports independently confirming executive and director signing locations as "Toronto, Ontario" or "Toronto, Canada" (e.g., ar-2020, f812670e205f; ar-2022, f73cb80f8d45). The IESO's own Future@Work office-renovation project reopened roughly 35,000 square feet of newly renovated office space "in downtown Toronto" to employees during 2025 (ar-2025, f48980b9c491).

Beyond the corporate address, the archived series shows Toronto and the GTA as a recurring, named subject of the IESO's substantive planning mandate, not merely a place it happens to be based: - The 2019 Annual Report's first integrated regional-planning cycle explicitly names "the Windsor-Essex, York and Toronto regions" (ar-2019, 54379bbe4264). - The December 2022 Pathways to Decarbonization report finds that even under an aggressive decarbonization (2027 gas-moratorium) scenario, natural gas generation would still be needed "to... meet local needs in the Greater Toronto Area (GTA) — an issue that the IESO is actively evaluating further" (oth-2022, 90d272f93f80). - The 2024 Annual Report lists a dedicated "Toronto" regional electricity planning process, run alongside (and distinct from) a separate "GTA West" bulk transmission planning process, both explicitly named among six regional planning engagements launched that year (ar-2024, a7e6bf55e723). - The 2025 Annual Report states that Toronto's electricity demand "is projected to double by 2044," and cites the IESO's recommendation for a new underwater transmission line to serve growth in "Canada's largest city" (ar-2025, f48980b9c491). - The IESO was named a "Greater Toronto Area Top Employer" in multiple archived years, including for a stated fifth consecutive year as of the 2025 Annual Report (ar-2022, f73cb80f8d45; ar-2025, f48980b9c491).

On this record, Toronto/GTA relevance for the IESO is substantive on two independent grounds — corporate domicile and a standing, named line of regional-planning work — a materially different finding than would be expected for an out-of-province or federal body.
