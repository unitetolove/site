Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-agriculture-food-agribusiness (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The ministry's continuation statute is the Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.16, consolidated text current to the e-Laws currency date, consolidation period effective March 22, 2023 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m16, fetched and verified via the e-Laws legislation service, section-by-section content confirmed). Section 2(1) continues the former "Ministry of Agriculture and Food" under the name "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs" (amended 1999, c. 12, Sched. A, s. 20(3), same source); s. 4 gives the Minister "direction and control" of the administration of law relating to agriculture, food and rural affairs, subject to the Executive Council Act. ⚠️ Still being checked: the Act's own definitions (s. 1) still name the ministry "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs," not the current public name "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness" seen in the registry row and on the ministry's own site — the precise instrument for the "Agribusiness" rename was not located this review.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Statutory functions include administering agriculture/food/rural-affairs law and appropriations, and reviewing related matters at the Minister's direction (Act s. 4, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m16). The ministry's own page describes its current mission as "growing Ontario's agri-food sector," covering business support to farmers, sustainability research, northern agricultural development, and food safety (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-agriculture-food-and-agribusiness, fetched directly). It oversees nine affiliated bodies including Agricorp, the Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs Appeal Tribunal, the Grain Financial Protection Board, and the Ontario Food Terminal Board (same source).

Governance & reporting line

The Minister presides over and has charge of the Ministry (Act s. 2(2), https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m16). As a Crown ministry it sits within the executive government, organized under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.25, ss. 1-2, which lets the Lieutenant Governor in Council appoint ministers and assign ministry duties by order in council (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e25, fetched and verified).

Budget scale

Institutional history

Established as "Ministry of Agriculture and Food," continued and renamed "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs" by 1999, c. 12, Sched. A, s. 20(3) — a precedent showing this Act's own text has historically been amended to track ministry renames (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m16, s. 2 amendment note, fetched and verified). The further rename to "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness" reflected on the ministry's current website (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-agriculture-food-and-agribusiness) has not yet been located in a corresponding Act amendment — ⚠️ still being checked.

Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: on-ministry-agriculture-food-agribusiness / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: Across three readable annual reports (2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26), the ministry's biggest priority addition is an explicit "Agribusiness" pillar and a named U.S.-tariff exposure/trade-defense posture, arriving with the 2025-26 rename from "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs" (OMAFRA) to "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness" (OMAFA). The quietest drop is the six-year-old "One Health" framing collapsing from a headline lens to a single passing mention, alongside the disappearance of the COVID-era secretariat and COVID-specific programming that anchored the 2023-24 report. The single largest load-bearing number is the FY2025-26 planned total budget of $1,050.1M (operating $1,037.8M + capital $12.3M), up 23.7% from FY2024-25 estimates — driven substantially by a one-time alcohol-support program transfer from the Ministry of Finance and a $100M increase to the Risk Management Program. Open question, flagged explicitly by the backgrounder and unresolved in any read document: what statutory instrument (if any) formalized the OMAFRA-to-OMAFA rename, since the ministry's own continuation Act still names it "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs" as of the consolidation currency date cited in the backgrounder.

Priority evolution

ar-2024 (cdf007a4e188), "Published Plans and Annual Reports 2023-2024" (ministry still "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs," Minister Lisa Thompson): Vision is framed around "innovation, increasing trade, safeguarding the food supply chain and ensuring the safe and sustainable production of food," organized under a "One Health" approach (explicit human/animal/plant/environment framing) and the newly-launched Grow Ontario Strategy's three priorities: strengthening agri-food supply-chain stability, increasing agri-food technology and adoption, and attracting/growing agri-food talent. A substantial "COVID-19 response" section is still present as a named, retrospective program area — the COVID Agri-Food Secretariat (established 2021) is reported as demobilized in December 2022, with COVID-specific programs (Enhanced Agri-food Workplace Protection Program, COVID-19 Agri-Food Prevention and Control Innovation Program, Provincial Antigen Screening Program) reported as wound down or completed. Sustainable CAP (the federal-provincial framework, effective April 1, 2023) and the Ontario Agri-Food Innovation Alliance (University of Guelph, also effective April 1, 2023, $343M over five years) are both reported as newly-launched partnership vehicles. Ministry total operating+capital budget: $822.4M planned for 2023-24.

ar-2025 (8f5b8a1995ad), "Published Plans and Annual Reports 2024-2025" (still OMAFRA/Lisa Thompson): The "One Health" framing persists but is demoted from a lede concept to one bullet among many. COVID-19 response as a distinct section is gone entirely — no COVID program area is reported. A new "Compliance Modernization" initiative appears as a named multi-year priority (risk-based, digital regulatory delivery model). Legislative activity is prominent: Bill 171 (Enhancing Professional Care for Animals Act, 2024) introduced to replace the Veterinarians Act; Bill 139 (Less Red Tape, More Common Sense Act, 2023) received Royal Assent; the Protecting Farmers from Non-Payment Act, 2023 replaces three older financial-protection statutes — described as "the first significant change of the financial protection legislation for the sector in nearly 40 years." Rural Economic Development Strategy development is underway (release "in 2024" per this report, not confirmed released by ar-2026). Ministry total operating+capital budget: $855.7M planned for 2024-25 (a $33.3M / 4.1% increase over the ar-2024 estimate).

ar-2026 (7ca916595228), "Published Plans and Annual Reports 2025-2026" — first report under the new name (ministry now "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness"/OMAFA, Minister Trevor Jones as of the April 2025 org chart): Vision statement is rewritten and shortened: "helps build a sustainable, resilient, and trusted agri-food system that drives economic growth" — the "One Health" phrase is now absent from the vision statement itself (though "animal and plant health" protection persists as a stated activity). Three explicit "key objectives" replace the looser prior framing: (1) ensuring a safe food system and animal/plant health, (2) fostering a thriving agri-food and agribusiness sector, (3) securing a sustainable/stable/resilient food supply. A new, dedicated "Agribusiness" subsection appears for the first time, describing Ontario's agribusiness value chain (input supply, wholesale distribution) as a named strategic asset. A new "Trade" subsection explicitly names U.S. tariff exposure as a live risk: "Ontario's agri-food sector is susceptible to impacts from U.S. tariffs and other actions that threaten export-dependent sectors," citing $45.1B CAD in Ontario-U.S. two-way agri-food trade (2023) and noting a provincial government plan "to protect Ontario from the potential impacts of U.S. tariffs." A new "Regulatory Compliance Ontario" unit is disclosed — a cross-government Centre of Excellence serving 19 OPS ministries on regulatory-compliance program delivery, housed within OMAFA. The Risk Management Program (RMP) is increased from $150M/year to $250M/year (Jan 2025), explicitly tied to a "$51 billion agri-food sector" figure. The ministry's agency count drops from 10 (ar-2024/ar-2025) to 9, with three non-board-governed adjudicative/regulatory bodies (Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs Appeal Tribunal, Normal Farm Practices Protection Board, Ontario Farm Products Marketing Commission) still listed individually but the "Grain/Livestock Financial Protection Board" and OFPMC retained; ARIO is renamed "Agricultural Research and Innovation Ontario" with a new constituting Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. A.13, amended 2024, c. 8). Ministry total operating+capital budget: $1,050.1M planned for 2025-26, a $201.4M / 23.7% jump — the report attributes this explicitly to "one-time funding for the alcohol support programming that OMAFA delivers on behalf of the Ministry of Finance" plus the RMP increase. This is the first report to publish a formal KPI dashboard (Government-directed + Ministry-identified, with baseline/target/trend/status columns).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is a province-of-Ontario ministry — its entire mandate is Ontario-scoped by definition, and rural Ontario (approximately 2.5 million people, ~17% of provincial population per ar-2024/ar-2025) is a named constituency throughout the series. Toronto/GTA-specific touchpoints across the read documents are narrower and largely logistical/regulatory rather than programmatic: international agri-food workers arriving under the federal Temporary Foreign Worker Program receive "welcome kits" (health/safety resources) at Toronto Pearson International Airport, with 6,000 kits distributed as of Sept 30, 2023 (8f5b8a1995ad) — this is the only direct Toronto-proper operational presence named in the read series. The ar-2026 report's Line Fences Act modernization explicitly cites alignment with the City of Toronto Act, 2001 as a reference point for the amended framework, though the underlying dispute-resolution program is rural-oriented (7ca916595228). The Agricultural System / Growth Plan land-use-planning work described in all three reports (Agricultural Land Base Map, Agricultural System Map Portal, technical advice to municipalities) explicitly protects farmland "across the Greater Golden Horseshoe," the region containing the GTA, positioning Toronto's exurban/peri-urban fringe as an indirect beneficiary of farmland-protection policy rather than a direct program site. No head-office, research-station, or inspection-facility presence in the City of Toronto itself is disclosed in any of the three read documents; ARIO's 14 research stations are described only as "spread across all regions of the province" without a Toronto-specific listing.
