Canadian Energy Regulator - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canadian-energy-regulator (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canadian Energy Regulator (CER) is established under Part 1 of the Canadian Energy Regulator Act, S.C. 2019, c. 28, s. 10 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-15.1/index.html, fetched and verified — consolidated version current to 2026-06-14). The Act created an independent regulatory body responsible for pipelines, power lines, and offshore renewable energy projects within Parliament's jurisdiction, so they are constructed, operated, and abandoned safely (same source; registry row fed-canadian-energy-regulator).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CER reviews energy-infrastructure project applications, conducts compliance verification (150+ inspections and 100+ technical meetings annually per its own account), can issue penalty notices, and can restrict or shut down pipeline operations; it also publishes energy-system information such as the Canada's Energy Future outlook (https://www.cer-rec.gc.ca/en/about/index.html, fetched).

Governance & reporting line

Two-tier structure under the Act: a Board of Directors (5-9 part-time members, Governor-in-Council-appointed, including a Chairperson/Vice-Chairperson, at least one Indigenous member) sets strategic direction; a Commission (up to 7 full-time Governor-in-Council-appointed Commissioners, including a Lead and Deputy Lead Commissioner) makes adjudicative and regulatory decisions; a Chief Executive Officer manages daily operations (CER "Organization and Structure" / governance pages, cer-rec.gc.ca, WebSearch-confirmed). The CER reports to Parliament through the Minister of Energy and Natural Resources.

Budget scale

~$117M planned spending for 2025-26 ($116,971,846 including internal services), 546.2 planned full-time-equivalent staff (Canada Energy Regulator 2025-26 Departmental Plan, https://www.cer-rec.gc.ca/en/about/publications-reports/departmental-plan/2025-26/canada-energy-regulator-2025-26-departmental-plan-full-page.html; registry strategy_current_url points to the successor 2025-26/2026-27 edition of this plan).

Institutional history

Created under the Canadian Energy Regulator Act, which came into force August 28, 2019, the CER replaced the National Energy Board as Canada's federal energy-infrastructure regulator (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-15.1/index.html, fetched).

Canadian Energy Regulator - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 / registry: fed-canadian-energy-regulator / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is "Preparing for the Energy Future" (net-zero/energy-transition readiness, including hydrogen-transport and offshore-renewables regulatory preparedness), added to the CER's four Strategic Priorities in the 2024-27 Strategic Plan following February 2023 ministerial direction to conduct net-zero-2050 scenario analysis. The biggest quietly dropped element is "Data and Digital Innovation," one of the CER's original four Strategic Priorities set when the CER launched in 2019, dropped as a standalone named priority in the 2024-27 refresh with no report explaining why. The most load-bearing number is FY2024-25 total actual spending of $130,962,833 (580.2 FTE) — the highest in the series — against a planned pullback to $116,971,846 for FY2025-26. One open question: why the cost-recovery ratio shifted from "approximately 90 per cent" (2012-2015 reports) to "approximately 97 per cent" from 2016-17 onward, a change no report in the series explains.

Backgrounder summary

The Canada Energy Regulator (CER) is established under Part 1 of the Canadian Energy Regulator Act, S.C. 2019, c. 28, s. 10, in force August 28, 2019, replacing the National Energy Board (NEB) as Canada's federal energy-infrastructure regulator. It regulates pipelines, power lines, and offshore renewable-energy projects within Parliament's jurisdiction so they are built, operated and abandoned safely; it reviews applications, conducts compliance verification, can issue penalties or restrict operations, and publishes energy-system information (Canada's Energy Future). Governance is two-tier: a part-time Board of Directors (5-9 Governor-in-Council appointees, including a Chairperson and at least one Indigenous member) sets strategic direction; a Commission (up to 7 full-time Governor-in-Council appointees) makes adjudicative decisions; a CEO runs daily operations. The CER reports to Parliament through the Minister of Energy and Natural Resources. Planned spending for 2025-26 was $116,971,846 (including internal services), with 546.2 planned FTEs.

Priority evolution

2012 (ar-2012, 002f7a6d9707), Chair Gaétan Caron: NEB describes itself as "an independent federal regulator established in 1959" whose mandate is "to promote safety and security, environmental protection and economic efficiency in the Canadian public interest, in the regulation of pipelines, energy development and trade," reporting to Parliament through the Minister of Natural Resources. Regulated ~98 companies, ~73,000 km of pipeline and ~1,400 km of international power lines; shipped ~$106.3B of product in 2012 at an estimated $6.9B transportation cost. The 2012 Jobs, Growth and Long-term Prosperity Act is described as bringing "some of the most significant changes to the NEB Act since its implementation in 1959": new legislated time limits for regulatory reviews and new Administrative Monetary Penalties (AMPs) as an enforcement tool, plus additional funding to raise inspections 50% and double comprehensive audits. Cost recovery from regulated companies ~90% of Board costs (002f7a6d9707). Budget/staff: FY2010-11 $58.8M/374 FTE; FY2011-12 $64.4M/393 FTE; FY2012-13 (est.) $68.4M/401 FTE (002f7a6d9707).

2013 (ar-2013, f85367f5534a; drr-2014, 3247a24a46a4): The Jobs, Growth and Long-term Prosperity Act toolkit is quantified at "an additional $13.5 million to hire additional staff and improve safety programs." A June 2013 Safety Forum produced three "fundamental ways to address pipeline safety": a zero-incident target, management systems, and safety-culture measurement. The Joint Review Panel report on the Enbridge Northern Gateway Project was released. ~500 applications received, ~550 import/export decisions issued, 18 hearing recommendations/decisions. Budget FY2013-14 (est.) $81.7-84.2M / 425.3-427.6 FTE, versus $68.4-69.6M / ~400 FTE the year before — a step-change tied to the 2012 legislative funding (f85367f5534a). Per drr-2014 (3247a24a46a4), inspections rose from 100 to 153 and audits from 3 to 6 during 2013-14, and oil-and-gas regulatory devolution to the Government of the Northwest Territories was completed.

2014 (ar-2014, b3dadc5d80f7; dp-2015, 2ce011494364): Peter Watson appointed Chair and CEO, June 2014. Safety and Performance Dashboard launched (March 2014). Enbridge Line 9B Reversal approved (March 2014). Trans Mountain Expansion hearing order issued (April 2014) with 400 intervenors, described as the largest in NEB history to that point; TransCanada's Energy East application submitted (October 2014), described as the longest pipeline project ever proposed to the Board. A National Engagement Initiative was announced in November 2014 for launch in early 2015. dp-2015 (2014-15 Report on Plans and Priorities) sets three strategic priorities: "continuous improvement of safety and environmental outcomes," "a robust regulatory framework in the North," and "a flexible and efficient organization" (2ce011494364). Budget FY2014-15 (est.) $84.7-90.3M / 423.6-440.6 FTE (b3dadc5d80f7; fs-2015 actual $90.282M vs. planned $87.156M, ce74bc007a44).

2015 (ar-2015, a728df42d898; drr-2016, 1eeda9312d19; dp-2016, 3be5b2de45ac): The National Engagement Initiative concluded: 80 meetings in 34 cities/towns across 9 provinces and 2 territories, ~40,000 km travelled, findings published December 2015. Two new regional offices opened in Montréal and Vancouver (spring 2015) — the Board's first physical expansion beyond Calgary HQ and its Northern offices (Yellowknife/Inuvik); an MOU was signed with the Communauté métropolitaine de Montréal. The Pipeline Safety Act passed June 2015. The three-priority framing is renamed to "Leading Regulatory Excellence, Taking Action on Safety and Engaging Canadians" (3be5b2de45ac; drr-2016, 1eeda9312d19), replacing the 2014-15 "North"-anchored framing. A Board Member Operating Model and Management System Manual were implemented in 2015-16. Cost recovery still stated at ~90% (a728df42d898). Budget FY2015-16 (est.) $82.4-87.6M / 457.6-460 FTE.

2016 (ar-2016, 89f659286cd6; dp-2017, be3df205ec7c): The federal government's NEB Modernization Initiative is underway, with interim measures published January 2016; the NEB states it "fully support[s] the Government of Canada's review to modernize the NEB's role, structure, and mandate" (89f659286cd6) — the first explicit acknowledgment in the series of what would become the NEB-to-CER transition. A data-visualization pilot (Energy Futures 2016) and an online Pipeline Incident Map were launched. Cost recovery is now stated at ~97% of Board costs (89f659286cd6), up from the ~90% cited in 2012-2015 reports — no report explains the change. Three regional offices (Vancouver, Montréal, Yellowknife) plus Calgary HQ are now standard language (89f659286cd6). Staff described as "450 skilled staff" (3be5b2de45ac).

2017 (ar-2017 [2017-18 report], fce712efc3d6; drr-2017, 79d4ce5bd072; dp-2018, 8b73cb789210): NEB becomes "one of the early adopters of the Departmental Results Framework (DRF) under the [Treasury Board] Policy on Results" (8b73cb789210), replacing the older Strategic Outcome/Program Activity Architecture used in 2012-2016 reporting. A government-mandated review of "the NEB's structure, role, and mandate" is underway; in "early 2018, the Government introduced Bill C-69: An Act to enact the Impact Assessment Act and the Canadian Energy Regulator Act" (fce712efc3d6) — the first documented legislative step toward the CER.

2018 (drr-2018, 9302cfce1464; dp-2019, df5ea748ea4d): dp-2019 (2018-19 Departmental Plan) formalizes the four Core Responsibilities that persist through the rest of the series: Energy Adjudication, Safety and Environment Oversight, Energy Information, Engagement (df5ea748ea4d). The NEB states it is "positioned to respond to the government of Canada's policy and legislative direction" on Bill C-69 and commits to improved Indigenous engagement including "ground-breaking new 'Indigenous Advisory Monitoring Committees' (IAMC)" (df5ea748ea4d).

2019 (drr-2019, 4a3c7d21ff64; dp-2020, 24185a6e604a; ar-2020, 84d8cad3f859): "On August 28, 2019, the National Energy Board (NEB) became the Canada Energy Regulator (CER), with the coming into force of Bill C-69 and the Canadian Energy Regulator Act" (4a3c7d21ff64). The new CER Act introduces a modern governance structure with "a clear separation between the strategic, operational and adjudicative functions": Board of Directors (strategic), CEO (operations), Commission (adjudication) (5dbaa00ef2b2, dp-2021). fs-2019 (aeabf14fb48b) shows FY2018-19 actual expenses of $124.392M against a planned $94.393M (prior year FY2017-18 actual $107.122M) — a marked overspend in the NEB's final full fiscal year, consistent with (though not explicitly attributed in the extracted text to) transition/modernization costs. ⚠️ still being checked.

2019-20 stub year (ar-2020, 84d8cad3f859; drr-2020, 281f54741f65; fs-2020, 515117a7ba1f): First CER "Annual Report," covering the partial period Aug 28, 2019-Mar 31, 2020. Cassie Doyle appointed Chairperson of the new Board of Directors; Sandy Lapointe served as Acting CEO through the transition before Gitane De Silva was confirmed as CEO. The COVID-19 pandemic hit at fiscal year-end, moving ~500 staff to remote work "in a period of just a few days" (84d8cad3f859). Actual expenses for the stub period were $70.112M against a planned $64.038M (515117a7ba1f).

2020-21 (ar-2021, b8be45eeca10; drr-2021, 3ca91e187fcf; dp-2022, 40013e091df2): CER's first full fiscal year. The Board of Directors approved the CER's first three-year Strategic Plan (April 2021), introducing a new Mission, Vision and four Strategic Priorities — Trust and Confidence, Reconciliation, Competitiveness, Data and Digital Innovation (3ca91e187fcf) — replacing the NEB-era "Leading Regulatory Excellence / Taking Action on Safety / Engaging Canadians" triad entirely. The Indigenous Advisory Committee (IAC), a statutory requirement of the CER Act, launched August 2020 (281f54741f65; 3ca91e187fcf). Gitane De Silva became CEO in August 2020. Energy Futures 2020 modeled a Net Zero scenario for the first time (3ca91e187fcf). A fatality on a construction project near Edmonton is reported among "several serious incidents" (3ca91e187fcf).

2021-22 (ar-2022, 7011c85e0fd7; drr-2022, bfe6a199ac10; dp-2023, c8f56a538c95): First implementation year of the Strategic Plan; the IAC's first Progress and Impact Report was published (March 2022). Three new Board Directors joined (Karen Leibovici, Karim Mahmud, François Tanguay). A comprehensive review of the Onshore Pipeline Regulations (OPR) launched — recurring as a named priority in every subsequent document through dp-2027. A Diversity and Belonging Roadmap was delivered and a Hybrid Workplace Pilot begun (March 2022). Canada's Energy Future 2021 explored net-zero-consistent modelling scenarios (bfe6a199ac10).

2022-23 (ar-2023, 6973601c6e3c; drr-2023, bec2a2d74f21): A CER Statement of Reconciliation was released (June 2022), co-endorsed by the IAC and Board. In February 2023 the Minister of Natural Resources directed the CER to "undertake scenario analysis consistent with Canada achieving net-zero emissions by 2050" (bec2a2d74f21) — the first documented instance of explicit ministerial direction shaping CER's energy-information mandate; Canada's Energy Future 2023 (released June 2023) responded to this direction. Tracy Sletto became CEO, succeeding Gitane De Silva, who resigned; George Vegh became Chairperson (August 22, 2023), succeeding founding Chair Cassie Doyle, who remained on the Board as a Director (bec2a2d74f21; 6973601c6e3c).

2023-24 (ar-2024, e999459333f1; dp-2025, ad619a70aef8): CER's fifth-anniversary year. The 2024-27 Strategic Plan was approved, restating the four priorities as Trust and Confidence; Reconciliation and Implementation of the United Nations Declaration; Competitiveness and Regulatory Excellence; and a new fourth priority, Preparing for the Energy Future (e999459333f1) — "Data and Digital Innovation" is dropped as a named standalone priority. The NorthRiver Midstream NEBC Connector Project was approved within the 90-day Governor-in-Council time limit "for the first time since 2015" with no extensions (e999459333f1). UN Declaration Act Action Plan Measure 34 (APM 34), on Indigenous involvement in CER oversight, was announced June 2023.
