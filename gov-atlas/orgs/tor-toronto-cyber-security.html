Toronto Cyber Security (Office of the Chief Information Security Officer) - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Batch 12 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: tor-toronto-cyber-security (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Toronto Cyber Security is not a separate corporation; it is a division/office of the City of Toronto, itself continued as a corporation with broad interpretive powers under the City of Toronto Act, 2006, S.O. 2006, c. 11, Sched. A, ss.6(1) and 7 ("the City has the capacity, rights, powers and privileges of a natural person") (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11, not re-fetched this pass, cited per house convention established in prior-batch backgrounders for City of Toronto divisions). Its operative mandate document, per its own budget books, is a City Council decision: "Extending the Mandate of the City's Chief Information Security Officer (EX14.3)," adopted May 22, 2024, which "provided visibility into the City agencies and corporations cyber practices to effectively identify and mitigate cyber risks" (2025 Program Summary — Office of the Chief Information Security Officer, sha256-12 bc3b4f4f1a6e, p.6).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The office's stated vision is "to advance our position as a Global Leader in Urban Cyber Innovation," with five focus areas: Cyber Governance Risk and Compliance, Identity Protection, Cyber Threat Management, Cyber Awareness, and Critical Infrastructure Protection, delivered through two service lines — Cyber Governance and Business Resilience (including Modernization and Recovery) (2025 Program Summary, sha256-12 bc3b4f4f1a6e, p.2, 3; 2026 Program Summary — Toronto Cyber Security, sha256-12 e8ef7f3f675e, p.1-2). Since the May 2024 EX14.3 mandate extension, services cover the City's own divisions plus its agencies and corporations (A&C); by the 2026 budget book, 39 of 40 identified A&C (98%) had executed service agreements (2026 Program Summary, sha256-12 e8ef7f3f675e, p.6).

Governance & reporting line

The office is led by the Chief Information Security Officer; its budget is grouped under "Corporate Services" in the City's Appendix 3 program listing (2025 Program Summary, sha256-12 bc3b4f4f1a6e, p.19). ⚠️ VERIFY — the precise reporting line above the CISO (e.g., to the City Manager or a Deputy City Manager) is not named in either budget book read for this org.

Budget scale

$35.992M gross / $35.563M net for 2026 (95.0 approved positions), up from $35.127M gross / $34.526M net in 2025 (96.0 approved positions) — a $1.814M (5.4%) net increase (2026 Program Summary, sha256-12 e8ef7f3f675e, p.7-10; 2025 Program Summary, sha256-12 bc3b4f4f1a6e, p.7-9). The office also carries a 10-year capital plan of $8.667M (2026-2035) for cyber infrastructure and initiatives (2026 Program Summary, sha256-12 e8ef7f3f675e, p.7).

Institutional history

⚠️ VERIFY — the office is named "Office of the Chief Information Security Officer" in the 2025 Program Summary (sha256-12 bc3b4f4f1a6e) and renamed "Toronto Cyber Security" in the 2026 Program Summary (sha256-12 e8ef7f3f675e); neither document states a reason or effective date for the rename. Both documents describe the office's most consequential mandate change as the May 22, 2024 Council-adopted EX14.3 report extending cyber services to agencies and corporations. No document in this archived set covers years before 2025.

Toronto Cyber Security - strategy evolution

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is extending cyber services beyond City divisions to the City's 40 agencies and corporations (A&C), funded in 2025 (4 new positions, $1.715M) under Council's May 2024 EX14.3 mandate extension and substantially executed by 2026 — 39 of 40 identified A&C (98%) had signed service agreements. The biggest quietly changed element is the office's own name, "Office of the Chief Information Security Officer" (2025) → "Toronto Cyber Security" (2026), with no stated reason. The load-bearing number is the net operating budget rising from $34.526M (2025) to $35.563M (2026), while the office's own 10-year cyber capital plan roughly halved, from $17.156M (2025-2034) to $8.667M (2026-2035), as Cyber Command Centre construction completed. One open question the two-document window cannot resolve: whether the "Toronto Cyber Security" rename reflects a formal governance change or is administrative relabeling.

Backgrounder summary

Toronto Cyber Security is a division/office of the City of Toronto (no division-specific enabling statute; the City itself operates under the City of Toronto Act, 2006, ss.6(1), 7), operating under Council's May 22, 2024 "Extending the Mandate of the City's Chief Information Security Officer (EX14.3)" decision, which extended cyber governance and services from City divisions to the City's agencies and corporations. It delivers cyber governance/risk/compliance, identity protection, threat management, awareness training, and critical infrastructure protection through two service lines (Cyber Governance; Business Resilience). The backgrounder flags the office's reporting line above the CISO role and the reason for its 2025→2026 rename as unresolved (⚠️ VERIFY, backgrounder); this brief does not resolve either gap.

Priority evolution

2025 (bc3b4f4f1a6e): Reports City Council's May 22, 2024 adoption of EX14.3, which "provided visibility into the City agencies and corporations cyber practices to effectively identify and mitigate cyber risks." The 2025 budget funds the mandate's first year: 4 new positions and $1.715M to begin extending cyber services to agencies and corporations (A&C), while maintaining a 90% SLA target. Priority Actions are grouped under three headings that recur in 2026: Enhance Cyber Foundation, Expand Cyber Services, Strengthen Business Resilience (including "Build Cyber Command Centre").

2026 (e8ef7f3f675e): Reports the mandate extension substantially executed — "Of the City's 40 agencies and corporations identified in 2024.EX14.3, 98% (39) have executed agreements," with "core cyber security services... fully deployed and operational... providing 24/7/365 monitoring." The office reports having "transitioned to the new workspace, the largest municipal Cyber Command Center in Canada" (up from "designed" the prior year), and a new partnership with Technology Services Division expanding Web Application Protection for PCI compliance. The same three-heading Priority Actions structure repeats, now emphasizing broadening the service portfolio further and elevating "cyber maturity" across divisions, agencies and corporations.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Renamed — the office itself: "Office of the Chief Information Security Officer" (2025) → "Toronto Cyber Security" (2026); no reason stated.
  • Added — Web Application Protection solution expansion in partnership with Technology Services Division, for PCI compliance (e8ef7f3f675e, p.6) — not mentioned in the 2025 document.
  • Completed, not newly added — the Cyber Command Centre: "designed... with rigorously defined requirements" in 2025 (bc3b4f4f1a6e, p.6) to "transitioned to the new workspace... the largest municipal Cyber Command Center in Canada" in 2026 (e8ef7f3f675e, p.6). Note the office's own superlative shifted from "largest... in the country" (2025) to "largest... in Canada" (2026) — functionally the same self-description at each publication's own date, not independently verified either year.
  • No priority is identifiably dropped; the five focus areas (Cyber Governance Risk and Compliance, Identity Protection, Cyber Threat Management, Cyber Awareness, Critical Infrastructure Protection) and two service lines (Cyber Governance, Business Resilience) are stable across both documents.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • Net operating budget: $34.526M (2025) → $35.563M (2026) per each year's own "BUDGET" section (bc3b4f4f1a6e, p.7; e8ef7f3f675e, p.7). The 2026 document's own Key Cost Drivers table states this is "$1.814 million, 5.4% greater than the 2025 Net Budget" (e8ef7f3f675e, p.10) — cited verbatim; see Residuals for a data-consistency flag on this comparator.
  • 10-year capital plan roughly halved: $17.156M (2025-2034) → $8.667M (2026-2035) (bc3b4f4f1a6e, p.15; e8ef7f3f675e, p.15), reflecting that the Cyber Command Centre build-out ($5.0M of the 2025 plan) and Cyber Foundation work were substantially spent down by 2025.
  • 2026 affordability measure: consolidating legacy Operational Technology licenses onto a unified OT/IT vulnerability-management platform, yielding $0.470M in annual savings (e8ef7f3f675e, p.11).
  • Service scale reported in 2026 only (no 2025 comparator given): 66 critical business applications under advanced cyber monitoring (+46% from 2024), 33,000 users completing cyber awareness training (93% completion rate), over 13,800 client interactions, and over 700 Cyber Risk Assessments completed (e8ef7f3f675e, p.6).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Direct — a City of Toronto corporate cyber-security office serving City divisions and, since the 2024 EX14.3 mandate extension, the City's agencies and corporations (bc3b4f4f1a6e, p.6; e8ef7f3f675e, p.6).
