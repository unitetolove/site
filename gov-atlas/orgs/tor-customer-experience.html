Customer Experience (311 Toronto) - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: tor-customer-experience (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Customer Experience Division (CXD) is a division of the City of Toronto's administrative organization, not a body created by its own enabling statute (no dedicated statute or Municipal Code chapter naming the division was located this review). It operates under the City corporation's general municipal authority as continued by the City of Toronto Act, 2006 (link verified by direct fetch: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11), which grants Council broad powers, including "the powers of a natural person," to organize and deliver City services (COTA 2006, Part II). ⚠️ still being checked - no by-law specifically establishing CXD as a division was located this review.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CXD provides "a one-window brand and customer service system" delivering non-emergency City services, programs and information 24/7 via phone, web, the 311 Toronto mobile app, email and social media (2026 Budget Notes, https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/bu/bgrd/backgroundfile-261725.pdf). The division's stated aim is to "drive standards, capabilities, and the scaling of platforms" for inclusive, accessible, integrated services "via channels of choice" (org page: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/accountability-operations-customer-service/city-administration/staff-directory-divisions-and-customer-service/311-toronto/).

Governance & reporting line

CXD sits within City Administration, under the Accountability, Operations & Customer Service structure (org page, above). Per the registry row, its parent organization is the Deputy City Manager, Corporate Services (tor-deputy-city-manager-corporate-services, this library's government-document registry). Danielle Seraphim is Executive Director, Customer Experience; Ilan Treiger is Manager, Financial Planning for the program (2026 Budget Notes, above).

Budget scale

~$24.4M gross 2026 operating budget; $16.5M net; $7.9M revenue; 223.5 approved positions. CXD has no 10-Year Capital Plan (2026 Budget Notes, https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/bu/bgrd/backgroundfile-261725.pdf).

Customer Experience (311 Toronto) - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: tor-customer-experience / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The Customer Experience Division (CXD) itself is the headline change: it launched January 1, 2023, folding 311, the Customer Experience Transformation Program, and parts of Customer Experience & Innovation into one division, and the "311" brand quietly shifted from the division's own name to just its best-known channel. The biggest quietly-changed element is the reporting committee — the annual report moved from General Government Committee (2022 cycle) to Service Excellence Committee (2023-24) — alongside a leadership change from Gary A. Yorke to Interim ED Roopy Chhina between the 2023 and 2024 reports. The load-bearing number is service volume: total service requests fell 5% from 487,243 (2023) to ~463,000 (2024) even as general inquiries kept climbing (712,750 → 848,000 → 886,000), and phone service levels improved each year (76% → 81% → 82% against an 80%-within-75-seconds target). One open question: no document in this three-report series discloses why SR volume declined in 2024 while inquiry volume rose, or whether that reflects the division's own "self-serve shift" success or something else.

Backgrounder summary

CXD is a City of Toronto administrative division (no dedicated enabling statute located) operating under the City's general municipal authority per the City of Toronto Act, 2006. It delivers a "one-window" non-emergency service system (311 phone, web, app, email, social media) and reports to Council through the Deputy City Manager, Corporate Services line. Backgrounder-cited 2026 budget scale: ~$24.4M gross operating budget, $16.5M net, 223.5 approved positions, no 10-Year Capital Plan.

This is a short, recent-only series: three consecutive annual reports (2022-2024 reporting years, filed 2023-2025), all filed after the CXD rename. No pre-2022 (legacy "311 Toronto") annual reports are in this archived set, so the series cannot show the division's pre-rename framing directly — only the 2022 report's retrospective description of it.

Priority evolution

2022 report (cf65525ab3d6, filed Apr 2023, to General Government Committee): Covers the last full year operating as "311" before the CXD rename took effect Jan 1, 2023 (the rename itself is announced in this report as a forward-looking fact). Frames four objectives: improve access to City services, provide consistent customer experience, optimize services cost-effectively, and build trust/confidence via data and metrics. Headline 2022 achievements: new mobile app (launched Jan 25, 2022; ~17,000 downloads, 9,262 SRs submitted via app), reopening of City Hall/Civic Centres to in-person service (63,033 face-to-face interactions), and a new Enterprise CRM driving a 17% reduction in talk time. Named forward priorities for 2023: standardizing complaints intake, digital SR-status communication, and digital payment options. ED: Gary A. Yorke.

2023 report (98e1dacb71a5, filed Mar 2024, to Service Excellence Committee — reporting committee changed from 2022): First full year as CXD. Reports a "City-wide collaborative customer experience governance structure" established (Strategic Alignment Table, Corporate Advisory Table, Customer Experience Leads Committee), a refreshed Service Promise initiative (network of 40 divisions convened), and launch of post-contact "Service Insights Surveys" (Dec 18, 2023). Major named capability projects: Closing the Loop (SR status/notes automation), Complaints & Compliments (new city-wide intake process, phased launch Feb 2024), Digital Payments (MyToronto Pay with PayIt — 1.1M transactions, 420,000 accounts by year-end), and Public Digital Access (My Child Care Account single-sign-on beta). Extensive divisional partnership list added: Parks Forestry & Recreation, Housing Secretariat (Housing Data Hub), Human Services Integration (HSI Online Hub), CaféTO, DonateTO refugee support, Fire Services phone-line transition (absorbed a ~8,000-call/year TFS line). ED still Gary A. Yorke.

2024 report (1bd32f36cdb0, filed Feb 2025, to Service Excellence Committee): ED changed to Roopy Chhina (Interim). Total interactions reported as 1.29 million (62% general inquiries, 32% SRs, 6% status-update requests) — a new interaction-mix framing not present in prior two reports. New SR categories added in 2024: excessive heat in rental units, noise complaints (seven new sub-categories), and City vehicle driver complaints/compliments/escalations. New cross-agency body named: Non-Emergency Call Response Steering Committee (CXD, Toronto Police Service, Municipal Licensing & Standards, Strategic Public & Employee Communications), tied to a renewed "Make the Right Call" 911-vs-311 public awareness campaign. Continuing projects from 2023 (Closing the Loop, Complaints & Compliments, Parks Services integration, digital channel improvements) are reported as ongoing rather than newly launched. New named training initiative: gender-based violence/intimate-partner-violence frontline staff training with the People & Equity Division's Gender Equity Unit.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — the Customer Experience Division itself: the 2022 report (filed 2023) announces the Jan 1, 2023 consolidation of 311, the Customer Experience Transformation Program, and parts of Customer Experience & Innovation into CXD; "311" persists as the public brand/channel name but is no longer the division's formal name (cf65525ab3d6).
  • Added — digital payments infrastructure (MyToronto Pay): first appears as a named, detailed initiative in the 2023 report with e-billing (Feb 2023) and Pre-Authorized Utilities Payments (Aug 2023) launches; absent by name from the 2022 report (98e1dacb71a5).
  • Added — new SR categories for excessive heat, noise, and City vehicle driver conduct: all first appear in the 2024 report as July 2024 launches; none are mentioned in 2022 or 2023 reports (1bd32f36cdb0).
  • Added — Non-Emergency Call Response Steering Committee and "Make the Right Call" campaign: first named in the 2024 report; no equivalent cross-agency 911/311 coordination body is named in 2022 or 2023 (1bd32f36cdb0).
  • Added — formal City-wide CX governance tables (Strategic Alignment Table, Corporate Advisory Table, Customer Experience Leads Committee): first described in the 2023 report as newly established that year; not present in the 2022 report (98e1dacb71a5).
  • Changed — reporting committee: 2022 report went to General Government Committee; 2023 and 2024 reports both went to Service Excellence Committee. No report states the reason for the change (cf65525ab3d6 vs. 98e1dacb71a5/1bd32f36cdb0).
  • Changed — Executive Director: Gary A. Yorke signs the 2022 and 2023 reports; Roopy Chhina signs the 2024 report as Interim Executive Director. No report states the transition circumstances (98e1dacb71a5 vs. 1bd32f36cdb0).
  • Changed — integrated service divisions (ISDs) list wording: "Urban Forestry" (2022, standalone) becomes "Parks, Forestry and Recreation – Urban Forestry" (2023) becomes "Urban Forestry within the Environment, Climate, and Forestry Division" (2024) — reflecting the underlying City division's own reorganization/rename rather than a CXD-side change (cf65525ab3d6; 98e1dacb71a5; 1bd32f36cdb0).
  • Quietly dropped — email as an SR-creation channel: the 2022 report states CXD "stopped creating SRs via the email channel" in September 2022 to push self-serve; by 2023 email is reframed only as a channel for non-integrated escalations (Court Services, Toronto Public Health, Revenue Services/VHT), not general SR intake (cf65525ab3d6; 98e1dacb71a5).
  • Quietly dropped — legacy phone infrastructure: the Centrex-based backup phone system is reported migrated to a modernized Bell system in the 2023 report; not mentioned again in 2024, implying completion (98e1dacb71a5).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • February 22, 2022 — Toronto City Hall and Civic Centres reopen to in-person service post-pandemic closures (cf65525ab3d6).
  • September 2022 — CXD stops creating service requests via email, pushing self-serve channels (cf65525ab3d6).
  • January 1, 2023 — Customer Experience Division formally launches, consolidating 311, the CX Transformation Program, and parts of CX & Innovation (cf65525ab3d6; confirmed 98e1dacb71a5).
  • 2023, ongoing — Vacant Home Tax (VHT) program emerges as a recurring, budget-relevant demand driver on 311, cited in all three reports with rising inquiry counts (46,000 in 2022 general-inquiry-adjacent volume; 49,903 general inquiries in 2023; 21,950 + 21,064 phone inquiries in Q1/Q2 2024 alone, plus a dedicated VHT support line launched Nov 1, 2024 handling 23,537 calls in its first two months) (cf65525ab3d6; 98e1dacb71a5; 1bd32f36cdb0).
  • February 24, 2024 — Complaints & Compliments process Phase 1 soft launch (98e1dacb71a5).
  • July 16, 2024 — record-breaking Toronto rainstorm/flooding event drives a documented surge in Sewer Service Line Blocked and Catch Basin Flooding SRs and elevated call volumes (1bd32f36cdb0).
  • July 2024 — new excessive-heat, noise, and City-vehicle-driver-conduct SR categories launched (1bd32f36cdb0).
  • November 1, 2024 — dedicated VHT support line launched (1bd32f36cdb0).
  • Late 2025 (forward-looking, stated in the 2024 report) — Parks Services integration with 311 planned for launch, not yet complete as of the last archived report (1bd32f36cdb0).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This entire series is a City of Toronto division reporting on City of Toronto service delivery; every document in the archived set is Toronto-specific by construction (all three reports are titled "Customer Experience Division Annual Report," addressed to Toronto Council committees, covering all Wards). No distinct provincial-versus-municipal analysis is required or present in these documents; CXD's relevance to Ontario is solely through its function as the City of Toronto's own non-emergency service-access channel. One province-adjacent link: the 2023 report notes 311 fielded 6,321 inquiries related to the 2023 Toronto mayoral by-election (polling locations, mail-in ballots, election jobs) — a municipal election administration touchpoint, reported descriptively with no campaign or candidate content (98e1dacb71a5).
