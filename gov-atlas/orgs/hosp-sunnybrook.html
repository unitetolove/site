Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: hosp-sunnybrook (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Sunnybrook operates as an approved public hospital under the Public Hospitals Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.40, which requires an approved governing board and empowers the Minister of Health to issue binding directives and set funding conditions (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p40, verified 2026-07-30). Its own consolidated audited financial statements state it is funded "under the Health Insurance Act (Ontario) and the regulations thereunder... primarily by the Province of Ontario in accordance with funding arrangements established by the Ministry of Health (MOH) and Ontario Health" (Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre Consolidated Audited Financial Statements, year ended March 31, 2026, document id hosp-sunnybrook-fs-2026, https://sunnybrook.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/SHSC-Consolidated-Financial-Statements-March-31-2026.pdf). ⚠️ Still being checked: the Hospital's own specific incorporating instrument was not stated explicitly in the sections of the FS reviewed this review (its consolidated research subsidiaries are separately confirmed as incorporated under the Corporations Act (Ontario) and the federal Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, document id hosp-sunnybrook-fs-2026).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Sunnybrook is "a teaching and research hospital affiliated with the University of Toronto," serving approximately 1.2-1.3 million patient visits annually with over 17,000 staff, physicians, learners and volunteers (document id hosp-sunnybrook-fs-2026; https://sunnybrook.ca/about-sunnybrook/performance-and-reporting/, fetched 2026-07-30). It maintains Hospital Service Accountability and Multi-Sector Service Accountability agreements with the province (same source).

Governance & reporting line

Sunnybrook is governed by a Board of Directors; its independent auditor's report is addressed "To the Board of Directors of Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre" (document id hosp-sunnybrook-fs-2026). Per the registry, Ontario Health (on-ontario-health) is the funding/oversight parent for this independent, non-profit hospital corporation (registry row hosp-sunnybrook).

Budget scale

~$1.759B total consolidated revenue against ~$1.750B total consolidated expenses for the year ended March 31, 2026, an excess of revenues over expenses of $9.6M, of which $1.258B came from Ontario Health/the Ministry of Health (Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre Consolidated Audited Financial Statements FY2026, document id hosp-sunnybrook-fs-2026).

Institutional history

Sunnybrook Military Hospital was commissioned in 1943 for veterans' care and officially opened by Prime Minister William Lyon Mackenzie King on June 12, 1948; the federal government transferred its administration to the University of Toronto in 1966 for a nominal one-dollar payment, and it was renamed Sunnybrook Hospital (1966) then Sunnybrook Medical Centre (1973). Ontario Bill 51 merged it with the Orthopaedic and Arthritic Hospital and Women's College Hospital in 1998 (forming Sunnybrook and Women's College Health Sciences Centre); the Women's College amalgamation was dissolved in 2006, and Sunnybrook merged with St. John's Rehab Hospital in 2012 (https://sunnybrook.ca/about-sunnybrook/about-us/our-history/, fetched 2026-07-30).

Backgrounder summary

Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre is an approved public hospital under the Public Hospitals Act (Ontario), "a teaching and research hospital affiliated with the University of Toronto," funded primarily by the Province of Ontario through the Ministry of Health (MOH) and Ontario Health under the Health Insurance Act (Ontario) (hosp-sunnybrook-fs-2026, 21252e7916c6). It is governed by its own Board of Directors, with Ontario Health as registry-listed funding/oversight parent. Consolidated revenue reached $1.759B against $1.750B in consolidated expenses for the year ended March 31, 2026, an excess of revenues over expenses of $9.581M, of which $1.258B came from Ontario Health/the Ministry of Health (hosp-sunnybrook-fs-2026, 21252e7916c6).

Priority evolution

FY2022-23 (fs-2023, a3982564e2c6): Consolidated revenue $1.428B, expenses $1.433B, a deficiency of revenues over expenses of $(4.591)M. A dedicated Note 17 "Impact of COVID-19" discloses ongoing incremental pandemic-related expenditures and MOH funding-adjustment uncertainty since March 2020 (fs-2023, a3982564e2c6). Note 14(d) discloses that the Ontario Superior Court of Justice ruled Bill 124 (public-sector compensation-cap legislation) unconstitutional in November 2022, triggering union reopener negotiations; a $38.950M liability is booked following April 2023 Ontario Nurses' Association arbitration decisions (fs-2023, a3982564e2c6).

FY2023-24 (fs-2024, db361aecd418): Revenue $1.522B, expenses $1.504B before Bill 124 items; a separate "Bill 124 revenue" of $80.607M and "Bill 124 expenses" of $52.619M are broken out as distinct line items, netting to a $27.988M positive contribution; total excess of revenues over expenses for the year: $45.389M — the strongest surplus in the archived four-year financial series (fs-2024, db361aecd418). The dedicated COVID-19 note present in fs-2023 does not recur in this or any later statement (confirmed by direct text search of all four financial statements; fs-2024, db361aecd418).

FY2024-25 (fs-2025, 0f1ebb4f9fc2; ar-2025, 0365e43da6e9): Revenue $1.681B, expenses $1.659M [sic, thousands], excess of revenues over expenses $21.785M (fs-2025, 0f1ebb4f9fc2). The Note 14(d) Bill 124 narrative is updated: the Ontario government "repealed Bill 124 in its entirety" (exact repeal date not captured in the excerpted text; fs-2025, 0f1ebb4f9fc2). The FY2024-25 annual report frames the year around four named "Strategic Directions" — Personalized & Precise Treatments, Integrated Care, Quality & Patient Experience, Thriving People & Teams — and three "Enablers" — Social Responsibility, Digital Health & Artificial Intelligence, Growth & Investment — under the newly launched "Invent 2030" strategic plan (ar-2025, 0365e43da6e9; consistent with sp-2025, a3f29f83b990). Named milestones: opening of the Garry Hurvitz Brain Sciences Centre (January 2025, Bayview campus, enabled by a $20M gift from Garry Hurvitz plus more than 7,000 donors and provincial government funding); launch of the Access to Emergent Care Task Force; arrival of "Canada's first 7T MRI scanner" (installation reported as imminent/underway); construction of the Peter Cipriano Centre for Seniors Health (targeted late 2025 opening); and preparation to implement a new Health Information System (ar-2025, 0365e43da6e9). Total patient visits reported at 1,140,156; total staff/physicians/learners/volunteers over 16,000+ combined figures on the activity page (ar-2025, 0365e43da6e9).

FY2025-26 (fs-2026, 21252e7916c6; ar-2026, 1381a125a6d2): Revenue $1.759B, expenses $1.750B, excess of revenues over expenses $9.581M — a narrower margin than the prior two years (fs-2026, 21252e7916c6). Note 13 discloses that, effective April 1, 2025, Sunnybrook "entered into a voluntary integration with Vibrant Community Health (Vibrant), a registered charity, and became its sole voting member"; Vibrant's assets, liabilities and operations are not consolidated into Sunnybrook's financial statements but its summarized financial information is disclosed as a related entity (fs-2026, 21252e7916c6). The FY2025-26 annual report frames this same event descriptively as an "integration with Vibrant Community Health," building "a stronger, more connected system of care" via a Management Services Agreement that preserves Vibrant's operational autonomy while Sunnybrook provides back-office/administrative support (ar-2026, 1381a125a6d2). Named milestones this year: opening of the Peter Cipriano Centre for Seniors Health (fall 2025, enabled by a $10M lead gift from Peter Cipriano); Toronto's first clinical 7-Tesla MRI system officially opened (2025) in partnership with the Toronto Academic Health Sciences Network (TAHSN); a July 2025 agreement with Oracle Canada to implement a new Health Information System (Oracle Health electronic health record, branded "Project REACH," expected launch 2027); an internal AI competition delivered with the Vector Institute (22 submissions, four projects advanced); and a new "Indigenous Health and Partnerships Lead" role (Leonard Benoit named in that role) alongside the October 2025 opening of the Gord Downie & Chanie Wenjack Legacy Space at the Holland Orthopaedic & Arthritic Centre Library — described as "the first of its kind in a Toronto hospital" (ar-2026, 1381a125a6d2). The FY2025-26 report is the first in this archived series to publish a formal "Key Performance Indicators — Invent 2030" table mapping each of the four Strategic Directions to named quantitative targets, e.g., Clinical Trials Median Days to Institutional Authorizations (result 170 vs. target ≤90), Number of New Primary Care Attachments (result 7,221 vs. target 7,500), Mean Overall Patient Experience Score (result 8.3 vs. target 8.0) (ar-2026, 1381a125a6d2).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Sunnybrook is a Toronto-based institution throughout the archived series: its main Bayview campus, its Holland Orthopaedic & Arthritic Centre, and its St. John's Rehab campus are all named as sites of care and capital investment (ar-2025, 0365e43da6e9; ar-2026, 1381a125a6d2). The CEO/Board Chair message in both annual reports explicitly frames the hospital's commitments as being "to the people of North Toronto and Ontario" (ar-2025, 0365e43da6e9; ar-2026, 1381a125a6d2). Sunnybrook is the fund holder for the North Toronto Ontario Health Team, receiving and fully spending Ontario Health-directed OHT funding of $750K in FY2022-23, $1.014M in FY2024-25, and $750K in FY2025-26 (fs-2023, a3982564e2c6; fs-2026, 21252e7916c6). The Vibrant Community Health integration (effective April 1, 2025) is explicitly tied to expanding "primary and community care in North Toronto" (ar-2026, 1381a125a6d2). Toronto's first clinical 7-Tesla MRI system opened at Sunnybrook's Bayview campus via the Toronto Academic Health Sciences Network (TAHSN) partnership (ar-2026, 1381a125a6d2). The Hospital also holds outstanding letters of guarantee in favour of the City of Toronto and Toronto Hydro (fs-2023, a3982564e2c6) and received a City of Toronto investment supporting the hiring of an additional BRAVE violence-prevention case manager (ar-2025, 0365e43da6e9). PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP's auditor's report was issued from an Oakville, Ontario address for fs-2023 and from a Toronto, Ontario (18 York Street) address for fs-2024 through fs-2026 — an auditor's-office relocation, not evidence of any change to Sunnybrook's own footprint (fs-2023, a3982564e2c6; fs-2024, db361aecd418; fs-2026, 21252e7916c6).
