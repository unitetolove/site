Ministry of Natural Resources - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-natural-resources (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Ministry is continued as a ministry of the Ontario public service under the Ministry of Natural Resources Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.31 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m31, verified — consolidation current to June 1, 2021, last amended 2021, c. 4, Sched. 6, s.63). Section 3 continues "the ministry of the public service known in English as the Ministry of Natural Resources"; section 5 establishes a Deputy Minister and a statutory Surveyor General role for land surveying, water-power investigation and engineering (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m31; registry row on-ministry-natural-resources).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Mandate is stated as "protecting Ontario's biodiversity while promoting economic opportunities in the resource sector and supporting outdoor recreation," covering sustainable management of fish, wildlife, Crown forests and Crown lands; oil, gas, salt and aggregate resource extraction oversight; forest-fire, flood and drought emergency response; hunting/fishing licensing; invasive-species management; and geographic/natural-heritage information services (ontario.ca Ministry overview, https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-natural-resources; registry row on-ministry-natural-resources).

Governance & reporting line

Headed by a Minister who "shall preside over and have charge of the Ministry," with a Deputy Minister as deputy head and a statutory Surveyor General, reporting through Cabinet to the Legislative Assembly (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m31, s.4-5).

Budget scale

$875.4M total planned expenditures for 2025-26 ($809.2M operating + $66.2M capital), up 12.3% ($88.7M) from $720.5M in 2024-25 estimates; largest 2025-26 operating lines were Forest Industry ($258.5M), Public Safety and Emergency Response ($181.1M), and Regional Operations ($145.3M) (ontario.ca Ministry of Natural Resources published plans and annual reports, 2025-2026, https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-natural-resources; registry strategy_current_url matches this page).

Institutional history

Established 1972 by amalgamating the Department of Lands and Forests with the Department of Mines and Northern Affairs, as the Ministry of Natural Resources; renamed Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry (2014-2021), briefly Ministry of Northern Development, Mines, Natural Resources and Forestry (2021-2022), back to Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry (2022-2024), and to Ministry of Natural Resources (2024-present, with forestry moved to an Associate Minister portfolio) (WebSearch/Wikipedia summary of Ministry name history, cross-checked against the confirmed governing Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.31, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m31; registry url_status flagged ⚠️ still being checked).

Ministry of Natural Resources - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 / registry: on-ministry-natural-resources / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition in the 2025-26 annual report is a rebranded "Geospatial Ontario" function, newly quantified as contributing "over 5 billion dollars to Ontario's economy each year," alongside a new Fishing and Hunting Licensing System targeted for a December 2026 go-live. The biggest dropped element is Bill 228, the Resource Management and Safety Act, 2024 — introduced to bundle several legislative changes but explicitly reported terminated by the legislature's January 28, 2025 dissolution, with no successor named. The load-bearing number is 2025-26 planned expenditure of $875.4 million, a 12.3% single-year increase, the largest jump across the three archived annual-report cycles. The open question the three-year record (2023-24 through 2025-26) cannot resolve: whether any successor to the dissolved Bill 228 was introduced afterward, since the archived series ends with the bill's termination and no replacement.

Backgrounder summary

The Ministry is continued under the Ministry of Natural Resources Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.31, with a Deputy Minister and a statutory Surveyor General role. Its mandate is "protecting Ontario's biodiversity while promoting economic opportunities in the resource sector and supporting outdoor recreation," spanning fish, wildlife, Crown forests and Crown lands; oil, gas, salt and aggregate extraction oversight; forest-fire, flood and drought emergency response; hunting/fishing licensing; invasive-species management; and geographic/natural-heritage information services. Planned 2025-26 total expenditure is $875.4M ($809.2M operating + $66.2M capital), up 12.3% from $720.5M in 2024-25 estimates, with Forest Industry ($258.5M), Public Safety and Emergency Response ($181.1M), and Regional Operations ($145.3M) the largest operating lines. Institutionally, the ministry has cycled through five names since its 1972 creation (merger of Lands and Forests with Mines and Northern Affairs): Natural Resources → Natural Resources and Forestry (2014-2021) → Northern Development, Mines, Natural Resources and Forestry (2021-2022) → back to Natural Resources and Forestry (2022-2024) → Natural Resources (2024-present, forestry moved to an Associate Minister portfolio) (backgrounder our research file for that body).

Priority evolution

2023-24 (ar-2024, ed52e60062d7), "Published Plans and Annual Reports 2023-2024": Mandate framed as five key activities — protect the public from natural hazards; deliver direct services; promote economic growth/job creation; conduct monitoring/this library's internal records; develop legislation/policy/programs — delivered through 11 named programs (Forest Industry, Natural Resources Policy, Natural Resource Science and Research, Mapping and Geographic Information, Forest Management, Fish and Wildlife, Regional Natural Resources Operations, Natural Resources Enforcement, Far North, Public Safety & Natural Hazard Emergency Response, Corporate Management). Headline initiatives: Sustainable Growth: Ontario's Forest Sector Strategy (FSS, released Aug 20, 2020, four pillars) and its Forest Biomass Action Plan; a new $23.652M/3-year legacy oil-and-gas-well and subsurface-gas-migration action plan; LiDAR-based Crown forest inventory (one of the largest LiDAR projects in Canada); Managing Risks of Subsurface Gas Migration; Niagara Escarpment modernization. Ministry planned expenditure: $823.2M ($757.1M operating + $66.1M capital) (ed52e60062d7).

2024-25 (ar-2025, d4f98cdb4aa7), "Published Plans and Annual Reports 2024-2025: Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry": Same five-key-activity / 11-program framing retained. New/expanded items: $8.3M/3-year Land Information Ontario (LIO) modernization investment (starting 2024-25); $4M/2-year Public Safety Radio Network project (replacing legacy first-responder radio infrastructure across 37 Forest Fire HQ/Attack Bases, 94 ministry offices, 340 Provincial Parks, 295 Conservation Areas); a $64M/4-year federal-provincial "Fighting and Managing Wildfires in a Changing Climate Equipment Fund" agreement, including $17.4M/3 years approved in this cycle for firefighting equipment, IT modernization and a medium-class helicopter, plus 107 permanent FTEs / $22.1M staffing over 3 years; up to $15M/4 years for federal Flood Hazard Identification and Mapping Program (FHIMP) participation; a new Surveying Profession initiative addressing surveyor shortages. Ministry planned expenditure: $815.1M ($753.2M operating + $61.9M capital) (d4f98cdb4aa7). Appendix reports the 2023-24 wildfire season as severe: 741 fires / 441,474.1 hectares burned vs. a 10-year average of 163,627 hectares — includes mutual aid received from Minnesota, Manitoba, Northwest Territories, and 104 Mexican wildland firefighters (d4f98cdb4aa7).

2025-26 (ar-2026, 78567901f47b), "Published Plans and Annual Reports 2025-2026: Ministry of Natural Resources": Structural reframing — the five "key activities" are replaced by five named "government and ministry priorities": Economic development, Service excellence, Sustainable resources management, Public safety, and Organizational leadership. The program count drops from 11 to 10 (Far North is folded into Regional Natural Resources Operations as a branch rather than a standalone program; see Priorities added/dropped/renamed). New/renamed initiatives: Wood Measurement Modernization Plan (WMMP, launched 2024-25); Advanced Wood Construction Action Plan (finalized Jan 2025); Geospatial Ontario (rebranding/expansion of prior "Geospatial Data Services," now framed as a $5B/year economic contributor); a new Fishing and Hunting Licensing System (FAWLS rebuild, targeted go-live December 2026); Ishkode Coordination Table (new in 2024-25, Indigenous wildfire-management engagement). ⚠️ Still being checked: the appendix (2024-25 results) reports Bill 228, Resource Management and Safety Act, 2024 was introduced to bundle several proposed legislative changes (Forest Fires Prevention Act updates, new Geologic Carbon Storage Act, Oil, Gas and Salt Resources Act amendments, Surveyors Act amendments) — the legislature was dissolved January 28, 2025 and "all incomplete business was terminated including Bill 228" (78567901f47b). Ministry planned expenditure: $875.4M ($809.2M operating + $66.2M capital) — matches the backgrounder figure (78567901f47b).

2023, Auditor General value-for-money audit (oth-2023, 221b9b803334), "Management of Aggregate Resources": Not a ministry-authored strategy document but an external oversight report on the Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry's administration of the Aggregate Resources Act (1990). Finds the Ministry "falling short in balancing its competing roles of facilitating the extraction of aggregate resources and minimizing the impacts of aggregate operations" — citing a shortage of experienced aggregate inspectors (34 designated ministry-wide as of May 2023, two districts with none), a 64% drop in inspection rates 2018-2022, non-enforcement of self-reporting suspensions, extraction fees "likely too low to cover the costs needed to effectively administer the program," 1,524 aggregate sites dormant/unrehabilitated for 10+ years (~25,000 hectares), and outdated/fragmented IT systems (paper records, five different information systems, Excel spreadsheets). Contains 18 recommendations / 31 action items; Ministry response frames findings as aligned with an already-underway modernization path (centralized approvals since 2020, added staffing capacity 2022) (221b9b803334).

  • Added — Wood Measurement Modernization Plan (WMMP): first launched 2024-25, appears in ar-2026 as an ongoing initiative; absent from ar-2024 (ed52e60062d7; 78567901f47b).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

The Ministry is a provincial (not federal) body whose entire mandate is Ontario-specific, so its relevance is direct and pervasive rather than indirect. Its head office contact is explicitly listed in both ar-2024 and ar-2025 as Whitney Block, Room 6630, 99 Wellesley Street West, Toronto, Ontario M7A 1W3 (ed52e60062d7; d4f98cdb4aa7) — i.e., the ministry's ministerial office is physically located in Toronto at Queen's Park. The Auditor General's aggregate-resources audit notes that demand for aggregates is especially high "in the Greater Golden Horseshoe region around Toronto," directly tying the ministry's aggregate-licensing function to GTA construction and infrastructure activity (221b9b803334). The ministry's Aggregates Supply and Demand Study is specific to the "Greater Golden Horseshoe" (ed52e60062d7). Toronto-area waterway/lake management appears through the ministry's Great Lakes fisheries management units (Lake Ontario Management Unit, referenced across all three annual reports) and flood-mapping/hydrometric programs affecting the wider Ontario watershed including the GTA (ed52e60062d7; d4f98cdb4aa7; 78567901f47b). No individual Toronto field office or park is separately named in the extracted text beyond the head-office address.
