Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-housing-infrastructure-communities-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada (HICC) is not established by its own dedicated Act; rather, the "Department of Housing, Infrastructure and Communities" is listed among federal departments in Schedule VI, Part I of the Financial Administration Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. F-11 — the schedule identifying departments whose deputy heads are accounting officers under s.16.4 (consolidated text verified by direct fetch of https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/F-11/page-25.html). ⚠️ still being checked - whether the department also carries a separate FAA Schedule I listing (departments generally) was not independently confirmed this review.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

HICC's mandate is "building resilient and inclusive communities for Canadians by providing funding and advice for locally designed and delivered housing and homelessness initiatives as well as new and upgraded infrastructure" (https://housing-infrastructure.canada.ca/index-eng.html). Its 2026-27 Departmental Plan organizes this into two core responsibilities: "Housing and Homelessness" (supporting projects advancing national housing outcomes and reducing/preventing homelessness) and "Public Transit, Sustainable Infrastructure, and Community Building" (transit, infrastructure and alternative financing investments), with the National Housing Council operating under the National Housing Strategy Act, S.C. 2019, c. 29, s. 313, as part of the housing portfolio (https://housing-infrastructure.canada.ca/pub/dp-pm/2026-27/2026-dp-pm-eng.html; registry row fed-housing-infrastructure-communities-canada).

Governance & reporting line

HICC is headed by the Honourable Gregor Robertson, Minister of Housing and Infrastructure (also Minister responsible for Pacific Economic Development Canada), consistent with departmental status under the Financial Administration Act, Schedule VI, Part I (https://housing-infrastructure.canada.ca/pub/dp-pm/2026-27/2026-dp-pm-eng.html; https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/F-11/page-25.html). ⚠️ still being checked - the Deputy Minister's name/title was not confirmed from a primary source this review.

Budget scale

Total planned spending for 2026-27 is $10,452,471,061 (~$10.45B), split between Housing and Homelessness ($1,044,870,486), Public Transit, Sustainable Infrastructure, and Community Building ($9,323,760,608), and Internal Services ($83,839,967), with 1,614 planned full-time equivalents (Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada 2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://housing-infrastructure.canada.ca/pub/dp-pm/2026-27/2026-dp-pm-eng.html).

Institutional history

HICC's predecessor, Infrastructure Canada, and its housing functions were consolidated under the current "Housing, Infrastructure and Communities" name and listed as a Financial Administration Act Schedule VI, Part I department (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/F-11/page-25.html). ⚠️ still being checked - the specific year and instrument of the merger/rename from Infrastructure Canada to Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada was not confirmed from a primary source this review.

Only dp-2027 (0baeb7dd9d3e) contains a substantive priorities list: six named 2026-27 key priorities spanning federal housing-supply leadership (with Build Canada Homes), homelessness reduction, transit-oriented community investment, general infrastructure investment, major-bridge stewardship, and catalyzing private investment.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Institutional rename — "Infrastructure Canada" became "Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada," per dp-2027's Corporate Information section giving "Year of incorporation / commencement: 2024" (0baeb7dd9d3e) and drr-2024/drr-2025's title framing "(formerly Infrastructure Canada)" (4a3d210249ee; a49fcdef9310) — consistent with the backgrounder's flagged-but-unconfirmed rename now resolved by direct primary-source text.
  • Core responsibilities restructured from three to two, per dp-2027's own footnotes 11 and 12: the Departmental Results Framework "was amended for fiscal year 2025-26, reducing the number of core responsibilities from three (in place through 2024-25) to two" (0baeb7dd9d3e). The prior three-responsibility structure ("Public Infrastructure, Communities, Affordable Housing and Homelessness Policy"; "Public Infrastructure, Communities and Homelessness Investments"; "Public Infrastructure and Communities Investment, Stewardship and Delivery") is visible only in dp-2027's own three-year retrospective spending table for 2023-24 and 2024-25 (0baeb7dd9d3e) — no readable document from those years describes the old structure in its own words.
  • Added — Build Canada Homes (BCH), a special operating agency "launched on September 14, 2025," planned to transition to "an arm's length organizational entity" (0baeb7dd9d3e). ⚠️ Still being checked: no prior-year readable document describes its creation in progress; dp-2027 is the first (and only) readable document to mention it.
  • Added — Build Communities Strong Fund (BCSF), $51 billion over 10 years starting 2026-27, announced in Budget 2025, folding the existing Canada Community-Building Fund in as its "Community Stream" (0baeb7dd9d3e).
  • Added — Comprehensive Expenditure Review (CER) reductions, first disclosed in dp-2027: no prior document in this set mentions any CER-branded reduction program for this department (0baeb7dd9d3e).
  • ⚠️ Still being checked: with no readable Departmental Plan or Results Report between dp-2020 (which is itself a stub) and dp-2027, this brief cannot establish what the actual FY2019-20 through FY2025-26 core-responsibility and priority wording was, beyond the minister names and titles visible in stub chrome.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2026-27 total planned spending: $10,452,471,061, of which $9,323,760,608 (89.2%) sits in Public Transit, Sustainable Infrastructure, and Community Building, $1,044,870,486 in Housing and Homelessness, and $83,839,967 in Internal Services, against 1,614 total planned FTEs (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e) — exactly matching the backgrounder's independently-cited figures.
  • Comprehensive Expenditure Review reductions, first disclosed in dp-2027: $488,708,832 (2026-27), $648,795,886 (2027-28), and $972,041,066 (2028-29), tied to an anticipated decrease of "approximately 190 full-time equivalents by 2028-29," delivered via "targeted reductions in contribution funding," "adjustments to legacy programming authorities," and "delayering and streamlining the management cadre" (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e). ⚠️ Still being checked: the backgrounder's budget-scale section does not mention the CER at all — this brief's CER figures come from its own direct read of dp-2027, not from backgrounder relay.
  • Planned spending trajectory 2026-27 to 2028-29: total planned spending rises to $11,506,099,293 in 2027-28 (driven by Canada Public Transit Fund ramp-up) before falling to $10,332,075,150 in 2028-29, "primarily attributable to funding currently winding down for Reaching Home... as well as the reprofile announced in Budget 2025 under the Investing in Canada Infrastructure Program" (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e). Planned FTEs fall from 1,614 (2026-27) to 1,177 (2028-29).
  • Build Communities Strong Fund launch: $51 billion over 10 years starting 2026-27, split into a Provincial and Territorial Stream ($17.2 billion, including $5 billion for a new Health Infrastructure Fund), a Direct Delivery Stream ($6 billion), and the rebranded Canada Community-Building Fund as its Community Stream ($27.8 billion, agreements in place to March 2034) (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e).
  • Build Canada Homes launched September 14, 2025 as a special operating agency, with 2026-27 first investments including "building 4,000 Direct Build homes on federal lands in Ottawa, Toronto, Winnipeg, Edmonton, Longueuil and Dartmouth," $1 billion for supportive/transitional housing, and a 750-home agreement with Nunavut (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e).
  • Predecessor-body wind-down (2017-18, PPP Canada): PPP Canada Inc., a federal Crown corporation created in 2008 to promote public-private partnerships, was dissolved effective March 29, 2018, following a Governor-in-Council decision of November 1, 2017; its P3 Canada Fund assets ($1,033,323 thousand) and remaining liabilities were distributed to the Minister of Finance and the Minister of Infrastructure, Communities and Intergovernmental Affairs, with "contractual obligations related to the P3 Canada Fund... novated to Infrastructure Canada" (ar-2018, e2bad12c90b5). This is predecessor-body history adjacent to HICC, not a HICC self-report.
  • 2022 OAG finding on Climate Lens weakening: the Auditor General found Infrastructure Canada "weakened the requirements of the Climate Lens tool while implementing a better approach," poorly managed climate-related project information, and did not publicly report expected climate outcomes for over $12.8 billion planned across three infrastructure programs through 2033 (oth-2022, 2618d236841a, tabled April 26, 2022) — an independent external account, not a HICC self-report, and pre-dating dp-2027 by four years with no readable intervening document to show whether the tool was subsequently strengthened.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Toronto/GTA relevance in this archived set is concentrated entirely in dp-2027 (the only substantive planning document) and is direct rather than incidental — HICC holds an ongoing governance role, not merely program-recipient status: - Waterfront Toronto, described in dp-2027's Corporate Information as "a non-share capital corporation established in 2001... In 2000, the Government of Canada, the Province of Ontario and the City of Toronto each announced a commitment of $500 million" for the Toronto Waterfront Revitalization Initiative, with a current flood-protection phase adding $1.25 billion in equal federal/provincial/municipal funding (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e). HICC "provides strategic advice, analysis, and support to the Minister... on WT's governance and accountability" through an Intergovernmental Steering Committee (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e). - Toronto Waterfront Enabling Initiative, named directly: a Housing Enabling Infrastructure Project ($325 million federal) and the Broadview Eastern Flood Protection Project ($200 million federal, Lower Don Valley), together expected to "enable the future development of over 14,000 homes"; HICC states it "will continue to work with the Ontario Ministry of Infrastructure, the City of Toronto and Waterfront Toronto on the administration of these projects" in 2026-27 (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e). - Port Lands Flood Protection Project, engineered to protect against "a regional storm or a 100-year flood event," named as a joint deliverable with Waterfront Toronto under the Intergovernmental Steering Committee (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e). - Build Canada Homes' first-wave "Direct Build" homes explicitly include Toronto as one of six named federal-land sites (with Ottawa, Winnipeg, Edmonton, Longueuil, Dartmouth) for the 4,000-home 2026-27 tranche (dp-2027, 0baeb7dd9d3e). - No document in this set names any Toronto-specific regulatory, enforcement, or standalone program distinct from the Waterfront Toronto/BCH items above. Given the department's genuinely national, PT/municipal-flow-through funding model (Community-Building Fund, Public Transit Fund, Housing Infrastructure Fund all flow through provinces to municipalities generically), most of HICC's mandate is felt in Toronto only as one recipient among many Canadian municipalities — the items above are the exception, representing a standing federal-provincial-municipal governance relationship specific to Toronto's waterfront, not general program boilerplate.
