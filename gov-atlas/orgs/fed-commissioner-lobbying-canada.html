Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada (Office of the) - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-commissioner-lobbying-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying commenced operations in 2008 under the Lobbying Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 44 (4th Supp.) (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/l-12.4/page-1.html), which "establishes the Office of Commissioner of Lobbying and gives the Commissioner powers for regulating the lobbying of the federal officials in Canada" (Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying Departmental Plan 2026-27, "Reference information," https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/en/reports-and-publications/departmental-plan-2026-27/). The Commissioner's mandate is threefold: maintaining the public Registry of Lobbyists, developing education/awareness programs, and conducting compliance reviews and investigations under the Lobbying Act and the Lobbyists' Code of Conduct (same source; Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying Annual Report 2024-25, https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/en/reports-and-publications/annual-report-2024-25/).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Office supports the Commissioner in administering the Lobbying Act and the Lobbyists' Code of Conduct through a single core responsibility, "Regulation of lobbying": maintaining and enhancing the Registry of Lobbyists, providing registration support and public education, and conducting compliance verification and investigations, including referring files to the RCMP where there are reasonable grounds to believe an offence occurred (Departmental Plan 2026-27). Federal legislation has required disclosure of regulated federal lobbying since 1989, and the Lobbyists' Code of Conduct has set ethical standards for over 29 years (same source). In fiscal 2024-25 the Registry reached record levels of activity, with 6,223 active registrations (November 2024) and 7,421 active lobbyists (January 2025) (Annual Report 2024-25).

Governance & reporting line

The Commissioner of Lobbying is an independent Agent of Parliament, appointed by the Governor in Council after consultation with the leader of each recognized party in the Senate and House of Commons and approval by resolution of both Houses, for a seven-year term (WebSearch summary of Lobbying Act provisions and lobbycanada.gc.ca "Mandate" page content, cross-checked against the Act's citation above; ⚠️ still being checked - the Office's own "About us/Mandate" pages returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch this review, so this appointment-process detail is sourced via search rather than a directly fetched primary page). The Commissioner reports annually to both Houses of Parliament pursuant to Lobbying Act s.11, with reports tabled by the Speaker of the Senate and the Speaker of the House of Commons (Annual Report 2024-25, opening notice). For administrative-tabling purposes only, departmental plans and results reports are tabled by the President of the Treasury Board (Ministerial portfolio: Treasury Board); the Commissioner otherwise operates independently of government (Departmental Plan 2026-27).

Budget scale

Planned total spending for fiscal 2026-27 is $6,146,000 ($4,125,000 for the "Regulation of lobbying" core responsibility plus $2,021,000 for internal services), against actual spending of $6,090,000 in 2024-25 and a forecast of $6,113,000 for 2025-26 (Departmental Plan 2026-27, Tables 8B-8C). Planned full-time equivalents for 2026-27 are 36 (27 in the core program, 9 in internal services), up from an actual utilization of 33 FTEs by the end of 2024-25 (same source, Tables 11A-11B). The Office describes itself as a "micro-organization" operating with roughly $4.6 million of its ~$6.2 million budget devoted to salaries and benefits, about $1.6 million to operations and maintenance, and roughly $700,000 paying other federal institutions (e.g., PSPC, CHRC, Parole Board of Canada, Office of the Privacy Commissioner) for shared corporate services (Annual Report 2024-25).

Institutional history

The Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying was established in 2008 under the Lobbying Act, succeeding a regulatory regime under which federal lobbying disclosure had been required since 1989 (Departmental Plan 2026-27, "Year commenced: 2008"; Annual Report 2024-25: "Since 1989, legislation has required disclosure of regulated lobbying"). The Lobbying Act itself has not been amended since its 2008 inception, and the Commissioner has continued to identify and recommend possible legislative improvements pending a parliamentary review (Annual Report 2024-25).

Priority evolution

2020-21 (bp-2021, drr-2021, ar-2021): The year is framed around COVID-19 disruption and, unexpectedly, record lobbying activity despite it — ar-2021 states lobbyists "communicated with federal decision-makers in record numbers" as pandemic funding programs rolled out "sometimes in a matter of weeks" (a674c87a6412). drr-2021 confirms a structural change already in effect: three previously separate programs (Registry of Lobbyists; education and outreach; compliance and enforcement) have been combined into one "Registry services and compliance" program, delivered with $3.6M actual spending and 24 FTE (4d01970992da). Communication-report volume hit ~29,000 for the year — the series' high point to that point.

2021-22 (bp-2022, ar-2022): Continued record-setting for active registrations and active lobbyists, with monthly records for oral-communication reporting; the Office explicitly frames this as "another busy" second consecutive pandemic year (de2eec44fc93). Total communication reports for the year (24,538) came in below 2020-21's ~29,000 peak — the first instance in the readable window where a superlative from an earlier document is not superseded, only equalled in kind (record-setting registrations/lobbyists continue, but total-report volume dips before resuming its climb the following year).

2022-23 (bp-2023, drr-2023): Planned spending/FTE step up materially (bp-2023: $5.45M planned / 33 FTE, core "Registry services and compliance" $4.24M / 27 FTE, a848a201fcdf); actual results land lower ($5.2M total / 28 FTE, core $3.7M / 23 FTE, ad9faaa879f5) — the first visible planned-vs-actual FTE gap in the series (33 planned vs. 28 actual).

2023-24 (ar-2024): The Commissioner's own message frames sustained multi-year growth explicitly: "the number of active lobbyists at any one time [has increased] from just over 6,000 in 2018 to nearly 9,000 in 2023," with active registrations "surpass[ing] the 4,000-mark in 2017 and climb[ing] to over 6,000 in 2023" (1ac271abaa3b) — the only place in the readable window where the Office narrates its own multi-year trend rather than year-over-year comparison alone. All-time highs of 6,032 active registrations and 7,336 active lobbyists (both March 2024) and a new all-time communication-report record (34,271, a 10.5% increase over the prior record) are reported (1ac271abaa3b).

2024-25 (bp-2025, drr-2025, ar-2025): Registration/lobbyist growth continues to new all-time highs (6,223 active registrations, November 2024; 7,421 active lobbyists, January 2025; October 2024's 4,509 monthly communication-report count breaks the prior monthly record by "more than 500 reports") (94c4a6361771). But total communication-report volume for the fiscal year (31,058) declines for the first time in the window — down ~3,000 from the prior year — which ar-2025 attributes explicitly to the January 2026 prorogation of Parliament and the March 2026 election call ending public-office-holder designations mid-year, consistent with "trends observed in previous election years" (94c4a6361771). This is the first explained downturn in an otherwise steadily-upward five-year run.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added/consolidated — the unified "Registry services and compliance" program: three separate prior programs (Registry of Lobbyists; education/outreach; compliance/enforcement) merged into one, visible from drr-2021 onward (4d01970992da) — the single clearest structural change in the readable window; no earlier document survives to date the merger precisely, so it may predate 2020-21.

  • Nothing identifiably dropped. No priority, program, or stated objective present in an earlier document of this window is absent from a later one; the Lobbying Act itself is repeatedly confirmed unamended since 2008 across every document that discusses it, consistent with the backgrounder.

Two named Investigation Reports are tabled within the window — one (February 2024, ar-2024, 1ac271abaa3b) finding a former parliamentary staffer-turned-lobbyist had contravened Rules 7 and 8 of the 2015 Lobbyists' Code of Conduct by lobbying his former employer, a sitting Member of Parliament; a second (referenced retrospectively in ar-2025, 94c4a6361771) where the RCMP formally laid charges under the Act, with Ontario Court of Justice proceedings ongoing as of a May 2025 court date.
