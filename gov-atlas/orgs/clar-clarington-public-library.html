Clarington Public Library - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: clar-clarington-public-library (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Governed under the Public Libraries Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.44, consolidated text at ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p44 (link fetch-verified this review). Act s. 20 requires the board to provide "a comprehensive and efficient public library service" (ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p44). Operating publicly as "Clarington Library, Museums & Archives," describing its purpose as helping the community "dive into a world of knowledge, art, and history" (cplma.ca, fetched this review).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The statutory library board governs a combined library, museums and archives service for the Municipality of Clarington (registry row notes, registry id: clar-clarington-public-library); board meeting agendas/minutes are cross-listed on the Municipality of Clarington's Council Meeting Calendar (cplma.ca, fetched this review).

Governance & reporting line

A statutory library board of nine members plus the CEO/Secretary-Treasurer, including one Municipal Councillor among its members (Lloyd Rang at time of research), with Chair and two Vice-Chair roles (cplma.ca/about-us/board-and-administration/library-board, fetched this review; registry row registry id: clar-clarington-public-library, parent_org: clar-municipality-of-clarington). Board meetings are open to the public (same source).

Backgrounder summary

Clarington Public Library operates publicly as "Clarington Library, Museums & Archives" (CLMA), a statutory library board under the Public Libraries Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.44, governing a combined library/museums/archives service for the Municipality of Clarington (backgrounder, citing cplma.ca). The backgrounder describes a nine-member board plus CEO/Secretary-Treasurer, including one Municipal Councillor (Lloyd Rang at time of research), and flags budget scale as ⚠️ still being checked — not located on public web pages. Both the board composition and the named councillor are independently confirmed by the documents read for this brief: ar-2025 names Lloyd Rang as "Ward 2 Councillor" board member alongside Chair Tenzin Shomar, Vice-Chairs Nancy Brandon and Katharine Warren, and CEO Monika Machacek (ca704f93861b) — matching sp-2023's board list naming the same individuals in the same official roles (4f971408786d). ar-2025 resolves the backgrounder's budget ⚠️ still being checked with a full 2025 revenue/expense statement (see Budget & mandate inflection points below) (ca704f93861b).

Priority evolution

sp-2023 organizes CLMA's strategy around four "Strategic Pillars": Knowledge-Rich Organization, Community Destination of Choice, Organizational Excellence, and Resource Utilization and Sustainability, each with detailed sub-commitments (e.g., a Maker's Space in Courtice, a Social Workers program, a bookmobile, a Repatriation and Decolonization strategy) (4f971408786d). ar-2025 does not organize itself around these same four named pillars at all — it is structured instead as a highlights-and-statistics report (2025 Highlights, By the Numbers, Revenue & Expenses) with no explicit cross-reference back to the four pillars (ca704f93861b). Several sp-2023 sub-commitments do appear delivered in ar-2025 under different framing: sp-2023's "Repair Cafes will build social infrastructure" commitment (pillar 4) matches ar-2025's report of 3 Repair Cafes hosted in 2025 (4f971408786d; ca704f93861b), and sp-2023's Courtice Maker's Space commitment (pillar 1) is consistent with ar-2025's "Library of Things" and Book Locker program at Darlington Sports Centre, though ar-2025 does not explicitly label these as Maker's Space outputs (4f971408786d; ca704f93861b).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — "Curious Communities" library-card discount program: launched June 2025, offering participating-business discounts to CLMA cardholders, shortlisted for a provincial award (ca704f93861b) — no antecedent commitment to a discount/loyalty program appears in sp-2023's pillar structure.
  • Added — Book Locker at Darlington Sports Centre: unveiled May 2025 with the Municipality of Clarington, allowing residents to borrow books from a non-library-branch community location (ca704f93861b) — not named in sp-2023, though broadly consistent with sp-2023's unspecified "growing our reputation" and access-expansion language.
  • Added — Golden Tales oral-history project and first Heritage Fair (November 2025): both reported in ar-2025 as 2025-specific initiatives (ca704f93861b) — the Heritage Fair is consistent with sp-2023's stated intent to "interpret and exhibit our rich local history" (pillar 1) but is not itself named as a planned recurring event in the 2023 plan.
  • Not confirmed dropped or renamed: no sp-2023 pillar or named initiative (e.g., the bookmobile, the Repatriation and Decolonization strategy, the Social Workers program, the Bowmanville Library renovation) is explicitly stated as complete, in-progress, or abandoned in ar-2025 — their absence from the annual report's highlights section is not evidence of being dropped, given the annual report's selective-highlights format rather than a full pillar-by-pillar progress accounting.
