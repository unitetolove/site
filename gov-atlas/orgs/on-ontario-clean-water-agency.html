Ontario Clean Water Agency - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ontario-clean-water-agency (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

OCWA is established as a corporation without share capital under the Capital Investment Plan Act, 1993, S.O. 1993, c. 23, s. 2(1); consolidated text verified at ontario.ca/laws/statute/93c23 (fetched 2026-07-30). Section 49(1) sets its objects: assisting municipalities and government "by financing, planning, developing, building and operating" water and sewage works, and carrying out activities "in a manner that protects human health and the environment" (ontario.ca/laws/statute/93c23).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

OCWA operates, maintains and manages water and wastewater treatment facilities and distribution systems for municipal, industrial and First Nation clients across Ontario, and provides engineering, advisory, technical and training services on a competitive/commercial basis (pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/226, fetched 2026-07-30; on-ontario-clean-water-agency). It is a Tier 1 crown-corp in the registry; its current strategy instrument is the OCWA Business Plan 2026-2028 (on-ontario-clean-water-agency, url_status "verified" per registry).

Governance & reporting line

The Agency is managed by a board with a Chair plus 4-12 other members, all appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council for up to three-year terms; the CEO is excluded from board membership (Capital Investment Plan Act, 1993, s. 5, ontario.ca/laws/statute/93c23; pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/226). It reports through the Minister of the Environment, Conservation and Parks (Capital Investment Plan Act, 1993, s. 3(3); pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/226).

Budget scale

~$335.6M in total revenue for calendar/fiscal year 2024, with a net income figure reported in the same period (OCWA Annual Report, ocwa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/OCWA-AR2025-FULL-Final-ENG-tagged-1.pdf, fetched and text-extracted 2026-07-30). The report also notes total operating revenue grew 16.5% year over year (same source); exact net-income figure and precise fiscal-year boundary (calendar vs. April-March) not independently disambiguated this review (⚠️ still being checked exact fiscal-year definition).

Institutional history

Created in 1993 under the Capital Investment Plan Act, 1993, when the province transferred direct ownership/operation of municipal water and sewage treatment plants from the Ministry of the Environment to the new Agency (Capital Investment Plan Act, 1993, s. 2(1) and s. 49(1), ontario.ca/laws/statute/93c23).

Ontario Clean Water Agency - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 / registry: on-ontario-clean-water-agency / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is a named "Reconciliation roadmap"/Reconciliation Action Plan, developed with the First Nations Advisory Circle and appearing for the first time in the 2024 annual report, the culmination of a First Nations engagement effort that grew steadily from 2019 onward. The biggest quietly-changed item is OCWA's head office itself relocating from Toronto to Mississauga (10-year lease signed January 2019, relocation completed August 2020), moving the Agency's own headquarters out of the city despite Toronto remaining prominent in its founding-era documents. The most load-bearing number is 2024 total revenue of $335.6 million, up 16.5% year over year and the largest figure in the archived series, matching the backgrounder. One open question the record cannot resolve: OCWA replaced its entire Vision, Mission and Values in 2022 as the basis for an updated Strategic Plan, but no archived document covers 2023 itself, so the exact new wording and the moment the new plan took formal effect are unread.

Backgrounder summary

OCWA is a corporation without share capital established under the Capital Investment Plan Act, 1993, S.O. 1993, c. 23, with objects (s. 49(1)) to assist municipalities and government "by financing, planning, developing, building and operating" water and sewage works in a manner that protects human health and the environment. It operates, maintains and manages water and wastewater treatment facilities for municipal, industrial and First Nation clients across Ontario, and sells engineering, advisory and training services commercially. Created in 1993 when the province transferred direct ownership/operation of municipal water and sewage plants from the Ministry of the Environment to the new Agency, OCWA is a Tier 1 Crown corporation governed by a Lieutenant-Governor-in-Council-appointed board and reporting through the Minister of the Environment, Conservation and Parks. Its current strategy instrument is the OCWA Business Plan 2026-2028; the backgrounder records ~$335.6M total revenue for 2024, with total operating revenue growing 16.5% year over year and the precise fiscal-year boundary flagged as unverified.

Priority evolution

2000 (oth-2000, 0b491dd78f17): OCWA's report to the Walkerton Public Utilities Commission "on the operational measures taken to address the E. coli water contamination in the town of Walkerton," published in Toronto. This is the earliest document in the series. Only the title, publisher ("Toronto: the Agency") and bibliographic record were recoverable on this review; the report's actual content is unread. ⚠️ still being checked substantive content.

2003-2005 (b94e80c84450; 3a892abded49; 6f1b55a0e545): Annual reports archived, but extraction captured only Internet Archive item-page navigation chrome (menus, download-format lists, OCR technical fields) with no title description or report content. No substantive priority claims are recoverable for these three years.

2006 (b6030fb392d0): First year with recoverable descriptive content. OCWA is described as "in the business of providing safe, reliable and cost-effective clean water services," with core business "the operation, maintenance and management of water and wastewater treatment facilities and their associated distribution and collection systems," plus "project management and advisory services" sold on a fee-for-service basis under multi-year contracts. Cover title: "Complete solutions, clear value."

2007 (6ae18cf3bd4b): Cover title "Poised for Change." Core business language (operation, maintenance and management, OM&M) repeated; report described as covering corporate profile, 2007 activities/accomplishments, and "key initiatives for 2008."

2008 (ac4313fd330f): First appearance of client-count and tenure language: "OCWA has been providing clean water services across the province for over 15 years… serves over 180 clients, including municipalities, First Nations communities, institutions and businesses." Cover title: "Building trust."

2009 (a1d110500a36): Same "over 15 years / over 180 clients" framing retained. Cover title: "Working together, building trust."

2010 (762b0a9ff1d7): Same framing retained. Cover title: "Performance through accountability."

2011 (bfe42ee08529): Same framing retained. Cover title: "A trusted world leader in water" — the first appearance in the series of language that becomes OCWA's formal Vision statement (confirmed verbatim in the full-text 2017 and 2019 reports).

2013 (7df825f7cdd3): Mandate language shifts to closely track the statutory objects: OCWA's mandate is "to provide water, wastewater and other related services to their clients in a manner that protects human health and the environment and encourages the conservation of water resources." Client base described as "municipalities, First Nations communities, private sector companies and institutions," still "over 180 clients."

2014-2016 (26a9caf27d55; 5c63cf10fe5f; bd0728b8dbdb): A stable mission formula appears and repeats across all three years: "to demonstrate service excellence through the delivery of safe, reliable and cost-effective clean water" — matching the Mission statement later printed in full in the 2017 and 2019 reports. Reports in this period are described as covering organization, governance/accountability, "providing value to the people of Ontario," "focusing on efficiency," "making a difference," annual results, the board, officers, and financial statements.

2017 (042faff71350) — first full-text year: The Vision ("a trusted world leader in water"), Mission (service excellence through safe/reliable/cost-effective water), and a five-part TRUST values framework (Transparent, Respectful, Understanding, Safe, Teamwork) are stated explicitly. Nevin McKeown became President and CEO in 2017 (with OCWA since its 1993 inception). Total Operating Revenue reached $192.1 million, exceeding an "ambitious revenue target of $190 million"; net income was $5.8 million. The Agency describes itself as a "Total Water Solutions Provider" operating more than 800 facilities for over 180 clients, with the Region of Peel (population up to 1.4 million) as its largest client and the Region of Waterloo as its second-largest. A four-part strategy structure is tabulated for the first time: (1) Delivering Total Water Solutions, (2) Increased Productivity/Efficiency/Effectiveness, (3) Delivering Value to Communities and the Province, (4) Supporting Our Employees — each broken into named Strategic Initiatives, Goals, Performance Measures and Year-End Results, a reporting format retained in subsequent full-text years. The OCWA Tools Evolution Program (OTEP) substantially completed in 2017; the Board approved a follow-on Business Transformation Program (BTP) for 2018 implementation. Corporate head office: 1 Yonge Street, Suite 1700, Toronto, with additional Mississauga offices (Erin Mills; South Peel Facilities) and eight Regional Hub offices across Ontario. The Capital Investment Plan Act founding date is given precisely: November 15, 1993.

2019 (05e4355360e3): Revenue grew from $205 million (2018, cited narratively) to $213.6 million; net income $9.6 million, 12.5% ahead of an $8.53 million plan. The Ontario government "temporarily suspended" the mandatory mandate-letter requirement pending an "Agency Review Task Force" review of expectations for all provincial agencies. A multi-year Diversity and Inclusion Strategy was developed for phased implementation; a Climate Resiliency Strategy began development, drawing on newly-collected operational and external data. OCWA's First Nations strategy continued, with a First Nations Advisory Circle planned but its first meeting deferred to 2020. The Chair/CEO letter closes noting the just-emerging COVID-19 pandemic as a forward risk for 2020.

2020 (5a04806e5fa3): COVID-19 dominates the year. OCWA maintained full facility operations with "no workplace transmission," using remote monitoring and remote work. OCWA partnered with the Ministry of the Environment, Conservation and Parks on a wastewater-based COVID-19 surveillance pilot (detecting SARS-CoV-2 genetic signatures for early outbreak warning). Total Operating Revenues rose 4.3% to $222.639 million; net income reached $10.0 million, "well ahead of the plan." New clients: Township of Ramara, Haldimand County. In January 2019 OCWA had signed a 10-year lease (through June 2030) for a new head office at 2085 Hurontario Street, Mississauga; the physical relocation from Toronto was completed in August 2020. Climate Resiliency Strategy work continued as a named performance measure; support for Neskantaga First Nation's water issues continued.

2021 (31d072f678f1): The Office of the Auditor General of Ontario completed a Value-for-Money audit with positive findings, quoted directly: "The four and a half million Ontarians who rely on OCWA for clean water can be confident they are being well served." Total Operating Revenues reached $241.376 million; net income was $17.2 million, well above a $6.1 million plan, boosted by a one-time recovery of a loan to the New Tecumseth Improvement Society. The First Nations Advisory Circle was formally established (orientation session held; six self-identified First Nations members; reports to the Board via a First Nations Committee). The report explicitly ties OCWA's Emergency Response Team program to the 2000 Walkerton Inquiry: "Justice O'Connor recommended that OCWA be mandated to develop standby capabilities to support provincial water emergencies." COVID-19 wastewater surveillance expanded to 32 municipalities, covering "almost 80% of Ontario's population." A leadership transition was announced: Nevin McKeown (CEO since 2017) would retire in March 2022, succeeded by Alicia Fraser (with OCWA since 2016).

2022 (7281549b3ebc): Alicia Fraser became President and CEO in March 2022. The Board and Executive Management Team undertook "a comprehensive review of OCWA's overall strategic direction," beginning with a new Vision, Mission and Values approved during the year, forming the foundation for an updated Strategic Plan "being finalized for approval by the Board later in 2023" (the exact new wording is not quoted in the sections read — ⚠️ still being checked). Net income was $7.3 million, ahead of plan, on Total Operating Revenues of $260.684 million. A stand-alone First Nations Regional Hub business unit was established (previously integrated regionally). Staffing attraction/retention is named explicitly as "the Agency's top priority for 2023," reflecting post-pandemic labour-market pressure. OCWA also contributed to the province's first Provincial Climate Change Impact Assessment (PCCIA) and began implementing Value-for-Money audit action plans from the 2021 audit.

2024 (f9d00a4bad7d): A second leadership transition occurred: Iman Hashemi (President and CEO) and Debbie Korolnek (Board Chair) both moved into their roles in late 2023/early 2024. A new multi-year Strategic Plan, "Vision for a Sustainable Future," launched, organized around "TRUSTED" values and "five key areas of focus, or Strategic Directions" (people, best-in-class operations and digital solutions named as core areas; the full five are not enumerated in the sections read — ⚠️ still being checked). Total revenue reached $335.6 million (Total Operating Revenue +16.5% year over year), net income $11.8 million — matching the backgrounder and the largest revenue figure in the archived series. At the direction of the Minister of the Environment, Conservation and Parks, OCWA delivered feasibility/research work on a Lake Simcoe (Holland Marsh) Phosphorus Reduction Project, submitting a research study to the Minister in June 2024. A "Reconciliation roadmap"/Reconciliation Action Plan was developed in consultation with the First Nations Advisory Circle — the first explicit "Reconciliation" framing in the archived series. A new Human Resources Information System (HRIS) and Enterprise Resources Planning (ERP) system were implemented, and the Diversity and Inclusion Strategy was renamed/expanded into an "Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) Strategy and Blueprint."

Ontario/Toronto relevance

OCWA's founding-era documents give a Toronto publisher address ("[Toronto]: the Agency" / "Toronto, ON") across the earliest catalog records (oth-2000, 0b491dd78f17; and multiple ar-2006 through ar-2016 catalog entries, e.g. b6030fb392d0, ac4313fd330f, 26a9caf27d55). By 2017 the Agency's corporate head office was at 1 Yonge Street, Suite 1700, Toronto, alongside Mississauga offices at Erin Mills (Sheridan Centre) and South Peel Facilities, plus eight Regional Hub offices spanning the province (042faff71350). In January 2019 OCWA signed a 10-year lease for a new head office at 2085 Hurontario Street, Mississauga, and completed the relocation there in August 2020 (5a04806e5fa3) — meaning OCWA's headquarters moved out of the City of Toronto during the period covered by this series.

OCWA's largest single client throughout the full-text years is the Region of Peel (Mississauga, Brampton, Caledon) — part of the Greater Toronto Area, though outside the City of Toronto's own boundaries — described as serving a population "as large as 1.4 million" (042faff71350; 05e4355360e3; 31d072f678f1; 7281549b3ebc; f9d00a4bad7d). The Region of Peel's G.E. Booth Wastewater Treatment Facility (formerly the Lakeview Water Pollution Control Plant), operated by OCWA, is named as the career-origin site for CEO Nevin McKeown and as the site of an ash-recycling partnership with Brampton Brick (042faff71350; 31d072f678f1). The Region of Waterloo (Kitchener, Cambridge, Waterloo) is OCWA's second-largest client (042faff71350; f9d00a4bad7d) — outside the GTA. The 2024 Lake Simcoe (Holland Marsh) Phosphorus Reduction Project places OCWA in a Ministerially-directed role in the Lake Simcoe watershed, immediately north of the GTA (f9d00a4bad7d).

Board-member biographies reference City of Toronto public-service careers — e.g., Board Chair Joseph Pennachetti (Chair from February 2018) is described as a former City of Toronto Chief Financial Officer (2002) and City Manager (2008-2015) who authored the City's 2014-2018 strategic plan — but this is a governance-appointee biography, not evidence of OCWA facilities inside Toronto (042faff71350). Similarly, the Auditor General of Ontario's audit-report letterhead address (20 Dundas Street West, Toronto) recurs across multiple years' financial statements as the auditor's own office, not an OCWA site (042faff71350; 5a04806e5fa3; 7281549b3ebc; f9d00a4bad7d).
