Housing Services Corporation - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Wave 1 / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-housing-services-corporation (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Housing Services Corporation (HSC) is continued by s.121 of the Housing Services Act, 2011, S.O. 2011, c. 6, Sched. 1, which provides: "The Social Housing Services Corporation established under the former Act is continued as a corporation without share capital under the name Housing Services Corporation" (consolidated text verified at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/11h06, Part IX, s.121). Section 125 specifies the Corporation "is not an agent of the Crown for any purpose ... and it is not an administrative unit of the Government of Ontario" (same source, s.125), and s.126 exempts it from the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010 except as provided by regulation.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Section 122 sets HSC's statutory objects as improving the operation, efficiency and long-term sustainability of housing providers serving moderate- and low-income households; improving management and long-term viability of their physical assets; and improving quality of life for residents (Housing Services Act, 2011, s.122, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/11h06). Section 124 requires HSC to operate insurance programs, capital-reserve pooling schemes, joint natural-gas purchasing schemes, and research/advisory services for the Province, service managers and housing providers (same source, s.124). HSC's own materials describe serving 1,500 housing providers and 47 service managers (2025 Annual Report, https://hscorp.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2025-HSC-Annual-Report-Final-June-29-2026-.pdf; registry row on-housing-services-corporation).

Governance & reporting line

HSC's members are statutorily defined as all service managers, all local housing corporations, and prescribed non-profit and co-operative housing providers (Housing Services Act, 2011, s.127, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/11h06). Its board of directors is composed under s.128(2): two directors appointed by the Minister, one selected by the City of Toronto, others jointly selected by the regional municipalities of Durham, Halton, Peel and York, by DSSAB service managers, by service managers generally (four directors), and by non-profit and co-operative housing-provider members (three and two directors respectively) (same source, s.128).

Budget scale

HSC reported over $626 million in capital reserve investments under management (as at December 31, 2025), oversaw $48 billion in insured assets sector-wide, and managed roughly $13.3 million/year in natural gas purchasing on behalf of members (2025 Annual Report, https://hscorp.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2025-HSC-Annual-Report-Final-June-29-2026-.pdf). These are program/portfolio scale figures rather than HSC's own operating budget; ⚠️ VERIFY - HSC's own annual operating budget/revenue was not separately grounded this pass.

Institutional history

HSC continues the Social Housing Services Corporation, established in 2002 by the Province of Ontario under the former Social Housing Reform Act, 2000, and was renamed and continued as Housing Services Corporation under the Housing Services Act, 2011, S.O. 2011, c. 6, Sched. 1, s. 121 (2025 Annual Report, https://hscorp.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2025-HSC-Annual-Report-Final-June-29-2026-.pdf; https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/11h06). ⚠️ VERIFY - the "former Act" naming the pre-2011 statute was not independently confirmed against the primary statute text this pass.
