Canadian Human Rights Commission - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-canadian-human-rights-commission (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canadian Human Rights Commission (CHRC) describes itself on its own homepage as "Canada's national human rights institution" (https://www.chrc-ccdp.gc.ca/en, fetched this review). Its About page identifies the Canadian Human Rights Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. H-6, as its primary statutory authority, alongside roles under the Accessible Canada Act, the Employment Equity Act, the Pay Equity Act, and the National Housing Strategy Act; it holds international accreditation "as Canada's National Human Rights Institution" (https://www.chrc-ccdp.gc.ca/about-commission, fetched this review).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CHRC fulfills three core functions: advocacy (advising Parliament on human rights issues, conducting research, engaging international bodies, and monitoring Canada's compliance with UN human rights conventions); prevention (auditing federally regulated employers for employment equity compliance, supporting pay equity enforcement, and promoting accessibility standards); and dispute resolution (receiving discrimination complaints, facilitating mediation and conciliation, and referring cases to the independent Canadian Human Rights Tribunal when warranted) (https://www.chrc-ccdp.gc.ca/about-commission).

Governance & reporting line

The Commission is led by a Chief Commissioner; per a WebSearch summary of the Canadian Human Rights Act (ss. 26 and 30) and a Department of Justice appointment announcement, the Chief Commissioner is appointed by the Governor in Council on the recommendation of the Minister of Justice, for a term reported as five years (⚠️ still being checked - not independently confirmed by direct fetch of the statute text or CHRC's own governance pages this review; CHRC's "About the Commission" and "corporate-planning-and-reporting" pages, fetched directly this review, did not themselves state the appointment mechanism or term length). The Commission's departmental plans are published under the Minister of Justice's portfolio (CHRC Departmental Plan 2026-27, https://www.chrc-ccdp.gc.ca/resources/publications/chrc-2026-2027-departmental-plan, fetched this review).

Budget scale

Planned total spending for fiscal 2026-27 is $38,268,771 (including internal services), broken out as Engagement and Advocacy $6,029,311, Complaints $12,503,045, Proactive Compliance $8,307,970, and Internal Services $11,428,445 (CHRC Departmental Plan 2026-27). Planned full-time equivalents for 2026-27 total 267: Engagement and Advocacy 35, Complaints 85, Proactive Compliance 53, and Internal Services 94 (same source).

Institutional history

⚠️ still being checked - CHRC's founding year is commonly cited elsewhere as 1977 (concurrent with the Canadian Human Rights Act coming into force), but this was not confirmed against the Commission's own site text this review; CHRC's homepage and "About the Commission" page, both fetched directly this review, did not themselves state a founding year. Registry row fed-canadian-human-rights-commission shows an archived documentary record running from a 2000 annual report through the 2026-27 Departmental Plan, with reporting format shifting from "Annual Report" (through roughly 2012) to the "Departmental Plan" / "Departmental Results Report" format used government-wide from approximately 2020-21 onward.

Priority evolution

Departmental Plan / Results Report series, FY2020-21 to FY2026-27 (unchanged from v1.0)

FY2020-21 (dp-2021, 780f80f64e50 / drr-2021, 3b64323ca74a): dp-2021 marks the first year of a renamed core-responsibility structure: "Human Rights Complaints" becomes "Complaints" and "Employment Equity Audits" becomes "Proactive Compliance," explicitly to "reflect expanded mandate because of new legislation (Pay Equity Act and Accessible Canada Act)." Three brand-new mandates are named as arriving this cycle: the Pay Equity Act, Accessible Canada Act, and National Housing Strategy Act, framed as "a transformational shift in Canada's approach to human rights protections" — the Commission would chair the Council of Federal Accessibility Agencies, monitor Canada's UNCRPD implementation, and support a not-yet-appointed Federal Housing Advocate. drr-2021's results show these mandates still mostly in planning: the Housing Advocate remained unappointed all year (flagged as a materialized risk), and one employment-equity audit was delayed by COVID. Total planned spending 2020-21: $33,604,272; total planned FTE 256. Chief Commissioner Marie-Claude Landry, Ad.E.; Minister David Lametti.

FY2021-22 (dp-2022, f2ed3059b40b / drr-2022, 0c3d126cf414): The plan announces a new Proactive Compliance Branch, formally combining employment-equity, accessibility, and pay-equity work "under the same umbrella" — implemented during the year per drr-2022. The results report confirms Canada's first Federal Housing Advocate, Marie-Josée Houle, appointed February 2022 — resolving dp-2021's unappointed-risk flag. drr-2022 also states this is Landry's "last Departmental Results Report of my current seven-year term as Chief Commissioner" — the clearest advance signal of the leadership transition that follows. Total actual spending: $35,786,850.
