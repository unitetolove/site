Grain Financial Protection Board - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-grain-financial-protection-board (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Board is established under the Protecting Farmers from Non-Payment Act (Regulating Agricultural Product Dealers and Storage Operators), 2023, which regulates agricultural-product dealers and storage operators and replaces the older Grains Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. G.10 farm-payment-protection scheme (Act name confirmed via O. Reg. 377/25 "GRAIN," made under this Act, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/r25377, fetched and verified; and via the Board's own page, https://www.ontario.ca/page/grain-financial-protection-board-gfpb, fetched, which states the GFPB was "established under the Protecting Farmers from Non-Payment Act (PFFNPA)"). ⚠️ still being checked — the direct e-Laws consolidated-text URL for the 2023 Act itself was not located/resolved this review; the R.S.O. 1990, c. G.10 Grains Act page (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90g10, fetched, shows no repeal banner) and c. F.10 Farm Products Payments Act page (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90f10, fetched) were checked and ruled out as the current governing text.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Per s. 29 of the PFFNPA (as described on the Board's own page, ontario.ca/page/grain-financial-protection-board-gfpb, fetched), the Board manages and administers its protection fund(s), investigates and determines the validity of claims, approves/denies claims and sets payment amounts, and may pursue recovery action; it protects producers of grain corn, soybeans, wheat and canola against licensed-buyer non-payment, and protects grain owners using licensed storage facilities (same source; registry row on-grain-financial-protection-board).

Governance & reporting line

Board membership draws representatives from Grain Farmers of Ontario, the Ontario Canola Growers' Association, and the Ontario Agri Business Association, serving up to three-year renewable terms, meeting two to three times annually; composition is further set by O. Reg. 377/25 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/r25377, fetched). The Board is described as an agency of the Ontario Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness (ontario.ca/page/grain-financial-protection-board-gfpb, fetched).

Grain Financial Protection Board - strategy evolution

The biggest disclosed development is the same pending legislative transition affecting the Board's sister agency (the Livestock Financial Protection Board): the Protecting Farmers from Non-Payment Act, 2023, passed but not proclaimed, targeted for fall 2025, will eventually replace the Farm Products Payments Act framework the Board still operates under. The most load-bearing number is the combined balance of the Board's four commodity funds (grain corn, soybeans, canola, wheat) reaching $20.21 million, up over $0.98 million from the prior year, with all four funds meeting their actuarially recommended minimum targets and zero claims paid in the year. Budget & mandate inflection points

  • Pending Act proclamation — same statute and fall-2025 proclamation target as the Livestock Financial Protection Board; the Board continued operating under the Farm Products Payments Act (FPPA) throughout 2024-25 (2c6e231268a8).
  • Actuarial review commitment stalled by election — the Board committed to engaging a new actuary in 2024-25 (its last review was 2021-22, and interest rates/commodity prices had shifted materially since); it prepared a scope document late in the fiscal year, but the work was "put on hold" during the provincial election "writ" period when agencies were required to operate in a custodial manner (2c6e231268a8).
  • Unimplemented 2022 fee-increase recommendation — the 2021-22 actuarial review recommended raising check-off fees for grain corn (from $0.001 to $0.019 per tonne) and canola (from $0.20 to $0.417 per tonne) to address insufficient funding margins; the Board's December 2022 recommendation to the Minister remains unimplemented as of this report, addressed instead through the broader Act-driven program review; wheat and soybean fees were not recommended for change (2c6e231268a8).
  • Combined fund performance: overall four-fund balance grew from $19.22 million to $20.21 million (up $0.98M); by fund — grain corn $6.04M→$6.14M, soybeans $7.14M→$7.70M, canola $1.12M→$1.16M, wheat $4.91M→$5.20M; all four funds met their 2021-22-actuarially-set minimum target balances (2c6e231268a8).
  • Total revenue $1,557,497 (fees $594,101, investment income $963,396) vs. $1,349,939 in 2023-24; average return on investment 5.1% (up from 4.5%), though the report separately notes interest rates "declined 225 basis points throughout fiscal 2025," which will pressure future reinvestment yields (2c6e231268a8).
  • Total expenses $572,549 (financial responsibility review/licensing/enforcement $515,493; governance/secretariat and financial services combined ~$57M; legal/investigation $173) vs. $498,258 in 2023-24; zero claims paid (2c6e231268a8).
  • Governance change: in August 2024, an existing Board member was appointed Vice-Chair (a role vacant since February 2024) following a Public Appointments Secretariat advertisement; one other member was reappointed and one member's term expired during the year (2c6e231268a8).
  • AAD-mandated MOU renewal delayed by election writ period — same pattern as the Livestock board: a required MOU update missed its February 1, 2025 deadline due to the election's custodial period; the Board and Minister had already affirmed the existing (2017) MOU in April 2024 ahead of that separate requirement (2c6e231268a8).
