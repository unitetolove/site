Administrative Tribunals Support Service of Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-administrative-tribunals-support-service-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Administrative Tribunals Support Service of Canada (ATSSC) is established by the Administrative Tribunals Support Service of Canada Act, S.C. 2014, c. 20, s. 375, in force November 1, 2014 (laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/a-1.5/page-1.html, fetched this review). Section 3 of the Act provides: "The Administrative Tribunals Support Service of Canada, consisting of the Chief Administrator and employees of the Service, is established as a portion of the federal public administration." Section 10 sets the Service's core function as providing "the support services and the facilities that are needed by each of the administrative tribunals to exercise its powers and perform its duties and functions" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

ATSSC delivers both tribunal-specific specialized services (registry services, legal services, and mandate and member services) and internal services (human resources, financial services, information management and technology, accommodation, security, planning, and communications) to the federal administrative tribunals it supports, consolidating functions that were previously delivered by separate tribunal secretariats prior to the Service's 2014 creation (WebSearch summary of ATSSC's mandate, cross-referenced against the Act's section 10 language above). The Departmental Results Report for 2024-25 breaks ATSSC's core responsibility as "Support services and facilities to federal administrative tribunals and its members," reported separately from the department's internal services (2024-25 Departmental Results Report, canada.ca/en/administrative-tribunals-support-service/reports/drr-2024-2025.html, referenced via search-derived figures this review - see Budget scale below).

Governance & reporting line

The Chief Administrator is "the organization's chief executive officer" with "control and management of the Service and all matters connected with it" (Act s.9), is "appointed by the Governor in Council for up to five years" and "holds deputy head rank" (Act s.6), and may enter contracts and engage professional counsel to assist tribunals but "cannot exercise powers reserved to the tribunals themselves" (Act s.12) (laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/a-1.5/page-1.html, fetched this review). "The ATSSC reports to Parliament through the Minister of Justice and Attorney General of Canada" (WebSearch summary, cross-referenced against the Act's deputy-head structure and standard federal-agency reporting convention for portions of the core public administration).

Budget scale

For fiscal year 2024-25, ATSSC's Departmental Results Report shows actual spending of $60,153,920 for "Support services and facilities to federal administrative tribunals and its members" and $25,804,760 for internal services, with forecasted statutory spending of $12,997,869 and voted spending of $72,960,811 for a total of $85,958,680 (WebSearch-derived summary of canada.ca/en/administrative-tribunals-support-service/reports/drr-2024-2025.html, direct WebFetch of this page returned repeated 403 errors this review). ATSSC's 2026-27 Departmental Plan projects planned statutory spending of $12,495,034 and voted spending of $69,190,038 for a total of $81,685,072, with spending "expected to decrease in 2026-27 and the following years due to the implementation of the Comprehensive Expenditure Review announced in Budget 2025" (same search-derived summary of canada.ca/en/administrative-tribunals-support-service/reports/dp-2026-27.html). ⚠️ still being checked - these budget figures are drawn from a WebSearch-aggregated summary of the primary Canada.ca report pages, not a direct-fetch confirmation, because canada.ca returned 403/404 errors to direct WebFetch and DNS-level failures to curl throughout this review (consistent with the registry's own note: "initial tribunal.gc.ca fetch failed (DNS)").

Institutional history

ATSSC was established with the Administrative Tribunals Support Service of Canada Act coming into force on November 1, 2014, consolidating registry, legal, and administrative support that had previously been provided separately to numerous federal administrative tribunals into a single shared-services organization (WebSearch summary citing the Act's 2014 commencement and Department of Justice Canada's October 27, 2014 announcement of the Service's launch, canada.ca/en/news/archive/2014/10/government-canada-announces-launch-administrative-tribunals-support-service-canada.html, referenced via search this review but not independently fetched).

Administrative Tribunals Support Service of Canada - strategy evolution

2026-08-05 / registry: fed-administrative-tribunals-support-service-canada / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: Across a clean 21-document, eleven-year run (Departmental Plans 2017-18 through 2026-27; Departmental Performance/Results Reports 2014-15 through 2024-25), ATSSC's biggest addition is a twelfth supported tribunal added to its original roster of 11 sometime between 2020-21 and 2022-23 (drr-2021, 62df4ee73d4d, still says 11; drr-2023, 0b56c29d2b41, says 12) — the only expansion of the Service's founding scope anywhere in the series. The biggest inflection isn't a dropped priority but a reversed budget trajectory: total actual spending/staffing climbed steadily from $69,530,990 / 609 FTE at 2018-19 (drr-2019, c50c9b600c8f) to a peak of $85,958,680 / 717 FTE at 2024-25 (drr-2025, 5396c50fe6b8), then reverses to $79,351,598 / 661 planned FTE for 2026-27 (dp-2027, 93294ff9c625) under a newly-announced Comprehensive Expenditure Review (Budget 2025) explicitly targeting $5.2M/$6.9M/$10.3M in reductions across 2026-27/2027-28/2028-29 and ~28 FTE by 2028-29. The most load-bearing number is that same $10.3M 2028-29 CER reduction, layered on top of an already-implemented $4.2M Budget-2023 "Refocusing Government Spending" cut. One open question the series cannot resolve: whether the new organizational structure and reduced tribunal-facing services the CER prescribes will hold caseload-support quality steady for the (now 12) tribunals ATSSC serves, or whether this reverses the "strengthening overall capacity" rationale stated at the Service's November 2014 founding.

Backgrounder summary

ATSSC is established by the Administrative Tribunals Support Service of Canada Act, S.C. 2014, c. 20, s. 375, in force November 1, 2014, consolidating registry, legal, and administrative support previously delivered separately by numerous federal tribunal secretariats into one shared-services organization "as a portion of the federal public administration" (Act s.3). The Chief Administrator holds deputy-head rank, is appointed by the Governor in Council for up to five years, and has "control and management of the Service" but cannot exercise powers reserved to the tribunals themselves (Act ss.6, 9, 12). ATSSC reports to Parliament through the Minister of Justice. The backgrounder's budget figures (FY2024-25 actual $85,958,680; FY2026-27 planned $81,685,072, "expected to decrease... due to the implementation of the Comprehensive Expenditure Review announced in Budget 2025") are independently re-confirmed below against the extracted drr-2025 and dp-2027 text (relay + re-verify) — with one refinement: dp-2027's own stated 2026-27 planned total is $79,351,598 (including internal services), not the $81,685,072 the backgrounder cites from a WebSearch-derived summary; both are planned-spending figures for the same year from what should be the same primary source, so this brief flags the ~$2.3M gap as an ⚠️ still being checked rather than silently picking one.

Priority evolution

2014-15 (drr-2015, 759454eca793), founding year: "I am very pleased to present the first Departmental Performance Report for [ATSSC]," writes founding Chief Administrator Marie-France Pelletier. Established November 1, 2014, "the ATSSC's mandate is to provide support services and facilities to 11 administrative tribunals by way of a single, integrated organization," consolidating what had been separate tribunal secretariats to "strengthen[] overall capacity and modernize[] operations... to improve access to justice for Canadians." The report period (Nov 2014-March 2015) is explicitly framed as governance-building: "identifying and establishing governance structures, increasing the use of common processes and systems, standardizing internal service processes."

2015-17 (drr-2016, drr-2017, 1faedfa30cb0; 7a7e41d64eec): Continued consolidation-era framing under Pelletier; drr-2017 is the first report titled "Departmental Results Report" rather than "Departmental Performance Report" — the report-series rename year (see Priorities added/dropped below).

2017-18 (dp-2018, drr-2018, 8fd6060c2a1c; d64880f7733e): Minister of Justice is the Honourable Jody Wilson-Raybould throughout. Still "11 federal administrative tribunals."

2018-19 (dp-2019, drr-2019, 3b1896f42e73; c50c9b600c8f): Three named priority initiatives appear for this year specifically: "Service excellence and planning for the future," "case management systems and IM/IT solutions," and "building and sustaining a healthy and respectful workplace" — including a new case management system for the Canada Industrial Relations Board and the ATSSC's first client satisfaction survey of tribunal members. drr-2019's Chief Administrator's message is signed "Orlando Da Silva," but the same document's Organizational Profile table separately lists "Marie-France Pelletier, Chief Administrator" as institutional head — an internal inconsistency in the primary source itself, not a citation error (see Residuals & gaps); Minister of Justice is now David Lametti.

2019-22 (dp-2020/2021/2022, drr-2020/2021/2022): Orlando Da Silva signs as Chief Administrator throughout (confirmed drr-2020, drr-2022: 65ed7dd36edf, c022dd8ceec0); still "11 federal administrative tribunals" through drr-2021 (62df4ee73d4d, 2020-21).

2022-23 (dp-2023, drr-2023, 029232f53b88; 0b56c29d2b41): First appearance of "12 federal administrative tribunals" (0b56c29d2b41) — the roster expansion (see Priorities added/dropped). Minister of Justice transitions from Lametti to Arif Virani within this window.

2023-25 (dp-2024/2025/2026, drr-2024/2025): dp-2024 (78a173a38abb) itself contains both "12 federal administrative tribunals" and, elsewhere in the same document, "the 11 tribunals" — a second internal-document inconsistency, consistent with a genuine mid-series roster-count transition rather than a single clean cutover. Total actual spending/FTE peak at $85,958,680 / 717 FTE for 2024-25 (drr-2025, 5396c50fe6b8) — the series' high point on both dimensions.

2026-27 (dp-2027, 93294ff9c625): The Comprehensive Expenditure Review, announced in Budget 2025, is disclosed for the first time: planned reductions of $5,200,000 (2026-27), $6,900,000 (2027-28), and $10,300,000 (2028-29), "anticipated" to involve "a decrease of approximately 28 full-time equivalents by 2028-29," achieved via "a new organizational structure" and "a reduction in services provided by the ATSSC to the 12 tribunals and the National Joint Council." Total planned spending for 2026-27 drops to $79,351,598 / 661 FTE — the first year-over-year decline in either dimension anywhere in the readable series.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — a twelfth supported tribunal. ATSSC's founding scope was "11 administrative tribunals" (drr-2015 through drr-2021, confirmed as late as 2020-21). By drr-2023 (2022-23) the stated count is "12 federal administrative tribunals," and dp-2024 (2023-24) shows both counts within the same document — placing the actual transition somewhere in FY2021-22 or FY2022-23. No document names which tribunal was added or removed to produce the net change; that is a genuine gap in this series, not an omission by this brief (see Residuals & gaps).
  • Renamed — "Departmental Performance Report" → "Departmental Results Report." drr-2015 and drr-2016 use "Departmental Performance Report"; drr-2017 (2016-17) is the first "Departmental Results Report" in the series — a government-wide reporting-framework rename (consistent with the same rename seen elsewhere in this program, e.g., Statistics Canada's series), not an ATSSC-specific policy change.
  • Added — the Comprehensive Expenditure Review (Budget 2025), first disclosed in dp-2027 (2026-27), layered on top of the already-implemented Budget 2023 "Refocusing Government Spending" initiative (dp-2027, 93294ff9c625: "[2025-26] forecast spending... is lower than 2024-25 actual expenditures by $4.2 million, primarily due to the continued implementation of the Refocusing Government Spending initiative") — two distinct, sequential federal spending-restraint exercises now stacked on this Service, the same two-exercise pattern seen in other federal orgs' 2026-27 plans in this program (e.g., Statistics Canada, Shared Services Canada).
  • Nothing else identifiably dropped. The Service's founding three-function mandate (specialized/expert tribunal support; registry services; internal/corporate services) is restated consistently from drr-2015 through dp-2027 with no visible structural change beyond the tribunal-count and rename items above.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • Total actual spending/FTE trajectory: $69,530,990 / 609 FTE (2018-19, drr-2019, c50c9b600c8f) → rising through the series → $85,958,680 / 717 FTE (2024-25, drr-2025, 5396c50fe6b8, the series peak) → $79,351,598 / 661 FTE planned (2026-27, dp-2027, 93294ff9c625) — a ~7.6% planned spending decline and ~7.8% planned FTE decline from the 2024-25 peak, the first reversal anywhere in the readable window.
  • Two stacked federal restraint exercises: Budget 2023 "Refocusing Government Spending" (already reducing 2025-26 forecast spending by $4.2M vs. 2024-25 actual) and the newly-announced Budget 2025 Comprehensive Expenditure Review ($5.2M/$6.9M/$10.3M reductions across 2026-27/2027-28/2028-29, ~28 FTE by 2028-29) — both confirmed in dp-2027 (93294ff9c625).
  • Founding-to-peak growth: from a November 2014 start (11 tribunals, governance-building focus, no comparable-year spending figure cleanly isolable in drr-2015's table formatting — see Residuals) to the 2024-25 peak of $85,958,680/717 FTE — roughly a decade of sustained growth in both dollars and staff before the CER-driven 2026-27 reversal.
  • Leadership continuity with one transition: founding Chief Administrator Marie-France Pelletier (2014-15 through at least drr-2018, 2017-18) gives way to Orlando Da Silva, whose signed message first appears in drr-2019 (2018-19, alongside the internal Pelletier/Da Silva inconsistency noted above) and who continues signing through drr-2025 (2024-25) — one leadership transition across the whole eleven-year window, against four ministerial changes at the Minister of Justice portfolio level (Wilson-Raybould → Lametti → Virani → Sean Fraser, the last confirmed in dp-2027).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Indirect only. No document in this series names a Toronto- or Ontario-specific ATSSC office, program, or initiative; the Service is a national, Ottawa-headquartered shared-services organization supporting federal tribunals with national jurisdiction. One tribunal named in this series' 2018-19 narrative — the Canada Industrial Relations Board, which received a new case management system that year (drr-2019, c50c9b600c8f) — adjudicates federal labour matters that include Ontario- and Toronto-based federally-regulated employers and unions, but this is general national-jurisdiction relevance, not a documented Toronto-specific presence or program.
