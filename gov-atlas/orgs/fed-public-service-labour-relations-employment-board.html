Public Service Labour Relations and Employment Board - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Wave 1 / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-public-service-labour-relations-employment-board (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Continued under the Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board Act, S.C. 2013, c. 40, s. 365 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-33.35/index.html - link verified, title/citation confirmed). Section 4(1) explicitly continues "the former Public Service Labour Relations and Employment Board under its new name" - i.e., the registry's org name reflects the Board's predecessor title, and its current legal/operating name is the Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board (FPSLREB) (statute, full text at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-33.35/FullText.html; confirmed by the Board's own current annual report, https://www.fpslreb-crtespf.gc.ca/en/about-us/annual-reports/2024-2025/fpslreb-ar-2024-2025.html). The Board describes itself as "an independent, quasi-judicial statutory tribunal dedicated to fostering fair and harmonious labour relations and employment environments in the federal public sector" (2024-25 Annual Report, as above).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Resolves federal public sector workplace disputes under multiple statutes, including the Federal Public Sector Labour Relations Act, the Public Service Employment Act, and the Canadian Human Rights Act (2024-25 Annual Report, as above; statute, s.4 area, FullText.html).

Governance & reporting line

The Governor in Council appoints the Chairperson and Vice-Chairpersons on the Minister's recommendation, for full-time terms of up to five years, renewable, serving during good behaviour and removable only for cause (statute, s.8(2), FullText.html). Other members are appointed from a list the Chairperson prepares after consulting the employer and bargaining agents, aiming for balanced employer/employee-side representation (statute, s.6, FullText.html). In FY2024-25 the Board comprised 1 Chairperson (Edith Bramwell), 2 Vice-Chairperson positions (one vacant for six months during the year), 12 full-time members, and 14 part-time members (2024-25 Annual Report, as above). Under section 42, the Board reports to the responsible Minister as soon as feasible after each fiscal year-end, and the Minister tables the report in Parliament within the first 15 sitting days after receiving it (statute, FullText.html). Registry lists org_type as "board" with no listed parent (registry row, org_id fed-public-service-labour-relations-employment-board).

Backgrounder summary

The Board (FPSLREB, the current legal name; the registry's org name reflects a predecessor title carried forward by statute) is continued under the Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board Act, S.C. 2013, c. 40, s. 365, and describes itself as "an independent, quasi-judicial statutory tribunal dedicated to fostering fair and harmonious labour relations and employment environments in the federal public sector," resolving disputes under the Federal Public Sector Labour Relations Act, Public Service Employment Act, Canadian Human Rights Act, and other statutes. The Governor in Council appoints the Chairperson and Vice-Chairpersons; in FY2024-25 the Board comprised Chairperson Edith Bramwell, two Vice-Chairperson positions (one vacant six months), 12 full-time members, and 14 part-time members — all independently re-confirmed against the extracted ar-2025 text below (D-042: this brief relays, and separately re-verifies against primary text, rather than asserting independent discovery). The backgrounder flags budget scale as ⚠️ VERIFY, noting the Board publishes no departmental/strategic plan; the archived annual report read for this brief also discloses no aggregate dollar budget figure, so that gap remains unresolved (see Residuals & gaps).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • Second consecutive year of positive clearance rate: 2,674 files closed versus 2,468 received in 2024-25 (net inventory reduction of 206), continuing a pattern the report states was also achieved in 2023-24 (6bf3e2edc273).
  • Staffing complaints intake overtook closures for the first time in 8 years: 448 new staffing complaints received in 2024-25 (up from 378 in 2023-24) against 418 closed, which the report frames as a "significant shift" the Board will focus on "as it seeks to re-establish an equilibrium" (6bf3e2edc273).
  • File-age reduction: average age of active labour-relations files fell from 31 to 29 months year-over-year; overall average age of active-and-inactive files fell from 44 months (2023-24) to 34 months (2024-25), attributed partly to "a large withdrawal closure of inactive files" (6bf3e2edc273).
  • Hearing volume sustained above 700/year for the second consecutive year (737 scheduled in 2024-25: 626 under FPSLRA, 111 under PSEA), a threshold the report says "had never been reached before" prior to 2023-24 (6bf3e2edc273).
  • Technology/infrastructure investment: modernized hearing rooms in the National Capital Region with hybrid audio-visual systems, and an upgraded electronic documents portal for secure document exchange (6bf3e2edc273).
  • COVID-19-vaccination-policy grievance inventory continued winding down: 96 new files received in 2024-25 (down from 239 in 2023-24 and a 2022-23 peak of 696), with 1,314 still in inventory and many held in abeyance pending a named Federal Court decision (6bf3e2edc273).
  • Phoenix pay-system grievance inventory remained substantial: 590 files at fiscal year-end, with a new expedited adjudication process launched for Phoenix claims specifically (15 filed, 4 closed in 2024-25) (6bf3e2edc273).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Indirect only. The Board's main offices are stated to be on "the traditional, unceded territory of the Algonquin Anishinaabe Nation" (i.e., the National Capital Region, Ottawa-Gatineau), and the report describes its jurisdiction as extending "across Canada" over the federal public sector generally, with no Ontario- or Toronto-specific office, program, or initiative named anywhere in the text (6bf3e2edc273). Federal public servants located in Ontario/Toronto would fall within the Board's national jurisdiction as a matter of course, but this document establishes no documented Toronto-specific presence or activity.
