Development Review - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Batch 12 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: tor-development-review (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Development Review is an operating division of the City of Toronto, exercising the City's broad by-law and administrative powers under the City of Toronto Act, 2006, S.O. 2006, c. 11, Sched. A. Its own function -- reviewing development applications (Official Plan Amendment, Zoning By-law Amendment, Site Plan Amendment, Committee of Adjustment minor variance/consent, Plan of Condominium, and Toronto Local Appeal Body matters) -- is exercised under Ontario's Planning Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.13, the province's general land-use-planning statute. ⚠️ VERIFY: the Planning Act is not itself named in either archived budget-book document read for this brief; it is cited here as the well-established Ontario statute governing the specific application types the division's own text does name -- OPA, ZBA, SPA, Committee of Adjustment, TLAB (tor-development-review-bud-2025, eebe69d4c5a5; tor-development-review-bud-2026, 1ca3c7f6ddc5).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

A new division established in early 2024 (Division Head hired April 2024) to consolidate development-application review previously split across three other City divisions: Community Planning and Business Performance/Standards functions (202 positions from City Planning), Development Engineering (85 positions from Engineering and Construction Services), and Transportation Review (63 positions from Transportation Services) -- 350 positions total, transferred through 2024-2025 (eebe69d4c5a5; 1ca3c7f6ddc5). Delivers "a transparent, efficient and people-centred approach to reviewing all development applications," including a Priority Development Review Stream for affordable-housing and other priority projects (eebe69d4c5a5; 1ca3c7f6ddc5). Released its first Strategic Plan, "Development Review 2025-2028," developed with input from 300 staff and internal/external partners (1ca3c7f6ddc5). The 2026 budget book additionally names a planned further transfer-in of the Parks Development team (from Parks and Recreation) and the Committee of Adjustment section (from City Planning) (1ca3c7f6ddc5).

Budget scale

As originally published, the 2025 Program Summary states a 2025 budget of $9.676M gross/revenue, $0 net, 50.0 approved positions -- described in its own text as covering only "the Concept 2 Keys unit that was transferred from the City Manager's Office in April 2024," with the document itself flagging that "budget adjustments reflecting the [350-position] complement realignment will follow in 2025" (eebe69d4c5a5). Consistent with that, the 2026 Program Summary's own retrospective "2025 Budget" column shows a materially larger, realigned figure: $54.592M gross, $53.482M revenue, $1.110M net, 399.0 positions (1ca3c7f6ddc5) -- a ~349-position gap from the originally-published 50.0, almost exactly matching the 350-position transfer named above. Against that realigned baseline, the 2026 Budget of $58.317M gross / $56.029M revenue / $2.288M net / 397.0 positions represents a modest, normal-scale +6.8% gross / -0.5% headcount year-over-year change (1ca3c7f6ddc5). See the accompanying brief's Budget & mandate inflection points section for the full reconciliation.

Institutional history

Division created in early 2024, per its own account, "to streamline decision-making; improve consistency, transparency and accountability; improve service delivery for applicants and City staff; and ultimately, accelerate the City's development application review process" (eebe69d4c5a5). No division of this exact name and scope existed before 2024; its functions were previously distributed across City Planning, Engineering and Construction Services, and Transportation Services (same source; see also this batch's tor-toronto-building and tor-engineering-construction-services backgrounders/briefs for the same 2024-2025 reorganization from the contributing divisions' own side). Priority evolution

Two documents one year apart, the earlier describing a division that had existed for well under a year, cannot show priority evolution in the ordinary sense -- the dominant change between them is structural absorption, not a shift in stated intent. What they do establish: Development Review's core commitment -- "a transparent, efficient and people-centred approach to reviewing all development applications," with a Priority Development Review Stream prioritizing affordable housing -- is named in both years in materially the same language (eebe69d4c5a5; 1ca3c7f6ddc5).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added -- a named Strategic Plan: no strategic plan is referenced in the 2025 book; the 2026 book announces the division's "inaugural Strategic Plan (2025-2028)," developed with input from 300 staff and partners, organized around three headline themes ("A well-run City," "Maintain and create housing that's affordable," "Invest in people and neighbourhoods") that structure the entire 2026 Priority Actions section (1ca3c7f6ddc5).
  • Added -- a named Equity and Reconciliation function: 2026 commits to "Establish a dedicated Equity and Reconciliation function within the division" and a "human rights-based approach to development review" -- language absent from the 2025 document, which reported "No Significant Equity Impacts" for both City Planning and Development Review that year (eebe69d4c5a5; 1ca3c7f6ddc5).
  • Added -- further absorption planned: 2026 names an intent to transfer in the Parks Development team (from Parks and Recreation) and the Committee of Adjustment section (from City Planning) "to streamline decision-making" -- a continuation of the same consolidation logic that created the division in 2024, not yet reflected in either year's budget numbers (1ca3c7f6ddc5).
  • Structural, not renamed: the 2025 book's City Planning "Development Review, Decision and Implementation" service line (explicitly noted in that same document as partially moving to Development Review in 2025) is fully absorbed into the single "Development Review" service line by 2026 -- the 2025 document itself flags this as a known, planned transition rather than an unexplained change (eebe69d4c5a5; 1ca3c7f6ddc5).
