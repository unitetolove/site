Commissioner for Federal Judicial Affairs Canada (Office of the) - backgrounder

Mandate & statutory basis

The Office of the Commissioner for Federal Judicial Affairs (FJA) was established under the Judges Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. J-1, which defines its authority and responsibilities (FJA Departmental Plan 2024-2025, https://www.fja.gc.ca/publications/dp-pm/2024-2025/dp-pm-2024-2025_eng.html, fetched this review).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The FJA provides services to approximately 1,200 federally appointed judges and 1,100 pensioners/survivors, including administering judicial appointments and Judicial Advisory Committees, supporting the Supreme Court of Canada appointments process, providing judges' official-language training, publishing Federal Court of Appeal and Federal Court decisions, processing judicial compensation, allowances and pension benefits, managing international judicial cooperation initiatives, and supporting the Canadian Judicial Council's conduct-review process (FJA Departmental Plan 2024-2025, https://www.fja.gc.ca/publications/dp-pm/2024-2025/dp-pm-2024-2025_eng.html).

Governance & reporting line

The Commissioner reports within the ministerial portfolio of the Minister of Justice and Attorney General of Canada (FJA Departmental Plan 2024-2025, https://www.fja.gc.ca/publications/dp-pm/2024-2025/dp-pm-2024-2025_eng.html).

Budget scale

Total planned spending for 2024-25 was $788,800,057, comprising $788,073,257 for core responsibilities and $726,800 for internal services, against a planned complement of 66 full-time equivalents (FJA Departmental Plan 2024-2025, https://www.fja.gc.ca/publications/dp-pm/2024-2025/dp-pm-2024-2025_eng.html). This total is driven overwhelmingly by statutory judicial salaries, allowances and pension payments administered by the FJA on behalf of the federally appointed judiciary rather than by the office's own operating costs - ⚠️ still being checked the FJA's own internal operating budget separate from these statutory pass-through payments, not isolated this review.

Institutional history

The Office of the Commissioner for Federal Judicial Affairs was established in 1978 (FJA Departmental Plan 2024-2025, https://www.fja.gc.ca/publications/dp-pm/2024-2025/dp-pm-2024-2025_eng.html).

Priority evolution

FY2014-15 (dp-2015, 86801fcd0853): Under Minister Peter MacKay and Commissioner William A. Brooks, FJA's four named "Organizational Priorities" were an improved financial control framework (new), improved performance reporting, HR/succession planning (new), and information management. Named international-cooperation projects were country-specific: "Judicial Education for Economic Growth" in Ukraine and "Justice Undertakings for Social Transformation" in Jamaica, both DFATD-funded. Planned spending: $511,708,846 (2014-15), rising to $538,348,975 by 2016-17.

FY2016-17 (dp-2017, 0eacdacd8abb): Priorities carry forward (control framework, succession planning, information management) plus a new fourth priority, "Modernization of FJA processes and tools," a multi-year plan to overhaul JUDICOM and internal web applications. The program itself is renamed "Federal Judicial Affairs" (from "Office of the Commissioner for FJA Canada"). Named country projects from dp-2015 disappear, replaced by generic "DFATD-funded international projects" language. Minister changes to Jody Wilson-Raybould; Commissioner still Brooks. Planned spending: $555,174,253.

FY2016-17 results (drr-2017, 188f57fa62f5 — NEW this revision, omitted from v1.0): Commissioner has already changed to Marc A. Giroux by this report (published 2017-11-09) — one document earlier than the v1.0 brief tracked, which first noticed the change at drr-2018. dp-2017's plan (published under Brooks) and drr-2017's results (signed by Giroux) bracket the transition. The report also shows FJA "given the responsibility of providing support and services to the new and independent Advisory Board" for Supreme Court of Canada appointments already active in FY2016-17 — its first documented use, for the Justice Malcolm Rowe appointment — one year earlier than the v1.0 brief's account, which credited this milestone to drr-2018. On the international file, FJA names continuing work on "Judicial Education for Economic Growth" in Ukraine and reports it "signed an Administrative Arrangement with Global Affairs Canada on a new 5-year judicial reform project" in Ukraine (⚠️ Still being checked: this brief infers this is the same project drr-2018 later names the "Support to Judicial Reform in Ukraine Project," SJRP — both are 5-year, Global Affairs Canada-funded, Ukraine-focused, but drr-2017 does not itself use the SJRP name; see Residuals). FJA served 1,156 judges and 995 pensioners/survivors — an intermediate point between dp-2015's ~1,100 and drr-2018's "more than 1,200." The Canadian Judicial Council's conduct-review secretariat is quantified for the first time in this readable set: 404 pieces of correspondence, 352 opened files, 326 processed and closed, institutionally reported (no complainant or subject judge named). GCDOCS electronic-records migration is reported finalized. Actual spending $546,796,447 (against $555,174,253 planned in dp-2017); actual FTE 63; Internal Services still exactly $726,800; the Program Alignment Architecture still lists "Judicial Compensation and Benefits Commission" as sub-program 1.3.5.

FY2017-18 results (drr-2018, eda4125b2e38): Two mandate items land or recur: a second round of Independent Advisory Board support, this one explicitly tied to the vacancy created by Chief Justice Beverley McLachlin's December 2017 retirement (the Board mechanism itself was not newly formed here — it was already FJA-supported the prior year for the Rowe appointment, per drr-2017 above); and a genuinely new item, support for a new Associate Judges of the Federal Court appointment process. FJA reports serving "more than 1,200" judges for the first time (up from 1,156 in drr-2017). Actual spending $568,449,931; actual FTE 64.

FY2018-19 (dp-2019, 5d7a5db4c868): First document under the government-wide "Core Responsibility" reporting model (replacing Program Alignment Architecture), naming "Administrative support to federally appointed judges" as FJA's single core responsibility. Named priorities: the August 2016 SCC open-appointment-process reform (dating the mechanism drr-2017 shows already active); a bilingual-capacity assessment tool for judicial candidates; proactive expense disclosure ahead of Bill C-58; and — first appearance in the series — Bill C-337, the "Judicial Accountability through Sexual Assault Law Training Act," a private member's bill that would require judges and candidates to complete sexual-assault-law education. First explicit diversity/merit framing tied to appointments ("gender parity," Indigenous/minority representation). New Commissioner Marc A. Giroux confirmed continuing (replacing Brooks, transition dated to FY2016-17 per drr-2017 above). One indirect Ontario mention: an MOU with the Superior Court of Ontario for vetting visiting international delegations.

FY2019-20 (dp-2020, 6a9b3ac9e14b): Core Responsibility formally renamed "Support to federally appointed judges" (from "Administrative support to..."), explicitly justified as better reflecting the office's wider mandate. All 17 Judicial Advisory Committees due to turn over in 2019 as members' terms expire. Minister changes to David Lametti.

FY2020-21 plan (dp-2021, 8df8f52e1eac) and results (drr-2021, 6fdf8a4aca54): The plan (written before pandemic operational impact) names Bill C-58 compliance (quarterly judges'-expense publication from July 30, 2020) and full deployment of the bilingual-capacity assessment tool. The results report is the series' COVID document: FJA "leveraged technology to work remotely," began a cloud migration ("the journey to a Cloud platform was initiated"), ran virtual Judicial Advisory Committee meetings and a virtual language-immersion session in January 2021 after cancelling in-person sessions, and the CJC Chairperson co-chaired an "Action Committee on Court Operations in response to COVID-19" with the Minister of Justice. Actual spending came in $28.5M under plan ($626.4M vs $654.9M), attributed to collapsed travel/conference costs. A new SCC appointment process launched February 19, 2021 to replace the retiring Justice Rosalie Silberman Abella.

FY2022-23 results (drr-2023, 5044afd74921): Explicit "post-pandemic recovery" framing and a new hybrid-work strategy. The judges'-allowance 10-day service standard collapses to 43% (from 76% the prior year, against a 90% target), attributed to claim-volume growth amid "dual digital and paper environments." TAP is named for the first time as a pilot, in Colombia and Mongolia. CJC begins preparing for Bill C-9. The JUDICOM-to-JUDICLOUD cloud-platform transition is prioritized. Minister changes to Arif Virani. Actual spending $712,161,752.

FY2023-24 results (drr-2024, 0d0ffb14b631): Bill C-9 receives Royal Assent (June 22, 2023), reforming the judicial conduct-review regime; the year is explicitly described as "transitional," with old- and new-regime complaint files running in parallel and six-month-closure statistics unavailable for comparison. TAP formalizes into a five-year program (Moldova 2022-25, Mongolia 2021-24, South Africa 2023-24 all named), with Mongolia's engagement credited with producing "the first ever Mongolian Ethics Guide for Judges" (bounded to this document; no later document revisits the claim). JUDICLOUD's pilot launches fall 2023 and officially goes live March 2024. The 10-day allowance-processing metric recovers to 80% despite 4,000 more claims than the prior year. Actual spending $751,993,164 — a $40.4M year-over-year jump, the largest single-year increase in the series.

FY2024-25 (dp-2025, db9d83262882): Bill C-9's first full statutory reporting period. Named priorities: implementing the new conduct regime; migrating legacy systems to the cloud; expanding TAP (Lithuania named as a 2024 visit); and workplace training/inclusion. A new self-identification form is introduced covering gender, 2SLGBTQIA+, disability, and racial/ethnic-origin categories, alongside a 5% mandatory minimum Indigenous-procurement target. Planned spending $788,800,057; planned FTE 66 (unchanged since 2014-15).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2024-25 planned spending $788,800,057 vs 2014-15 planned spending $511,708,846 (db9d83262882; 86801fcd0853) — a ~54% decade-long rise, repeatedly attributed across every document in the set to statutory judges'-salary indexation (the Industrial Aggregate) and a growing pensioner population, not to FJA's own operating costs. FY2016-17 actual spending of $546,796,447 (drr-2017, 188f57fa62f5) sits early in this rise, already above the FY2014-15 planned baseline.
  • Internal Services budget: exactly $726,800 in every single document from dp-2015 through dp-2025, drr-2017 included (86801fcd0853 through db9d83262882; confirmed again in 188f57fa62f5) — an unbroken decade-long flat line, never once adjusted.
  • Bill C-9 (Royal Assent June 22, 2023) — the series' clearest statutory inflection point, reforming judicial conduct review; drr-2024 explicitly frames FY2023-24 as transitional, with pre- and post-Bill-C-9 complaint regimes running in parallel (0d0ffb14b631).
  • COVID-19 (drr-2021, 6fdf8a4aca54) — $28.5M underspend against plan, cloud-migration initiation, and a joint FJA/CJC/Minister of Justice pandemic court-operations task force.
  • Service-standard decline and partial recovery: judges'-allowance 10-day processing standard 83% (2020-21) → 76% (2021-22) → 43% (2022-23, drr-2023) → 80% (2023-24, drr-2024), against a 90% target throughout — the sharpest tabulated performance swing in the record.
  • Total FTE stable at 66 planned across the entire 2014-15 to 2026-27 span (86801fcd0853 through db9d83262882) even as spending grew by more than half; FY2016-17 actual FTE was 63 (drr-2017, 188f57fa62f5), FY2017-18 actual 64 (drr-2018) — both slightly under the 66 planned figure.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

FJA is a national, Ottawa-based federal body administering payments and services to judges across Canada; the readable record shows no Toronto- or GTA-specific programming, office, or initiative. The one recurring, indirect mention is an institutional MOU between FJA's International Programs Division and the Superior Court of Ontario for protocol liaison support to visiting international judicial delegations, appearing as early as drr-2017 (FY2016-17, 188f57fa62f5 — where FJA credits the MOU with vetting visiting delegations from Mongolia, Latvia and China) and recurring in dp-2019, dp-2020, and drr-2018 (5d7a5db4c868; 6a9b3ac9e14b; eda4125b2e38) — an Ontario-institution reference, not a Toronto-specific program or presence.
