Natural Resources Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-natural-resources-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) is established under s.3(1) of the Department of Natural Resources Act, S.C. 1994, c. 41: "There is hereby established a department of the Government of Canada called the Department of Natural Resources" (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/N-20.8/FullText.html, fetched and verified; registry row fed-natural-resources-canada).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Section 5 extends the Minister's authority to "natural resources," explosives, and technical surveys; s.6 requires the Minister to have regard to sustainable development, coordinate related federal policy, promote Canadian scientific capability, and disseminate resource-related information (same source). NRCan's own site groups its work into energy (oil, gas, renewables, electricity, nuclear/uranium), minerals and mining, forestry (including wildland fire), and natural-resource science/geospatial data (https://natural-resources.canada.ca/, fetched).

Governance & reporting line

A federal department presided over by the Minister of Energy and Natural Resources (currently Tim Hodgson), with a Deputy Minister as deputy head (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/N-20.8/FullText.html, fetched; https://natural-resources.canada.ca/, fetched).

Budget scale

~$4.60B total planned expenses for 2026-27 ($4,595,148 thousand), split across Innovative and Sustainable Natural Resources Development ($1,488.1M), Globally Competitive Natural Resource Sectors ($2,041.2M), Natural Resource Science and Risk Mitigation ($846.7M) and Internal Services ($219.0M); NRCan also has planned Comprehensive Expenditure Review reductions of $266.4M in 2026-27 rising to $557.9M by 2028-29, per Natural Resources Canada's 2026-27 Departmental Plan (registry strategy_current_url: https://natural-resources.canada.ca/corporate/planning-reporting/departmental-plan/nrcan-2026-27-departmental-plan).

Institutional history

Created January 12, 1995 by the Department of Natural Resources Act (S.C. 1994, c. 41), which abolished the separate Minister of Energy, Mines and Resources and Minister of Forestry portfolios and merged their departments into a single Department of Natural Resources (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/N-20.8/FullText.html, fetched; WebSearch corroboration of the 1995 in-force/portfolio-merger date, The Canadian Encyclopedia entry "Department of Natural Resources").

Backgrounder summary

Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) is established under s.3(1) of the Department of Natural Resources Act, S.C. 1994, c. 41. Section 5 extends the Minister's authority to "natural resources," explosives, and technical surveys; s.6 requires regard to sustainable development, coordination of related federal policy, promotion of Canadian scientific capability, and dissemination of resource-related information. NRCan groups its work into energy (oil, gas, renewables, electricity, nuclear/uranium), minerals and mining, forestry (including wildland fire), and natural-resource science/geospatial data. It is a federal department presided over by the Minister of Energy and Natural Resources (currently Tim Hodgson), with a Deputy Minister as deputy head. Budget scale per the backgrounder: ~$4.60B total planned expenses for 2026-27 ($4,595,148 thousand), split across Innovative and Sustainable Natural Resources Development ($1,488.1M), Globally Competitive Natural Resource Sectors ($2,041.2M), Natural Resource Science and Risk Mitigation ($846.7M) and Internal Services ($219.0M), plus planned Comprehensive Expenditure Review reductions of $266.4M in 2026-27 rising to $557.9M by 2028-29. NRCan was created January 12, 1995 by the Department of Natural Resources Act, merging the former Energy, Mines and Resources and Forestry portfolios into a single department.

Priority evolution

FY2016-17 baseline (fs-2017, e313617387aa): The earliest real-content document in the series is a consolidated financial statement, not a plan. Total departmental expenses were $1,349,957 thousand (~$1.35B) actual against $1,637,636 thousand planned, versus $1,243,617 thousand (~$1.24B) the prior year — the archived series' only visibility into pre-2020 NRCan activity is through this financial-statement lineage (fs-2017 through fs-2025), not through any plan or results-report narrative (those are all landing-page stubs before FY2023-24).

FY2023-24 (drr-2024, 951ac75703d9): The earliest real-content plan-cycle narrative. NRCan organized its work under three Core Responsibilities (Natural Resource Science and Risk Mitigation; Innovative and Sustainable Natural Resources Development; Globally Competitive Natural Resource Sectors) and eight named priorities: three "Implementation" priorities (Climate Mitigation and Economic Transformation; Energy and Mineral Security: Oil and gas pathways and critical minerals; Adaptation, Emergency Preparedness, and Nature-Based Solutions), three further items (Future of #OurNRCan; Employee Wellness and Wellbeing; Digital Transformation), and two horizontal priorities (IDEA; Reconciliation). Actual spending was $3,466,610,323 (FTE 5,499). Under the Budget 2023 "Refocusing Government Spending" exercise ($14.1B government-wide over five years, $4.1B/year after), NRCan reported achieving an $8.7 million reduction in 2023-24. Landmark activity: 553 million trees planted (of a 2-billion 10-year goal), the Wildfire Resilient Futures Initiative and FireSmart Canada expansion, launch of the $1.5B Critical Minerals Infrastructure Fund, and the Canada-Germany Hydrogen Alliance MOU.

FY2024-25 planning (dp-2025, a872fa789cbb): The Implementation-priority set is retained with the same three items (Climate Mitigation and Economic Transformation; Energy and Mineral Security; Adaptation, Emergency Preparedness, and Nature-Based Solutions), but the Organizational side is restructured into three items: "#OurNRCan, Modernization and Digital Transformation" (merging the prior year's separate "Future of #OurNRCan" and "Digital Transformation" items), "Our People's Wellness and Wellbeing," and a newly named "Our Resources, Stewardship and Management." Refocusing Government Spending reductions are now disclosed on a forward schedule: $58,388,000 (2024-25), $86,998,000 (2025-26), $126,157,000 (2026-27 and after). Planned three-year spending by core responsibility shows Globally Competitive Natural Resource Sectors and Innovative and Sustainable Natural Resources Development both declining year-over-year from 2024-25 to 2026-27, while total planned FTEs also decline (from 618 to 533 in Globally Competitive Natural Resource Sectors alone).

FY2024-25 results (drr-2025, 90ec55b7da48): The full (non-abridged) report — the only full DRR in the archived series. The Minister's message is signed "The Honourable Tim Hodgson, P.C., M.P.," who explicitly credits "the leadership of my predecessor, the Honourable Jonathan Wilkinson" for the year's progress — the only ministerial-transition marker in the archived text. Total actual spending reached $4,419,629,197 (FTE 5,887), a $953M (27%) year-over-year increase, driven by $610M in incremental grants/contributions (Greener Homes, Electricity Pre-Development, 2 Billion Trees, Energy Efficiency) and $348M more in Newfoundland Offshore Petroleum Resource Revenue Fund statutory flow-through (Hibernia/Hebron production). Landmark events: Parliament's Royal Assent of Bill C-49 (modernizing the Nova Scotia and Newfoundland and Labrador offshore energy regimes), a $200M federal contribution to the Cedar LNG project via the Strategic Response Fund, enactment of the Canadian Sustainable Jobs Act with a new Sustainable Jobs Partnership Council and Secretariat, release of Canada's first Clean Electricity Strategy, and $764M invested across 113 projects under the Canadian Critical Minerals Strategy (production up 15% in the strategy's first year). Institutional head (Deputy Minister): Michael Vandergrift. Ministerial portfolio still lists "Canada-Newfoundland and Labrador Offshore Petroleum Board" and "Canada-Nova Scotia Offshore Petroleum Board" (pre-rename), and the enabling-instruments list is the short three-act form (Department of Natural Resources Act; Resources and Technical Surveys Act; Forestry Act).

FY2025-26 planning (dp-2026, 4e6de5aee0c5): An abridged "at a Glance" capture. The priority framework is relabeled again: "Strategic" priorities (renamed from "Implementation") now number four — Clean Energy & Climate Mitigation (renamed from Climate Mitigation and Economic Transformation), Energy & Mineral Security, Adaptation, Emergency Preparedness, and Nature-Based Solutions, and a newly added "Competitiveness" — alongside three "Operational" priorities (renamed from "Organizational"): Our People's Wellness and Wellbeing, Our Resources, Stewardship & Management, and "Modernization & Digital Transformation" (the "#OurNRCan" branding is dropped). Horizontal Reconciliation and IDEA are retained. Total planned spending (including internal services) is $5,132,577,391 with 6,017 planned FTEs — this abridged capture contains no "Refocusing Government Spending" or comparable spending-reduction disclosure (a grep of the full text confirms no match), though this may reflect the abridged format rather than an actual policy discontinuation; the full 2025-26 plan is not in the archived series.

FY2026-27 planning (dp-2027, a16744fb4e07): The largest and most recent capture, and the only other full (non-abridged) plan in the series. The entire Strategic/Operational/Horizontal priority architecture is replaced with three unstructured "key priorities": Accelerate Major Projects; Advance Critical Minerals and Energy Interests; Diversify Canada's Trade Markets. Signed by "The Honourable Tim Hodgson P.C., M.P." Institutional head (Deputy Minister): Greg Orencsak — a change from Michael Vandergrift (last seen in drr-2025 and fs-2025). Ministerial portfolio now reads "Canada-Newfoundland and Labrador Offshore Energy Regulator" and "Canada-Nova Scotia Offshore Energy Regulator" (renamed from "...Offshore Petroleum Board"), and the enabling-instruments list grows to five acts, adding the Canada-Newfoundland and Labrador Atlantic Accord and Offshore Renewable Energy Management Act and the Canada-Nova Scotia Petroleum Resources Accord Implementation and Offshore Renewable Energy Management Act — consistent with Bill C-49's offshore-renewables mandate expansion reported in drr-2025. A new "Comprehensive Expenditure Review" replaces (in scale, if not explicitly by name) the earlier Refocusing Government Spending exercise: reductions of $266,428,023 (2026-27), $431,717,521 (2027-28), and $557,871,364 (2028-29), with an anticipated ~807-FTE decrease by 2028-29. Total planned spending (incl. internal services) for 2026-27 is $4,878,808,102 (expenditure basis) / $4,595,148,703 (accrual basis, Table 16) with 5,401 planned FTEs, falling to $3,679,912,696 by 2028-29. A new AI Strategy (four pillars: governance, data/platform foundations, tools/solutions, change management) includes a planned AI Oversight Board and GC AI Register participation. Core Responsibility 3's results framework is consolidated from three departmental results to two ("refocus the story on competitiveness," per the document's own change note), and Core Responsibility 2's program inventory replaces "Sustainable Forest Management, Fibre Solutions and Cumulative Effects" with "Innovative Forestry Solutions" and "Integrated Landscape Dynamics."

External accountability check (oth-2024, 95139e2a46a2): The Office of the Auditor General / Commissioner of the Environment and Sustainable Development's Report 6 (tabled 7 November 2024) audited two of the Canadian Critical Minerals Strategy's five objectives (advancing reconciliation with Indigenous peoples; promoting climate action and environmental protection) and found NRCan "did not have strong enough governance or robust risk analysis, nor did the department sufficiently assess the environmental and climate impacts" of the strategy. The 2022 federal budget had allocated up to $3.8 billion over eight years to the strategy. The audit found Indigenous peoples were consulted during the strategy's development but that ongoing-engagement planning had "just begun," and recommended continuous environmental-risk assessment and better integration of Indigenous priorities into the Critical Minerals Geoscience and Data Initiative.
