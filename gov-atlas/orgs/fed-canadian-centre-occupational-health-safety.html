Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canadian-centre-occupational-health-safety (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Established by the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. C-13 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-13/index.html - link verified, title/citation/section structure confirmed, current to 2026-06-14). Section 2 states the Act's purpose is to "promote the fundamental right of Canadians to a healthy and safe working environment" via a national occupational health and safety research institute whose governing body represents workers, trade unions, employers, governments, the professional community, and the public (statute, full text at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-13/FullText.html).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Under section 5, the Centre advances workplace health and mental wellness, facilitates federal/provincial/territorial cooperation with labour and management participation, develops policy to eliminate occupational hazards, and serves as Canada's national repository for occupational health and safety statistics and information (statute, FullText.html). Its stated mandate is "to provide easy access to credible information on occupational health and safety to help workers in Canada be safe at work, and support employers, labour groups and governments in their efforts to create healthy and safe workplaces" (CCOHS 2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://www.ccohs.ca/ccohs/reports/dp/2026-27/dp-full-page.html).

Governance & reporting line

Governed by a tripartite Council of roughly 22 members appointed by the Governor in Council: one Chair, 13 governors (one per province/territory), 4 nominated by worker organizations, and 4 nominated by employer organizations, plus a President as institutional head/CEO (statute, s.7, s.10; 2026-27 Departmental Plan names Anne Tennier, P.Eng., as President and CEO). Section 26 requires the Council to submit an annual report (activities, public submissions, financial statements) to the responsible Minister within four months of fiscal year-end, who must table it in Parliament within ten sitting days and distribute copies to every province and territory (statute, FullText.html). As of the 2026-27 Departmental Plan, the reporting minister is the Minister of Jobs and Families and Minister Responsible for the Federal Economic Development Agency for Northern Ontario (2026-27 Departmental Plan, as above). Registry lists org_type as "agency" with no listed parent (registry row, registry id fed-canadian-centre-occupational-health-safety).

Budget scale

~$15.5M total planned spending for FY2026-27 (110.7 planned FTEs) (CCOHS 2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://www.ccohs.ca/ccohs/reports/dp/2026-27/dp-full-page.html).

Institutional history

Established 1978 under the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety Act (now R.S.C., 1985, c. C-13); the Act has been amended since (last amendment 2012-06-29 per the consolidated text), but ⚠️ still being checked - specific renaming/reorganization events beyond the 2012 amendment were not independently confirmed this review (2026-27 Departmental Plan states 1978 year of establishment; statute).

Backgrounder summary

The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety (CCOHS) was established in 1978 by the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-13, to "promote the fundamental right of Canadians to a healthy and safe working environment" through a national OH&S research institute. Under section 5, it advances workplace health and mental wellness, facilitates federal/provincial/territorial cooperation, develops hazard-elimination policy, and serves as Canada's national repository of OH&S statistics and information. It is governed by a tripartite Council of roughly 22 members (Chair, 13 provincial/territorial governors, 4 worker-nominated, 4 employer-nominated) plus a President/CEO; the Council must submit an annual report to the responsible Minister within four months of fiscal year-end for tabling in Parliament. The 2026-27 Departmental Plan names Anne Tennier, P.Eng., as President and CEO and lists the reporting minister as the Minister of Jobs and Families and Minister Responsible for the Federal Economic Development Agency for Northern Ontario, with ~$15.5M total planned spending and 110.7 planned FTEs for FY2026-27. Registry lists org_type as "agency" with no parent.

Priority evolution

FY1998-99 (ar-1999, 127ce4994969): The earliest document in the series shows no formal named "strategic plan" or numbered priorities — the President's message (S. Len Hong) frames the mission simply as helping "everyone in our field attain our collective OH&S goals and objectives," under the Act's 1978 mandate "to promote health and safety in the workplace." The Council of Governors is already tripartite (Chair, labour, employer, provincial/territorial seats). Financials: Total expenditures $6,898,172 (vs. $6,724,454 prior year); Total non-tax revenue $4,239,754; Net cost of operations $2,658,418. Audited by the Office of the Auditor General of Canada (Richard Flageole, Assistant Auditor General).

FY2012-13 (ar-2013, e81b8988e2ec): President and CEO Steve Horvath, one year into his term, frames the year as one of "transformation" toward becoming "more effective as a customer-centric organization." No formal numbered strategic-priorities framework is present. Total expenses: $10,230,279 (planned) / $10,271,966 (actual) vs. $10,759,236 (prior actual). CCOHS spoke at the NAOSH Week national launch in Toronto, Ontario (May 7, 2012).

FY2014-15 and FY2015-16 (ar-2015, 6d4ecf410d82; ar-2016, 59a9a4522826): Still no formal numbered strategic-priorities framework — the FY2014-15 message references "a strategic plan that affirms our guiding principles" without naming discrete priorities. FY2015-16 (ar-2016) is a leadership-transition year: outgoing president Steve Horvath is thanked for "his leadership and contributions to the Centre during his five-year term," and Gareth Jones is named "Acting President." The Occupational Health Clinics for Ontario Workers (OHCOW) partnership (StressAssess) begins appearing this period.

FY2016-17 (ar-2017, e925bcae0021): Gareth Jones now signs as (non-acting) "President and CEO." No formal strategic-priorities framework yet; "strategy" language remains generic. Total expenses actual $11,167,506 (per ar-2018's prior-year comparative).

FY2017-18 (ar-2018, 28d7809c78ee): Anne Tennier signs as "President and CEO" for the first time in the series — a second leadership change within three years. The year's "overall focus and strategy" is described narratively (forefront of health and safety, growth, geographic coverage) without a named/numbered plan; identified "high risk sectors" are listed as "construction, transportation, mining, and healthcare" — a sector list that does not match the four sectors formalized the following year. Total expenses actual $11,472,743 (vs. $11,167,506 prior).

FY2018-19 (ar-2019, 12dddbef54f5): The Council/President message states "we were pleased in 2018 to launch our new strategic plan" — the first appearance in this series of a formally named, numbered strategic-planning framework: the "5 Year Strategic Plan" (2018-2023), built on three Key Strategic Priorities — "developing national leadership on emerging issues," "addressing priority sectors," and "building a national repository of current knowledge, standards, statistics, and other information tools." Four named priority sectors are set out for the plan's duration: healthcare, construction, agriculture and fishery, and Indigenous enterprises. Current health-and-safety issues named alongside the sectors: workplace mental health, impairment, harassment and violence, and occupational disease. Total expenses: $11,340,211 (planned) / $11,771,209 (actual) vs. $11,472,743 (prior actual).

FY2019-20 (ar-2020, 85e87ef4247a): The "Five Year Strategic Plan 2018-2023" and its three Key Strategic Priorities continue unchanged, restated with an infographic. A planned XXII World Congress on Safety and Health at Work, to be co-hosted with the Institute for Work and Health in Toronto, Ontario, October 4-7, 2020, is disclosed as "postponed to September 19-22, 2021" due to COVID-19 (footnote, at time of report production). Total expenses actual $12,201,850 (vs. $11,771,209 prior).

FY2020-21 (ar-2021, 5774c53c4306): Same three Key Strategic Priorities carried through "into the third year of the five year strategic plan." The Work Place Harassment and Violence Prevention Regulations came into force January 1, 2021; CCOHS begins hosting a "Roster of Investigators" and develops three supporting online courses this year — a new mandate-adjacent deliverable not present in earlier documents. The financial notes disclose a new Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC) agreement (total $1.5M over two years, $1.015M recognized this year) for COVID-19 workplace guidance content, plus a separate "Business Resumption Funding" agreement ($2.5M over two years, ~$1.28M recognized this year) for personnel/operating costs. Total expenses: $12,365,191 (planned) / $13,177,926 (actual) vs. $12,201,850 (prior actual).

FY2021-22 (ar-2022, 62e28e3de635): "Completing the fourth year of its five-year strategic plan," the same three Key Strategic Priorities are restated. The postponed World Congress is delivered as "the first ever virtual XXII World Congress" (September 2021, with the Institute for Work and Health) rather than in person in Toronto. Remaining PHAC ($0.485M) and Business Resumption Funding ($1.22M) amounts are recognized this year. Total expenses actual $14,590,070 (vs. $13,177,926 prior).

FY2022-23 (ar-2023, e5c4c9d98904): The Council/President message states explicitly: "this year we retired our strategic plan and mapped out a new four-year strategy to take us into 2027," developed via stakeholder/staff surveys and interviews and synthesized into the new "CCOHS Strategic Plan 2023-2027." Named priorities for the new plan include "service excellence and commitment to our staff." The document's own title format also reverts this year from "CCOHS Annual Report" (used ar-2013 through ar-2022) back to the 1999-era "Annual Report of the Council." Gender-based Analysis Plus (GBA Plus) survey questions are added to user-experience surveys for the first time in the series this year. Total expenses actual $15,081,735 (vs. $14,590,070 prior).

FY2023-24 (ar-2024, 1516d9fd7ac9): The Strategic Plan 2023-2027 is now in force ("guided by our new strategic plan"). The named priority-sector framing changes materially from the retired plan: priority sectors are now "small business" and federal government agencies/public administration, and a new "Workers at Higher Risk" category (new and young workers, international guest workers) replaces the former healthcare/construction/agriculture-and-fishery/Indigenous-enterprises sector list as the headline framing (though construction- and agriculture-related content continues to appear as embedded topic examples, not as named priority-sector headers). CCOHS reports completing its first-ever Sustainable Development Strategy, the "2023 to 2027 Departmental Sustainable Development Strategy," tabled October 2023 under the Federal Sustainable Development Act. The reporting minister is named as "the Honourable Seamus O'Regan Jr., Minister of Labour and Seniors" — a title distinct from the plain "Minister of Labour" used through ar-2022. Total expenses actual $15,987,005 (vs. $15,081,735 prior).

FY2024-25 (ar-2025, 6edf16473aa6): The Strategic Plan 2023-2027 continues, with priority sectors restated as small and medium-sized businesses, federally regulated organizations, and workers at higher risk (now described as "young, new, aging, gig, and guest (temporary international) workers"). The CCOHS Business Safety Portal launches nationally in November 2024, with a new partnership giving all Ontario Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB)-registered businesses free access. The 2023-2024 Departmental Sustainable Development Strategy Report is published, with the plan itself "updated in January 2025 to more accurately align targets with departmental capacity." The reporting minister/department line changes again, to "the Minister of Jobs and Families and Minister responsible for the Federal Economic Development Agency for Northern Ontario" — matching the backgrounder's 2026-27 Departmental Plan citation and confirming this rename had already taken effect within the fiscal year this document covers. Total expenses actual $16,454,159 (vs. $15,987,005 prior) — the highest in the series.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

CCOHS's sole disclosed physical address across every document in the series that states one (ar-1999 127ce4994969 through ar-2025 6edf16473aa6) is Hamilton, Ontario — not Toronto. Hamilton is roughly 70 km from Toronto and is never described in the text as part of a "Greater Toronto Area" designation; no Toronto office, facility, or physical presence is disclosed anywhere in the series. Toronto-specific mentions that do appear are incidental rather than programmatic: an on-site training delivered for the "Ministry of Labour (Toronto)" (ar-1999, 127ce4994969); the NAOSH Week national launch, at which President Steve Horvath spoke, held in Toronto, Ontario in May 2012 (ar-2013, e81b8988e2ec); University of Toronto researchers and students appearing as podcast guests and scholarship winners (ar-2018 28d7809c78ee, ar-2023 e5c4c9d98904); and generic press citations (Toronto Star, Toronto Sun) among dozens of media outlets listed in annual media-coverage tallies (ar-2016 59a9a4522826, ar-2017 e925bcae0021, ar-2019 12dddbef54f5, ar-2020 85e87ef4247a). A concrete Toronto-hosted event — the XXII World Congress on Safety and Health at Work, planned for October 2020 in Toronto with the Institute for Work and Health — was first postponed due to COVID-19 (ar-2020, 85e87ef4247a) and then delivered as a virtual event in September 2021 rather than in person (ar-2022, 62e28e3de635); no in-person Toronto event materialized within the archived series.

Ontario's relevance to CCOHS is real but is a province-wide usage/partnership pattern, not a Toronto-specific one: Ontario is consistently the top or near-top province across nearly every usage metric disclosed in every year that reports one — website visits, e-course enrolments, and Safety InfoLine inquiries — from ar-2013 (e81b8988e2ec, "Ontario 47%") through ar-2025 (6edf16473aa6, "Ontario 44%" on the English site). It also maintains a recurring web/app partnership with the Infrastructure Health and Safety Association (IHSA), an Ontario construction-safety body, from at least ar-2017 (e925bcae0021) through ar-2024 (1516d9fd7ac9). None of these — usage share, OHCOW, IHSA, or WSIB — are described in the text as Toronto-specific; the text does not state where WSIB, OHCOW, or IHSA are headquartered, so no Toronto-specific claim should be inferred from them.
