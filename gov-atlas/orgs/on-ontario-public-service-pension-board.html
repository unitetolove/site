Ontario Public Service Pension Board - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-ontario-public-service-pension-board (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Ontario Public Service Pension Board, operating as Ontario Pension Board (OPB), was created effective January 1, 1990 when the Province of Ontario enacted the Public Service Pension Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.48 ("PSP Act"), which continues the pension plan for Province of Ontario employees and certain of its agencies; the terms of the Public Service Pension Plan (PSPP) are set out in Schedule 1 to the PSP Act (Ontario Pension Board 2024 Annual Report, Note 1 to the Financial Statements, https://www.opb.ca/publications/2024-annual-report.pdf). OPB is the administrator of the PSPP; the Government of Ontario is the Plan Sponsor, acting through the member of the Executive Council assigned responsibility for the PSP Act under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990 (same source). OPB states its mission as to "Advise & Protect for the future of the PSPP and its stakeholders" (https://www.opb.ca/).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

OPB administers pension benefits and services for PSPP members and employers across their full life cycle - enrollment, active employment, retirement and survivor benefits - and is responsible for the investment of the Public Service Pension Fund and adjudication of the Plan (https://www.opb.ca/; Ontario Pension Board 2024 Annual Report, https://www.opb.ca/publications/2024-annual-report.pdf). The PSPP is a contributory defined-benefit plan; membership is mandatory for eligible employees, with some classes (e.g. Deputy Ministers, contract employees) permitted to elect participation (2024 Annual Report, Note 2, https://www.opb.ca/publications/2024-annual-report.pdf). Since 2015, day-to-day investment management has been delegated to the Investment Management Corporation of Ontario (IMCO), created by the Investment Management Corporation of Ontario Act; OPB retains responsibility for setting the Plan's Strategic Asset Allocation and overseeing IMCO (same source). Effective January 1, 2022, OPB was also appointed trustee of the Retirement Compensation Arrangement (RCA) Trust Fund, which provides supplementary pension benefits above Income Tax Act limits (same source). As of 2024, OPB served over 100,000 active, deferred and retired PSPP members and survivors (https://www.opb.ca/).

Governance & reporting line

Board members are appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council; the PSP Act requires a minimum of four members, at least one of whom must represent plan members in a bargaining relationship with the employer (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/422). As of this pass the Board comprised nine part-time directors "appointed based on their expertise, commitment, integrity and vision," including representation from labour organizations alongside investment and governance expertise (https://www.opb.ca/about-opb/board-of-directors); the Board Chair and Vice-Chair may be designated by the Lieutenant Governor in Council or, absent designation within one month of a vacancy, elected by the Board itself (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/422). OPB is classified as a provincial board (not an operational service agency) and reports through the Treasury Board Secretariat as its parent ministry (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/422), consistent with its registry classification (registry row on-ontario-public-service-pension-board, source_authority https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agencies-list).

Budget scale

As at December 31, 2024, OPB reported total assets of $36,333.3 million and net assets available for benefits of $34,089.3 million (≈$34.1 billion), up from $31,738.1 million at the end of 2023; total pension obligations stood at $39,697.6 million at year-end, up from $37,362.5 million (Ontario Pension Board 2024 Annual Report, Statement of Financial Position and Statement of Changes in Pension Obligations, https://www.opb.ca/publications/2024-annual-report.pdf). During 2024, contributions from all OPB members and employers totalled $1.445 billion (versus $1.325 billion in 2023), and OPB paid out $1,895.5 million in total benefits per the audited Statement of Changes in Net Assets Available (the Annual Report's narrative separately cites $1.74 billion in "pension payments" for 2024, up from $1.62 billion in 2023 - ⚠️ VERIFY reconciliation between the two figures, likely reflecting different benefit-payment scopes) (same source). Administrative and investment costs together totalled 0.62% of average net assets available for benefits (62 cents per $100) in 2024, consistent with 2023 (same source).

Institutional history

The Public Service Pension Plan itself dates to 1920, making it one of Canada's oldest continuously operating pension plans; OPB was established as its administrator effective January 1, 1990 under the newly enacted Public Service Pension Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.48 (Ontario Pension Board 2024 Annual Report, Note 1, https://www.opb.ca/publications/2024-annual-report.pdf). Since 1990, roughly two-thirds of pension payments have come from investment returns rather than contributions (same source). The organization marked the 100th anniversary of the PSPP and its own 30th anniversary in 2020 (https://www.opb.ca/news/celebrating-30-years-of-the-ontario-pension-board). OPB's oldest registered annual report on file dates to 2007, with a continuous annual-report series through 2024 (research/atlas/government_documents.csv rows for org_id on-ontario-public-service-pension-board).
