House of Commons - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-house-commons (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The House of Commons is a constitutional body created directly by the Constitution Act, 1867, not by ordinary statute. Part IV establishes: "There shall be One Parliament for Canada, consisting of the Queen, an Upper House styled the Senate, and the House of Commons" (s.17), that "The House of Commons shall, subject to the Provisions of this Act, consist of" members apportioned among the provinces (s.37, membership count since amended by later representation formulas), and that "Every House of Commons shall continue for Five Years from the Day of the Return of the Writs for choosing the House..., and no longer" (s.50) (Constitution Act, 1867, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/page-2.html, fetched this review). Administrative and financial authority over the House's own operations is delegated by the Parliament of Canada Act to the Board of Internal Economy (House of Commons 2024-2025 Annual Financial Report, https://www.ourcommons.ca/Content/Boie/pdf/FinancialStatements-2024-2025-E.pdf, p.3, fetched this review; registry row fed-house-commons, org_type: other).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The House of Commons Administration, under the non-partisan Clerk of the House, provides services to Members of Parliament across five main areas - Procedural Services, Law Clerk and Parliamentary Counsel, Administrative Services, Audit and Improvement, and Corporate Communications - "supporting lawmakers in their duties and constituent representation" (ourcommons.ca reporttocanadians overview, fetched this review). Financially and administratively the institution is organized into two programs: "the Members and House Officers program and the House Administration program," the former also covering "Committees, Parliamentary Associations and Parliamentary Exchanges" (2024-2025 Annual Financial Report, p.3).

Governance & reporting line

"The Board is the governing body of the House of Commons. The Board makes decisions and provides direction on financial and administrative matters of the House of Commons, specifically concerning its premises, services employees and Members. This authority is given to the Board by the Parliament of Canada Act. The Board consists of the Speaker, who acts as Chair, and the appointed Members representing all recognized parties. The Clerk of the House of Commons, who reports to the Speaker, serves as Secretary to the Board" (2024-2025 Annual Financial Report, p.3). The Constitution Act, 1867 separately provides that "The House of Commons on its first assembling after a General Election shall proceed with all practicable Speed to elect One of its Members to be Speaker" (s.44), who then "shall preside at all Meetings of the House of Commons" (s.46) (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/page-2.html). Legislation amending the Parliament of Canada Act on June 22, 2017 opened Board of Internal Economy meetings to the public, webcast live and on-demand via ParlVU (ourcommons.ca/boie/en, fetched this review).

Budget scale

The House of Commons' total net cost of operations for fiscal 2024-2025 was $757,869,000 ($757.9 million), against a total budget of $757.9 million inclusive of services received without charge and statutory adjustments, realizing a surplus of $17.5 million (4% of voted Main Estimates, within the maximum allowable 5%/$22.4 million carry-forward) (House of Commons 2024-2025 Annual Financial Report, pp.2-4, https://www.ourcommons.ca/Content/Boie/pdf/FinancialStatements-2024-2025-E.pdf). Within that total, net operating expenses split as: Members and House Officers $353.2 million (a $15.8 million surplus), Committees/Parliamentary Associations/Exchanges $6.1 million (a $3.7 million surplus), and House Administration $266.8 million (a $2.0 million shortfall against its own budget, but a $4.8 million/2% decrease from the prior year) (same source, pp.3-8). Financial statements were audited by KPMG LLP with an unqualified opinion (same source, p.3).

Institutional history

The House of Commons has existed continuously as the elected chamber of the federal Parliament since Confederation in 1867 under the Constitution Act, 1867 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/page-2.html; registry row fed-house-commons). Its own annual public accountability report, "Report to Canadians," has been published in some form since at least 2005 per the registry's archived-document shelf for this org (registry doc shelf, registry id fed-house-commons); exact founding/first-sitting date of the institution itself in 1867 - as opposed to the Report to Canadians series - is a matter of settled constitutional record rather than a single citable "founding" webpage, so no further inline citation is added here beyond the Constitution Act text above.
