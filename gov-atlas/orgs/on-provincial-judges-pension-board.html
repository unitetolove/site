Provincial Judges Pension Board - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-provincial-judges-pension-board (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Provincial Judges Pension Board is established under the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43, s. 53(1)(a.3), with its operating rules set out in O. Reg. 290/13 (Provincial Judges' Pension Plan) (Public Appointments Secretariat agency profile, https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/423). The regulation link was fetched and confirmed live: it defines the "Provincial Judges Pension Fund" (continued under s. 62) and repeatedly names the "Provincial Judges Pension Board" as administrator of the plan (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/130290). Its mandate is to "oversee the administration of the Provincial Judges Pension Plan and investment of two trust funds for sitting and retired judges, and their survivors including eligible children" (PAS agency profile, https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/423).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Board administers pension benefits for Ontario's provincial (Ontario Court of Justice) judges and holds fiduciary responsibility for the invested trust funds on behalf of sitting and retired judges and their eligible survivors (PAS agency profile, https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/423; O. Reg. 290/13, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/130290). Scope is limited to pension-plan governance; it does not administer judicial appointments or discipline.

Governance & reporting line

Five members, one designated Chair, are appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council for three-year renewable terms; the Board meets quarterly at its Toronto office (PAS agency profile, https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/423). It reports through the Treasury Board Secretariat, whose President is defined as "Minister" for purposes of the pension regulation's Framework Agreement (O. Reg. 290/13, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/130290; PAS agency profile).

Provincial Judges Pension Board - strategy evolution

TL;DR: The archived series is a single document — the Board's 2024 Annual Report — so no evolution across time can be shown; this brief reports only what that one report establishes about 2024. The biggest disclosed development is governance-and-investment maturation: the Board launched a public website (PJPP.ca) and, in December 2024, established a standing Investment Committee to sharpen oversight of the Plan's assets under an active-management mandate with the Investment Management Corporation of Ontario (IMCO). The most load-bearing number is the Plan's asset growth from $406 million to $443 million during 2024 (net assets available for benefits: $447.6 million at RPP year-end), against a $448.0 million pension obligation — a near-balanced funded position (a $435,000 deficit, down sharply from $3.93 million the prior year). One open question the single-document window cannot resolve: how the November 2025-approved 11th Provincial Judges Remuneration Commission report (implementation deferred into 2025) will affect the Plan's cost-of-living and funding trajectory going forward, since this report covers only the year before implementation.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • December 2024 — Investment Committee established. A new standing committee of the full Board, launching January 2025, to provide "focused oversight and strategic support related to the investment of assets held in trust," including periodic review of the Statement of Investment Policies and Procedures (SIP&P) and Strategic Asset Allocation (SAA) for the RPP and RCA (04f043f0ba0a).
  • May 2024 — PJPP.ca public website launched, developed with the Ontario Pension Board (OPB), completing "Phase 1" of a digital communications strategy; 2,525 active users May–December 2024 (04f043f0ba0a).
  • 2024 delegation of authority — the Board delegated approval of new pension payments, survivor allowances, and lump-sum benefits to OPB (previously requiring direct Board approval each time), explicitly framed as separating "operational functions from adjudicative responsibilities" (04f043f0ba0a).
  • Plan asset growth — $406 million to $443 million during 2024, "driven by strong investment returns"; RPP-specific return was 7% net of expenses for the year (04f043f0ba0a).
  • Funded position improved — RPP deficit (net assets available for benefits vs. pension obligation) narrowed from $3.93 million (2023) to $435,000 (2024) (04f043f0ba0a).
  • Province funding payments to the RCA: just under $49 million in 2024, in addition to member matching contributions, continuing to improve the RCA's funded ratio toward a target of five times expected benefit payments (RCA assets covered approximately four times benefit payments at 2024 year-end) (04f043f0ba0a).
  • 11th Provincial Judges Remuneration Commission Report — approved by Cabinet December 12, 2024; implementation (affecting judicial-salary-linked indexation for pre-2007 retirees) deferred to "early 2025," outside this report's coverage window (04f043f0ba0a).
  • Pension administration expenditures $1.791 million in 2024, 1.3% under the Board's $1.815 million budget estimate, "due largely to lower-than-expected project fees" — resolves the backgrounder's budget ⚠️ still being checked, though this figure covers only RPP-proxy administration costs, not the full scope of "Board operating budget" the backgrounder was checking for (04f043f0ba0a).
