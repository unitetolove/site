Livestock Financial Protection Board - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-livestock-financial-protection-board (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

As of January 1, 2026, the Board's governing statute is the Protecting Farmers from Non-Payment Act (Regulating Agricultural Product Dealers and Storage Operators), 2023, S.O. 2023, c. 9, Sched. 30 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/23p09, verified — consolidation period "from January 1, 2026," no amendments yet). Part VII of that Act ("Funds and Boards," ss. 26-38) provides the general statutory basis for establishing compensation funds and boards such as this one, including board constitution, functions/powers, and claims-adjudication procedures (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/23p09, Part VII). The new Act repealed and consolidated three predecessor statutes — the Farm Products Payments Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. F.10 (confirmed repealed effective January 1, 2026, per https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90f10), the Grains Act, and the Livestock and Livestock Products Act — under which the Board previously operated.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Board administers the Fund for Livestock Producers (including the Ontario Beef Cattle Financial Protection Program, established 1982), investigates and adjudicates producer claims arising from dealer/buyer non-payment, and can pursue recovery of amounts owed (ontario.ca Livestock Financial Protection Board overview, https://www.ontario.ca/page/livestock-financial-protection-board; registry row on-livestock-financial-protection-board).

Governance & reporting line

The Board is an agency of the Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness (OMAFA), comprising 7 members appointed for up to three-year terms with representation from industry stakeholder groups (e.g., Beef Farmers of Ontario, veal producers, livestock dealers); it operates at arm's length under a Memorandum of Understanding, Agencies and Appointments Directive, and the Minister's annual letter of direction (https://www.ontario.ca/page/livestock-financial-protection-board; registry row on-livestock-financial-protection-board, source authority pas.gov.on.ca Agencies list).

Budget scale

For fiscal 2025-26: opening fund balance $10,165,543; projected total revenue $637,400; projected total expenses $636,625; projected closing fund balance $10,166,318 (Livestock Financial Protection Board Business Plan, fiscal years 2025-2028, https://www.ontario.ca/document/livestock-financial-protection-board-business-plans/fiscal-years-2025-2028). Fund balance as of March 31, 2024 was reported at $9.694M (same source).

Institutional history

The compensation fund traces to 1982, established by regulation under the former Farm Products Payments Act (Ontario Beef Cattle Financial Protection Program); the Board's governing legislation was substantially reorganized effective January 1, 2026 when the Protecting Farmers from Non-Payment Act, 2023 came into force, repealing and replacing the Farm Products Payments Act, the Grains Act, and the Livestock and Livestock Products Act (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/23p09; https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90f10).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • Pending Act proclamation — Protecting Farmers from Non-Payment Act, 2023 passed Spring 2023 but not proclaimed during 2024-25; ministry targeting fall 2025 for proclamation of the new statute and associated regulations; the Board continued operating under the Farm Products Payments Act (FPPA) throughout 2024-25 (73172c3ceff1).
  • Board formally requested priority action — following the March 2025 appointment of the Minister of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness, the Board sent a letter asking that priority be given to creating and implementing the new livestock regulation required for the Act's proclamation (73172c3ceff1).
  • Fund performance: opening balance $9,693,843, closing balance $10,281,489 (up $587,646); total revenue $754,952 (fees $230,947, interest income $524,005); total expenses $167,306; rate of return on investment 5.83% (up from 5.66% in 2023-24) (73172c3ceff1).
  • Essentially no claims activity — one claim application received in 2024-25, withdrawn after payment was received directly, requiring no Board investigation or adjudication; no claim payouts and no recoveries in the fiscal year (73172c3ceff1). Aggregate 1982–2025 history: 84 defaulters, 1,131 claim applications received, 656 claims paid, $10,944,038.29 total paid out, $3,749,120.00 recovered — reported in aggregate by fiscal year only, no individual claimant or defaulter named (73172c3ceff1).
  • Unresolved recommended check-off fee increase — a 2021-22 actuarial review recommended raising the per-head check-off fee from $0.10 to $0.30; the Board's recommendation to the Minister (December 2022) remains unimplemented as of this report, "being addressed as part of the financial protection program review" tied to the pending Act (73172c3ceff1).
  • AAD-mandated MOU renewal delayed by election writ period — a required Memorandum of Understanding update (per an October 2024 Agencies and Appointments Directive change) missed its February 1, 2025 deadline because the Legislature was dissolved for the provincial election; on track for early 2025-26 signing (73172c3ceff1).
