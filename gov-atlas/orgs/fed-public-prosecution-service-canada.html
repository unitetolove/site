Public Prosecution Service of Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-public-prosecution-service-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Public Prosecution Service of Canada (PPSC) operates under the Director of Public Prosecutions Act, which came into force on December 12, 2006, and created the independent office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/d-2.5/FullText.html). The Act empowers the Director to initiate and conduct federal prosecutions and to advise law enforcement and investigative bodies on prosecution-related matters, acting "under and on behalf of the Attorney General" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The PPSC is responsible for prosecuting drug-related offences, regulatory and economic offences, national security offences, and all Criminal Code offences in the territories, and provides legal advice to investigative agencies across Canada; it also conducts all prosecutions related to elections law (PPSC Departmental Plan 2026-2027, https://www.ppsc-sppc.gc.ca/eng/pub/dp-pm/2026_2027/index.html; Director of Public Prosecutions Act, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/d-2.5/FullText.html).

Governance & reporting line

The Director of Public Prosecutions is appointed by the Governor in Council on the Attorney General's recommendation, following a selection process in which a committee (including representatives of provincial law societies, recognized political parties, the Departments of Justice and Public Safety, and the Attorney General) recommends three candidates for Parliamentary approval; the Director holds deputy-head rank for a non-renewable seven-year term (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/d-2.5/FullText.html). The Director remains subordinate to the Attorney General of Canada, who may issue written directives on specific prosecutions (published in the Canada Gazette) or assume direct conduct of a prosecution after consultation, and to whom the Director must submit an annual report by June 30 (same source).

Budget scale

For 2026-27, total planned spending (including internal services) for the PPSC is $229,440,707, with total planned full-time-equivalent staff of 1,120 (PPSC Departmental Plan 2026-2027, https://www.ppsc-sppc.gc.ca/eng/pub/dp-pm/2026_2027/index.html).

Institutional history

The PPSC was established under the Director of Public Prosecutions Act, which came into force December 12, 2006 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/d-2.5/FullText.html). ⚠️ still being checked - the Act's predecessor arrangement (federal prosecutions conducted through the Department of Justice prior to 2006) is widely understood but was not confirmed by a source directly read this review.

Public Prosecution Service of Canada - strategy evolution

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition across the series is the equity/anti-racism/reconciliation reframing that begins with the 2020-21 "bias-free workplace" review, hardens into a rewritten mission and six-value statement in 2022-23 (ar-2023, 33a151f9ea47), and stays the organizing frame for organizational priorities through the 2026-27 Departmental Plan (31d754dafe32). The biggest quietly dropped element is the founding-era architecture itself: the 2007-13 five-"program-activity" structure and the 2009-10 four-value statement (Respect/Integrity/Excellence/Leadership) are both gone without explanation by the mid-2010s, each replaced by at least two further priority-framework rewrites before 2026. The most load-bearing number is a rising-cost/falling-volume divergence the PPSC states plainly itself: actual spending grew from $177,584,797 (2016-17, 980 FTE, drr-2017 0e4977f8812e) to $254,250,062 (2024-25, 1,148 FTE, drr-2025 4f4fb2bd532b) while total prosecution files fell from 70,028 to 44,446 (ar-2017 ab028fb63565; ar-2026 31d3c9980575) over the same span, attributed in ar-2026 to rising case complexity (encrypted communications, digital evidence volume). One open question: whether the "Enhancing prosecutorial effectiveness in a complex and evolving legal landscape" priority named for 2026-28 (dp-2027, 31d754dafe32) marks a durable pivot away from the 2022-26 EDI-centred priority framework or will sit alongside it — the archived series ends before this is resolved.

Backgrounder summary

Per the backgrounder, the PPSC operates under the Director of Public Prosecutions Act (in force December 12, 2006), which created the independent Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) and empowers the Director to initiate and conduct federal prosecutions and advise investigative bodies, acting "under and on behalf of the Attorney General." The PPSC prosecutes drug, regulatory/economic, and national-security offences, all Criminal Code offences in the territories, and all federal election-law prosecutions; it reports through the Attorney General, who may issue written directives (published in the Canada Gazette) but must consult the Director first. For 2026-27, planned spending is $229,440,707 with 1,120 planned FTEs (PPSC Departmental Plan 2026-27) — figures independently confirmed in the archived dp-2027 (31d754dafe32) read for this brief.

Priority evolution

2006-07 (ar-2007, 9dbf716597c0): First annual report, covering only Dec 12, 2006 – March 31, 2007. PPSC created by Part 3 of the Federal Accountability Act, absorbing the Department of Justice's Federal Prosecution Service. Five "program activities" named for 2007-08: prosecuting drug/organized-crime/Criminal Code offences; prosecuting federal offences protecting environment/natural resources/economic-social health; addressing criminal issues "to contribute to a safer world for Canada" (terrorism, war crimes, extradition); promoting a fair and effective justice system; and ensuring the PPSC's own sustainability. Reporting line: Attorney General of Canada, via a June 2007 letter of transmittal.

2009-10 (ar-2010, ca91014b7cd4): First PPSC mission and values statement adopted, built around four values: Respect, Integrity, Excellence, Leadership. Four "corporate priorities" set for 2010-11: recruitment and retention of staff; safety and security of employees; development of performance measures; and knowledge-management tools implementation — a narrower, more internally-facing set than the 2007 program-activity list.

2012-13 (ar-2013, 7237a405e182): Priorities for 2013-14: personal security of employees, effective staff deployment, continued training investment, and working more collaboratively with investigative agencies. First-ever prosecution under the Corruption of Foreign Public Officials Act (CFPOA) against an individual concluded this year (institutional description only per the private-individual rule); Security of Information Act prosecution also concluded. Census-refusal prosecutions under the Statistics Act continue as a recurring, low-volume regulatory category.

2016-17 (ar-2017, ab028fb63565), 10th-anniversary report: Landmark jurisprudential inflection: the Supreme Court's R. v. Jordan decision (2016) sets presumptive 18/30-month ceilings for trial delay, triggering a new PPSC Deskbook chapter ("Ensuring Timely Prosecutions") and a nation-wide network of regional "Jordan coordinators." Fentanyl prosecutions appear as a named, growing priority category for the first time, with a dedicated cross-regional fentanyl-coordinator network and an internal sentencing-precedent database. Brian Saunders, DPP since 2006, retired in 2016; Kathleen Roussel became DPP. Three renewed strategic priorities for 2016-17: excellence in prosecution management, employee training and development, working collaboratively with investigative agencies — the "three-priority" template that recurs (with different content) through the following organizational-priority cycles.

2018-19 (ar-2019, 84ca8635a46a): Two major legal-environment shifts inside one year: the Cannabis Act came into force October 17, 2018 (PPSC stood up a Cannabis Act Implementation Committee); and Part XXII.1 of the Criminal Code created remediation agreements ("deferred prosecution agreements") as a new prosecutorial tool — the PPSC reports having built an assessment/management framework but had not yet concluded one. On November 30, 2018, the Attorney General issued a formal published directive instructing the PPSC not to prosecute HIV non-disclosure cases absent a realistic transmission risk — a rare, explicit exercise of the AG's Gazette-published-directive power. A corporate rebranding initiative and first social-media presence also launched this year.

2020-21 (ar-2021, 54f60d3e3da7): COVID-19 dominates: over 1,000 staff sent home; cloud disclosure tooling and virtual-hearing infrastructure piloted under pressure; new Quarantine Act prosecution stream stood up (50 prosecutions by year-end). Substantively larger: a new Deskbook chapter (August 17, 2020) makes prosecution "an exception rather than the default position" for simple drug possession — a genuine charging-policy liberalization, not merely a pandemic accommodation. First formal "bias-free workplace" review and EDI action-planning begins. Three organizational priorities for 2020-21: nurture a culture of civility/wellness/inclusion; strengthen accountability and transparency; foster efficiency and innovation.

2022-23 (ar-2023, 33a151f9ea47): Mission and values rewritten wholesale. The 2009-10 four-value statement (Respect/Integrity/Excellence/Leadership) is replaced by six values (Respect, Trust, Professionalism, Courage, Equity and Inclusion, Commitment to Truth and Reconciliation), and the mission is reframed around "contributing to the change necessary to support a criminal justice system that is fair to all." Deskbook Guideline 2.3 ("Decision to Prosecute") — the single most consequential day-to-day policy in the organization — is revised for the first time to explicitly require prosecutors to weigh factors contributing to the over-representation of Indigenous and Black accused. New offices open in Kenora, Ontario, and Surrey, British Columbia, the Kenora opening explicitly framed as a reconciliation measure serving 13 fly-in, primarily Indigenous communities. Four organizational priorities set: take action against systemic discrimination and racism; advance equity/diversity/inclusion in the workplace; foster a culture of trust and engagement; modernize the way we work — this four-priority frame persists through the read 2026-27 documents.

2025-26 (ar-2026, 31d3c9980575): Under DPP George Dolhai. Files handled continue their multi-year decline (44,446, down from 70,028 in 2016-17) while the report explicitly attributes rising per-file burden to encrypted communications, large-scale digital/body-worn-camera evidence, and AI-assisted case work; a secure, org-wide AI tool was introduced this year with accompanying policy guidance. The organization reports reviewing its priorities (a stated every-two-year exercise) and sets an updated four-priority frame for 2026-28 in the companion dp-2027 (below) that leads with prosecutorial effectiveness rather than EDI framing for the first time since 2022. Terrorism-related youth involvement is reported rising in trend terms — 9 charges (2021-22) to 42 (2023-24) to 24 (2025-26) — with 2025-26 explicitly lower than the 2023-24 figure, so no series-wide "record" is asserted here.

dp-2018 (59c621a31e5b) and dp-2027 (31d754dafe32), sampled for cross-check: dp-2018 confirms the Jordan-driven "Excellence in Prosecution Management" priority was already framed as the lead item for 2017-18, consistent with ar-2017. dp-2027 confirms the 2026-28 four-priority refresh (Take Action Against Systemic Discrimination and Racism; Build a Resilient, Inclusive Workplace Grounded in Trust, Engagement, Anti-racism, and Reconciliation with Indigenous Peoples; Modernize the Way We Work; Enhance Prosecutorial Effectiveness in a Complex and Evolving Legal Landscape) and discloses a Comprehensive Expenditure Review reduction of $55,505 (2026-27) rising to $940,555/year by 2028-29, roughly 9 FTEs — the only explicit budget-cut disclosure found in the sampled documents.

drr-2017 (0e4977f8812e) and drr-2025 (4f4fb2bd532b), sampled for cross-check: confirm the spending/FTE/file-volume figures used in the TL;DR and budget-inflection sections, and confirm the Office of the Commissioner of Canada Elections (OCCE) — transferred into the same corporate structure October 1, 2014 — reports its own compliance/enforcement statistics (complaints, compliance agreements) inside the same document series while remaining functionally independent of the DPP.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — timely-prosecution case management as a standing discipline: absent before 2016; created wholesale in response to R. v. Jordan (ar-2017, ab028fb63565); institutionalized as a Deskbook chapter and regional-coordinator network that persists through the rest of the series.
  • Added — fentanyl/opioid-specific prosecution infrastructure: first named ar-2017 (ab028fb63565); still a headline theme in ar-2026 (31d3c9980575), with the underlying charging policy liberalized further in 2020 (simple-possession "exception not default," ar-2021 54f60d3e3da7) and then codified into the CDSA itself by Parliament in 2022 (per ar-2026's own account).
  • Added — remediation agreements (deferred prosecution agreements): created by 2018 Criminal Code amendment (framework-building reported in ar-2019, 84ca8635a46a); first agreement concluded only in ar-2023 (33a151f9ea47) — a five-year gap between tool creation and first use.
  • Added — equity/anti-racism/reconciliation as the organizing mission frame: first substantial treatment as a "bias-free workplace" review in ar-2021 (54f60d3e3da7); becomes the rewritten mission/values and the Deskbook 2.3 revision in ar-2023 (33a151f9ea47); remains a named priority (though no longer the lead one) in dp-2027 (31d754dafe32).
  • Added — "prosecutorial effectiveness in a complex and evolving legal landscape": first named as a standalone top-line priority in dp-2027 (31d754dafe32) for 2026-28, reflecting digital-evidence volume and AI adoption themes that ar-2026 (31d3c9980575) treats at length but that had not previously been a named organizational priority.
  • Quietly dropped — the 2007 five-"program-activity" structure (drug/OC/CC; environmental-regulatory; safer-world/terrorism; fair-justice promotion; sustainability): present ar-2007 (9dbf716597c0), gone by the priorities sampled from 2010 onward, with no stated reason for retirement — the disappearance coincides with the shift from a Program Activity Architecture to a Departmental Results Framework government-wide, not a PPSC-specific decision.
  • Quietly dropped — the 2009-10 four-value statement (Respect/Integrity/Excellence/Leadership): present through ar-2019 language patterns; fully replaced by the six-value 2022-23 statement (33a151f9ea47) with no report explaining the earlier statement's retirement.
  • Renamed — "Report on Plans and Priorities" → "Departmental Plan": a government-wide Treasury Board reporting reform explained in dp-2018 (59c621a31e5b, "to improve reporting to Canadians... simplified report"), not a PPSC-specific choice.

  • Jan 31, 2023 — BC's federal CDSA s.4(1) possession exemption pilot requires the PPSC's BC regions to adapt prosecutorial discretion regionally (ar-2023, 33a151f9ea47) — ⚠️ still being checked current status; not tracked past this document in the sampled set.
