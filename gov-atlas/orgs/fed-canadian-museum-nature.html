Canadian Museum of Nature - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-canadian-museum-nature (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canadian Museum of Nature (CMN) was established by the Museums Act on July 1, 1990, and is an agent Crown corporation named in Part I of Schedule III of the Financial Administration Act (CMN Annual Report 2024-25, "Authority and Mission", https://nature.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ann24-25nature_e.pdf). Its legislated mission is "to increase, throughout Canada and internationally, interest in, knowledge of and appreciation and respect for the natural world by establishing, maintaining and developing for research and posterity a collection of natural history objects, with special but not exclusive reference to Canada, and by demonstrating the natural world, the knowledge derived from it and the understanding it represents" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CMN is one of Canada's six national museums; it acquires, develops, preserves and makes accessible collections of natural history specimens (biodiversity and geodiversity specimens, genomic samples, library and archives, and art/photo/historical materials) for research, education, and public engagement through galleries, programs and events (CMN Annual Report 2024-25, "Authority and Mission" and financial notes, https://nature.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ann24-25nature_e.pdf).

Governance & reporting line

CMN reports to Parliament through the Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages (CMN Annual Report 2024-25, p.3, https://nature.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ann24-25nature_e.pdf; https://nature.ca/en/about-the-museum/). Its Board of Trustees - a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson, and up to nine additional trustees appointed by the Governor-in-Council for terms up to four years - governs the Museum, evaluates the Director's performance, and is responsible for stewardship and accountability (CMN Annual Report 2024-25, "Board of Trustees", same URL).

Budget scale

Total revenue and parliamentary appropriations were $57.1 million in fiscal 2024-25, up $4.2 million (8%) from $52.9 million in 2023-24, with parliamentary appropriations representing 74% of the total; parliamentary appropriations recognized as revenue were $42.5 million (Note 16) (CMN Annual Report 2024-25, "Appropriations" and financial statements, https://nature.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ann24-25nature_e.pdf).

Institutional history

Established by the Museums Act on July 1, 1990; the Museum's own site states it "first opened its doors to the public in 1912" (CMN Annual Report 2024-25, "Authority and Mission", https://nature.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ann24-25nature_e.pdf; https://nature.ca/en/about-the-museum/).

Priority evolution

FY2014-15 (referenced retrospectively in ar-2016, 480ac1b2688f): "In 2014-2015, the Museum began to advance a new strategic plan that leverages its research and collections strengths in Arctic knowledge and species discovery" — the first of what becomes a recurring five-year strategic-plan cycle. Strategic Objective #1 as of this plan: "Create a Centre for Arctic Knowledge and Exploration that transforms people's understanding of Canada's Arctic and its relationship with Canada as a country in a 21st century global context," delivered via a five-year Arctic this library's internal records program.

FY2015-16 (ar-2016, 480ac1b2688f), "year two" of the plan: Prime Minister Justin Trudeau held the inaugural Climate Summit First Ministers Meeting at the Museum in November 2015 — a notable federal-government-relations touchpoint. Planning progressed on a new Arctic Gallery (opening June 23, 2017, as the Museum's contribution to Canada's 150th-anniversary programming) and the Landscapes of Canada Gardens outdoor exhibit (opened June 2016). Revenue and parliamentary appropriations decreased to $39.9M. Net surplus $0.9M against a sustainability target of 5% commercial-revenue growth / <1% cost growth.

FY2016-17 through FY2018-19 ("year three" through "year five" of the same plan; grep-sampled, ar-2017 7147ab3e3595, ar-2018 3fde5f41bc51, ar-2019 7bbc734103ec): Budget 2016 recurs as a named funding source across all three years — for essential scientific equipment, the Canada Goose Arctic Gallery's completion, base PILT (Payment in Lieu of Taxes) funding, and deferred capital projects. Revenue and appropriations rose to $41.6M (FY2016-17). By FY2018-19 the Museum describes itself as in "year five of a strategic plan" — i.e., the FY2014-15-launched plan's final year.

FY2019-2024 (a second five-year Strategic Plan, per ar-2025's retrospective framing, 06fd9f06d25a; individual years grep-sampled): A "2019-24 Strategic Plan" governs this period (named explicitly only in the FY2024-25 AR looking back). COVID-19 dominates FY2019-20 through FY2022-23: the Museum was closed to the public for much of this window; a "COVID-19 pivot" section appears in ar-2021 (764c1695bbbc); revenue and appropriations fell to $45.8M in FY2019-20 (a stated decrease). The Museum's Advancement team launched a new Nature Foundation in October 2020 — an online-only launch (no in-person VIP event possible under pandemic restrictions) — described as key to the Museum's long-term sustainable-future fundraising, paralleling the CMHR's "Friends of the CMHR" foundation model. By FY2022-23 the Museum reports "strong revenue recovery coming out of the pandemic," approaching pre-COVID revenue levels.

FY2024-25 (ar-2025, 06fd9f06d25a): "We delivered the final year of our 2019-24 Strategic Plan while launching a new five-year Strategic Plan that will guide the Museum through 2030" — the transition is stated explicitly for the first time in the read series (unlike the 2014→2019 handoff, which is only inferable from "year X" phrasing). The new 2025-30 Strategic Plan is "grounded in our public mandate" and organized to "grow our reach, deepen our relationships, and expand our relevance." Reconciliation with Indigenous Peoples is named as a fourth pillar alongside this library's internal records excellence, public engagement, and inclusion/accessibility — operationalized via a new senior position "dedicated to advancing the Museum's journey of Reconciliation," created this year, plus co-developed exhibitions and Indigenous-knowledge-grounded fieldwork (Ellesmere Island to Newfoundland and Labrador). Total revenue and appropriations reached $57.1M (74% from appropriations, $42.5M). The reporting Minister is named "Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages" — the same rename independently confirmed in the CMHR and CMH briefs this batch.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — formal Reconciliation function: a dedicated senior position "to advance the Museum's journey of Reconciliation" is new in FY2024-25 (ar-2025, 06fd9f06d25a); earlier reports (ar-2016 through grep-sampled later years) do not show a comparably formalized Indigenous-relations role, though land acknowledgements and Indigenous-knowledge fieldwork partnerships appear increasingly in later grep hits (ar-2024 references "next strategic plan, including Crown-Indigenous [relations]").
  • Added — a dedicated development foundation ("Nature Foundation"): launched October 2020 (ar-2021, 764c1695bbbc); no comparable standalone foundation is named in the FY2015-16 report, which instead frames philanthropy as direct Museum fundraising.
  • Renamed/restructured — strategic-plan cycle: three consecutive five-year plans run back-to-back (2014-19 → 2019-24 → 2025-30), but only the most recent handoff (2019-24 → 2025-30) is narrated explicitly as a transition; the first handoff (2014-19 → 2019-24) is not documented in any read-or-sampled material — it is inferred from the FY2018-19 "year five" language and the FY2024-25 report's retrospective naming of "our 2019-24 Strategic Plan."
  • Renamed — reporting Minister: "Minister of Canadian Heritage" / "Minister of Canadian Heritage and Multiculturalism" (varying between these two forms across FY2015-16 through FY2018-19 grep hits) → "Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture and Minister responsible for Official Languages" (ar-2025, 06fd9f06d25a) — the same federal machinery-of-government rename independently confirmed across all three museum briefs this batch; likely a single 2025 Canadian Heritage portfolio-wide rename.
  • Quietly growing — the appropriations/revenue base: $39.9M (FY2015-16) → $41.6M (FY2016-17) → $45.8M (FY2019-20, itself a stated decrease from an unread FY2018-19 peak) → $57.1M (FY2024-25) — no single report narrates this as a multi-year growth trend; only visible across years, and punctuated by the COVID-era dip.

No Toronto-specific partnership, funding relationship, or programming is documented in the material read or grep-sampled this review. Relevance to Ontario runs through the Ottawa campus itself and through the same federal Canadian Heritage cultural-institution policy space (Museums Act governance, parliamentary appropriations, the 2025 Minister rename) shared with the other three orgs in this batch, rather than through any GTA-specific footprint.
