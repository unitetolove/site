Canada Border Services Agency - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canada-border-services-agency (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) is governed by the Canada Border Services Agency Act (S.C. 2005, c. 38), consolidated and current to 2026-06-14 at laws-lois.justice.gc.ca (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-1.4/, fetched and verified directly). Section 3 sets out the Agency's establishment and mandate; section 7 establishes the President and Executive Vice-president (same source). CBSA's own site states its responsibility is "providing integrated border services that support national security and public safety priorities and facilitate the free flow of persons and goods, including animals and plants" (https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/agency-agence/menu-eng.html, fetched directly).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CBSA's 2025-26 Departmental Plan organizes its work into two core responsibilities: Border Management (border security, processing of admissible persons and goods, threat identification/mitigation; $1,998,495,608 planned) and Border Enforcement (immigration investigations, detention management, removal of inadmissible foreign nationals, prosecution of border-related violations; $513,244,952 planned) (https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/agency-agence/reports-rapports/rpp/2025-2026/glance-apercu-eng.html, fetched directly).

Governance & reporting line

CBSA is one of five partner agencies within the Public Safety Portfolio, alongside CSIS, Correctional Service of Canada, the Parole Board of Canada, and the RCMP, per Public Safety Canada's 2026-27 Departmental Plan (https://www.publicsafety.gc.ca/cnt/rsrcs/pblctns/dprtmntl-pln-2026-27/index-en.aspx, fetched directly); the Agency's registry row lists no parent_org (registry row: fed-canada-border-services-agency). The President and Executive Vice-president lead the Agency under CBSA Act s. 7 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-1.4/).

Budget scale

~$2.99 billion total planned spending for 2025-26 (including internal services), with 17,289 planned full-time equivalents (CBSA 2025-26 Departmental Plan at-a-glance, https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/agency-agence/reports-rapports/rpp/2025-2026/glance-apercu-eng.html, doc-shelf doc_type: departmental-plan).

Institutional history

CBSA operates under the Canada Border Services Agency Act (S.C. 2005, c. 38) (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-1.4/); ⚠️ still being checked - background indicates the Agency was originally created administratively in December 2003 by consolidating customs, immigration-enforcement, and food/plant/animal import-inspection functions from predecessor bodies, but an official CBSA source confirming this founding date and predecessor mergers was not successfully fetched this review.

Priority evolution

Because 21 of 24 documents (20 stubs plus dp-2025's outright failure) yielded no CBSA-authored content, this section can only trace CBSA's stated priorities across roughly a five-year window (2020 through mid-2025), not the two-decade span the registry's year range implies. No claim is made here about CBSA priorities in any year from 2005 through 2022 — the archived text for that period is not available.

2020-2023, reported retrospectively (oth-2024, a569bcdc35e2, OAG "Report 1—ArriveCAN," tabled 12 February 2024): The Auditor General found that CBSA, the Public Health Agency of Canada, and Public Services and Procurement Canada "repeatedly failed to follow good management practices in the contracting, development, and implementation of the ArriveCAN application," launched by CBSA on 29 April 2020 to collect travellers' contact and health information. The audit could not determine ArriveCAN's precise cost because of poor CBSA documentation and financial controls; it estimated the cost at approximately $59.5 million. It found little documentation supporting the non-competitive award of the initial contract to GC Strategies, and found that GC Strategies was later involved in drafting requirements for the competitive contract that followed. CBSA released 177 application versions between April 2020 and October 2022 with "often little to no documentation of testing"; one June 2022 update wrongly instructed roughly 10,000 travellers to quarantine. The audit also found no evidence that some CBSA employees disclosed gifts (dinners, other activities) from contractors under the Code of Conduct, prompting a CBSA-launched internal investigation (a569bcdc35e2).

FY2023-24 (drr-2024, a6dc64db6ecf), Departmental Results Report: Reporting minister was the Honourable Dominic LeBlanc, under the Public Safety, Democratic Institutions and Intergovernmental Affairs portfolio. The report frames the year's work around border security/facilitation, combatting firearms and drug smuggling, human trafficking, and trade-based money laundering, plus "efficient security screening of individuals seeking to immigrate to Canada." It names the National Summit on Combatting Auto Theft (February 2024) as a coordinated response that contributed to recovery of over 1,800 stolen vehicles in calendar 2023 and over 1,500 in 2024 to date. On the organizational side, CBSA states it "created an Executive Procurement Review Committee to provide oversight of all contracting activities and ensure value for money, while also taking steps to reduce its reliance on external consultants," and that "efforts are underway to establish an independent review body for the CBSA via Bill C-20, which is currently before Parliament." The report's corporate-information block lists CBSA's "Year of incorporation / commencement" as 2003 — the first and only document in this readable set to state a founding year, partially corroborating the backgrounder's flagged ⚠️ still being checked item (though it does not narrate the predecessor-agency mergers the backgrounder describes). Traveller Modernization (biometric, digital self-service infrastructure at ports of entry) and the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system (internally launched, Release 2, in Q1 FY2024-25) are both named as major in-flight initiatives. Indigenous reconciliation work is described under Border Management (UNDA Action Plan, June 2023; Indigenous Affairs Secretariat; seven regional roundtables in 2023-24; a March 2024 joint report with IRCC) and separately under Internal Services (an Indigenous Employee Experience Centre; two Indigenous Career Navigators). A performance indicator under Immigration National Security Screening shows the "recommendations for admissibility completed within service standard" rate falling from 46% (2022-23) to 27% (2023-24), against an 80% target, attributed to "historically high referral volumes." Removals reached 16,218 in 2023-24 (+59% year-over-year) (a6dc64db6ecf).

Budget trajectory named in drr-2024: total actual net spending rose from $2,478,717,991 (2021-22) to $2,594,542,451 (2022-23) to $2,693,911,151 (2023-24), and total FTEs from 15,057 to 15,717 to 16,470 over the same years. The report's own forward table projected planned spending declining thereafter — to $2,649,606,362 (2024-25), $2,465,267,413 (2025-26), and $2,331,485,430 (2026-27) — "primarily due to reductions arising from the Refocusing Government Spending initiative and sunsetting funding for various initiatives not yet renewed," naming the Gordie Howe International Bridge Project and Traveller Modernization as examples of initiatives with reduced future-year funding (a6dc64db6ecf).

FY2024-25 (dp-2025, f6bd7dff97a5): not recoverable. This is the one document the registry itself flags as a failed extraction (0 bytes). It sits directly between the two readable documents above and below, so it likely would have shown how CBSA first articulated the priorities that appear fully formed in the FY2025-26 plan (see Residuals & gaps).

FY2025-26 (dp-2026, 2f5441366031), "at a glance" summary of the Departmental Plan: This is the first document in the series to present an explicit named "Key priorities" list (six items): (1) Effective border operations — border security and efficient processing, with focus on "high-priority contraband such as firearms and fentanyl," human smuggling, and inadmissible persons; (2) Effective management of the CBSA — internal systems/controls/processes, "with a particular focus on procurement"; (3) Asylum sustainability — immigration reforms for "integrity and sustainability of the asylum system," continued implementation of the National Immigration Detention Framework "through an updated approach to address current challenges"; (4) Traveller Modernization — continued planning and delivery, "working toward the border of the future"; (5) Anti-racism, equity, diversity and inclusion; (6) Reconciliation with Indigenous Peoples. Under Border Management, the plan states it will "support the implementation of Canada's Border Plan, including a key focus on detecting and disrupting the fentanyl trade, enhancing tools for law enforcement, improving operational coordination, and expanding information sharing with key partners" — the only document in this readable set to name "Canada's Border Plan." It also names continued support for the "Federal Action Plan on Combatting Auto Theft" (institutionalizing the one-off February 2024 summit named in drr-2024 into a standing action plan), and states a target of increasing removals "from 16,000 to 20,000 (a 25% increase) for fiscal years 2025 to 2026 and 2026 to 2027." CARM is described as now fully external-facing, "following its successful launch in October 2024 as the official system of record," making CBSA "the second-largest revenue collector behind the Canada Revenue Agency" (managing "approximately $40 billion a year"). Governance oversight has advanced from "currently before Parliament" (drr-2024) to CBSA committing to "support the function of the Public Complaints and Review Commission, which will serve as an independent review body for the CBSA following the Royal Assent of Bill C-20" — meaning Bill C-20 received Royal Assent sometime between the two documents (⚠️ still being checked exact date; not stated in either archived text). Total planned spending for 2025-26 is stated as $2,992,971,949 with 17,289 planned FTEs (2f5441366031) — matching the backgrounder's cited figures exactly, confirming this document is the backgrounder's own cited source.

Budget divergence, FY2025-26: the $2,992,971,949 planned-spending figure in dp-2026 is roughly $527 million higher than the $2,465,267,413 that drr-2024's own forward-looking table (produced roughly a year earlier) had projected for the same fiscal year. The archived series does not itself explain this gap with a dollar-figure citation; it is consistent with new funding tied to "Canada's Border Plan" named in dp-2026, but no document in this set states an announcement date or budget line for that Plan. ⚠️ still being checked.
