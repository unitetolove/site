Mandate & statutory basis

Agricorp is established under the Agricorp Act, 1996, S.O. 1996, c. 17, Sched. A: "The AgriCorp Act, 1996 established Agricorp as a provincial Crown corporation without share capital on January 1, 1997" (Agricorp 2024-25 Annual Report, notes to financial statements, https://www.agricorp.com/SiteCollectionDocuments/Agricorp-AnnualReport-2025-en.pdf, fetched and parsed directly; doc-shelf document id on-agricorp-ar-2025). The consolidated e-Laws pointer for this citation, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/96a17, returns HTTP 200 (confirmed by direct fetch) but its content is client-side (JavaScript) rendered and could not be read through available tools this review — ⚠️ still being checked the deep-linked text against the Ontario e-Laws portal (https://www.ontario.ca/laws) directly. Agricorp's mandate, "as outlined in the AgriCorp Act," is to administer crop insurance plans under the Agricultural Products Insurance Act, 1996, S.O. 1996, c. 17, Sched. C (independently confirmed by direct fetch of the sibling schedule, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/96c17, via r.jina.ai text-rendering fallback, which defines "AgriCorp" as "the corporation established in section 1 of the AgriCorp Act, 1996"), and to perform other duties conferred by other Ontario statutes, Lieutenant Governor in Council order, or agreement (Annual Report 2024-25, cited above).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Agricorp delivers financial and risk-management programs to Ontario's agricultural industry on behalf of the provincial and federal governments, including Production Insurance (dating to 1966, operating under the Agricultural Products Insurance Act) and the Risk Management Program Fund (established by Minister's Order in 2021) (Annual Report 2024-25, cited above). "The Ontario Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness (OMAFA) and Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada (AAFC) are responsible for program policy, while Agricorp is responsible for program delivery and customer service" (same source). In 2024-25 the programs supported over 47,000 Ontario farmers with $538 million in payments and underwrote $6-8 billion in risk (same source).

Governance & reporting line

Agricorp is "a crown agent of the Government of Ontario," "board-governed," operating with independent decision-making within a governance framework tied to OMAFA (Annual Report 2024-25, cited above; registry row on-agricorp, source authority: Ontario Public Appointments Secretariat agencies list). The Annual Report is signed by the Board Chair and the Finance and Audit Committee Chair (same source).

Budget scale

~$458.4 million in total revenue against $345.2 million in total expenses for fiscal year ended March 31, 2025 (Statement of Operations, government contributions, premiums, and investment income across the General, Production Insurance, and Risk Management Program funds), per the Agricorp 2024-25 Annual Report (https://www.agricorp.com/SiteCollectionDocuments/Agricorp-AnnualReport-2025-en.pdf, fetched and parsed directly; doc-shelf document id on-agricorp-ar-2025, financial statements section).

Institutional history

Established January 1, 1997 under the Agricorp Act, 1996 as a provincial Crown corporation without share capital, succeeding earlier program-specific delivery (Production Insurance itself dates to 1966 under the predecessor Crop Insurance Act) (Annual Report 2024-25, notes to financial statements, cited above). No further renaming has been identified in the sources reviewed.

Backgrounder summary

Agricorp is a provincial Crown corporation without share capital, established January 1, 1997 under the AgriCorp Act, 1996, S.O. 1996, c. 17, Sched. A, succeeding earlier program-specific delivery (Production Insurance itself dates to 1966 under the predecessor Crop Insurance Act). Its statutory mandate is to administer crop insurance plans under the Agricultural Products Insurance Act, 1996 and to perform other duties conferred by other Ontario statutes, Lieutenant Governor in Council order, or agreement. Agricorp delivers financial and risk-management programs to Ontario's agricultural industry on behalf of the provincial and federal governments — chiefly Production Insurance and the Risk Management Program Fund (established by Minister's Order in 2021) — with the Ontario Ministry of Agriculture (OMAFA/OMAFRA) and Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada responsible for program policy while Agricorp is responsible for delivery and customer service. It is a board-governed crown agent tied to the ministry through a governance framework, and in 2024-25 its programs supported over 47,000 Ontario farmers with $538 million in payments, underwriting $6-8 billion in risk annually, against ~$458.4 million in total revenue and $345.2 million in total expenses for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025.

Priority evolution

2006-07 (ar-2007, c6df6b22d4b9): Vision was "to be the recognized providers of agricultural risk management products." The Board had set five strategic objectives (customer needs, delivery quality/efficiency, product innovation, employee environment, community contribution) tied to a five-pillar Balanced Scorecard (customer service, delivery, product-market commitment, people, community), with two more pillars — Public Accountability and Future Orientation — announced as forthcoming additions. Core programs: CAIS, Production Insurance, Advance Payments Program, Dairy Credit Program, Farm Business Registration (FBR), Grain Financial Protection Program (GFPP), Plum Pox Eradication Program, Self-Directed Risk Management (SDRM), plus several ad hoc programs. The Chair's message describes an "ambitious business improvement plan" needed to keep pace with "an unprecedented number of program changes and ad hoc programs" (c6df6b22d4b9).

2007-08 (ar-2008, 4925e226c851): CAIS is renamed AgriStability; a new core program, the Ontario Risk Management Program for Grain and Oilseed Producers, appears for the first time, alongside continuing ad hoc programs (Ontario Cattle, Hog and Horticulture Payment; Ontario Cost-Recognition Top-up; Ontario Juice Grape Transition; two CAIS transition/top-up programs). Agricorp reports administering "nine core, and five ad hoc" programs and delivering almost $613 million in program benefits through 134,000 cheques (4925e226c851).

2008-09 (ar-2009, 9e10bdbcf9d7): The Auditor General of Ontario conducted a Value for Money Audit "at the request of the Minister." Agricorp's CEO's message states the organization "worked closely with the Ministry to develop a Renewal Plan, which lays out specific plans for the next one to two years to make our service and delivery better," committing to faster/more accurate processing, better service, and clearer program information (9e10bdbcf9d7).

2009-10 (ar-2010, ff509e2b282d): First year of a new Board Chair, who reports a new Governance and Human Resources Committee was created in 2009 to formalize board governance and HR oversight, and that customer-satisfaction scores improved following execution of the prior year's Renewal Plan (ff509e2b282d).

2011-12 (ar-2012, 2e4ba3d84380), 15th-anniversary report: A new Risk Management Program (RMP) for livestock launches, covering four commodity groups. Agricorp describes delivering "eight risk management programs," reports growth since 1997 from "53 different agricultural commodities" and ~24,000 producers to over 200 commodities and roughly double the producer base, and names three "key foundational pieces" of strategy: operational effectiveness, a holistic customer experience, and leveraging core strengths. Board oversight secures new funding for a technology-infrastructure investment (2e4ba3d84380).

2012-13 (ar-2013, 71c039a30cc3): In spring 2012 the Ontario government announced it would cap RMP government funding at $100 million per year (covering both payments and administration costs) starting with the 2013 program year onward — a new, capped funding approach developed jointly with OMAF/MRA and RMP reference committees, replacing open-ended funding (71c039a30cc3).

2013-14 (ar-2014, eb3371edc945): The Board sets "three directional themes" for the year: operational efficiency/effectiveness ("to continue to demonstrate value for money"), infrastructure investment for a holistic customer experience, and reducing producer burden. An internal "Agricorp Review," including a KPMG benchmarking assessment against other jurisdictions, concludes Agricorp is "an efficient and effective delivery agent." The federal-provincial-territorial Growing Forward 2 policy framework (2013-2018) takes effect, changing AgriStability's coverage formula (eb3371edc945).

2014-15 (ar-2015, da05cc63e6aa): A new ad hoc beekeeper program is launched; preliminary work begins on two new programs for the grape and wine industry announced in March 2015 (da05cc63e6aa).

2015-16 (ar-2016, 76e521729c6e): RMP funding is reported at $118.4 million "for multiple program years," reflecting carryover within the $100-million-per-year cap set in 2012-13 (76e521729c6e).

[Fiscal 2017-18 — no annual-report narrative archived; see Residuals & gaps. Financial statements only: fs-2018, b8c17d317107, showing total revenue $188.834 million against total expenses $151.775 million.]

2018-19 (ar-2019, 469a6ecca21c): The 2018-21 Business Plan frames three standing goals — "Agricorp is easy to do business with," "Agricorp has efficient and effective operations," "Agricorp is ready to deliver" — that persist, with the same wording, through every later business-plan cycle read in this series. The federal-provincial-territorial Canadian Agricultural Partnership (CAP, April 2018-March 2023, a five-year $3-billion investment across six priority areas: science/this library's internal records, markets and trade, environmental sustainability and climate change, value-added agriculture and agri-food processing, public trust, and risk management) replaces Growing Forward 2, changing AgriStability's reference-margin floor to a guaranteed 70% and simplifying enrolment via industry benchmarks. New programs begin: the Edible Horticulture Support Program, the Provincial Premises Registry, the Farm Property Class Tax Rate Program, and the Beef Cattle Financial Protection Program. The plan also names implementation of "recommendations from the Auditor General of Ontario's value-for-money audit of farm support programs" — a second, later AG audit distinct from 2008-09 — as a business-plan goal, and reports that an emergent "DON situation" (a grain-contamination issue) forced resources to be shifted from planned initiatives such as agricorp.com redesign and a Farm Business Registration review (469a6ecca21c).

2019-20 (ar-2020, 9c2507b9c74b): The fiscal year ends with COVID-19 business-continuity implementation in March 2020, moving staff to remote work while maintaining customer service; RMP call volumes rise as customers seek coverage information (9c2507b9c74b).

2020-21 (ar-2021, 0e8525d27fab): Two ad hoc AgriRecovery programs are delivered — the Canada-Ontario COVID-19 Hog Maintenance Feed Initiative ($1.2 million in payments) and the Canada-Ontario COVID-19 AgriRecovery Beef Emergency Feed Maintenance Initiative ($1.4 million) — both federal-provincial, both COVID-specific. Agricorp reports working with the ministry "to implement changes following the Minister's announcement of increased funding for the Risk Management Program" (0e8525d27fab). Financial-statement notes from later years (fs-2024, d1cf11a3db42) confirm the Risk Management Program Fund itself "was established in 2021 for Cattle, Grains and Oilseeds, Hogs, Sheep and Veal components," replacing/superseding the flat $100-million funding-cap model set in 2012.

2021-24 Business Plan (bp-2021, 73a9fc9b337a): Restates the same three strategic goals (easy to do business with; efficient and effective operations; ready to deliver) and describes CAP's six priority areas, including a government caveat that pandemic-level program spending "is not sustainable over the long term" (73a9fc9b337a).

2021-22 (ar-2022, 608137723c53), Agricorp's 25th-anniversary report: The Board refreshes Agricorp's vision and mission for the first time since inception — new vision "Excellence in delivery to help grow Ontario agriculture," new mission "We provide financial and risk management programs for the farmers of Ontario and empower employees to deliver a trusted customer experience" — explicitly timed to the 25th anniversary. The RMP Fund enhancements (livestock classification changes) are implemented via a new Guidewire IT system. Seven one-time/emergency programs are delivered, including drought relief for northwestern Ontario, agri-tourism support, and pork-sector support; AgriStability coverage is increased and COVID-19 labour-disruption coverage under Production Insurance continues (608137723c53).

2022-23 (ar-2023, a52ed403b2e6): In December 2022 federal, provincial, and territorial agriculture ministers reach agreement on the Sustainable Canadian Agricultural Partnership (SCAP, April 2023-March 2028), replacing CAP; the same agreement raises the AgriStability compensation rate from 70% to 80% starting with the 2023 program year. Language shifts toward "modernizing and redeploying resources to priority areas" (a52ed403b2e6).

2023-24 (ar-2024, 5e183f165c27): Following an October 2022 letter of direction from the Minister of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs to Agricorp's Board Chair, the 2023-26 Business Plan restates the same three strategic goals. The report still refers throughout to "OMAFRA" (Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs) (5e183f165c27).

2024-25 (ar-2025, faacac085a46; corroborated by fs-2025, ab25b2e045d4): For the first time in the archived series, the ministry is named "Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness (OMAFA)" rather than OMAFRA — the rename occurred sometime between publication of ar-2024 (still OMAFRA) and ar-2025/fs-2025 (both OMAFA). The 2024-27 Business Plan is referenced, funding a new Winery Agri-Tourism Economic Relief Grant program and Provincial Premises Registry changes as "Strategic Investments." The report states the 2025-28 Business Plan was submitted to the Minister on February 28, 2025. Agricorp reports $538 million in 2024-25 payments to over 47,000 farmers and $6-8 billion in risk underwritten annually (faacac085a46).

Fiscal 2025-26 (fs-2026, f06f6fe1b899) — financial statements only, no annual-report narrative yet archived: Total revenues of $425.944 million against total expenses of $437.533 million — a shortfall year, in contrast to the prior year's (2024-25) surplus of $458.436 million in revenue against $345.200 million in expenses (the same comparative figures the backgrounder cites for 2024-25). This is the single most recent document in the archive; no narrative explanation for the swing is available in this document set (f06f6fe1b899).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Agricorp's own operational address, printed in annual reports from ar-2007 through ar-2014 (c6df6b22d4b9 through eb3371edc945), is in Guelph, Ontario (postal code N1H 8M4) — not Toronto. Its Board of Directors, per biographical listings in ar-2025 (faacac085a46), is drawn from farming regions across the province (Guelph, Halton Region, Tillsonburg, Niagara Region, Perth County, and others named in earlier reports such as Leamington, Middlesex County, Nipissing District, and Berwick), consistent with a mandate to serve producers province-wide rather than an urban or Toronto-specific constituency. Agricorp's core mandate — Production Insurance, AgriStability, and the Risk Management Program — serves crop and livestock producers, a customer base concentrated in rural and small-town Ontario; the archived series gives no indication of urban Toronto program delivery, Toronto-area customer concentration, or a Toronto office presence for Agricorp itself.
