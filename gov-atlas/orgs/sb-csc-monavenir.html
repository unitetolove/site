Conseil scolaire catholique MonAvenir - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: sb-csc-monavenir (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

MonAvenir is a French-language Catholic district school board constituted and governed under the Education Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.2 (consolidated text verified at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e02, title confirmed via direct fetch). Its parent ministry per the registry is the Ontario Ministry of Education (registry row sb-csc-monavenir, parent_org on-ministry-education).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

MonAvenir describes its mission as academic excellence and the "développement intégral de chaque élève" (holistic development of each student), grounded in Catholic values (cscmonavenir.ca, fetched via jina). It operates across a large south-central Ontario jurisdiction spanning Toronto, Hamilton, Kawartha Lakes and the regional municipalities of Durham, York, Peel, Halton, Niagara and Waterloo, running dozens of elementary and secondary schools (English Wikipedia summary of MonAvenir's board profile, cross-referenced against cscmonavenir.ca's own regional description; ⚠️ still being checked current school/enrollment counts against MonAvenir's own annual report, as the ~47 elementary/12 secondary/13,600-student figures found are dated to 2010-11). Its current strategy document is "Plan stratégique Horizon 2027" (registry row sb-csc-monavenir, strategy_current_url).

Governance & reporting line

Governance is by an elected Conseil scolaire (board of trustees) chaired by a board chair, with an administrative team led by a Director of Education, operating from the Centre d'éducation catholique Omer-Deslauriers in Toronto (cscmonavenir.ca, fetched). As a district school board under the Education Act, MonAvenir reports to the Minister of Education for funding, accountability and provincial policy compliance (Education Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.2, general school-board governance framework, verified via title fetch; registry row sb-csc-monavenir).

Budget scale

~$320M approved budget for the 2025-2026 school year, with an approved deficit of 0.86% (below the ministry's 1% ceiling) (cscmonavenir.ca, "Le Conseil scolaire catholique MonAvenir adopte son budget pour l'année 2025-2026," fetched via jina reader).

Institutional history

MonAvenir was established in 1998 as French-language Separate District School Board No. 64 during Ontario's province-wide school-board restructuring, consolidating several French Catholic boards including the Conseil des écoles catholiques du Grand Toronto; it was renamed Conseil scolaire de district catholique Centre-Sud in 1999 and adopted its current name, Conseil scolaire catholique MonAvenir, in May 2017. ⚠️ still being checked - this history is drawn from Wikipedia's board profile, not independently confirmed against a primary Ministry of Education or board source this review.

Backgrounder summary

MonAvenir is a French-language Catholic district school board constituted under the Education Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.2, reporting to the Ontario Minister of Education. It serves a large south-central Ontario jurisdiction spanning Toronto, Hamilton, Kawartha Lakes and the regional municipalities of Durham, York, Peel, Halton, Niagara and Waterloo. Its current strategy document is "Plan stratégique Horizon 2027." Approved budget for 2025-2026 was reported at ~$320M with an approved deficit of 0.86% (below the ministry's 1% ceiling). Institutional history traces to 1998 creation as French-language Separate District School Board No. 64, renamed Centre-Sud in 1999, and MonAvenir in May 2017 (backgrounder flags this history as ⚠️ still being checked, sourced to Wikipedia only, not corroborated in the archived series below).

Priority evolution

Fall 2021 - Fall 2022, strategic plan development (sp-2022, d9d2eb867428): MonAvenir undertook development of "Plan stratégique Horizon 2027" (2022-2027) starting fall 2021, through a consultation process the plan states involved 4,000 people from the school community (staff, students, parents, partners). Three axes were adopted and remain unchanged through every later document in this series: (1) "École catholique de langue française bienveillante et équitable" (caring, equitable French Catholic school), (2) "Pédagogie innovante, engageante et authentique" (innovative, engaging, authentic pedagogy), (3) "Organisation agile" (agile organization). Implementation began September 2022 (sp-2022, d9d2eb867428; ar-2023, c08c872bbff8).

FYE Aug 31, 2022 (fs-2022, bd3c3c28045c): Consolidated actual revenue $321,966,166 against actual expenses (originally reported) $306,898,669, an operating surplus of $15,067,497; accumulated surplus (originally reported) $191,816,804; net debt (originally reported) $(267,658,252); capital assets $457,810,965. The board carried $20,500,000 in temporary borrowing at 3.57% interest during this year, the only year in the series to show temporary borrowing before it recurs in FY2024-25.

2022-2023 school year, Year 1 of Horizon 2027 (ar-2023, c08c872bbff8): First-year implementation results: 96% of schools engaged with the components of the "école catholique de langue française bienveillante et équitable" (ECLFBE) concept, against a target of 75-95% (later exceeded); student positive-mental-health-perception at 67%, below the 75-90% target. 13% of students were reported born outside Canada. The board reported an adopted budget of $297.3M for 2022-2023. A leadership transition in the Director of Education role occurred during this reporting year, alongside a change of board chair; the report credits the outgoing administration with the plan's development. MonAvenir Virtuel (MAVI), a virtual school opened September 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic, ceased operations at the end of the 2022-2023 school year "suite à la fin du financement" (following the end of funding) — it does not reappear in any later document in this series. The "Services à l'élève" unit was renamed "Service de l'éducation spécialisée" during 2023-2024, per the report's own footnote, "conformément à la terminologie privilégiée par le ministère de l'Éducation" (ar-2023, c08c872bbff8).

FYE Aug 31, 2023 (fs-2023, 10a2dc695f83): Actual revenue $328,893,311 against actual expenses $312,317,367, surplus $16,575,944 — the highest operating surplus in the archived series. Temporary borrowing fell to zero (from $20.5M the prior year). A new PSAB accounting standard for asset-retirement obligations (primarily building-asbestos decommissioning liabilities) was adopted this year and applied retroactively, restating FY2022's accumulated surplus down by $2,977,360 to $188,669,071 and its net debt to $(273,366,857) — a technical restatement, not a change in board priorities, but material to reading the multi-year trend (fs-2023, 10a2dc695f83, note 4).

2023-2024 school year, Year 2 of Horizon 2027 (ar-2024, 54dcf8ca05ee): ECLFBE school-engagement rate reached 100% (up from 96%). Student mental-health perception remained below 70% for a second consecutive year; the report explicitly cites a 2023 provincial CAMH survey (38% of surveyed students reporting mental health "not very good or bad") and names a new "Plan stratégique de santé mentale et de lutte contre la dépendance 2024-2027," layered onto the pre-existing 2022-2025 three-year mental-health action plan. In April 2024 the board published the first "Plan pour la réussite des élèves" (Student Achievement/Success Plan, PRÉ) — a new, ministry-indicator-driven planning layer distinct from and additional to Horizon 2027. 14% of students were reported born outside Canada (up from 13%). Webmarketing recruitment impressions grew from 8.4 million (2022-2023) to 22 million (2023-2024), described in the report as "près de trois fois plus" (nearly three times more). Adopted budget for 2023-2024: $302.6M. 1,026 graduates in June 2024, described as a new record (ar-2024, 54dcf8ca05ee).

FYE Aug 31, 2024 (fs-2024, 50060d382886): Actual revenue $360,509,500 against actual expenses $353,648,449, surplus $6,861,051 — roughly 40% of the prior year's surplus dollar-for-dollar despite higher revenue and expense totals. Temporary borrowing reappeared at $1,550,000. Accumulated surplus $212,106,066; net debt $(309,482,903); capital assets $520,543,052, up from $497,614,406 the prior year, consistent with active school construction reported in the same-year annual report (fs-2024, 50060d382886).

2024-2025 school year, Year 3 of Horizon 2027 (ar-2025, 40bdb173b919): ECLFBE engagement held at 100% (stable since 2023-2024). Student affinity groups for equity-seeking student populations expanded to seven in secondary schools, described as "quatre de plus que l'an dernier" (four more than last year). A pedagogical day on September 27, 2024 for the first time in this series named artificial intelligence explicitly as a professional-development and classroom topic ("exploration d'outils d'intelligence artificielle"), and a pilot AI-literacy workshop reached 150 students in grades 4-8 — the first appearance of AI-specific programming anywhere in the archived series. A new skilled-trades promotion initiative, "prendsTECH," launched for students in grades 5-8 and their families. In August 2025 the board created a new "chef du Service de la planification et de la gestion immobilière" (Chief of Real Estate Planning and Management Service) position, which the report frames explicitly as a response to "la croissance soutenue du Csc MonAvenir" (the board's sustained growth) — a mandate/organizational-structure addition not present in any earlier report in this series. Adopted budget for 2024-2025: $309.9M. 1,026 graduates in June 2025, matching the prior year's record. Recruitment marketing scaled further: 3.1 million website views and 22 million web-marketing impressions across three seasonal campaigns (fall secondary, winter elementary/secondary, spring kindergarten) (ar-2025, 40bdb173b919).

FYE Aug 31, 2025 (fs-2025, 665ad200e218): Actual revenue $337,348,726 against actual expenses $337,086,496 — a near break-even year, surplus of just $262,230, continuing a three-year decline from $16.6M (FY2023) to $6.9M (FY2024) to $0.26M (FY2025). Temporary borrowing rose again to $2,175,000. Accumulated surplus essentially flat at $212,368,296 (from $212,106,066); net debt continued to worsen to $(325,157,701); capital assets continued to grow to $536,621,070 (fs-2025, 665ad200e218).

FY2025-2026 adopted budget (bud-2026, a40035e516db): Estimated total revenue $317,042,027 (down from the 2024-2025 in-year revised figure of $322,713,930); estimated total expenses $319,602,602; a planned deficit of $(2,560,575) — the first explicitly budgeted deficit anywhere in this archived series, following three years of shrinking-but-positive actual operating surpluses. Core provincial education funding (Financement principal de l'éducation, FPE) rose to $299,217,086 from $290,827,436 revised the prior year, driven mainly by growth in the classroom-staffing envelope ($152,929,777, up from $148,287,079) and the special-education envelope ($31,813,415, up from $30,737,831). Capital-related revenue fell sharply to $16,321,614 from $26,527,254 the prior year, driven by a drop in the "amélioration de l'état des écoles" (school-condition-improvement) allocation from $18,346,201 to $8,949,167 (bud-2026, a40035e516db).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — "Plan pour la réussite des élèves" (PRÉ): absent from sp-2022 and ar-2023; first published April 2024 and reported from ar-2024 (54dcf8ca05ee) onward as a standing, ministry-driven accountability layer distinct from Horizon 2027.
  • Added — dedicated mental-health strategic plan (2024-2027): first named in ar-2024 (54dcf8ca05ee), supplementing (not replacing) the pre-existing 2022-2025 three-year mental-health action plan; both are still cited together in ar-2025 (40bdb173b919).
  • Added — AI/STEM literacy programming: absent from sp-2022, ar-2023, and ar-2024; first appears in ar-2025 (40bdb173b919) via a September 2024 pedagogical day and a 150-student pilot workshop.
  • Added — dedicated real-estate/facilities-planning leadership role: the Chief of Real Estate Planning and Management Service position, created August 2025, appears only in ar-2025 (40bdb173b919), explicitly tied to sustained enrollment/facilities growth.
  • Added — "prendsTECH" skilled-trades initiative: first appears in ar-2025 (40bdb173b919), targeting grades 5-8.
  • Added — expanded student affinity-group structure: individual named groups first appear in ar-2024 (54dcf8ca05ee, two named secondary schools); systemized to seven secondary-level groups by ar-2025 (40bdb173b919).
  • Dropped — MonAvenir Virtuel (MAVI): the pandemic-era virtual school (opened September 2020) ceased operations at the end of the 2022-2023 school year due to the end of its funding, per ar-2023 (c08c872bbff8); it is absent from every subsequent document in the series.
  • Renamed — "Services à l'élève" → "Service de l'éducation spécialisée": renamed during 2023-2024 to align with Ministry of Education terminology, per a footnote in ar-2023 (c08c872bbff8).
  • Unchanged — the Horizon 2027 three-axis framework: "École catholique de langue française bienveillante et équitable," "Pédagogie innovante, engageante et authentique," and "Organisation agile" remain the organizing structure across every document from sp-2022 (d9d2eb867428) through ar-2025 (40bdb173b919) — unlike some peer organizations in this series, MonAvenir's top-level strategic architecture shows no restructuring across the archived window; only underlying initiatives, indicators, and results shift year to year.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

The archived series confirms MonAvenir's operations span the full jurisdiction described in the backgrounder, with Toronto named specifically in multiple contexts rather than only in boilerplate. Toronto-area schools with named activity or facility investment include an elementary school in Toronto that gained additional daycare space (ar-2023, c08c872bbff8; ar-2024, 54dcf8ca05ee) and a Toronto secondary school that hosted student-wellbeing workshops for Black-identifying students (ar-2023, c08c872bbff8). Nine schools from the Toronto region visited the Ontario Science Centre's "Génie autochtone" exhibit as part of Indigenous-education programming (ar-2023, c08c872bbff8). For the province-wide "Jour des Francos" flag-raising, ar-2025 names two separate Toronto sites — Toronto City Hall (organized by ACFO-Toronto) and Queen's Park (organized by the Assemblée de la francophonie de l'Ontario) — alongside a further eleven municipalities/regions spanning the board's jurisdiction: Kitchener, Cambridge, Guelph, Barrie, Penetanguishene, Orillia, Collingwood, CFB Borden, York Region, Brampton, Mississauga, Burlington, Milton, Oakville, Hamilton, and the Niagara region (ar-2025, 40bdb173b919). Facility investment across the series concentrates outside the City of Toronto proper but within the broader jurisdiction: a new secondary school in Vaughan (opened and blessed in 2024-2025), a fourth MonAvenir elementary school under construction in Mississauga, land purchased for a Brampton secondary school, provincial funding announced for a new secondary school in Milton, expansion work in Hamilton (a new secondary school requiring board lobbying of the municipality), and renovation/daycare projects in Markham, Welland, and Brantford (ar-2023, c08c872bbff8; ar-2024, 54dcf8ca05ee; ar-2025, 40bdb173b919). ⚠️ Still being checked: none of the archived documents break out a Toronto-specific enrollment count, school count, or budget share from board-wide totals; any claim of a distinct Toronto-only fiscal or enrollment figure would not be grounded in this series.
