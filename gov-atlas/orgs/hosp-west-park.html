West Park Healthcare Centre - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: hosp-west-park (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

West Park operated as a public hospital under the Public Hospitals Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.40, which requires every Ontario public hospital to have an approved governing board and empowers the Minister of Health to set standards and issue binding directives (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p40, verified). In April 2024 West Park was amalgamated into University Health Network (UHN) by statutory amalgamation under the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010 (Ontario); the combined entity continues under the UHN name and UHN's own special act, the University Health Network Act, 1997 (BLG deal summary, https://www.blg.com/en/about-us/deals-and-suits/2024/04/uhn-merges-with-west-park-healthcare-centre-creating-world-class-rehabilitation-centre; registry row hosp-west-park). ⚠️ Still being checked: the precise current statutory status of the West Park site (e.g., whether it remains separately "approved" as a public hospital under the PHA or operates solely as a UHN facility) was not confirmed this review — West Park's own site was TLS-unreachable this review (registry notes).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Prior to and through the transition, West Park provided complex rehabilitation, respiratory, and long-term/complex continuing care in west Toronto (registry row hosp-west-park; source authority financial statements, below). Its FY2023-24 financial statements record a dedicated "Amalgamation" expense line of $5,089,425, consistent with an active integration into UHN during that fiscal year (West Park Healthcare Centre Financial Statements, year ended March 31, 2024, PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP audit, https://www.uhn.ca/corporate/AboutUHN/Fiscal_Accountability/Documents/WP-AFS-2024.pdf).

Governance & reporting line

The FY2024 audited financial statements are addressed "To the Board of Trustees of University Health Network," indicating West Park's governance had moved under UHN's board by the close of that fiscal year, with UHN reporting in turn to Ontario Health per the registry's listed parent_org (on-ontario-health) and to the Minister of Health under the Public Hospitals Act framework (https://www.uhn.ca/corporate/AboutUHN/Fiscal_Accountability/Documents/WP-AFS-2024.pdf; registry row hosp-west-park). ⚠️ Still being checked: the exact post-amalgamation board/committee structure specific to the former West Park site was not independently confirmed this review.

Budget scale

~$156.7M total revenue and ~$154.8M total expenses for the year ended March 31, 2024, of which $132.9M was Ministry of Health/Ministry of Long-Term Care funding (West Park Healthcare Centre Financial Statements, FY2024, https://www.uhn.ca/corporate/AboutUHN/Fiscal_Accountability/Documents/WP-AFS-2024.pdf). This is the last fiscal year reported for West Park as a distinct financial reporting entity ahead of full UHN consolidation.

Institutional history

Founded 1904 by philanthropist Sir William Gage (originating from the 1896 National Sanitarium Association's tuberculosis-hospital work); operated for decades as an independent non-profit hospital corporation under the Public Hospitals Act before amalgamating into University Health Network in April 2024 under the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010 (WebSearch summary of West Park history; BLG deal summary, https://www.blg.com/en/about-us/deals-and-suits/2024/04/uhn-merges-with-west-park-healthcare-centre-creating-world-class-rehabilitation-centre).

West Park Healthcare Centre - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: hosp-west-park / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: Read across five consecutive audited financial statements (FY2020-FY2024), the dominant story is a roughly $1.25B P3 hospital-redevelopment project (the new West Park patient care building, opened April 15, 2024) moving from early construction financing into substantial completion, pushing total assets from $119.3M (2021) to $1.14B (2024) and introducing an entirely new balance-sheet architecture (long-term receivable/payable pair, PS 3160 public-private-partnership accounting) not present in the earlier years. The most significant quietly-recorded change is institutional: FY2024's auditor's report is addressed "To the Board of Trustees of University Health Network" rather than West Park's own Board of Directors, and a dedicated $5,089,425 "Amalgamation" expense line appears for the first time, confirming West Park's April 2024 absorption into UHN mid-fiscal-year. The most load-bearing number is the $1,246,287,000 guaranteed cost of the 30-year design-build-finance-maintain agreement with EllisDon Infrastructure Healthcare, of which $107,643,400 was West Park's own local capital share. One open question this five-document, financials-only record cannot resolve: what West Park's ongoing governance, budget, and reporting identity looks like now that it operates as a UHN facility rather than an independent public hospital corporation (the backgrounder's own ⚠️ still being checked on this point stands unresolved by this series, which stops at FY2024 year-end, days before the amalgamation's effective date).

Backgrounder summary

West Park operated as an independent public hospital under the Public Hospitals Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.40, providing complex rehabilitation, respiratory, and long-term/complex continuing care in west Toronto, funded primarily by the Ministry of Health and Ministry of Long-Term Care (backgrounder, our research file for that body). In April 2024 it amalgamated into University Health Network (UHN) under the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010. The backgrounder's cited FY2024 budget scale (~$156.7M total revenue, ~$154.8M total expenses, $132.9M Ministry funding) is independently re-confirmed against the extracted fs-2024 text below (this brief relays, and separately re-verifies against primary text, rather than asserting independent discovery) — actual figures read: total revenue $156,717,098, total expenses $154,812,341, MOH/MLTC revenue $132,859,874 (hosp-west-park-fs-2024, sha256-12 46b1ce1ea2c6). The backgrounder's open ⚠️ still being checked on West Park's precise post-amalgamation statutory status is not resolved by this financials-only series (see Residuals & gaps).

Priority evolution

FY2021 (c6f187904f13): Baseline year, dominated by COVID-19 response. West Park describes itself simply as "a public hospital that provides specialized rehabilitation for patients recovering from life-altering accidents and illnesses," operating a Prosthetics and Orthotics manufacturing facility, an Assessment Centre for insurance-industry third-party opinions, and a 200-bed long-term care facility operated by Extendicare (Canada) Inc. COVID-19 funding claims total $16,771,681 for the year. The campus development project (new patient care building, EllisDon P3 agreement signed August 8, 2018) is in early construction, with only $34,849,912 in cumulative restricted MOH funding received to date and no draws yet made against the $127,000,000 credit facility.

FY2022 (99c2cbc7baa8): Construction accelerates sharply — restricted assets for the redevelopment jump from $2.5M to $107.0M as MOH funding flows ($188,532,786 cumulative), and property, plant and equipment more than doubles to $129.2M. Governance references begin naming Ontario Health explicitly as the post-LHIN funder ("effective April 1, 2021, OH assumed all Toronto Central Local Health Integration Network responsibilities"). COVID-19 funding claims decline to $5,317,700.

FY2023 (92c50956a1f4): Construction continues at scale (property, plant and equipment reaches $273.1M; cumulative MOH funding $282.7M). A new priority appears for the first time: Asset Retirement Obligations (PS 3280 adoption), recognizing a $1,339,000 obligation for estimated asbestos-abatement costs on specific buildings. Labour relations become a live financial item — the Bill 124 unconstitutionality ruling (Ontario Superior Court, November 2022) triggers a reopener clause with the Ontario Nurses Association, and West Park accrues a $2,958,284 liability for the resulting compensation award.

FY2024 (46b1ce1ea2c6): Two major, simultaneous shifts. First, substantial completion of the new patient care building (November 21, 2023) and its April 15, 2024 opening trigger adoption of PS 3160 (Public-Private Partnerships) accounting — the single largest structural change in the series, restating opening 2023 property, plant and equipment upward by $313,386,684 and introducing a new $216,222,417 long-term payable to EllisDon alongside a matching long-term receivable from the Ministry of Health. Total assets leap to $1,136,250,537. Second, the April 2024 amalgamation into UHN appears directly: the auditor's report is now addressed to "the Board of Trustees of University Health Network," a dedicated $5,089,425 Amalgamation expense line appears (restructuring salaries/benefits $4,120,397 + transaction expenses $969,028), and the Bill 124 liability is resolved to nil after the province repealed Bill 124 in February 2024, with West Park recognizing $12,882,495 in associated MOH revenue against $11,949,996 in associated expenses.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — Asset Retirement Obligations accounting (PS 3280): absent fs-2021/fs-2022; first adopted fs-2023 (92c50956a1f4), recognizing $1,339,000 in estimated asbestos-related retirement costs.
  • Added — Public-Private Partnership accounting (PS 3160): absent fs-2021 through fs-2023; adopted fs-2024 (46b1ce1ea2c6) upon the new building's substantial completion, restating FY2023 opening property, plant and equipment by +$313,386,684 and introducing long-term payable/receivable line items not present in any prior year.
  • Added — "Amalgamation" as a named expense category: absent from every prior year; appears for the first time, and only, in fs-2024 ($5,089,425) (46b1ce1ea2c6), reflecting the April 2024 UHN amalgamation.
  • Renamed — governing board addressee: the independent auditor's report is addressed "To the Directors of West Park Healthcare Centre" in fs-2021 through fs-2023, then "To the Board of Trustees of University Health Network" in fs-2024 (46b1ce1ea2c6) — the clearest single textual marker of the change in ultimate governance.
  • Quietly resolved — Bill 124 labour liability: added as a new $2,958,284 accrued liability in fs-2023 (92c50956a1f4) following the Ontario Superior Court's unconstitutionality ruling; reduced to nil in fs-2024 (46b1ce1ea2c6) after the province repealed Bill 124 in February 2024 and the arbitrated award was paid out and MOH-funded.
  • Quietly dropped — "Due from/to Ministry of Health and Long-Term Care" as a distinct current-asset/liability line: present through fs-2023; by fs-2024 funding-flow language shifts toward the long-term receivable/payable structure tied to the P3 accounting change, though a current "Due to Ministry of Health and Ministry of Long-Term Care" balance persists (46b1ce1ea2c6).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • August 8, 2018 — original EllisDon Infrastructure Healthcare P3 project agreement signed: guaranteed cost of construction, financing, and 30-year maintenance $1,246,287,000, of which West Park's local share is $107,643,400 (present in all four readable years, most recently 46b1ce1ea2c6).
  • FY2021-FY2024, cumulative — Ministry of Health capital funding received for the redevelopment climbs from $34,849,912 (c6f187904f13) to $494,363,906 cumulative by FY2024 (46b1ce1ea2c6).
  • November 21, 2023 / April 15, 2024 — new West Park patient care building reaches substantial completion and opens at 170 Emmett Avenue on the same campus as the former facility (46b1ce1ea2c6).
  • March 15, 2024 — credit agreement amended, converting a further $53,500,000 of available credit into drawn long-term debt and shifting the interest-rate basis from CDOR to CORRA (46b1ce1ea2c6).
  • April 1, 2024 — statutory amalgamation into University Health Network effective, approved by West Park's Board on November 13, 2023 and by the Ministry of Health/Ministry of Long-Term Care on February 27, 2024; $5,089,425 in amalgamation expenditures recorded in the FY2024 statement of operations (46b1ce1ea2c6).
  • February 2024 — Bill 124 repealed by the Ontario government; West Park's related $2,958,284 accrued liability drops to nil, with $12,882,495 in associated MOH revenue and $11,949,996 in associated compensation expense recognized in FY2024 (46b1ce1ea2c6).
  • March 28, 2024 — a further MOH-funded agreement signed to renovate the existing (pre-redevelopment) building into a "West Park Healthcare Centre Reactivation Care Centre," addressing "system bed capacity shortages" — final completion of the overall site transition expected before end of 2025 (46b1ce1ea2c6).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Direct — West Park is a west-Toronto public-hospital/long-term-care operator throughout this series, funded through Ontario Health's Toronto Region service accountability agreements (99c2cbc7baa8; 92c50956a1f4) and, prior to April 2021, the Toronto Central Local Health Integration Network (99c2cbc7baa8). Its physical campus, the $1.25B redevelopment project, and its 200-bed long-term care facility are all sited in Toronto. Post-amalgamation (fs-2024), it continues operating as a UHN facility at the same Toronto campus (46b1ce1ea2c6). This is core GTA municipal-adjacent health-system relevance, not indirect national-scope relevance.
