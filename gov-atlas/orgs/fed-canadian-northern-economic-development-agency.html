Canadian Northern Economic Development Agency - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Wave 1 / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canadian-northern-economic-development-agency (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

CanNor is a non-statutory federal agency: it was created in 2009 by Order in Council under the general executive-reorganization power of the Public Service Rearrangement and Transfer of Duties Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. P-34 (confirmed at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-34/, current to 2026-06-14 — no dedicated "CanNor Act" exists). It is formally constituted as a department for financial and accountability purposes by its listing in Schedule I.1 of the Financial Administration Act, which assigns it its own minister ("Minister of the Canadian Northern Economic Development Agency") (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/f-11/page-20.html). Its mandate is to "support the conditions for a sustainable, diversified, and innovative economy" across Nunavut, the Northwest Territories and Yukon (org_id: fed-canadian-northern-economic-development-agency; https://www.cannor.gc.ca/eng/1351104567432/1351104589057).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CanNor delivers regional economic-development funding programs, works with Northerners, Indigenous peoples, territorial and municipal governments, and businesses "to help build diversified and dynamic economies" in the three territories (https://www.cannor.gc.ca/eng/1351104567432/1351104589057). It is one of Canada's regional development agencies (RDAs), the federal network that delivers place-based economic programming outside the national ISED envelope (org_id: fed-canadian-northern-economic-development-agency).

Governance & reporting line

CanNor reports through its own designated minister — currently the Minister of Northern and Arctic Affairs, the Honourable Rebecca Chartrand, who is also "Minister responsible for the Canadian Northern Economic Development Agency" (https://www.cannor.gc.ca/eng/1351104567432/1351104589057). Its Schedule I.1 status under the Financial Administration Act gives it standard departmental accountability and appropriations treatment (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/f-11/page-20.html).

Budget scale

Total planned spending of $114,839,371 for 2026–27, comprising $106,881,378 for the Economic Development in the Territories core responsibility and $7,957,993 for Internal Services (Canadian Northern Economic Development Agency's 2026–27 Departmental Plan, https://www.cannor.gc.ca/eng/1768408333948/1768408407077).

Institutional history

Established in 2009 by Order in Council under the Public Service Rearrangement and Transfer of Duties Act, transferring Northern economic-development functions previously held by Indian and Northern Affairs Canada into a standalone agency and adding it to FAA Schedule I.1 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-34/; https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/f-11/page-20.html; org_id: fed-canadian-northern-economic-development-agency). ⚠️ VERIFY — exact 2009 order-in-council number not independently fetched this pass.

Backgrounder summary

CanNor is a non-statutory federal agency created in 2009 by Order in Council under the Public Service Rearrangement and Transfer of Duties Act, formally constituted as a department by its Schedule I.1 listing under the Financial Administration Act (its own minister, currently the Minister of Northern and Arctic Affairs). Its mandate is to "support the conditions for a sustainable, diversified, and innovative economy" across Nunavut, the Northwest Territories and Yukon, and it is one of Canada's regional development agencies. Planned spending for 2026-27 is $114,839,371 ($106.9M core, $8.0M internal services).
