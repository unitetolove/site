Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ontario-lottery-gaming-corporation (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation (OLG) is established by s.2(1) of the Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation Act, 1999, S.O. 1999, c. 12, Sched. L: "A corporation without share capital is hereby established to be known in English as the Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation" (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/99o12, fetched and verified, s.2(1); registry row on-ontario-lottery-gaming-corporation). Section 2(3) makes it "for all its purposes an agent of Her Majesty" (same source). The Act's stated purposes (s.0.1) are to enhance Ontario's economic development, generate revenue for the Province, promote responsible gaming, and ensure this is done for the public good (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Section 3 sets OLG's objects as developing, undertaking and managing lottery schemes "on behalf of the Crown in right of Ontario," operating gaming sites, ensuring lottery schemes and gaming sites comply with the Criminal Code and the Gaming Control Act, 1992, operating related businesses at gaming sites, and (since 2020) supporting live horse racing in Ontario (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/99o12, fetched, s.3). OLG's own annual report describes itself as reporting to a shareholder ministry and generating gaming/lottery revenue for the Province (OLG Annual Report 2024-25, registry strategy_current_url).

Governance & reporting line

The Corporation is "composed of at least five members" appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council (Act, s.2(2)), with a Minister designated by the LGIC to administer the Act (s.1, "Minister" definition) (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/99o12, fetched). Per OLG's 2024-25 Annual Report, the Board's reporting relationship changed in June 2024: "the Board began reporting to the Minister of Tourism, Culture and Gaming — transitioning from the Minister of Finance, to whom we had reported since 2009" (OLG Annual Report 2024-25, p.[chair's letter], registry strategy_current_url: https://about.olg.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/OLG-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf).

Budget scale

~$4.8B total revenues for fiscal 2024-25 (net gaming revenue, lease revenue and non-gaming revenue combined), up $108M/2% year-over-year, per audited results in the OLG Annual Report 2024-25 (registry strategy_current_url: https://about.olg.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/OLG-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf).

Institutional history

Established as a Crown-agent corporation without share capital by the Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation Act, 1999, S.O. 1999, c. 12, Sched. L, which consolidated Ontario's lottery and gaming operations under one corporation; its ministerial reporting line has shifted over time, most recently from the Ministry of Finance (reporting since 2009) to the Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming in June 2024 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/99o12, fetched; OLG Annual Report 2024-25, registry strategy_current_url).

Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 (rolling continuation) / registry: on-ontario-lottery-gaming-corporation / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is "Game ON," OLG's post-pandemic strategy launched at the start of fiscal 2021-22 with five (later six) priorities and a three-line-of-business restructuring (Lottery, Land-based Gaming, Digital). The most consequential drop is the "Path Forward" four-theme strategic plan (Digital Development, Culture of Performance, Know Our Customers, Strong and Responsible Growth), absent from the pandemic-transition-year ar-2021 and never revived. The load-bearing number is Net Profit to the Province collapsing from $2,303 million (FY2019-20) to $359 million (FY2020-21) — an approximately 84% year-over-year drop — before recovering to $2.5 billion by FY2022-23. The open question the brief flags most directly: the Windsor Casino procurement, the ninth and final gaming bundle in OLG's modernization program, remains unresolved as of the most recent archived report (fiscal 2024-25), with transition only "planned for late fiscal 2025-26."

Backgrounder summary

The Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation (OLG) is established by s.2(1) of the Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation Act, 1999, S.O. 1999, c. 12, Sched. L, as "a corporation without share capital," and is "for all its purposes an agent of Her Majesty" (s.2(3)). Its statutory purposes (s.0.1) are to enhance Ontario's economic development, generate revenue for the Province, promote responsible gaming, and do so for the public good. Its objects (s.3) are to develop, undertake and manage lottery schemes and gaming sites "on behalf of the Crown in right of Ontario," ensure Criminal Code and Gaming Control Act compliance, operate related businesses at gaming sites, and — since a 2020 amendment — support live horse racing in Ontario. Governance: a Board of at least five members appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council, with a Minister designated to administer the Act; per OLG's own 2024-25 Annual Report, the Board's reporting relationship shifted in June 2024 from the Minister of Finance (its reporting minister since 2009) to the Minister of Tourism, Culture and Gaming. Budget scale: ~$4.8B in total revenues (net gaming revenue, lease revenue and non-gaming revenue combined) for fiscal 2024-25, up 2% year-over-year. Institutionally, OLG consolidated Ontario's lottery and gaming operations under one Crown-agent corporation in 1999/2000.

Priority evolution

2011-12, launch of the Modernization Plan (ar-2012, f8860452dcae): The Chair's message frames the year around "the hiring of a new President and CEO; and the launch of OLG's modernization initiative." The plan proposes shifting day-to-day operation and capital development of gaming facilities to the private sector, launching full internet gaming, and shrinking OLG into "a smaller organization that is focused on market management and leading in responsible gambling" — projected to enable roughly $3 billion in new private capital investment and an additional $1.3 billion in annual net profit to the Province by 2017-18.

2012-13 (ar-2013, 2eb4b41d0100): PlayOLG.ca launches in January 2013, described as "the future home of online gaming." The first Requests for Pre-Qualification (RFPQs) for Land-based Gaming modernization are issued November 30, 2012, covering the Ottawa Area, East and North gaming bundles, with a Southwest RFPQ following May 29, 2013.

2013-14 (ar-2014, 5065925c3ffa): RFPQs are released for a further wave of bundles, including for the first time three GTA-area groupings: "GTA, Central and West GTA Gaming Bundles."

2014-15 (ar-2015, d82ad5e85bf8): PlayOLG launches province-wide in January 2015. The East Gaming Bundle RFP process is held out as the template "for future RFPs for the other gaming bundles," with the GTA Gaming Bundle named as "remaining in the market."

2015-16 (ar-2016, a78f84f65456): OLG completes the transition of the East Gaming Bundle to Ontario Gaming East Limited Partnership (OGELP), "the first such transition" of Land-based Gaming operations to a private-sector service provider. Fiscal 2015-16 delivers record Net Profit to the Province of "more than $2.2 billion" on $7.45 billion in revenue.

Fiscal 2018-19 business plan, the "Path Forward" strategic plan (bp-2018, 0ec775473fb0): "The foundational work completed under the modernization strategy has become the starting point of a new strategic plan – the Path Forward." Four Strategic Themes are introduced to "guide OLG's priorities over the next three to five years": Digital Development, Culture of Performance, Know Our Customers, and Strong and Responsible Growth — each with its own numbered strategic priorities and supporting tactics. The GTA Gaming Bundle (Woodbine, Ajax Downs, Great Blue Heron Casino) is reported awarded in August 2017 to Ontario Gaming GTA LP (Great Canadian Gaming Corporation and Brookfield Business Partners LP as primary partners); the West GTA Gaming Bundle (Casino Brantford, Mohawk Raceway) is pending award. OLG frames itself as targeting Top-100-Employer status and "$3.0 billion annually" in Net Profit to the Province.

FY2018-19 (ar-2019, 0bb65f2cd62e): "OLG's Path Forward" is restated with the same four customer-focused themes, now presented as a table (Strong and Responsible Growth, Digital Development, Know Our Customers, Culture of Performance) under a stated Vision ("develop world-class gaming entertainment for Ontario") and Mission (generate provincial revenue, stimulate economic development, promote responsible gambling). West GTA and Central Gaming Bundle assets/operations transfer to service providers this year.

FY2019-20 (ar-2020, 0d4886fa80ec): Path Forward continues ("OLG's Path Forward strategy puts the customer at the centre of everything we do"), but "Our Strategic Priorities" are now presented under four different labels: (1) Land-based Gaming, (2) Digital Implementation, (3) Customer Experience, (4) Refine Organizational Operating Model and Culture — a re-framing of the same four themes rather than a formal renaming. In January 2020, OLG announces its intention to bring the Windsor Gaming Bundle — the ninth and final bundle, consisting of Caesars Windsor — into the modernization program, extending the existing operator contract to August 1, 2023 to allow time for procurement. COVID-19 casino closures begin in March 2020, addressed in a dedicated "Addressing the Challenges of COVID-19" section; fiscal 2019-20 still closes with $2.3 billion in Net Profit to the Province.

FY2020-21, pandemic trough and leadership change (ar-2021, f24cd5d076e2): Duncan Hannay becomes President and CEO in October 2020, succeeding Stephen Rigby, and is described as "pursuing a new strategy to drive the next horizon of growth." No named successor strategic plan or "Strategic Priorities" section appears in this document — it is a transition year between "Path Forward" and the plan that follows. Net Profit to the Province collapses to $359 million (from $2,303 million the prior year) due to pandemic closures.

FY2021-22, launch of "Game ON" (ar-2022, 1d967f9e91ea; bp-2022, 208c6e2bd8d7 for the fiscal 2022-23 business plan restating the same framework): A new strategy named "Game ON" launches at the start of fiscal 2021-22, organized around a stated aspiration to be an "Innovative Business Leader," "Top Employer with a Winning Culture," and "Exemplary Partner known for Value Creation," delivered through five numbered strategic priorities: (1) build a fun, inclusive, performance-driven culture; (2) drive share growth in digital gaming and accelerate speed to market; (3) re-open Land-based Gaming operations and identify new growth opportunities; (4) remove barriers to expand lottery distribution and products; (5) obsess over understanding customers across products and channels. The organization is restructured around three customer-centric lines of business — Lottery, Land-based Gaming, Digital — supported by shared services. Net Profit to the Province is $1.6 billion this year, still well below pre-pandemic levels.

FY2022-23 (ar-2023, d609ad336aef): The same five Game ON priorities continue with near-identical wording. Net Profit to the Province recovers to $2.5 billion. The Great Canadian Casino Resort Toronto launch (at Woodbine) is reported delayed from Q4 fiscal 2022-23 into fiscal 2023-24 due to supply-chain and labour issues. The Windsor Casino procurement process begins, described as "the final site to go through OLG's Land-based Gaming procurement process."

FY2024-25 (ar-2025, 18143f0b23c1): Game ON expands to six strategic priorities. Priority 3 is reworded from "Re-open Land-based Gaming operations and identify new opportunities for growth" to "Optimize Land-based Gaming and execute on new opportunities for growth" (dropping the pandemic-recovery framing). A new Priority 6 is added: "Ensure internal operations enable efficient, effective and successful execution of our strategic priorities and organizational objectives." The Chair's message discloses that in June 2024 the Board's reporting relationship shifted from the Minister of Finance (its minister since 2009) to the Minister of Tourism, Culture and Gaming. The Great Canadian Casino Resort Toronto (Woodbine) is confirmed launched, in May 2024. The Windsor Casino procurement is still described as continuing, "with a transition to the modernization model planned for late fiscal 2025-26" — the ninth and final gaming bundle remains unresolved within the archived series. OLG reports working with iGaming Ontario (the AGCO-affiliated body regulating Ontario's private internet-gaming market) on anti-money-laundering measures and a shared self-exclusion system — the only mention of iGaming Ontario found anywhere in this org's archived series. Net Profit to the Province is $2.2 billion ($2.4 billion excluding a one-time provision).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — Land-based Gaming modernization / "gaming bundles" model: introduced ar-2012 (f8860452dcae) as the Modernization Plan; operationalized through nine geographically defined bundles procured 2012-2020 (RFPQs first issued per ar-2013, 2eb4b41d0100), transferring day-to-day casino/slots operations (not underlying Crown ownership) to private-sector service providers under Casino Operating and Services Agreements (COSAs), while "OLG continues to conduct and manage Land-based Gaming sites in the bundles" (ar-2021, f24cd5d076e2).
  • Added — Digital/Internet Gaming (PlayOLG): launched January 2013 (ar-2013, 2eb4b41d0100), province-wide by January 2015 (ar-2015, d82ad5e85bf8); renamed OLG.ca by fiscal 2020-21 (ar-2021, f24cd5d076e2, "OLG.ca (formerly PlayOLG)").
  • Added — "Path Forward" four-theme strategic plan (Digital Development, Culture of Performance, Know Our Customers, Strong and Responsible Growth): introduced bp-2018 (0ec775473fb0) as the successor to the Modernization Plan; restated ar-2019 (0bb65f2cd62e); still named but re-labelled into four differently-titled priorities in ar-2020 (0d4886fa80ec).
  • Dropped — "Path Forward" framework and its four named themes: absent from ar-2021 (f24cd5d076e2), the pandemic/leadership-transition year, and not revived; superseded by "Game ON."
  • Added — "Game ON" strategy and its five strategic priorities: launched at the start of fiscal 2021-22 per ar-2022 (1d967f9e91ea) and restated in bp-2022 (208c6e2bd8d7); organized around a three-line-of-business structure (Lottery, Land-based Gaming, Digital) replacing the four-theme model.
  • Renamed — Game ON Priority 3, from "Re-open Land-based Gaming operations and identify new opportunities for growth" (ar-2022, 1d967f9e91ea; ar-2023, d609ad336aef) to "Optimize Land-based Gaming and execute on new opportunities for growth" (ar-2025, 18143f0b23c1) — dropping the pandemic-recovery ("re-open") framing once operations had normalized.
  • Added — Game ON Priority 6, "Ensure internal operations enable efficient, effective and successful execution of our strategic priorities and organizational objectives": first appears ar-2025 (18143f0b23c1), expanding the framework from five to six priorities.
  • Added — horse racing support relationship: the Act's objects (s.3) were amended in 2020 to add live-horse-racing support per the backgrounder; OLG's own annual reports show continuous "Amended and Restated Funding Agreement for Live Horse Racing" payments across the archived series (e.g., $120 million in ar-2020, 0d4886fa80ec; $102 million in ar-2025, 18143f0b23c1) — a declining figure over that window, though the trend's cause is not stated in the archived text.
  • Added — iGaming Ontario coordination: first and only appearance in ar-2025 (18143f0b23c1), describing joint work with iGaming Ontario and AGCO on responsible-gambling/AML measures and a shared self-exclusion system for Ontario's regulated internet-gaming sites.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

OLG's own text places substantial, continuous Toronto-area content throughout the archived series. OLG has maintained a Toronto corporate office (4120 Yonge Street) alongside its Sault Ste. Marie office across every real-content document in the series, from ar-2012 (f8860452dcae, Suite 500) through ar-2025 (18143f0b23c1, Suite 600) — confirming an ongoing physical Toronto presence, not merely a mailing address. Most significantly, this org's own series directly documents a Greater Toronto Area Gaming Bundle: awarded in August 2017 to Ontario Gaming GTA LP (a partnership of Great Canadian Gaming Corporation and Brookfield Business Partners LP), covering Woodbine Racetrack, Ajax Downs and the Great Blue Heron Casino, with a Casino Operating and Services Agreement (COSA) effective January 23, 2018 running to January 22, 2039 (bp-2018, 0ec775473fb0; ar-2021, f24cd5d076e2). A separate, adjacent "West Greater Toronto Area Gaming Bundle" (Casino Brantford, Mohawk Raceway, Flamboro Downs, Grand River Raceway) was awarded to Ontario Gaming West GTA Limited Partnership, COSA effective May 1, 2018 (ar-2021, f24cd5d076e2). This corroborates, from OLG's own archived text, the "Greater Toronto Area Gaming Bundle" referenced in the companion Financial Accountability Office of Ontario brief. One important precision: the transaction OLG's own documents describe is a long-term operating concession (a COSA transferring day-to-day operational control and private capital investment obligations to a service-provider consortium, with OLG retaining Crown ownership and overall conduct-and-management responsibility), not a sale of Crown assets outright — ar-2021 (f24cd5d076e2) states explicitly that "OLG continues to conduct and manage Land-based Gaming sites in the bundles while the Service Providers assume control of the day-to-day operations." OLG's own series contains no mention of any subsequent resale of the GTA bundle concession or change in its private-sector partners beyond the original 2017-18 award. Separately, the GTA-area Woodbine site was rebranded the "Great Canadian Casino Resort Toronto," launching in May 2024 (ar-2025, 18143f0b23c1) after a reported delay (ar-2023, d609ad336aef).
