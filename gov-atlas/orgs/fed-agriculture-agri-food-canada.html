Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-agriculture-agri-food-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada (AAFC) is established under the Department of Agriculture and Agri-Food Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. A-9 (consolidated text verified at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/A-9/FullText.html). Section 2 states: "There is hereby established a department of the Government of Canada called the Department of Agriculture and Agri-Food over which the Minister of Agriculture and Agri-Food appointed by commission under the Great Seal shall preside" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Under s.4 of the Act, the Minister's powers and duties extend to all matters not assigned to another department relating to agriculture, agricultural products, and agricultural research including experimental farm stations (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/A-9/FullText.html). AAFC's 2026-27 Departmental Plan organizes this mandate into three core responsibilities: Domestic and International Markets, Science and Innovation, and Sector Risk, under the overarching aim to "create the conditions for the long-term profitability, sustainability and resilience of the Canadian agriculture and agri-food sector" (https://agriculture.canada.ca/en/department/transparency/departmental-plan/2026-27-departmental-plan; registry row fed-agriculture-agri-food-canada).

Governance & reporting line

AAFC is a federal government department headed by the Minister of Agriculture and Agri-Food, with a Governor-in-Council-appointed Deputy Minister as deputy head, reporting to Parliament (Department of Agriculture and Agri-Food Act, ss.2-3, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/A-9/FullText.html).

Budget scale

AAFC's total planned spending for 2026-27, including internal services, is $3,677,569,159 (~$3.68 billion), with a planned workforce of 4,801 full-time equivalents, split across Domestic and International Markets ($702.4M), Science and Innovation ($780.4M), and Sector Risk ($2.03B) (Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada's 2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://agriculture.canada.ca/en/department/transparency/departmental-plan/2026-27-departmental-plan).

Institutional history

The Department of Agriculture and Agri-Food traces to the Department of Agriculture Act era and operates today under the Department of Agriculture and Agri-Food Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. A-9, with its name and mandate having been updated to "Agriculture and Agri-Food" to reflect the expanded agri-food policy role (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/A-9/FullText.html; registry row fed-agriculture-agri-food-canada). ⚠️ still being checked - the specific year of the "Agriculture and Agri-Food" name change was not confirmed from a primary source this review.

Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: fed-agriculture-agri-food-canada / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: Across a 2020-2025 window of Departmental Plans and Departmental Results Reports, AAFC's single biggest strategic event is the April 1, 2023 replacement of the five-year, $3B Canadian Agricultural Partnership (CAP) with the five-year, $3.5B Sustainable Canadian Agricultural Partnership (Sustainable CAP) — a 25% cost-shared-funding increase and a five-priority reframing (climate change and environment; science, research and innovation; market development and trade; building sector capacity, growth and competitiveness; resiliency and public trust) that replaced CAP's simpler three-priority frame. The most quietly consequential change is budgetary, not rhetorical: starting with the 2024-25 Departmental Plan, AAFC began disclosing "Refocusing Government Spending" reductions tied to Budget 2023's government-wide $14.1B/5-year restraint commitment (rising from $17.5M in 2024-25 to $39.4M by 2026-27-and-after per dp-2024, later revised upward to $43.3M by 2026-27 per drr-2025), a fiscal-restraint frame entirely absent from every plan before 2024. The most load-bearing number is 2023-24 total actual spending of $4,163,441,399 (5,073 FTEs) — the series peak — against a smaller 2024-25 planned figure of ~$3.68B (per the backgrounder's 2026-27 figure of $3.68B, independently consistent with the declining post-CAP-transition trend visible in the archived DRRs). One open question the record cannot resolve: whether the Sustainable CAP's 2028 sunset will trigger another restructuring on the scale of the 2023 transition, or whether Refocusing/CER-style restraint becomes the dominant frame for the next framework instead.

Backgrounder summary

AAFC is established under the Department of Agriculture and Agri-Food Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. A-9, headed by the Minister of Agriculture and Agri-Food with a Governor-in-Council-appointed Deputy Minister reporting to Parliament. Its mandate spans all matters relating to agriculture, agricultural products, and agricultural research not assigned to another department. The 2026-27 Departmental Plan (not itself in this archived set, per the backgrounder) organizes the mandate into three core responsibilities — Domestic and International Markets, Science and Innovation, and Sector Risk — which the archived 2020-2025 series confirms held constant as the department's Departmental Results Framework across the entire read window. Backgrounder-cited budget scale: ~$3.68B total planned spending for 2026-27, 4,801 planned FTEs (from a document outside this archived set); the archived series' own most recent comparable figure, 2023-24 total actual spending of $4,163,441,399, is higher, consistent with the post-CAP-transition/pre-restraint peak identified below rather than a discrepancy with the backgrounder.

Priority evolution

2020-21 (dp-2020, 1a2ad6d94e9a): Three core responsibilities — Domestic and International Markets, Science and Innovation, Sector Risk — each already established, delivered through the existing five-year $3B Canadian Agricultural Partnership (2018-2023). Minister's message emphasizes trade diversification (CPTPP, CETA, CUSMA), the newly-launched Food Policy for Canada (June 2019, $134M), and a $75B agri-food/seafood export target by 2025. African swine fever prevention named as an emerging risk.

2021-22 (dp-2021, f2565beed725) and drr-2021 (77a242b13c17), reporting on 2020-21: COVID-19 response dominates both documents — Emergency Food Security Fund ($200M to food banks, 3,215 projects/$95.5M disbursed per drr-2021), Surplus Food Rescue Program ($50M), labour-shortage and supply-chain measures. The $185M Natural Climate Solutions for Agriculture Fund is announced (2020 Fall Economic Statement) as a forward climate commitment. 2020-21 actual spending: $3,037,615,395 (4,865 FTEs) (drr-2021).

2022-23 (dp-2022, 94c2ce46cd6c) and drr-2022 (e3165592c5d8), reporting on 2021-22: Continued CAP delivery in its next-to-last year; first explicit signal that the CAP's successor framework is coming — the November 2021 "Guelph Statement" is named as the shared FPT vision that will become Sustainable CAP, with five forward priority areas previewed (climate change and environment; science, research and innovation; market development and trade; building sector capacity, growth and competitiveness; resiliency and public trust). Export value reported at $82.3 billion in 2021, already surpassing the $75B-by-2025 target four years early (drr-2022). 2021-22 actual spending: $3,461,924,485 (4,984 FTEs) (drr-2022).

2023-24 (dp-2023, 69d3ca7b35c7) and drr-2023 (be849bce8fdc), reporting on 2022-23 — the transition year: dp-2023 announces the April 1, 2023 launch of the five-year (2023-2028) Sustainable Canadian Agricultural Partnership, replacing CAP with $3.5B in FPT investment (a $500M/25% increase in cost-shared funding over CAP) and the five-priority frame previewed the year before. A National Agricultural Labour Strategy is announced as a new commitment. drr-2023, reporting on the final CAP year (2022-23), confirms CAP's formal conclusion March 31, 2023 and Sustainable CAP's launch effective April 1, 2023 — both documents describe the same transition from opposite sides of the fiscal-year boundary, corroborating each other. 2022-23 actual spending: $3,606,544,918 (5,045 FTEs) (drr-2023) — the last full year under the CAP frame.

2024-25 (dp-2024/glance, b9c92f50da36) and drr-2024/glance (29b2effd5c75), reporting on 2023-24: First year fully under Sustainable CAP; dp-2024 reports its first-year performance directly — $36.4M in federal Sustainable CAP program investment plus ~$423M in FPT cost-shared programming (drr-2024). First appearance of "Refocusing government spending" reductions (dp-2024): $17,504,000 (2024-25) rising to $39,434,000 (2026-27-and-after), tied explicitly to Budget 2023's $14.1B/5-year, $4.1B/year-after government-wide restraint commitment — a budget-restraint frame with no precedent in dp-2020 through dp-2023. 2023-24 actual spending: $4,163,441,399 (5,073 FTEs) — the series' spending peak (drr-2024).

2025-26 (dp-2025/glance, 3016699abee9) and drr-2025 (84f342337e0b), reporting on 2024-25: Second full Sustainable CAP year; federal program investment nearly doubles to $62.5M with ~$481M in FPT cost-shared spending (drr-2025), evidence of a genuine ramp-up rather than a stalled framework. The Refocusing Government Spending reduction figures are revised upward from dp-2024's projections: $18,973,582 (2024-25, up from the $17.5M originally planned), $30,663,000 (2025-26), $43,334,000 (2026-27-and-after) (drr-2025) — the restraint exercise deepens rather than resolves. 2025-26 total planned spending reaches $3,935,670,445 (5,149 FTEs) (dp-2025) — a rebound above the 2022-23 actual level, consistent with Sustainable CAP ramp-up outpacing the smaller Refocusing reductions. 2024-25 actual spending: $4,030,500,570 (5,134 FTEs) (drr-2025).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — Sustainable Canadian Agricultural Partnership (2023-2028), replacing the Canadian Agricultural Partnership (2018-2023): the central structural change in the series. CAP's three priorities (growing trade and expanding markets; innovative and sustainable growth; diversity and a dynamic, evolving sector) are replaced, not merely renamed, by Sustainable CAP's five (climate change and the environment; science, research and innovation; market development and trade; building sector capacity, growth and competitiveness; resiliency and public trust) — first previewed in dp-2022 (94c2ce46cd6c), formally launched in dp-2023 (69d3ca7b35c7).
  • Added — Refocusing Government Spending reductions: absent from dp-2020 through dp-2023 and drr-2020 through drr-2023; first appears dp-2024 (b9c92f50da36) and recurs in every subsequent document through drr-2025 (84f342337e0b), with the reduction figures revised upward year over year rather than holding steady.
  • Added — National Agricultural Labour Strategy: first announced as a forthcoming commitment in dp-2023's Minister's message (69d3ca7b35c7); labour shortage is a recurring "key risk" from 2020-21 onward but a dedicated named strategy is new to 2023-24.
  • Added — Indo-Pacific Agriculture and Agri-Food Office: first appears as an opened/operating initiative in drr-2023 (be849bce8fdc, "official opening"), tied to trade diversification.
  • Quietly retired — the $75 billion agri-food/seafood export target by 2025: a headline metric in every document from dp-2020 through dp-2022, it is reported as already exceeded ($82.3B in 2021) in drr-2022 (e3165592c5d8); dp-2023 replaces it with a new target of $95 billion by 2028 (69d3ca7b35c7) — a renamed/escalated goal rather than a dropped one, but the original number disappears from later documents without being referenced again as "achieved" in later at-a-glance summaries.
  • Quietly diminished — Food Policy for Canada as a headline initiative: prominent as a named, budgeted priority (Local Food Infrastructure Fund, Food Waste Reduction Challenge, Canadian Food Policy Advisory Council) in dp-2020 through drr-2023, it is not named as a standalone "key priority" in dp-2024, dp-2025, or drr-2025's "at a glance" summaries — those documents fold food-related work into the broader Sustainable CAP frame instead. The two thinnest documents in this set (dp-2024, dp-2025 — "at a glance" only) limit how confidently this can be read as retirement versus summary compression; ⚠️ still being checked against the full-length 2024-25/2025-26 Departmental Plans if available.
  • COVID-19 response — added then fully retired: a dominant frame in dp-2021/drr-2021 (Emergency Food Security Fund, Surplus Food Rescue Program, supply-chain measures), still present as a residual theme in drr-2022, and entirely absent as a named priority from dp-2023 onward — the cleanest complete drop-off in the series, consistent with the pandemic emergency genuinely ending rather than being deprioritized.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • April 1, 2023 — Sustainable CAP launch: $3.5B five-year FPT framework replaces CAP's $3B five-year framework, a $500M/25% increase in cost-shared funding (dp-2023, 69d3ca7b35c7; drr-2023, be849bce8fdc) — the single largest structural funding event in the series.
  • 2023-24 — spending peak: total actual spending $4,163,441,399, 5,073 FTEs — the highest total spending figure recorded anywhere in this archived series (drr-2024, 29b2effd5c75).
  • dp-2024 onward — Refocusing Government Spending reductions begin, tied to Budget 2023's government-wide $14.1B/5-year restraint commitment: $17,504,000 (2024-25) → $39,434,000 (2026-27-and-after) as first projected (dp-2024, b9c92f50da36); revised to $18,973,582 (2024-25) → $43,334,000 (2026-27-and-after) in drr-2025 (84f342337e0b) — reductions delivered via contracting alignment, automation/legacy-system decommissioning, fleet right-sizing, travel reduction, and a "small proportion" of grants/contributions cuts. Both documents state the reductions are "not anticipated to negatively impact AAFC's ability to deliver results."
  • 2024-25 — Sustainable CAP federal investment nearly doubles: from $36.4M (year one, per drr-2024) to $62.5M (year two, per drr-2025), with FPT cost-shared programming rising from ~$423M to ~$481M — evidence the framework is scaling up even as Refocusing reductions are simultaneously being applied.
  • 2019-20 → 2020-21 → 2021-22 spending trajectory: not established for 2019-20 within this set (drr-2020 does not state a single top-line total in the sections read), but 2020-21 ($3,037,615,395) → 2021-22 ($3,461,924,485) → 2022-23 ($3,606,544,918) → 2023-24 ($4,163,441,399, peak) → 2024-25 ($4,030,500,570) shows steady growth through the CAP-to-Sustainable-CAP transition, peaking in the first full Sustainable CAP year before a slight pullback the following year (drr-2021; drr-2022; drr-2023; drr-2024; drr-2025).
  • Sector Risk remains the dominant spending line throughout: from $1,531,629,689 planned for 2020-21 (dp-2020) to $2,035,048,761 planned for 2025-26 (dp-2025) — consistently the largest of the three core responsibilities in every year read, driven by the demand-driven, cost-shared Business Risk Management program suite (AgriStability, AgriInvest, AgriInsurance, AgriRecovery, AgriRisk).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

AAFC is a federal department with no Toronto-specific mandate; its Ontario relevance in this archived series is functional and cost-shared rather than site-specific. The Sustainable CAP and its predecessor CAP are federal-provincial-territorial cost-shared frameworks — Ontario, as one of the ten provinces, receives a share of the ~$423M-$481M annual FPT cost-shared programming reported in drr-2024/drr-2025, but the archived documents do not break this out by province. The Living Laboratories Initiative explicitly named an Ontario site as planned for 2020 (dp-2020) and again for 2021 launch timing shifted (dp-2021) — the only Ontario-specific program element identified in this series, and even this is a rural/agricultural-landscape initiative with no GTA/Toronto-municipal dimension. No document in this set names a Toronto office, program, or initiative. Toronto/GTA relevance is therefore indirect: AAFC's national trade, food-policy, and business-risk-management work affects Ontario's agri-food sector and, via food-price and food-security channels named in the Food Policy materials, urban consumers including those in Toronto — but this is general national-policy relevance, not a documented Toronto-specific presence.
