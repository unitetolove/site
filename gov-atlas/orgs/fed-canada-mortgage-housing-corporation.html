Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canada-mortgage-housing-corporation (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) is established by s.3 of the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. C-7, which states "There is hereby established a corporation to be called the 'Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation'" with head office in Ottawa (consolidated text verified at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-7/FullText.html, ss.3-4). Section 17 gives the Corporation authority to "have, exercise and perform all rights, powers, duties, liabilities and functions of the Minister under the Housing Acts," including the National Housing Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. N-11 (same source, s.17, s.25).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CMHC's core functions under the Act include making loans, insuring and managing mortgages, and conducting housing-finance research (Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation Act, s.25, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-7/FullText.html). CMHC's own materials describe its role as "shaping housing solutions with our partners in industry, other levels of government and communities across Canada" toward safe, affordable housing, and it administers federal housing programs including the National Housing Strategy (https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/en/about-cmhc; registry row fed-canada-mortgage-housing-corporation).

Governance & reporting line

CMHC is a Crown corporation governed by a Board of Directors comprising a Governor-in-Council-appointed Chairperson, the President, two Deputy Ministers (Finance and the designated Minister), and eight additional directors; the Governor-in-Council-appointed President serves as chief executive with day-to-day operational authority (Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation Act, ss.6-7, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-7/FullText.html). The Corporation must comply with directions from the designated Minister (s.5(2), same source) and reports to Parliament through that Minister and to the public via annual reports and quarterly financial statements (https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/en/about-cmhc).

Budget scale

CMHC's 2026 operating budget is $745.9 million (~$0.75B), with a separate 2026 capital budget of $10.6 billion supporting its lending and investment programs (2026-2030 Summary of the Corporate Plan, https://assets.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/sites/cmhc/about-cmhc/corporate-reporting/summary-corporate-plan/2026/summary-corporate-plan-2026-2030-cmhc-en.pdf).

Institutional history

CMHC was established under the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation Act (originally the National Housing Act framework administered by predecessor Central Mortgage and Housing Corporation, created 1946); it operates today under R.S.C., 1985, c. C-7 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-7/FullText.html). ⚠️ still being checked - the exact 1946-era founding statute and the name change from "Central" to "Canada" Mortgage and Housing Corporation were not confirmed from a primary source this review.

Priority evolution

The commercial-institution era (ar-2010 to ar-2012). The series opens with CMHC describing itself through a stable, enumerative mission — "Promoting housing quality, affordability and choice for all Canadians" — and a formal three-objective / seven-strategic-priority corporate structure (ar-2010, 3884653ca8c8). Its self-portrait is explicitly bifurcated: an "About CMHC" diagram splits "Canadians served" into "80%" whose housing needs are met by the market, served by Mortgage Loan Insurance and Securitization, and "20%" whose needs are not, served by renovation, affordability and distinct-housing-needs programs (3884653ca8c8). The three objectives are "Help Canadians in need," "Facilitate access to more affordable, better quality housing for all Canadians," and "Ensure the Canadian housing system remains one of the best in the world" (3884653ca8c8). Commercial framing dominates the financial narrative: total insurance-in-force reaches $514 billion at end-2010 and $567 billion at end-2011, with $95.1 billion in NHA MBS and Canada Mortgage Bonds guaranteed in 2010 (3884653ca8c8; d952f66afbf5). The Corporation reports having "contributed over $14 billion to reducing the Government's annual deficit" over the preceding decade through income taxes and net income (3884653ca8c8).

Financial stability enters the mission (ar-2013). ar-2013 rewrites the mission statement itself, and the addition is the whole story: "To promote housing quality, affordability and choice for all Canadians and contribute to the stability of Canada's financial system and housing market" (0e0bc3bfdc49). This follows 2012 legislative amendments that added the Deputy Ministers of Finance and Human Resources and Skills Development as ex-officio directors, gave the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions an annual examination role over CMHC's insurance and securitization activities, and tasked CMHC with administering the legal framework for Canadian covered bonds (92abf60771bb; four covered-bond programs registered in 2013 per 0e0bc3bfdc49). A dedicated Board Risk Management Committee was established in 2012, separated from the Audit Committee (92abf60771bb).

Wholesale rebrand (ar-2014 to ar-2015). The 2014 report states outright that "We moved quickly to articulate a new mission and vision," replacing the enumerative 2013 mission with a deliberately plain one — Mission: "We help Canadians meet their housing needs"; Vision: "the heart of a world-leading housing system" (056960d863f8). A "CMHC in Motion" change programme and the Three Lines of Defence risk-governance model arrive the same year (056960d863f8). By ar-2015 the mandate/mission/vision triad is formalised — Mandate: "Facilitate access to housing · Contribute to financial stability" — under the tagline "Canada's authority on housing," with three numbered Strategic Directions: Align Risk With Mandate, Lead Through Information and Insight, Be a High-Performing Organization (f19a49e080c5). This triad is reproduced verbatim in ar-2016 and ar-2017 (87d39169b711; 12e7df80e120, read directly). Simultaneously CMHC tightens its commercial book, discontinuing second-home mortgage loan insurance and tightening self-employed-borrower rules "in support of the Government's efforts to reduce taxpayer exposure to the housing sector" (056960d863f8).

The National Housing Strategy arrives (ar-2016 to ar-2017). The phrase "National Housing Strategy" does not appear anywhere in ar-2010 through ar-2015; it appears first in ar-2016, where CMHC reports leading the "Let's Talk Housing" consultation, "a National Housing Strategy, the first in 40 years," reaching more than 1.5 million Canadians (87d39169b711, read directly). ar-2017 records the Strategy's November 2017 announcement as a $40-billion, ten-year plan targeted to "reduce chronic homelessness by 50% and remove 530,000 households from housing need" (12e7df80e120). The same report has CMHC describing itself as "a systemically important financial institution in Canada" and declaring $4.7 billion in dividends to the Government during 2017 (12e7df80e120) — the commercial and social identities at their point of maximum simultaneous expansion.

The aspiration era (ar-2020 to ar-2022). After the 2018-2019 registry gap, the series resumes with a fundamentally different document. ar-2020 opens its strategy section with a headed statement — "Our aspiration / By 2030, everyone in Canada has a home that they can afford and that meets their needs" — followed by four strategic objectives framed as paths to it: Experiment with new ideas, Understand Canadians' needs, Build an open housing data exchange, Modernize our company (14b88e3a3646, read directly). ar-2021 keeps the aspiration verbatim and states "Our overall direction and aspiration remain the same," while reorganising delivery around three outcomes (016338ab1118, read directly). ar-2022 gives it the most emphatic treatment in the series, calling it "a guiding star for our efforts" and adding a striking concession: "It is indeed aspirational, especially in recent years as housing affordability continues to worsen" (5581eda85c09, read directly). ar-2022 is also the first year CMHC publicly reports missing its own numeric targets — 115,378 units funded affordable to those in core housing need against a 130,000 target, and 350,216 units assisted against 380,000 (5581eda85c09).

Retrenchment (ar-2023 to ar-2025). ar-2023's strategy section presents three Outcomes and seven Strategic Results with no 2030 aspiration statement; the word survives only as the phrase "We have a bold aspiration" in a values graphic, detached from the 2030 wording and from any date (8aed3194bce4, read directly). By ar-2024 the housing aspiration is gone entirely — a full-text search returns "aspiration" only in a greenhouse-gas reduction target and the glossary (888463b199d5, verified directly). ar-2025 then makes the change explicit and total: "In 2025, we updated our strategy to reflect the unique role our organization plays in the broader Canadian housing system... Our mission has been updated to reflect shifts in the external environment and evolving system needs." The new mission reads: "We contribute to a well-functioning housing system by providing housing finance solutions, delivering programs on behalf of the Government, making funding available, and providing trusted expertise on housing" (b56d83667926, read directly). Four strategic objectives replace the outcomes framework, and the first of them is Housing Finance Solutions — mortgage loan insurance, securitization and the covered bond framework restored to the head of the list (b56d83667926). The driver is stated without euphemism: a new federal agency, Build Canada Homes, "will focus on building and financing affordable housing projects, allowing CMHC to focus most of its activities on market housing" (b56d83667926).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2012 — statutory financial-stability role. Amendments to the CMHC Act and National Housing Act add two Deputy Minister ex-officio directors, subject CMHC's insurance and securitization activities to annual OSFI examination, and assign CMHC administration of the Canadian covered bond legal framework (92abf60771bb).
  • 2014 — commercial retrenchment. Second-home mortgage loan insurance discontinued and self-employed-borrower rules tightened, explicitly to "reduce taxpayer exposure to the housing sector"; net income $2,625 million, a 43.5% increase over 2013 driven substantially by realized investment gains (056960d863f8).
  • Insurance-in-force peaks then declines. $514B (2010) → $567B (2011) → $566B (2012) → $543B (2014) → $526B (2015) → $512B (2016) (3884653ca8c8; d952f66afbf5; 92abf60771bb; 056960d863f8; f19a49e080c5; 87d39169b711), and $431B in 2020 falling to $401B in 2021 (14b88e3a3646; 016338ab1118). The share of outstanding Canadian residential mortgages carrying CMHC insurance falls from 51.1% (2011) to 42.7% (2014) (056960d863f8).
  • 2016-2017 — first dividends to the Government. A dividend framework is contemplated in ar-2015 (f19a49e080c5), announced in ar-2016 for payment in 2017 (87d39169b711), and executed at $4.7 billion declared during 2017 plus a further $1 billion declared 22 March 2018 (12e7df80e120).
  • November 2017 — National Housing Strategy, $40 billion over ten years, targeting a 50% reduction in chronic homelessness and removal of 530,000 households from housing need (12e7df80e120). The Auditor General's later account gives the Strategy's scale as "a 10-year, $78.5 billion federal strategy" as of 2022 (f53512044494) — a near-doubling of the announced envelope over five years, though no document in this series narrates that growth.
  • 2020 — pandemic response. The Insured Mortgage Purchase Program is relaunched with up to $150 billion of mortgage purchase capacity; roughly $3.5 billion in mortgage payments are deferred at peak, with more than 17% of insured mortgage holders using deferral; the $1 billion Rapid Housing Initiative launches in October 2020; the dividend to the Government is temporarily suspended (14b88e3a3646).
  • Dividend arc after the pandemic. Suspended in 2020, then $5,080M declared (2021) → $2,180M (2022) → $1,080M (2023) → $290M (2024), the last reflecting capital retention to support multi-unit insurance growth ahead of new regulatory capital requirements effective 1 January 2026 (14b88e3a3646; 016338ab1118; 5581eda85c09; 8aed3194bce4; 888463b199d5; b56d83667926).
  • 15 November 2022 — external accountability finding. The Auditor General reports that CMHC, as National Housing Strategy lead, "spent about $4.5 billion and committed about $9 billion but did not know who was benefiting from its initiatives," and that CMHC "took the position that it was not directly accountable for addressing chronic homelessness," leaving "minimal federal accountability" for the 50%-reduction target (f53512044494).
  • 2023 — accounting regime change. IFRS 17 for insurance contracts takes effect 1 January 2023, changing financial-statement presentation and prior-year comparatives (8aed3194bce4).
  • Balance-sheet arc. Total assets $293,218M (2010) → $248,490M (2014) → $267,115M (2017) → $300,970M (2020) → $299,569M (2023) → $327,355M (2024) → $357,013M (2025) (3884653ca8c8; 056960d863f8; 12e7df80e120; 14b88e3a3646; 8aed3194bce4; 888463b199d5; b56d83667926). Net income: $1,768M (2010) → $1,829M (2013) → $2,625M (2014) → $1,378M (2016) → $1,803M (2017) → $1,662M (2020) → $1,310M (2023) → $1,823M (2025) (same sources).
  • Workforce arc. Approximately 2,125 employees thanked in the ar-2010 President's message and ~2,100 given in that report's governance section (3884653ca8c8) → ~2,000 (2011, d952f66afbf5) → ~1,900, stable from 2012 through 2017 (92abf60771bb; 0e0bc3bfdc49; 056960d863f8; f19a49e080c5; 87d39169b711; 12e7df80e120) → 2,150 (2020, 14b88e3a3646). No consolidated corporate FTE figure was located in ar-2021 through ar-2025; ar-2025 reports only that "Ottawa had 940 employees operating within the [hybrid] model" (b56d83667926), which is a location-and-policy subset, not a corporate total. See Residuals & gaps.
  • 2025 — Comprehensive Expenditure Review and Build Canada Homes. The Government requires CMHC, among most departments and Crown corporations, "to develop savings proposals up to 15 percent of their assigned spending base." Stated reductions in federal funding for CMHC housing programs and related operating expenditures: $284.1 million (2026-2027), $451.9 million (2027-2028), $808.6 million (2028-2029), $856.4 million (2029-2030), and $859.9 million ongoing (b56d83667926, read directly). The Canada Secondary Suite Loan Program will not be implemented; other programmes wind down with objectives transferring to Build Canada Homes; Apartment Construction Loan Program funding is maintained (b56d83667926). CMHC's own risk section names this as an elevated strategic risk: "The launch of Build Canada Homes and the Comprehensive Expenditure Review (CER) has created programming and budgeting uncertainty" (b56d83667926).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

CMHC is a national Crown corporation headquartered in Ottawa, so its Ontario and Toronto relevance is indirect and market-analytic rather than jurisdictional — but it is more substantial than boilerplate, because Toronto is one of the two housing markets the Corporation repeatedly singles out by name.

  • Regional presence. ar-2010 lists Toronto as one of five regional business centres, alongside Halifax, Montréal, Calgary and Vancouver, under a national office in Ottawa (3884653ca8c8).
  • Toronto as a named market-risk concern. ar-2016's Chairperson's message identifies "Overheated housing markets, declining affordability, lack of supply and higher levels of household debt, particularly in Vancouver and Toronto" as key concerns of the year, connected to the October 2016 federal mortgage-insurance parameter changes (87d39169b711, read directly). The same report records Toronto's rental vacancy rate at 1.3%, against 10.3% in Saskatoon and 7.1% in Edmonton (87d39169b711).
  • Toronto market data as standing content. ar-2010 reports a Toronto average house price of $349,632 (3884653ca8c8); ar-2025 reports Ontario and British Columbia posting declines in sales, prices and starts while Quebec and Alberta performed better (b56d83667926).
  • A named Toronto project. Regent Park in Toronto is one of four sites selected for the EQuilibrium™ Communities Initiative sustainable-neighbourhood demonstrations, alongside Ty-Histanis (British Columbia), Ampersand (Ottawa) and Station Pointe (Edmonton) (3884653ca8c8).
  • Ontario-specific affordability finding. The Auditor General's audit found that units CMHC considered affordable under the National Housing Co-Investment Fund were often unaffordable to low-income households, and the gap was wider in Ontario than nationally: an Ontario low-income renter household could afford $640 per month, while typical approved "affordable" rent ran about $800 — 37.2% of before-tax income, against 31.1% nationally (f53512044494).
  • A caution against inflation. ar-2025 contains 26 occurrences of "Toronto," far more than any other year, but direct inspection shows these are overwhelmingly supplier and procurement disclosure lines listing Toronto-domiciled vendors, plus a note that the Board met in Toronto among other cities (b56d83667926). This is corporate-disclosure boilerplate and should not be read as increased Toronto programme presence.
