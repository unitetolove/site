Peel Police Service Board - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: peel-peel-police-services-board (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Peel Police Service Board is a municipal police service board constituted under the Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019, S.O. 2019, c. 1, Sched. 1 (CSPA), Part IV, ss.37-39, which set out a board's duties, policy-making powers, strategic-plan responsibility and reporting obligations (fetch-verified https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c01; https://www.peelpoliceboard.ca/about-us/board-mandate/). The Board's own page cites these as the "Legislated Responsibilities" under CSPA.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Board is the civilian body governing policing for the cities of Brampton and Mississauga, delivered operationally by Peel Regional Police; in the Town of Caledon, policing is provided by the OPP, but CSPA still defines a Board role there (registry row, registry id: peel-peel-police-services-board; https://www.peelpoliceboard.ca/about-us/board-mandate/). Statutory duties include: setting policing objectives/priorities after consulting the Chief; establishing management policies; recruiting/appointing the Chief and Deputy Chief(s) and setting their remuneration; establishing public-complaints guidelines and receiving quarterly reports; negotiating the collective agreement; and approving the capital and operating budget (https://www.peelpoliceboard.ca/about-us/board-mandate/). The Board does not direct day-to-day operations or specific operational decisions, which remain with the Chief (https://www.peelpoliceboard.ca/about-us/board-mandate/).

Governance & reporting line

The Board sits under the Region of Peel (registry parent_org: peel-region-of-peel) and is distinct from, and oversees, Peel Regional Police (registry row registry id: peel-peel-regional-police). Membership mixes municipal representatives (e.g., the Mayor of Brampton, a Mississauga councillor), provincial appointees and citizen appointees (https://www.peelpoliceboard.ca/board-members/).

Budget scale

The Board's own 2026 operating-budget line is $1,521,300, forming part of the Board-approved consolidated 2026 Operating Budget of $837,270,000 for policing in Peel (Peel Regional Police portion: $835,748,700), a 9.9% increase over 2025 (2026 Peel Regional Police Operating & Capital Budget Report, https://www.peelpoliceboard.ca/media/f5hdpur3/report-2026-police-budget.pdf).

Institutional history

The Board's governance role dates to January 1, 1974, when the Region of Peel and its regional police service were created by amalgamation of five municipal forces (Mississauga, Port Credit, Streetsville, Brampton, Chinguacousy); the Board's statutory footing has since migrated from the Police Services Act to CSPA (https://www.peelpolice.ca/who-we-are/history/; https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c01).

Priority evolution

2013 (ar-2013, 394731dcedd7), closing out the 2011-2013 Strategic Plan: Framed under the vision "A Safer Community Together" with five goal areas: (1) Community Safety — "enhance and promote community safety as our highest priority" through effective investigation/enforcement, proactive strategies, education, and police visibility, with a road-safety sub-focus; (2) Member Focused Workplace — health/wellness/safety of employees and professional-development opportunities, framed as being "an employer of choice"; (3) Communication & Collaboration; (4) Quality Service & Fiscal Responsibility; (5) Growth & Changing Demographics. Peel Regional Police served approximately 1,280,000 residents with an authorized complement of 2,805 (394731dcedd7). More than $12 million was received that year in inter-governmental funding, including $3,721,752 through the Provincial Community Policing Partnerships program and $971,598 through the Provincial Anti-Violence Intervention Strategy (PAVIS) targeting gun and gang violence (394731dcedd7).

2015 (ar-2015, da1f569b2c0d), under the 2014-2016 Strategic Plan: Same five-goal architecture as 2013 retained (Community Safety; Member Focused Workplace; Communication & Collaboration; Quality Service & Fiscal Responsibility; Growth & Changing Demographics), now serving over 1.3 million residents with an authorized complement of 2,863 (da1f569b2c0d). Community Safety goal is elaborated with three named sub-priorities: road safety through education/enforcement, crime-prevention initiatives and awareness, and strengthened emergency-management planning/response. First appearance in this series of an explicit "Community Safety Model" partnership initiative bringing police and social-service agencies together in identified neighbourhoods (da1f569b2c0d). Peel Regional Police participated in Pan Am/Parapan Am Games 2015 security as part of the multi-agency Integrated Security Unit (da1f569b2c0d). More than $14 million was received in funding that year, including a new line item — Federal Victims Fund funding ($1,260) to support outreach to sellers of sexual services including trafficking victims, introduced in 2015 (da1f569b2c0d).

2020 (ar-2020, 9edfcf164570), under the 2020-2023 Strategic Plan (year one), amid COVID-19: Structural shift in framing — the org's own account describes "a new organizational structure that modernizes our approach to community policing," including establishment of Community Safety and Well-Being Operations and Innovation & Technology Command, plus creation of a dedicated Diversity, Equity and Inclusion bureau and DEI strategy (9edfcf164570). The Community Safety and Well-Being (CSWB) Plan — sourced by the report to the Government of Ontario's Bill 175, the Safer Ontario Act — is adopted (with the Region of Peel, October 2020) as the organizing foundation, naming four current priorities: Mental Health and Addiction, Family and Intimate Partner Violence, Road Safety, and Priority Populations (9edfcf164570). Body-Worn Cameras were deployed as part of a "Digital Officer Transformation Program." The report states the Board "signed a groundbreaking agreement with the Ontario Human Rights Commission to address systemic racism in the service" (9edfcf164570); §3.2.3 of the same report dates this precisely: "In October 2020, Peel Regional Police, Peel Police Services Board and Ontario Human Rights Commission signed an MOU committing to develop and implement legally binding remedies to identify and eliminate potential systemic racism in policing" (ar-2020, 9edfcf164570).

2023 (sp-2023, caadafdcd761), 2020-2023 Strategic Plan document: A condensed strategic-plan artifact (10,387 characters, mostly a graphical framework) organized under a new three-"Pillar" architecture — Our Community, Our People, Our Work — a structural break from the five-goal-area framing used in ar-2013/ar-2015. Under "Our Community": Crime Prevention and Response; Emergency Management Planning and Response; Intimate Partner and Family Violence; Mental Health and Addictions; Priority Populations; Road Safety (explicitly tied to "the Community Safety and Well-Being Vision Zero goal"); Victim Assistance and Community Support. Under "Our People": Cultural/Physical/Psychological Well-Being and Safety of Employees; Employee Collaboration and Engagement; Diversity, Equity and Inclusivity in the workplace and community; Professional and Skilled Employees representing the community. Under "Our Work": Collaboration/Partnerships/Shared Services (explicitly naming co-leadership of a "Community Safety and Well-Being System Leadership Table"); Public Trust and Confidence through Accountability and Transparency; Service Excellence through Innovation, Technology and Risk Mitigation; Fiscal Responsibility and Sustainability; Sustainable Environmental Practices (caadafdcd761).

2024 (ar-2024, 08a0686e5014), first-year progress report under the 2024-2027 Strategic Plan: The three-Pillar architecture from sp-2023 (Our Community / Our People / Our Work) is retained and now reports against 16 numbered "Performance Indicators" tied to specific targets (e.g., "Achieve an employee engagement index of 70 per cent," "Improve response times for all priorities") (08a0686e5014). Marked the 50th anniversary of the Peel Regional Police (founded 1974), and reports the Peel Regional Police 9-1-1 communications centre became "the first communications centre in Canada to implement the Next Generation 9-1-1 system," with average 9-1-1 wait times improving from 64 seconds (2023) to 21 seconds (2024) (08a0686e5014). New named initiatives in this cycle: an Extortion Investigative Task Force, launched "in response to extortion threats primarily targeting Peel Region's South Asian business community"; the South Asian Community Engagement (SACE) framework and its associated Cross-Functional Support Team and Community Ambassador Program; a Countering Hate training module; and the Data and Analytics ("DNA") Program, a 27-month initiative launched November 2024 (08a0686e5014). The Board is separately credited with being "the first Ontario Service to conduct Governance and Human Rights Consultations," publishing a "Report on Community Consultations on Human Rights and Police Governance" (08a0686e5014). Total 2024 grant/funding partnerships reached $26.32 million, up from $15.9 million in 2019 (08a0686e5014).

2026-2029 (bp-2026, 31cd6f379500), the Board-approved 2026 Business Plan and Budget: Vision/mission/goals language is now explicitly dated to a "2024-2027 Strategic Plan" (the successor to sp-2023's 2020-2023 plan), retaining the same three-goal structure ("Our community," "Our people," "Our work") (31cd6f379500). The 2026 proposed net operating budget is $837.27 million, a 9.9% increase ($75.41 million) over the 2025 approved budget of $761.86 million, driven substantially by the addition of "175 officers and 25 civilian professionals to address historical underinvestment in staffing, regional growth... growing community needs and demands, increasing complexity of crime" (31cd6f379500). The 2026 capital budget is $378.5 million within a 2026-2035 ten-year capital plan of $1,778.5 million, the single largest line being $293.7 million for "Divisional & Operational Facilities" (new/expanded buildings) (31cd6f379500). New/expanded named priorities versus ar-2024: a Youth Engagement Strategy (launched March 2025); an Equity, Diversity and Inclusion Strategy; the Peel Police Foundation (a new charitable non-profit, launched April 2025); Ontario's first policing-led Victims and Survivors Symposium (with Durham, Halton, and York Regional Police); a Human Trafficking Symposium (March 19, 2025); a Covert Online Digital Enforcement Section (CODES) pilot targeting dark-web criminal activity; and a Disparity Analysis and Well-Being Plan tied to the ongoing Human Rights Project with the Ontario Human Rights Commission, under which "over 260 OHRC recommendations have been adopted" (31cd6f379500). "Project 300" (300 new officers hired in 2025) is named as the immediate predecessor staffing surge to the 175 additional officers proposed for 2026 (31cd6f379500).

  • Renamed/restructured — the org's top-level goal architecture: five goal areas (Community Safety; Member Focused Workplace; Communication & Collaboration; Quality Service & Fiscal Responsibility; Growth & Changing Demographics), present in ar-2013 and ar-2015 (394731dcedd7; da1f569b2c0d), gives way by sp-2023 to a three-"Pillar" structure (Our Community / Our People / Our Work) (caadafdcd761) — retained through ar-2024 and bp-2026 (08a0686e5014; 31cd6f379500). The archived series does not contain a document from the transition years (2016-2019) that would pin down exactly when this restructuring occurred; ⚠️ still being checked exact year of the five-goal-to-three-pillar transition.
  • Added — Community Safety and Well-Being (CSWB) framework as organizing lens: absent from ar-2013/ar-2015; explicitly adopted with the Region of Peel in October 2020 per ar-2020, sourced to the provincial Safer Ontario Act (Bill 175) (9edfcf164570); by bp-2026 CSWB is described as fully integrated into service delivery via the "Our Way Forward" CSWB Plan (31cd6f379500).
  • Added — Priority Populations as a named standing category: first appears as one of four CSWB priorities in ar-2020 (9edfcf164570); retained through sp-2023 and elaborated in bp-2026 with named sub-programs (Divisional Mobilization Teams, Safe Centre Response Team, Project Lantern for dementia/Alzheimer's safeguarding, South Asian Community Engagement) (caadafdcd761; 31cd6f379500).
  • Added — Diversity, Equity and Inclusion (DEI) as a dedicated organizational function: a "Diversity, Equity and Inclusion bureau" and strategy are reported as newly created in ar-2020 (9edfcf164570); by bp-2026 a distinct "Equity, Diversity and Inclusion Strategy" document has been launched (31cd6f379500).
  • Added — human rights / systemic-discrimination accountability track: first appears in ar-2020 as a "groundbreaking agreement with the Ontario Human Rights Commission to address systemic racism," an MOU signed October 2020 (9edfcf164570, §3.2.3); by ar-2024 formalized as the "Human Rights Project" with a Governance and Human Rights Consultations report (08a0686e5014); by bp-2026 tracked via a published "2024 Human Rights Project and annual Use of Force report" citing "over 260 OHRC recommendations... adopted" and a 79% reported decrease in use-of-force injury rates since 2020 (31cd6f379500).
  • Added — extortion/hate-crime-specific response units: absent from ar-2013/ar-2015/ar-2020; the Extortion Investigative Task Force and a centralized Hate Crime Unit first appear in ar-2024/bp-2026, both explicitly tied to impacts on Peel's South Asian community (08a0686e5014; 31cd6f379500).
  • Added — dark-web/online covert enforcement: the Covert Online Digital Enforcement Section (CODES) pilot is new to bp-2026, with no antecedent in the earlier reports read (31cd6f379500).
  • Quietly dropped — "Growth & Changing Demographics" and "Member Focused Workplace" as standalone named goal areas: both present as top-level 2013/2015 goals (394731dcedd7; da1f569b2c0d); by sp-2023 their substance is redistributed into "Our People" (workplace/wellness content) and cross-cutting "Our Community" priorities (demographic/priority-population content) rather than surviving as distinct pillars (caadafdcd761) — no report states this as a deliberate consolidation; it is inferred from the structural comparison.
  • Quietly dropped — the standalone "Communication & Collaboration" goal area: present in ar-2013/ar-2015 (394731dcedd7; da1f569b2c0d); its content (partnerships, shared services, community engagement) is folded into "Our Work" by sp-2023 with no report explaining the change (caadafdcd761).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Peel Regional Police is directly adjacent to and operationally interlinked with Toronto and York Regional Police through recurring joint-forces operations: a 2020 street-gang dismantlement operation explicitly named Toronto Police Service as a partner across "15 different cities" (9edfcf164570); Project ERASE, a street-racing enforcement partnership dating to 1999 with "the Ontario Provincial Police and the Toronto and York police services," operates "across the Greater Toronto Area" and is still active in the 2024 and 2026 reports (08a0686e5014; 31cd6f379500). Peel Regional Police also polices Toronto Pearson International Airport (46 million passengers in 2024) via its dedicated Airport Division, funded in part by the Greater Toronto Airports Authority (08a0686e5014; 31cd6f379500). The 2024 Victims and Survivors Symposium was delivered jointly with Durham Regional Police, Halton Regional Police and York Regional Police (31cd6f379500), and organized-crime/drug-trafficking investigations (Project Pelican, Project Ghost, Project Night Train) are explicitly described as spanning "the Greater Toronto Area" (31cd6f379500). No document in this archived set describes Peel Police Service Board or Peel Regional Police as having direct jurisdiction inside the City of Toronto; relevance is through the shared GTA policing/joint-forces ecosystem and Pearson Airport rather than an operational footprint within Toronto's municipal boundary.
