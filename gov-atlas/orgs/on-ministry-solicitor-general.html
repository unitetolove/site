Ministry of the Solicitor General - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-solicitor-general (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Ministry is continued under the Ministry of the Solicitor General Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.34, consolidated text fetched and verified at e-Laws (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m34). Section 2(1): "The ministry of the public service known in English as the Ministry of the Solicitor General...is continued." Section 2(2): "The Solicitor General shall preside over and have charge of the Ministry." Section 4 makes the Solicitor General "responsible for the administration of this Act and any Acts...assigned...by the Legislature or by the Lieutenant Governor in Council" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Three domains: correctional services (adult institutions, probation/parole, rehabilitation programs); public safety and security (fire services, emergency management, the coroner's system, municipal safety grant programs); and policing (oversight of the OPP, licensing of private investigation/security agencies) (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-solicitor-general). Statutes administered include the Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019, the Coroners Act, the Fire Protection and Prevention Act, 1997, and the Private Security and Investigative Services Act, 2005 (same source).

Governance & reporting line

Headed by the Solicitor General, who presides per s.2(2) of the Act, with a Deputy Solicitor General; the Minister must, "after the close of each year," submit an annual report to the Lieutenant Governor in Council and lay it before the Assembly (Ministry of the Solicitor General Act, s.5, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m34). The Ministry is accountable for three independent bodies: the Death Investigation Oversight Council, the Fire Marshal's Public Fire Safety Council, and the Ontario Police Arbitration and Adjudication Commission (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-solicitor-general).

Budget scale

$4,463.0 million total planned expenditure for 2025-26 ($4,298.4M operating + $164.5M capital), up 16.2% from $3,841.7 million in 2024-25 estimates (ontario.ca Ministry of the Solicitor General published plans and annual reports 2025-2026, https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-solicitor-general).

Institutional history

Established 1972 as the Ministry of the Solicitor General (taking policing/public-safety functions from the Attorney General); merged with corrections in 1993 as Ministry of the Solicitor General and Correctional Services; renamed Ministry of Public Safety and Security (2002), then Ministry of Community Safety and Correctional Services (2003); reverted to Ministry of the Solicitor General in 2019 (WebSearch/Wikipedia summary, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Solicitor_General_(Ontario); dates ⚠️ still being checked not independently cross-checked against a primary government source this review).

Backgrounder summary

The Ministry is continued under the Ministry of the Solicitor General Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.34, headed by the Solicitor General (s.2(2)) with a Deputy Solicitor General, and covers three domains: correctional services, public safety/security, and policing (oversight of the OPP, licensing of private investigation/security). It administers the Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019, the Coroners Act, the Fire Protection and Prevention Act, 1997, and the Private Security and Investigative Services Act, 2005, and is accountable for three affiliated bodies — the Death Investigation Oversight Council, the Fire Marshal's Public Fire Safety Council, and the Ontario Police Arbitration and Adjudication Commission. Planned 2025-26 expenditure is $4,463.0 million ($4,298.4M operating + $164.5M capital), up 16.2% from $3,841.7 million in 2024-25 estimates. The Ministry has been renamed several times since its 1972 founding, last reverting to "Ministry of the Solicitor General" in 2019 (backgrounder, ⚠️ still being checked on institutional-history dates per the backgrounder's own caveat).

Priority evolution

ar-2024 (a1241701cfe2), covering 2023-24 plans / 2022-23 results: Ministry vision framed around "two distinct parts": community safety and correctional services. Four stated priority areas: build safer communities against guns/gangs/human trafficking/intimate partner violence; improve fire safety via new firefighter certification standards; modernize the justice system and transform adult corrections (infrastructure); improve mental health/wellness supports for public safety personnel. Headline 2022-23 achievements include winding down GO-VAXX mobile vaccination clinics, a $267 million Community Safety and Policing Grant program, a new Inspectorate of Policing (Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019 not yet in force), and groundbreaking on a $1.2 billion Thunder Bay correctional complex. Effective June 24, 2022, Emergency Management Ontario and the Emergency Management and Civil Protection Act had already transferred out of the Ministry to Treasury Board Secretariat (a1241701cfe2).

ar-2025 (715cf8f14bd9), covering 2024-25 plans / 2023-24 results: Same four-priority framing retained, with wording shifts — "combatting auto theft, bail compliance, gun and gang violence, human trafficking and hate crime" (auto theft and bail compliance newly named), plus a new fourth-priority line item on "cracking down on puppy mills and harmful breeding practices." The Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019 comes into force April 1, 2024, formally replacing the Police Services Act. Major new 2023-24 investments: $51 million for an Organized Crime Towing and Auto Theft Team plus Preventing Auto Thefts (PAT) Grant; $112 million bail-compliance package including a Bail Compliance Unit inside OPP ROPE and a province-wide bail compliance dashboard; the Preventing Unethical Puppy Sales Act, 2023 (PUPS Act) introduced; Basic Constable Training tuition covered 100% retroactive to Jan 1, 2023, with a fourth training cohort added at Ontario Police College. Reports on Strengthening Safety and Modernizing Justice Act, 2023 and Enhancing Access to Justice Act, 2024 as the vehicles bringing the CSPA into force and amending the Fire Protection and Prevention Act and Coroners Act (715cf8f14bd9).

ar-2026 (7d05416b74aa), covering 2025-26 plans / 2024-25 results: Ministry vision restated more tersely as "committed to protecting Ontario" (dropping the prior "safer place to live, work and raise a family" framing from the vision line, though that phrase persists elsewhere in the same document). Same four priority areas retained verbatim from ar-2025. New administrative office named for the first time: "Associate Solicitor General of Auto Theft and Bail Reform" — signalling auto theft/bail elevated to a dedicated political-office-level portfolio. New legislation administered: Measures Respecting Premises with Illegal Drug Activity Act, 2025; Restricting Public Consumption of Illegal Substances Act, 2025; Community Safety and Crime Prevention Month Act, 2024; Accommodation Sector Registration of Guests Act, 2021. Headline 2024-25 investments: $134 million for five new police helicopters (Joint Air Support Unit, GTA/Ottawa); a $514 million investment supporting the Nishnawbe Aski Police Service Board becoming the first First Nations police board under the CSPA; Christopher's Law amended to add digital-identifier and travel-notification reporting duties for registered sex offenders; $30 million for firefighter cancer-prevention equipment; over $35 million combined in new mental-health support programs for public safety personnel and families (Immediate Family Wellness Program, Mental Health Supports for Public Safety Personnel); a $48M/$40M "New Deal for Ottawa" public-safety and homelessness/asylum-seeker package; correctional-bed expansions at Elgin-Middlesex, Toronto South, Quinte, and a new 250-bed Brockville Correctional Complex (7d05416b74aa).

oth-2021 (8a2cd5620c69), "Ontario Provincial Police Independent Review Panel: Final Report," published Sept 16, 2021 (panel dated Dec 9, 2019): An OPP-commissioned independent panel report on workplace culture, mental health, bullying, harassment and suicide within the OPP. The extracted text is the report's landing-page framing and "message from the Panel" section only (short — 3,825 characters), not the full report body; it names the review as commissioned with cooperation from OPP leadership, including the sitting Commissioner at the time (Thomas Carrique), and states the report is intended to support "the Solicitor General" and provide guidance to "the Commissioner." Full recommendations are not present in this extraction (8a2cd5620c69).

oth-2021-2 (93193be56d58), "Safe with intervention: Report of the expert panel on the deaths of children and youth in residential placements," published July 26, 2021: Extracted text is a bare table-of-contents/landing-page shell (1,280 characters) — page furniture, a one-line description ("This report examines the deaths of young people in residential placements in Ontario"), and a Chief Coroner's office contact address. Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — auto theft as a named standalone priority: absent as a distinct line item in ar-2024 (a1241701cfe2); appears folded into the "build safer communities" bullet in ar-2025 (715cf8f14bd9, "combatting auto theft, bail compliance..."); elevated further in ar-2026 (7d05416b74aa) with a dedicated "Associate Solicitor General of Auto Theft and Bail Reform" office.
  • Added — bail compliance/reform: same trajectory as auto theft — first named ar-2025 (715cf8f14bd9, $112M bail package), retained and organizationally elevated in ar-2026 (7d05416b74aa).
  • Added — animal welfare / anti-puppy-mill enforcement as a headline priority: absent from ar-2024's four priority bullets (a1241701cfe2); added as the second of four priority bullets from ar-2025 onward (715cf8f14bd9; 7d05416b74aa), operationalized via the PUPS Act (introduced 2023-24, passed June 6, 2024, first convictions reported Jan 8, 2025 in ar-2026).
  • Added — Inspector General of Policing / Inspectorate of Policing as an active oversight body: named as not-yet-in-force in ar-2024 (a1241701cfe2, "will fulfill the duties... once in force"); the CSPA comes into force April 1, 2024 and ar-2026 reports the Inspector General's mandate as actively underway with concrete oversight actions (7d05416b74aa).
  • Quietly dropped — Emergency Management Ontario: transferred out of the Ministry to Treasury Board Secretariat effective June 24, 2022 (noted retrospectively in ar-2024, a1241701cfe2); "Emergency planning and management" nonetheless persists as a budget-table line item through ar-2026 (7d05416b74aa), suggesting residual financial/administrative overlap not fully explained in these reports. ⚠️ still being checked exact nature of the post-transfer residual line item.
  • Renamed — "Strategic Policy, Research and Innovation" to "Strategic Policy Division": shown as a formal rename in the ar-2025 budget table ("Strategic Policy Division (formerly Strategic Policy Research and Innovation)," 715cf8f14bd9), carried into ar-2026 (7d05416b74aa).
  • Renamed — "Correctional Services Oversight and Investigations" to "Correctional Services Investigations and Intelligence Branch": named as the former in ar-2024/ar-2025 org text (a1241701cfe2; 715cf8f14bd9) and as the latter in ar-2026's ministry-programs description (7d05416b74aa), though the org chart in ar-2026 still labels the same chief position "Correctional Services Oversight and Investigations."
  • Ministry vision statement reworded each cycle: "committed to ensuring that all communities in Ontario are kept safe" (ar-2024, a1241701cfe2) → "committed to keeping Ontario communities safe" (ar-2025, 715cf8f14bd9) → "committed to protecting Ontario" (ar-2026, 7d05416b74aa) — progressively terser phrasing across three consecutive cycles; substance of the two-part community-safety/corrections mandate is unchanged.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • June 24, 2022 — Emergency Management Ontario and the Emergency Management and Civil Protection Act transferred from the Ministry to Treasury Board Secretariat (a1241701cfe2, retrospective note in ar-2024).
  • April 13, 2022 — Keeping Ontario Open for Business Act passed, giving police new roadside-suspension and seizure powers against blockades such as the Ambassador Bridge protest (a1241701cfe2).
  • March 2019 / April 1, 2024 — Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019 received Royal Assent in March 2019 but only came into force April 1, 2024, replacing the Police Services Act (715cf8f14bd9).
  • 2023-24 — $51 million auto-theft package and $112 million bail-compliance package announced (715cf8f14bd9).
  • June 6, 2024 — Preventing Unethical Puppy Sales Act, 2023 (PUPS Act) passed (715cf8f14bd9; first convictions reported Jan 8, 2025 per 7d05416b74aa).
  • 2024-25 — $134 million Joint Air Support Unit (five helicopters, GTA/Ottawa) and $514 million Nishnawbe Aski Police Service Board investment announced (7d05416b74aa).
  • 2025-26 estimates — planned expenditure reaches $4,463.0 million, a 16.2% year-over-year increase, the largest single-year percentage jump disclosed across the three archived annual-report cycles (7d05416b74aa; matches backgrounder figure).
  • March 4, 2025 — CRTC-mandated deadline for emergency communications centres to complete the Next Generation 9-1-1 analog-to-digital transition, part of a $208 million three-year (2022-23 to 2024-25) investment (715cf8f14bd9).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is an Ontario provincial ministry with its full operational footprint inside the province; Toronto/GTA relevance is direct and extensive rather than indirect. The OPP's Toronto Region is a named regional structure in every archived org chart (a1241701cfe2; 715cf8f14bd9; 7d05416b74aa). The 2024-25 Joint Air Support Unit investment explicitly names the Greater Toronto Area as a primary deployment zone, with two of five new helicopters supporting Toronto Police Service directly (7d05416b74aa). The ministry is repurposing and reopening the Toronto Intermittent Centre at the Toronto South Detention Centre, adding up to 320 beds by 2026 (7d05416b74aa). The Office of the Chief Coroner and Ontario Forensic Pathology Service partner with the University of Toronto on Royal College-accredited forensic pathology training (a1241701cfe2; 715cf8f14bd9; 7d05416b74aa). The Inspector General of Policing, Ryan Teschner, is described in ar-2024 as a University of Toronto and Osgoode Hall Law School graduate previously serving as executive director and chief of staff at the Toronto Police Services Board (a1241701cfe2). oth-2021-2's Office of the Chief Coroner contact address is listed at 25 Morton Shulman Avenue, Toronto (93193be56d58).
