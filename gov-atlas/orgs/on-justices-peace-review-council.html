Justices of the Peace Review Council - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-justices-peace-review-council (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Justices of the Peace Review Council (JPRC) is an independent body established by the Province of Ontario under the Justices of the Peace Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. J.4 (Justices of the Peace Review Council, Annual Report 2025, "Introduction," p.3, https://www.ontariocourts.ca/ocj/conduct/files/JPRC-Annual-Report-2025-EN.pdf; consolidated statute text at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90j04). Its mandate is "to receive and investigate complaints about the conduct of justices of the peace and to fulfil other functions" set out in the Act, with the stated purpose of the complaints process being "to preserve public confidence in the administration of justice" (same source, p.3; https://www.ontariocourts.ca/ocj/jprc/, fetched this review).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Council establishes complaints committees to receive and investigate allegations of judicial misconduct by justices of the peace, decides on dispositions (dismissal, advice, referral to the Chief Justice, or ordering a public hearing), reviews and approves standards of conduct and continuing-education plans for justices of the peace, considers accommodation-of-needs applications, and rules on applications by justices of the peace to engage in other remunerative work (JPRC Annual Report 2025, "Functions of the Review Council," p.8, same URL). In the 2025 reporting year the Council had jurisdiction over approximately 398 provincially appointed justices of the peace, received over 60 letters of complaint plus several hundred phone inquiries, opened 23 new complaint files, and closed 17 - 12 dismissed as outside jurisdiction, unsubstantiated, or not amounting to misconduct; 4 resolved by advice letter; and 1 referred to the Chief Justice, with no public hearings held (same source, pp.3, 19-21). The Review Council explicitly is not an appellate body and cannot alter a justice of the peace's court decision (same source, p.8). Where a hearing is ordered, a three-member hearing panel may impose sanctions ranging from a warning to unpaid suspension, or recommend removal from office to the Attorney General (same source, pp.16-18).

Per this project's identity/neutrality firewall, no complainant, witness, or justice-of-the-peace names from the Council's published case summaries are reproduced in this backgrounder.

Governance & reporting line

Council composition is fixed by the Justices of the Peace Act: the Chief Justice of the Ontario Court of Justice (or a designated judge) sits as ex officio Chair; the Associate Chief Justice Co-ordinator of Justices of the Peace also sits; the Chief Justice appoints three justices of the peace, two judges, and one regional senior justice of the peace to the Council; the Attorney General appoints one Law Society of Ontario licensee from a list of three names submitted by the Law Society; and the Lieutenant Governor in Council appoints four community members on the Attorney General's recommendation, with appointments intended to reflect Ontario's linguistic duality, population diversity and gender balance (JPRC Annual Report 2025, "Composition and Terms of Appointment," pp.4-5, https://www.ontariocourts.ca/ocj/conduct/files/JPRC-Annual-Report-2025-EN.pdf). The Law Society licensee and community members serve four-year renewable terms; judicial members are appointed by the Chief Justice (same source, p.4). The Council submits its Annual Report to the Attorney General for Ontario under s.9(7) of the Act (same source, cover letter, p.1).

Budget scale

The JPRC does not publish a standalone operating budget in its Annual Report. Its administrative work is carried out by a six-member staff (a Registrar, two Counsel/Deputy Registrars, two Assistant Registrars and an Administrative Assistant) shared with the Ontario Judicial Council, funded through the Ministry of the Attorney General's administration of the Ontario Court of Justice (JPRC Annual Report 2025, "Council Administration and Staff," p.7, same URL). ⚠️ still being checked - a specific dollar budget figure for the Council's own operations was not located this review.

Institutional history

The Council's statutory basis is the Justices of the Peace Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. J.4 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90j04). This project's document registry holds JPRC annual reports back to the 2007 reporting year (this library's government-document registry doc on-justices-peace-review-council-ar-2007), the earliest year for which an annual report is archived. The Council's exact founding/establishment date is ⚠️ still being checked - not confirmed in the 2025 Annual Report or on the Council's website this review.
