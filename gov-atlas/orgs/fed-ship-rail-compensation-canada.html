Ship and Rail Compensation Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-ship-rail-compensation-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Ship and Rail Compensation Canada is the operating name for two separate statutory compensation funds. The Ship Fund's role "is set out in the Marine Liability Act (the MLA)" - legally the Ship-source Oil Pollution Fund, "a specified purpose account in the accounts of Canada" (Ship Fund Annual Report 2024-2025, https://ship-rail.gc.ca/sites/default/files/2025-10/ship_fund_annual_report_2024-2025.pdf, fetched this review). The Rail Fund's role "is set out in the Canada Transportation Act" - legally the Fund for Railway Accidents Involving Designated Goods, also "a specified purpose account in the accounts of Canada" (Rail Fund Annual Report 2024-2025, https://ship-rail.gc.ca/sites/default/files/2025-09/rail_fund_annual_report-2024-2025.pdf, fetched this review).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The organization is "an independent federal office, financed by industry, managing two compensation funds" for those affected by oil spills from ships/boats and by major rail accidents involving crude oil (https://ship-rail.gc.ca/, fetched this review). It assesses and pays claims, then seeks recovery of costs from the responsible shipowner or railway company (Ship Fund AR 2024-25; Rail Fund AR 2024-25, same URLs).

Governance & reporting line

Both funds are led by a single Administrator (Caroline Healey, as of March 2025), with a separate Deputy Administrator per fund reporting to the Administrator (Ship Fund AR 2024-25, same URL). Annual reports for each fund are tabled in Parliament by the Minister of Transport and Internal Trade, pursuant to s.121 of the Marine Liability Act for the Ship Fund and s.155.93 of the Canada Transportation Act for the Rail Fund (same sources).

Budget scale

As at March 31, 2025 the Ship Fund held an accumulated surplus of $426,633,109 (2024: $421,172,245) on total financial assets of $436,967,948, with total revenues of $13,356,409 for the year (Ship Fund AR 2024-25, financial statements, same URL). The Rail Fund held a smaller accumulated surplus of $111,761,302 (2024: $107,222,603) as at the same date (Rail Fund AR 2024-25, financial statements, same URL).

Institutional history

The Ship Fund "was created on April 24, 1989 by amendments to the Canada Shipping Act and succeeded the Maritime Pollution Claims Fund," and is now governed by Part 7 of the Marine Liability Act as modified in 2009 (Ship Fund AR 2024-25, Note 1 "Governing statutes and purpose of the organization", same URL). The Rail Fund "was created on June 18, 2016, under the Safe and Accountable Rail Act," which amended the Canada Transportation Act (Rail Fund AR 2024-25, Note 1, same URL).

Ship and Rail Compensation Canada - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: fed-ship-rail-compensation-canada / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is a legislated mandate expansion: Bill C-86, in force December 13, 2018, removed the Ship Fund's $174-million per-occurrence liability cap, authorized Consolidated Revenue Fund loans if the Fund is depleted, gave the Administrator power to provide emergency funding to the Canadian Coast Guard for major spills, and created a fast-track process for claims under $35,000. The biggest quietly changed element is the organization's public identity, in two steps: "Ship-source Oil Pollution Fund" (SOPF) and "Fund for Railway Accidents Involving Designated Goods" (FRAIDG) were rebranded in plain language to "Ship Fund" and "Rail Fund" around the 2022-23 report, then unified under a single new public brand, "Ship and Rail Compensation Canada," launched with its own website in October 2024. The most load-bearing number is the Rail Fund's accumulated surplus of over $101 million as of March 2023 (per the backgrounder, $111.8 million by March 2025) sitting entirely unclaimed: no accident has activated the Rail Fund since its creation in 2016. One open question the record cannot resolve: whether the Administrator's Office's self-described governance problem — an "independent" statutory office that is nonetheless bound by many federal-department obligations, "stretched to its limits" per the outgoing Administrator — has been addressed by the legislator since the 2022-23 report raised it.

Backgrounder summary

Ship and Rail Compensation Canada is the operating name for two separate statutory compensation funds sharing one office. The Ship Fund (legal name: Ship-source Oil Pollution Fund) operates under the Marine Liability Act as "a specified purpose account in the accounts of Canada," compensating those affected by ship-source oil pollution; the Rail Fund (legal name: Fund for Railway Accidents Involving Designated Goods) operates under the Canada Transportation Act on the same specified-purpose-account basis, compensating victims of major rail accidents involving crude oil. Both funds are financed by industry (shipowners/insurers for the Ship Fund; a per-tonne levy on crude oil shippers for the Rail Fund) rather than by parliamentary appropriation, and both are led by a single Administrator (Caroline Healey as of March 2025) with a separate Deputy Administrator per fund. Annual reports for each fund are tabled in Parliament by the Minister of Transport and Internal Trade. As at March 31, 2025 the Ship Fund held an accumulated surplus of $426.6 million and the Rail Fund $111.8 million. The Ship Fund traces to an April 24, 1989 amendment to the Canada Shipping Act, succeeding the Maritime Pollution Claims Fund; the Rail Fund was created June 18, 2016 under the Safe and Accountable Rail Act, in direct legislative response to the 2013 Lac-Mégantic rail disaster.

Priority evolution

2016-17 (ar-2017, Ship, 6992f1d28912; ar-2017-2, Rail, 7068fa9f9e3d): Anne Legars is Administrator of both funds (Ship Fund since a prior appointment; Rail Fund from its creation). The Ship Fund report notes the prior Administrator "had been in the position for the past 9 years" and "retired on March 31, 2016," with Legars cross-appointed as interim Administrator of the newly created Rail Fund. The Rail Fund's first annual report documents its founding: statutory provisions and a $1.67/tonne crude-oil levy entered into force June 18, 2016; the claims framework was explicitly built "drawing from the Lac-Mégantic experience" (over 4,300 claims from the 2013 disaster). No rail accident has yet activated the Fund; year-end accumulated surplus $8.28 million.

2018-19 (ar-2019, Ship, sampled): Bill C-86, in force December 13, 2018, amends the Marine Liability Act: removes the Fund's $174-million per-occurrence liability cap, authorizes temporary Consolidated Revenue Fund transfers if the Fund is depleted, creates new statutory authority for the Administrator to provide emergency funding to the Canadian Coast Guard for major incidents, and establishes an expedited small-claims process (claims ≤$35,000, paid within 60 days on claimant attestation). The report frames this as ending a period of stalled international-indemnification work "pending announced amendments to the MLA."

2022-23 (ar-2023, Ship, 9e64ebf3448f; ar-2023-2, Rail, 56a4e1e49d43): Both reports are structured as a retrospective by the outgoing Administrator, Anne Legars, marking almost seven years in the role (interim from 2016, five-year reappointment from March 2018) and explicitly declining reappointment after April 2023. She credits her own predecessor, Alfred Popp, with building "Fund 2.0" (first Special Examination, 2015) and describes her own tenure as building "Fund 3.0": a three-pillar governance structure (ship program, rail program, corporate services) shared across both funds, doubled claims volume since 2015, cloud migration, and two new international agreements (with the International Group of P&I Clubs and, via MOU, with ITOPF). She names unresolved structural problems for her successor: a "peculiar governance structure" (an "independent" office still bound by many federal-department obligations), unresolved limits on handling derelict/abandoned vessels despite the 2019 Wrecked, Abandoned or Hazardous Vessels Act, and an open question of whether the Administrator's Office should formally be an administrative tribunal. Deputy Administrator Mark Gauthier (in role since April 2018) agrees to serve as interim Administrator for a one-year term. Both fund titles shift in this report from "SOPF"/"FRAIDG" acronym-first framing to plain-language "Ship Fund"/"Rail Fund." Rail Fund accumulated levies and interest: over $101 million as of March 31, 2023, with no accident having activated the Fund since 2016.

2024-25 (ar-2025, Ship, c0cab798cbda; ar-2025-2, Rail, 15922b479b44): A new Administrator, Caroline Healey — external hire from the transportation industry (VIA Rail, the Railway Association of Canada, Genesee & Wyoming), not promoted from within the office — succeeds Mark A.M. Gauthier on March 31, 2025; Gauthier had served as Administrator since April 2023 (interim, then apparently confirmed) following five years as Deputy Administrator. In October 2024 the two funds launch a unified public brand, "Ship and Rail Compensation Canada," with a single new website (ship-rail.gc.ca) replacing the funds' separate identities, explicitly framed as boosting outreach and public awareness. The Rail Fund remains unactivated by any accident nine years after its creation, monitoring five past accidents for potential ramifications. The Ship Fund's cover story (the "Old Chum" vessel, sunk 2022, a $157,516 CCG claim submitted November 2024) illustrates ordinary-course claims activity continuing alongside the rebrand.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Indirect, and structurally so: both funds are financed by industry levies/liability recoveries rather than tax appropriations, and both compensate claimants nationally wherever an oil-pollution or crude-by-rail incident occurs, with no GTA-specific program, office, or mandate language found anywhere in the read series. The Ship Fund's cover-story claim for 2024-25 (the Old Chum, sunk near Mars Island, British Columbia) illustrates the national, not regional, character of its caseload. No claim, incident, or accident located in Ontario or the Greater Toronto Area was named in any document read this review; this brief does not claim none exists in the fuller unread record. Both funds report to the Minister of Transport and Internal Trade federally and have no documented Ontario-specific governance role.
