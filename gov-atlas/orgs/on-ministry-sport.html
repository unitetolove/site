Ministry of Sport - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-sport (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

No dedicated "Ministry of Sport Act" was found on e-Laws (a targeted search returned only unrelated Ministry statutes) ⚠️ still being checked — absence not exhaustively proven. As a ministry of the public service, it exists via order-in-council under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.25, s.2(1)-(2), which lets the Lieutenant Governor "appoint...ministers of the Crown" and "prescribe the duties of the ministers...and the duties of any ministries" (fetched and verified, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e25). The Ministry directly administers two statutes: the Combative Sports Act, 2019, and Rowan's Law (Concussion Safety), 2018 (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-sport).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Investing in the sustainability of Ontario's sport organizations and amateur athlete development; concussion-safety implementation under Rowan's Law; maximizing the economic impact of sport tourism/event hosting; youth sport and recreation participation; and funding community sport/recreation infrastructure (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-sport; registry row on-ministry-sport).

Governance & reporting line

Headed by a Minister with ministerial and employee-directory contacts based at College Park, Toronto; standard ministry structure of Minister/Deputy Minister reporting through Cabinet to the Legislative Assembly (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-sport).

Budget scale

$212.8 million total planned expenditure for 2025-26 ($62.8M operating + $150.0M capital), up 92.4% from $110.7 million in 2024-25 estimates ($60.7M operating + $50.0M capital) (ontario.ca Ministry of Sport published plans and annual reports 2025-2026, https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-sport).

Institutional history

Split off, along with the Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming, from the former Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Sport on June 6, 2024; that lineage traces to a 1924 Tourist and Publicity Bureau, becoming the Department of Travel and Publicity (1946), Ministry of Industry and Tourism (1972), and passing through repeated splits/mergers of tourism, culture, recreation and sport portfolios (1975, 1982, 1993, 2001, 2011 addition of sport, 2019 Ministry of Heritage, Sport, Tourism and Culture Industries, 2022 Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Sport) before the 2024 split (WebSearch/Wikipedia summary, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Tourism,_Culture_and_Gaming; dates ⚠️ still being checked not independently cross-checked against a primary government source this review).
