Central Lake Ontario Conservation Authority - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: durh-central-lake-ontario-conservation-authority (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Central Lake Ontario Conservation Authority (CLOCA) is established and continued under the Conservation Authorities Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.27 (consolidated text verified at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c27). Section 0.1 states: "The purpose of this Act is to provide for the organization and delivery of programs and services that further the conservation, restoration, development and management of natural resources in watersheds in Ontario" (same source). Section 3(4) provides that "every authority is a body corporate" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CLOCA manages watershed resources - flood and erosion hazard regulation, conservation lands, and natural-resource programs - across roughly 639 km² draining four major and 18 minor watercourses in the Region of Durham (registry row durh-central-lake-ontario-conservation-authority; https://www.cloca.com/about-cloca). It is one of 36 conservation authorities operating under the Act province-wide (https://www.cloca.com/about-cloca).

Governance & reporting line

CLOCA's board is composed of members appointed by its participating municipalities under the Conservation Authorities Act (s.14 area, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c27); its municipal partners are the Region of Durham, the Cities of Oshawa and Pickering, the Towns of Ajax and Whitby, the Municipality of Clarington, and the Townships of Scugog and Uxbridge (https://www.cloca.com/about-cloca). The Minister of the Environment, Conservation and Parks administers the Act provincially (s.1 definitions, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c27).

Institutional history

CLOCA was established in 1958 under the Conservation Authorities Act, and is now one of 36 Ontario conservation authorities operating under that Act, which was substantially amended in 2020-2024 to narrow authorities' regulatory scope to their own lands (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Lake_Ontario_Conservation_Authority - ⚠️ still being checked primary-source confirmation of the 1958 establishment date; registry row durh-central-lake-ontario-conservation-authority).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

CLOCA is a Durham Region conservation authority with a direct, non-Toronto GTA-municipal-adjacent mandate — its jurisdiction is explicitly Durham Region and its member municipalities (Oshawa, Pickering, Ajax, Whitby, Clarington, Scugog, Uxbridge), not the City of Toronto itself (6dc01fc1b052). The one documented direct Toronto/GTA-scale connection in ar-2019 is CLOCA's participation in the "YPDT-CAMC (York, Peel, Durham, Toronto and The Conservation Authorities Moraine Coalition) Oak Ridges Moraine Groundwater Program," described as supporting "a regional hydrologic feature and source of drinking water for over 200,000 people in the Greater Toronto Area" through a 13-agency, 12-consulting-company coalition (6dc01fc1b052) — this is a genuine, named cross-boundary program with GTA-wide scope, not indirect boilerplate. ar-2019 also references CLOCA's role supporting "Source Water Protection Planning across the Credit Valley, Toronto and Region [Conservation Authorities]" (6dc01fc1b052). Beyond the shared groundwater/source-water programs, CLOCA's core watershed-management, flood-hazard, and conservation-lands work is Durham-specific rather than Toronto-specific.
