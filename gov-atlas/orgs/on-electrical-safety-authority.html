Electrical Safety Authority - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-electrical-safety-authority (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Electrical Safety Authority (ESA) administers and enforces Part VIII (Electrical Safety) of the Electricity Act, 1998, S.O. 1998, c. 15, Sched. A (consolidated text verified at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98e15). Section 2(1) defines "Electrical Safety Authority" as "the person or body designated by the regulations as the Electrical Safety Authority," and s.113.18 allows the designated Authority to exercise regulatory powers by agreement (same source). ESA is Ontario's delegated administrative authority (DAA) for electrical safety - a not-for-profit corporation carrying out this statutory function on behalf of the province, not a body created directly by its own founding statute (https://esasafe.com/role/).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

ESA's mandate covers inspection, authorization, investigation, licensing, enforcement, training and field-evaluation activity for electrical safety across Ontario, and it administers the Ontario Electrical Safety Code under O. Reg. 164/99 (https://esasafe.com/role/; registry row on-electrical-safety-authority). It also licenses electrical contractors and master electricians; since January 1, 2007 it has been illegal to operate an electrical contracting business in Ontario without an ESA/ECRA contractor licence (same source).

Governance & reporting line

As a self-funded, not-for-profit delegated administrative authority, ESA reports to the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery and Procurement (https://esasafe.com/role/). It is listed among Ontario's public agencies on the Public Appointments Secretariat's agency roster (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/247; registry row on-electrical-safety-authority).

Budget scale

ESA reported total operating revenues of $136.9 million for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, against operational expenses of $148.1 million (up 8.7% from the prior year) — an operating deficit of $11.3 million; net assets were $63.9 million at March 31, 2025 (ESA 2024/25 Annual Report, financial highlights, archived copy document id on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2025, sha256:5557842cc15e; live: https://esasafe.com/assets/files/esasafe/pdf/Corporate_Reports/ESA-AR-2024-25-Final.pdf). (Figure corrected against the archived copy at the this library's government-strategy research lead gate, 2026-07-30 — an earlier draft mislabeled the expense figure as revenue.)

Institutional history

ESA was designated Ontario's delegated administrative authority for electrical safety under Part VIII of the Electricity Act, 1998 when electrical safety regulation was transferred out of Ontario Hydro/the province's direct inspection function around 1999 (https://esasafe.com/role/; O. Reg. 89/99, https://www.canlii.org/en/on/laws/regu/o-reg-89-99/latest/o-reg-89-99.html - ⚠️ still being checked precise transition date/mechanics).

Electrical Safety Authority - strategy evolution

Its mandate covers inspection, licensing, investigation, enforcement and administration of the Ontario Electrical Safety Code. Priority evolution

2015-2020, Harm Reduction Strategy 2.0 set three co-equal strategic goals: Safety (20% reduction in the five-year rolling combined rate of electrical fatalities and critical injuries), Compliance (7.5% increase in renovation wiring work captured by ESA's compliance processes), and Public Accountability (a new multi-stakeholder perception index, baselined in FY16) (on-electrical-safety-authority-sp-2020, sha256:0a96e429574f). Organizational capabilities - communications, business processes, people/growth, financial sustainability - were framed as enabling capacities supporting those three goals, not a fourth goal in their own right (same doc).

FY2019/20 (on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2020, sha256:1cbe8e839b27) reports HRS2.0 essentially met: 0.56M renovation work items captured against a 0.52M baseline (7.5% target achieved) and a Public Accountability Index of 8.2 versus an 8.1 baseline. The same report announces the successor plan, Safely Powering Tomorrow: The Journey Towards the Modern Regulator, 2020-2025, launched April 1, 2020, alongside the phased rollout of Risk-Based Oversight (RBO), delayed to July 2020 by COVID-19 (on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2021, sha256:7bfb31855656).

2020-2025, Safely Powering Tomorrow restructures around four strategic goals: (1) Further reducing electrical-related harm - target halved to a 10% reduction in the combined fatality/critical-injury rate; (2) Creating a regulatory environment that enables electrical safety - compliance goal re-cast around RBO-driven risk prioritization and the underground economy; (3) Operating with excellence to maximize the electrical safety of Ontarians - a new, explicitly named goal tracked via a "Corporate Excellence Index" (10% five-year improvement target); (4) Being a publicly accountable regulator - continuation of the accountability index, target set at "maintain 8.2" (on-electrical-safety-authority-sp-2025, sha256:1a1ac1a80513).

FY2020/21-FY2023/24 annual reports track these four goals under the section headers Safety / Compliance / Organizational Excellence / Public Accountability, reporting incremental Corporate Excellence Index gains (+3% by FY21/22, +5% by FY22/23 against an FY18/19 baseline) (on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2022, sha256:f36ca441da4b; on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2023, sha256:82dd5be2b00d). The FY2021 chair's letter also records the completed 2020 Ontario Auditor General Value-for-Money Audit of ESA, with recommendations folded into the strategy (on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2021, sha256:7bfb31855656). A direct product of that audit surfaces in FY2022/23: effective April 1, 2023, the Electricity Act was amended and new Ontario Regulation 12/23 (Administrative Penalties) came into force, giving ESA a new enforcement instrument alongside its four existing regulations (on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2023, sha256:82dd5be2b00d). ESA issued 34 Administrative Penalty Orders (aggregate value $116,000) in the tool's first reporting year, FY2023/24 (on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2024, sha256:c6d2b23bd82e).

FY2024/25 closes out Safely Powering Tomorrow and opens the next cycle. The report states the five-year plan launched in April 2025 (i.e., FY2025/26) is titled Empowering Safety, Energizing Tomorrow, spanning 2025-2030 and framed explicitly around Ontario's "electrification transformation" and a projected 75% growth in electricity demand by 2050 (on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2025, sha256:5557842cc15e). Its four strategic pillars - Increase Electrical Safety Awareness, Foster Compliance, Enhance Collaboration, Evolve Service Delivery - are confirmed and elaborated with FY2026/27 activities and metrics in the Business Plan 2026/27 (on-electrical-safety-authority-bp-2027, sha256:5e15f313a2c0), which also lists a fifth regulation, Administrative Penalties (O. Reg. 12/23), alongside the four legacy regulations as part of ESA's current statutory toolkit (same doc).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added as a named, stand-alone goal: "Operating with Excellence" / Corporate Excellence Index first appears as a co-equal strategic goal only in the 2020-2025 plan; in 2015-2020 the same territory (people, process, IT, financial sustainability) was described as supporting capability, not a headline goal (sp-2020 sha256:0a96e429574f vs sp-2025 sha256:1a1ac1a80513).
  • Added as a mandate tool: Administrative Penalty Orders / O. Reg. 12/23, effective April 1, 2023 - new enforcement power not present in the 2015-2020 or 2020-2025 planning documents at the time they were written, added mid-cycle following the Auditor General's 2020 recommendation (ar-2023 sha256:82dd5be2b00d; ar-2024 sha256:c6d2b23bd82e).
  • Renamed / re-scoped: "Safety" (2015-2020) becomes "Further reducing electrical-related harm" (2020-2025); the numeric target is halved from a 20% to a 10% combined fatality/critical-injury reduction (sp-2020 vs sp-2025, same sha256s as above). "Compliance" becomes "Creating a regulatory environment that enables electrical safety" (2020-2025), then "Foster Compliance" (2025-2030 pillar, per bp-2027 sha256:5e15f313a2c0).
  • Added as a stand-alone pillar: "Increase Electrical Safety Awareness" and "Enhance Collaboration" are named top-level pillars only from 2025-2030; in the two prior five-year plans, awareness activity (UNDERSTAND/COLLABORATE) and partnership activity ("Developing Partnerships") existed as major activities nested under other goals, not as their own named pillar (sp-2020 sha256:0a96e429574f; sp-2025 sha256:1a1ac1a80513; bp-2027 sha256:5e15f313a2c0).
  • Quietly dropped as a distinct named category: "Public Accountability," a named strategic goal with its own numeric index in both the 2015-2020 and 2020-2025 plans, does not appear as a stand-alone pillar name in the four 2025-2030 pillars documented in the archived Business Plan 2026/27 (bp-2027 sha256:5e15f313a2c0). Its subject matter overlaps partially with the new "Enhance Collaboration" and "Evolve Service Delivery" pillars, but no accountability-index target is named under either pillar in the material read for this brief. Descriptive only - no motive is implied, and the standalone 2025-2030 strategic-plan document itself is not in this archive to check for a fuller accountability treatment (see gaps below).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • FY2020/21 (COVID-19): total operating revenue fell 7.0%; operating deficit widened to $7.7M from $2.1M the prior year, though investment gains produced an $11.5M bottom-line surplus (ar-2021, sha256:7bfb31855656).
  • 2020 Ontario Auditor General Value-for-Money Audit of ESA: cited by the Board Chair as shaping strategy execution; its recommendations are the stated origin of the April 2023 Administrative Penalties regulation (ar-2021 sha256:7bfb31855656; ar-2023 sha256:82dd5be2b00d).
  • FY2022/23 accounting standard change: amendments to CPA Handbook Section 3462 (employee future benefits), first applied April 1, 2022, produced a one-time $27.1M downward adjustment to opening net assets; combined with an operating deficit for the year, total net assets went negative (-$985K) at March 31, 2023, before recovering to $47.2M in FY2023/24 (ar-2023, sha256:82dd5be2b00d; ar-2024, sha256:c6d2b23bd82e).
  • FY2024/25: operating deficit widened further to $11.3M (from $9.4M in FY2023/24), attributed to labour-cost growth outpacing revenue; net assets nonetheless rose $16.7M to $63.9M, driven mainly by a $15.6M positive pension re-measurement rather than operating performance (ar-2025, sha256:5557842cc15e).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Neither the brief's cited series nor the backgrounder establishes a head-office location or any Toronto/GTA-specific facility for ESA; ESA's mandate as Ontario's delegated administrative authority for electrical safety runs province-wide — inspection, licensing, investigation and enforcement of the Ontario Electrical Safety Code apply uniformly across Ontario, with no city- or region-level breakdown of activity, revenue, or licensed contractors in the material read (backgrounder; on-electrical-safety-authority-ar-2020 through -ar-2025; sp-2020; sp-2025; bp-2027). Relevance to Toronto is therefore indirect only: Toronto-area electrical contractors, master electricians, and renovation/compliance activity fall under ESA's province-wide licensing and Risk-Based Oversight regime (ar-2021, sha256:7bfb31855656) as they would anywhere else in Ontario, and the FY2025-30 plan's framing around Ontario's "electrification transformation" and projected demand growth (ar-2025, sha256:5557842cc15e) implicates the province's largest electricity market by extension — but nothing in the cited record identifies a Toronto-specific office, program, or figure.
