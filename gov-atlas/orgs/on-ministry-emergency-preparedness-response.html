Ministry of Emergency Preparedness and Response - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-emergency-preparedness-response (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The ministry's statutory basis is the Emergency Management and Civil Protection Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.9, consolidated text current to the e-Laws currency date, consolidation period effective December 3, 2025 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e09, fetched and verified via the e-Laws legislation service, section-by-section content confirmed). Section 1's definitions expressly name it: "'Minister' means the Minister of Emergency Preparedness and Response or such other member of the Executive Council to whom administration of this Act is assigned under the Executive Council Act" (same source) — a direct, current match to the registry's org name. Section 2(1), as amended by 2025, c. 16, Sched. 1, s. 3(1), provides the Minister "shall provide leadership in and ensure co-ordination of emergency management in Ontario" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Section 2(2) empowers the Minister to monitor/assess hazards, risks and vulnerabilities and authorize related surveys and studies (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e09). The Act also establishes provincial emergency-management-strategy, Commissioner of Emergency Management, and provincial emergency-management-organization functions (s. 2.0.1-2.0.4, same source). The ministry's own page describes coordinating provincial emergency management/disaster response, delivering public education and training, supporting municipal emergency planning, and overseeing the Ontario Corps volunteer network, operated through Emergency Management Ontario as its primary operational division (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-emergency-preparedness-and-response, fetched directly).

Governance & reporting line

The Minister of Emergency Preparedness and Response has direct statutory responsibility under the Act (s. 1, s. 2(1), https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e09); as a Crown ministry it is structurally organized under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.25, ss. 1-2 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e25, fetched and verified).

Budget scale

⚠️ not yet confirmed against an original source, though program-level figures are documented: the 2025 Annual Report cites a $15 million, two-year Ontario Corps investment and a Community Emergency Preparedness Grant totalling $15 million over three years (342 communities) (https://www.ontario.ca/page/safe-practiced-and-prepared-ontario-2025-annual-report, fetched directly; doc-shelf document id: annual-report). A ministry-wide total operating/capital budget figure was not located this review.

Institutional history

The Emergency Management and Civil Protection Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.9, is a Revised Statutes consolidation of earlier Ontario emergency-management law, substantially updated by the Emergency Management Modernization Act, 2025 — described in the ministry's 2025 Annual Report as "the first major update to the province's emergency management legislation in more than 15 years," which formalized Ontario Corps and clarified coordination roles (https://www.ontario.ca/page/safe-practiced-and-prepared-ontario-2025-annual-report, fetched directly; amendment confirmed in the Act's own history as 2025, c. 16, Sched. 1, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e09).

Backgrounder summary

The ministry's statutory basis is the Emergency Management and Civil Protection Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.9, whose s.1 defines "Minister" as expressly including "the Minister of Emergency Preparedness and Response" and whose s.2(1), as amended by 2025, c. 16, Sched. 1, s.3(1), directs the Minister to "provide leadership in and ensure co-ordination of emergency management in Ontario." Section 2(2) empowers hazard/risk monitoring; ss.2.0.1-2.0.4 establish provincial emergency-management-strategy, Commissioner of Emergency Management, and provincial emergency-management-organization functions. The ministry delivers its mandate primarily through Emergency Management Ontario (EMO), coordinating provincial disaster response, public education/training, municipal emergency-planning support, and the Ontario Corps volunteer network.   • Emergency Management Modernization Act, 2025 — described as "the first major update to the province's emergency management legislation in more than 15 years," amending the EMCPA (7d51372f1dd0).
  • Ontario Corps launched — a group of skilled partners/volunteers deployable to help communities respond to disasters; cited as a "Key Program and Initiative" under "Transformation and Modernization" (7d51372f1dd0).
  • Provincial Emergency Response Plan (PERP) update targeted for completion by 2026, and Provincial Nuclear Emergency Response Plan (PNERP) update targeted for the 2025-26 fiscal year (7d51372f1dd0).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Direct and specific, more so than a typical province-wide ministry: the document names active planning for "the 2026 FIFA games in Toronto" as a specific "Key Program and Initiative," with MEPR staff "embedded... in the core FIFA planning team" to ensure "Ontario is prepared to safely host this globally high-profile event" (7d51372f1dd0). Beyond that named Toronto commitment, the ministry's core mandate is explicitly province-wide (coordination for "16 million people across the province," support to municipalities, First Nations, and unincorporated territories) — a province-wide emergency-coordination function that also names Greater Toronto Airport Authority exercise partnership ("Exercise Flaming Dart") and Niagara Region/Regional Municipality of Niagara solar-eclipse-response deployment as concrete non-Toronto-proper GTA-adjacent examples (7d51372f1dd0).
