Canada-Nova Scotia Offshore Energy Regulator - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canada-nova-scotia-offshore-energy-regulator (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canada-Nova Scotia Offshore Energy Regulator (CNSOER) is established "by the joint operation of" the federal Canada–Nova Scotia Offshore Petroleum Resources Accord Implementation and Offshore Renewable Energy Management Act, S.C. 1988, c. 28, and a mirror Nova Scotia statute (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-7.8/FullText.html, fetched and verified — s.9(1): "There is established by the joint operation of this Act and the Provincial Act a board to be known as the Canada–Nova Scotia Offshore Energy Regulator"). It is a joint federal-provincial body, not a purely federal one (registry row fed-canada-nova-scotia-offshore-energy-regulator).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CNSOER describes itself as "the lead and lifecycle offshore energy regulator" for both petroleum and, since a 2024 mandate expansion, offshore renewable (wind) energy in the Canada-Nova Scotia offshore area (https://cnsoer.ca/, fetched). Statutory functions (s.18(1)-(2) of the Act, same source) include administering the safety/environmental-protection regime for offshore activities, issuing submerged land licences, and recommending legislative amendments to both governments; it may also hold public reviews and hearings.

Governance & reporting line

A Board of up to seven members: Canada and Nova Scotia each appoint two members and one alternate, for six-year terms, and the Chair is jointly appointed by both governments (CNSOER 2024-2025 Annual Report, https://cdn.cnsoer.ca/sites/default/files/2025-08/CNSOER%202024-2025%20Annual%20Report%20(English)R.pdf, fetched). The Chief Executive Officer reports to the Board and leads a staff of 24 (same source).

Budget scale

~$5.9M total expenses for the year ended March 31, 2025 (actual $5,894,216 against a $6,050,536 budget; net revenue $6,040,612), funded by equal Canada/Nova Scotia government grants plus industry cost-recovery charges of up to 100% of the approved budget (CNSOER 2024-2025 Annual Report, Statement of Operations and Accumulated Operating Surplus, same URL, fetched).

Institutional history

Established in 1990 as the Canada-Nova Scotia Offshore Petroleum Board; renamed Canada-Nova Scotia Offshore Energy Regulator effective January 31, 2025 under 2024 amendments (Bill C-49, assented October 3, 2024) that expanded its mandate to offshore renewable energy (CNSOER 2024-2025 Annual Report, fetched, "Message from the Chief Executive Officer" and "Overview of the CNSOER" sections; Canada Gazette Part 2, Vol. 159, No. 1, https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-01-01/html/si-tr5-eng.html).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • January 31, 2025 — mandate expansion and rename takes effect. Following Nova Scotia's Bill 471 (Royal Assent Sept 20, 2024) and federal Bill C-49 (Royal Assent Oct 3, 2024), the Accord Acts were amended and CNSOPB became CNSOER (da0fbc20e0c7).
  • March 4-7, 2025 — Georges Bank Prohibition Order. On joint ministerial direction, CNSOER issued a Prohibition Order under ss. 60(1A) and 57(1.1) of the Accord Acts barring submerged land licences for offshore renewable energy projects in the Georges Bank Prohibition Area (Schedule IV), effective March 7, 2025 through December 31, 2032 (da0fbc20e0c7).
  • July 25, 2024 — four Production Licences (Sable Offshore Energy Project: PL 2903, 2904, 2905, 2906, all ExxonMobil Canada Ltd.) expired and reverted to Crown lands (da0fbc20e0c7).
  • FY2024-25 financials: actual expenses $5,894,216 against budget of $6,050,536; net revenue $6,040,612 (budget $5,912,000), funded by equal $2,956,000 government grants from Canada and Nova Scotia plus $128,612 interest/other income; operating surplus (deficit) before other items of $146,396, ending accumulated operating surplus $2,831,868 (da0fbc20e0c7, audited Statement of Operations).
