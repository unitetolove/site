Parks & Recreation - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: tor-parks-recreation (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Parks & Recreation is a City of Toronto administrative division, not a separately incorporated body; the City's general organizational authority derives from the City of Toronto Act, 2006, S.O. 2006, c. 11, Sched. A, consolidated text verified via ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11 (fetched 2026-07-30). Section 8(1) ("The City may provide any service or thing that the City considers necessary or desirable for the public") and s. 141 (power to establish city boards) are the operative organizational provisions (ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11). No division-specific statute was found, consistent with a Council-created administrative unit.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The division stewards Toronto's parks and recreation spaces, working "to ensure these areas are beautiful, safe and accessible" and contribute to "the city's social and environmental resilience" (toronto.ca staff-directory page for the division, fetched 2026-07-30; tor-parks-recreation). It comprises eight business units including Community Recreation, Parks, Capital Projects Design & Delivery, and Parks Planning & Strategic Initiatives (same source). Its page URL still carries the legacy path "parks-forestry-recreation," indicating a recent rebrand from "Parks, Forestry and Recreation" to "Parks & Recreation" (toronto.ca, fetched 2026-07-30; exact rename date ⚠️ still being checked).

Governance & reporting line

Led by General Manager Terry Ricketts (toronto.ca staff directory, fetched 2026-07-30). The registry row lists no parent_org value for this entry (tor-parks-recreation); it reports to City Council through the City's management structure under the City of Toronto Act, 2006 (ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11).

Budget scale

2026 Net Operating Budget of $375.3M, up $19.9M (5.6%) from 2025; Gross Expenditures of $494.8M for 2026 (projected to $569.0M by 2028); approved staff complement of 5,125.5 positions for 2026 (BudgetTO 2026 Budget Notes - Parks and Recreation, toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/bu/bgrd/backgroundfile-261733.pdf, fetched and text-extracted 2026-07-30). The 2026-2035 capital plan shows $415.1M gross capital expenditure for 2026 alone, rising to $4,310.2M cumulative gross expenditure through 2035 (same source).

Institutional history

Non-statutory: created and periodically reorganized administratively by City Council under its general organizational powers (City of Toronto Act, 2006, s. 8(1)-(2) and s. 141, ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11); recently rebranded from "Parks, Forestry and Recreation" to "Parks & Recreation" (evidenced by legacy URL path on toronto.ca, fetched 2026-07-30), with the exact rename date and any forestry-function transfer ⚠️ still being checked.

Parks & Recreation - strategy evolution

Backgrounder summary

Parks & Recreation is a City of Toronto administrative division (not separately incorporated), operating under the City's general organizational authority in the City of Toronto Act, 2006, ss. 8(1) and 141. It stewards the city's parks and recreation spaces across eight business units. Per the registry/backgrounder, it carries a 2026 Net Operating Budget of $375.3M and an approved staff complement of 5,125.5 positions, with a 2026-2035 capital plan reaching $4,310.2M cumulative gross expenditure through 2035. The backgrounder flagged a recent rebrand from "Parks, Forestry and Recreation" to "Parks & Recreation" with the exact date and any forestry-function transfer marked ⚠️ still being checked — this series resolves that question (see Budget & mandate inflection points below).

Priority evolution

2022 (oth-2022, f70c65e80184), "Ravine Strategy Implementation Update": First archived-series update (three years after the January 2020 implementation report) on the 2017-adopted Ravine Strategy's five guiding principles (Protect, Invest, Connect, Partner, Celebrate) and 20 actions. Reports $118.1M planned 2022 investment ($12.6M PFR operating + $105.5M capital across PFR, Transportation Services, Toronto Water and TRCA), $47.9M in committed/applied federal-provincial funding, and launches two flagship philanthropic/partnership initiatives: the Loop Trail (with Evergreen and TRCA) and InTO the Ravines (with Park People). Frames a new "Ravine Campaign" concept, retaining KCI Philanthropy as fundraising advisors, and recommends exploring a Toronto Foundation collaboration. Strong emphasis on Indigenous reconciliation, including the Lodge at the Humber River (Memtigwaake Kinomaage Maawnjiding) and a proposed co-management agreement with ENAGB Youth Program, tied to the City's April 2022 Reconciliation Action Plan (f70c65e80184).

2023 (oth-2023, 60241c1e977d), "Growing Space for Trees": A distinct policy track from the ravines series — housing-intensification-driven tree canopy protection, jointly authored by City Planning and (Acting GM) Parks, Forestry and Recreation. Responds to a chain of Council directions (2021-2023) to reconcile Toronto's Expanding Housing Options in Neighbourhoods (EHON) infill program (garden suites, laneway suites, multiplexes) with the Council-reaffirmed 40%-tree-canopy-by-2050 target. Proposes Official Plan and Zoning By-law reviews, Committee of Adjustment application-requirement changes, and continued EHON tree-impact monitoring. Reports early EHON monitoring data: Nov 2019-Jun 2023, 569 laneway/garden/multiplex permit notices, 101 tree by-law applications, 41 permits issued authorizing 29 tree injuries and 23 removals (~7% of notices) (60241c1e977d).

2024 (oth-2024, cabd3c7e9256), "Refreshing the FMP and Building a Cricket Strategy" (presentation): A City-School Boards Advisory Committee presentation, not a Council report — distinct genre from the other four. Announces the 5-year review/refresh of the 2019-2038 Parks and Recreation Facilities Master Plan (FMP), running summer 2024-spring 2025, with equity-focused community engagement. Showcases existing City-school co-location partnerships (Canoe Landing CRC, Davisville Community Aquatic Centre, Mary Ward/L'Amoreaux fields) as a template. Surfaces a new "Cricket Strategy," responding to cricket as "one of the fastest-growing sports across the region": FMP(2019) had targeted upgrading 3 and building up to 5 new cricket pitches over 20 years; this presentation lists specific 2024-2025 funded pitch projects (McCleary Park, Parma Park, Farquharson Park, Byng Park) and proposes re-evaluating the FMP's cricket provision target (cabd3c7e9256).

2025 (oth-2025, c51cdc4302da), "Growing Space for Trees and Iceberg Homes - Proposals Report": Direct sequel to the 2023 report, now co-authored by City Planning and a NEW division, "Environment, Climate and Forestry" (Executive Director James Nowlan) — Parks, Forestry and Recreation is no longer a listed co-author of this tree-policy track. Adds "iceberg homes" (houses with larger below-grade than above-grade footprints) as a new named concern, driven by an April 2024 Council direction. Proposes concrete Official Plan and Zoning By-law amendments (soft-landscaping definition excluding artificial turf/permeable pavement/pools; below-grade building setbacks for iceberg homes) for Q4 2025 finalization. Updated EHON monitoring: Nov 2019-Dec 2024, 1,305 permit notices, 189 tree permit applications, 100 permits issued (92 injuries, 56 removals; 7.6% of notices) — a rising trend (60 EHON-related applications and 26 permitted removals in 2024 alone, vs. 46/14 in 2023 and 41/2 in 2022) (c51cdc4302da).

2026 (oth-2026, 9095028f265e), "Ravine Strategy 2026 Implementation Update": Second three-year Ravine Strategy update (per the Council-directed reporting cadence), co-authored by Environment, Climate and Forestry (ECF) and a renamed "Parks and Recreation" (PR) — confirming and dating the org rebrand and a substantive mandate transfer (see next section). Reports cumulative since-2020 achievements: 3,760 ha restored, 521 tonnes of litter/metal removed, 181 students engaged, 265 Ravine Days events, 20,000+ participants, 16 capital projects ($12.3M), and PIA capital-need reduction from $104.9M to $50.2M. New elements: a formalized, permanent "Community-led Restoration Program" (piloted with Toronto Nature Stewards since 2021, expanding to groups like "Don't Mess with the Don"/Project Buckthorn); a new City-wide Environmentally Significant Areas (ESA) Management Plan and Framework; Loop Trail scaled up from 65km (2022) to 72km, with the Toronto Foundation now formalized as fiduciary partner for donations; and a new international-recognition angle — Toronto named a UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration "Role Model City" (2023) for Generation Restoration, with a recommended follow-on membership in "Cities with Nature" (9095028f265e).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — Iceberg homes as a named tree-canopy/infill concern: absent from the 2023 report; introduced by an April 2024 Council direction and given full proposals-report treatment in oth-2025 (c51cdc4302da).
  • Added — Cricket Strategy as a distinct facilities-planning priority: not present as a standalone strategy in the FMP(2019) beyond a pitch-count target; elevated to its own draft strategy with three goals ("More Fields," "Better Fields," "More Opportunities to Play") by oth-2024 (cabd3c7e9256).
  • Added — Community-led Restoration Program, permanent status: began as a 2021 pilot with Toronto Nature Stewards (referenced retrospectively in oth-2026); recommended for permanent, expandable implementation in oth-2026 (9095028f265e).
  • Added — International/UN engagement: Toronto's 2023 "Role Model City" designation for Generation Restoration first appears in the archived series in oth-2026, paired with a new recommended "Cities with Nature" membership (9095028f265e).
  • Renamed/restructured — Division split: "Parks, Forestry and Recreation" (PFR), the byline on oth-2022, oth-2023, and oth-2024, is absent from oth-2025 (tree-canopy policy authored solely by City Planning + the new Environment, Climate and Forestry division) and is confirmed renamed "Parks and Recreation" (PR) by oth-2026, which explicitly states Urban Forestry (UF) "transitioned from the Parks and Recreation Division (PR) to the Environment, Climate and Forestry (ECF) Division" in 2025, with UF budgets fully integrated into ECF by 2026 (9095028f265e). This resolves the backgrounder's ⚠️ still being checked on rebrand timing and forestry-function transfer: the rename and the forestry-function transfer both trace to 2025, landing before the 2026 report.
  • Renamed — General Manager title: "General Manager, Parks, Forestry and Recreation" (oth-2022 signature: Janie Romoff; oth-2023: Howie Dayton acting) becomes "General Manager, Parks and Recreation" (oth-2026 signature: Terry Ricketts, matching the backgrounder's current GM) (9095028f265e; backgrounder).
  • Shifted — Ravine Strategy lead authorship: oth-2022 is authored solely by "General Manager, Parks Forestry and Recreation"; oth-2026 is co-authored by "Executive Director, Environment, Climate and Forestry" (first-listed) and "General Manager, Parks and Recreation" (second-listed) — consistent with ravine ecological/forestry work moving to ECF while PR retains trail capital work (9095028f265e).
  • Quietly dropped — PFR as a distinct budget line in tree-canopy reporting: present as budget-holder in oth-2023's Committee of Adjustment/tree by-law discussion; by oth-2025 tree by-law administration and by-law amendments are handled entirely under ECF, with PR absent from that report's financial-impact and contact sections (c51cdc4302da).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2017 (Oct 2-4, EX27.8) — City Council adopts the Toronto Ravine Strategy (referenced retrospectively in both ravine reports as foundational) (f70c65e80184; 9095028f265e).
  • Jan 29, 2020 (EX12.1) — Council adopts the 10-year Ravine Strategy implementation plan and directs 3-year progress reporting; also adopts the 2018 Tree Canopy Study the same meeting (f70c65e80184; 60241c1e977d).
  • 2021 — Community-led Restoration piloted with Toronto Nature Stewards (referenced retrospectively, oth-2026) (9095028f265e).
  • April 2022 — City's Reconciliation Action Plan (RAP) adopted, cited as the policy basis for the Lodge at the Humber River co-management proposal in oth-2022 (f70c65e80184).
  • March 26, 2018 / 2023 — Committee of Adjustment tree-related application requirements set in 2018 (PE25.1), then reviewed/amended in 2023 following a KPMG Committee of Adjustment review (PH2.5) (60241c1e977d).
  • 2019-2038 — Parks and Recreation Facilities Master Plan (FMP) approved as a Council 20-year plan; 5-year review/refresh launched summer 2024, running to spring 2025 (cabd3c7e9256).
  • April 2024 — Council direction (PH11.4) initiates the "iceberg homes" strategy strand, folded into the tree-canopy policy track (c51cdc4302da).
  • Nov 20, 2024 — Ontario Regulation 462/24 (Additional Residential Units) takes effect, creating a new provincial layer the City must reconcile with its own EHON/garden-suite/laneway-suite tree-protection setback and lot-coverage rules (c51cdc4302da).
  • 2025 (dated precisely by oth-2026) — Urban Forestry transitions from Parks and Recreation to the new Environment, Climate and Forestry division; division renamed from "Parks, Forestry and Recreation" to "Parks and Recreation" — the mandate inflection the backgrounder flagged ⚠️ still being checked (9095028f265e).
  • Feb 11, 2025 (MPB27.1) — 2025 Operating Budget adds two permanent staff positions for ravine volunteer/stewardship programs, funding the Community-led Restoration pilot's expansion (9095028f265e).
  • July 2024 storm (cited retrospectively in oth-2026) — an over-100-year storm causes major erosion damage in the Don Valley Brick Works ravine wetlands, cited as evidence for the climate-resilience rationale behind continued ravine capital investment (9095028f265e).
  • 2026 — Second 3-year Ravine Strategy update; PIA capital need reported reduced to $50.2M (from $104.9M in 2020); Q4 2025 target for finalized Growing Space for Trees/Iceberg Homes Official Plan and Zoning By-law amendments (per oth-2025) falls just before this report — outcome ⚠️ still being checked, not covered in the archived series (9095028f265e; c51cdc4302da).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This entire series is Toronto-native by construction — a City of Toronto division reporting to Toronto City Council on Toronto-specific land, facilities, and by-laws (all five docs). Provincial-level touchpoints recur throughout: the City of Toronto Act, 2006 underpins both the tree by-laws (s. 104) and the Ravine Strategy's fine-setting authority (Part III offences) (60241c1e977d; f70c65e80184); the Planning Act and the Provincial Planning Statement (2024) frame the Official Plan/Zoning By-law amendment process (c51cdc4302da); and Ontario Regulation 462/24 (Additional Residential Units), effective Nov 20, 2024, directly intersects with City tree-protection setback and lot-coverage rules for garden suites and laneway suites (c51cdc4302da). The Toronto and Region Conservation Authority (TRCA), a provincially-rooted conservation authority whose jurisdiction spans York, Peel, Durham and Dufferin in addition to Toronto, is a recurring co-implementer across both ravine reports (f70c65e80184; 9095028f265e). No federal-statute touchpoints appear in this series beyond federal funding programs (ICIP, DMAF, Natural Infrastructure Fund, Active Transportation Fund) cited as capital-investment partners (f70c65e80184; 9095028f265e).
