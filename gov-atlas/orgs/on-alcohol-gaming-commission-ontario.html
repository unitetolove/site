Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-alcohol-gaming-commission-ontario (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) operates under the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario Act, 2019, and administers several related statutes including the Liquor Licence and Control Act, 2019 and the Cannabis Licence Act, 2018 (AGCO Business Plan April 1, 2024 to March 31, 2027, https://www.agco.ca/en/book/export/html/396931, fetched this pass). Its own site states: "The AGCO regulates alcohol, lottery and gaming, cannabis retail and horse racing in Ontario" (https://www.agco.ca/en/about-agco, fetched this pass).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The AGCO oversees four regulated sectors - alcohol (manufacturing and retail), recreational cannabis retail, horse racing (thoroughbred, standardbred and quarter horse), and lottery/gaming (charitable, land-based and internet gaming) - through licensing and permitting, compliance standards, inspections and investigations, hearings and appeals, and consumer complaint handling (https://www.agco.ca/en/about-agco). In 2021 the AGCO established iGaming Ontario as a subsidiary corporation, regulated under O. Reg. 517/21, to manage internet gaming operations (AGCO Business Plan 2024-2027, https://www.agco.ca/en/book/export/html/396931).

Governance & reporting line

The AGCO is headed by a Chair and Board of Directors appointed under the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario Act, 2019, with a separate Horse Racing Appeal Panel serving as an independent adjudicative tribunal; the agency reports to Ontario's Ministry of the Attorney General (AGCO Business Plan 2024-2027, https://www.agco.ca/en/book/export/html/396931). Board member biographies are published at https://www.agco.ca/en/about-agco.

Budget scale

For fiscal 2024-25 (year ending March 31, 2025), the AGCO Business Plan reports total operating expenses of $111.5 million against recoveries of $60.8 million, a net funding requirement of $50.6 million, and projected revenue of $50.0 million; staffing as of September 30, 2023 was 581 (524 permanent plus contracted staff and 149 seconded OPP officers) (AGCO Business Plan April 1, 2024 to March 31, 2027, https://www.agco.ca/en/book/export/html/396931).

Institutional history

The AGCO was established on February 23, 1998 (AGCO Business Plan April 1, 2024 to March 31, 2027, https://www.agco.ca/en/book/export/html/396931); a dedicated "History of the AGCO" page is linked from its about-agco page but ⚠️ VERIFY - its narrative content was not fetched this pass.

Priority evolution

The seven usable documents span only a three-fiscal-year business-plan window (2022-23 through 2026-27, as forward planning) plus a four-fiscal-year financial window (2022 through 2025); no earlier Business Plan or annual report predating 2022 is readable in this set, so priority evolution before 2022 cannot be traced from this archived series (see Residuals & gaps).

FY2022-23 (bp-2022, 0aa9ef462855): Framed around three "AGCO Strategic Goals" — Modern Regulator, Service Excellence, People First — stated as the AGCO's five-year Strategic Plan (introduced 2019, entering its "third year"). Named 2022-23 priorities: complete the response to the Auditor General's 2020 Value for Money Audit, implement a "focussed diversity, inclusion and accessibility strategy" (KPMG-developed), and continue igaming-market build-out ahead of iGaming Ontario's operational launch. A new Hybrid Work model launched November 15, 2021, alongside a mandatory COVID-19 vaccination policy (staff, Board, and Horse Racing Appeal Panel members) effective November 12, 2021.

FY2023-24 (bp-2023, 6338563e4b13): Same three strategic goals retained verbatim, now framed as "the fourth year of its five-year Strategic Plan." Igaming market described as having "launched on April 4, 2022" (past tense — a completed milestone since bp-2022). New named priority: a Financial Framework Review with the Ministry of the Attorney General, explicitly tied to Auditor General recommendations that AGCO become "more financially self-sufficient" — the first appearance of a formal cost-recovery-framework initiative in this series. The Auditor General's two-year follow-up report on the 2020 VFM audit is disclosed as released December 2022. AGCO records a formal submission to the federal Cannabis Act expert-panel review (new engagement). DI&A work matures from strategy-development to implementation: an employee census is introduced to benchmark workforce diversity.

FY2024-25 (bp-2024, 1b1d559077d2): The five-year Strategic Plan (2019-2024) is stated as "reach[ing] the end of its intended cycle," with a successor plan "prioritised" but not yet finalized as of this document — an explicit strategic-cycle transition captured mid-stream. The Financial Framework Review continues, now explicitly aimed at "full cost recovery and self-sufficiency over the long term." Three new, not-previously-named initiatives appear for the first time: (1) a Brand Strategy project ("A new brand strategy project will be undertaken for the AGCO in this planning cycle"); (2) completion of the AS/400 legacy-system retirement ("AGCO successfully retired its AS/400 legacy system after 20 years of operations"); (3) an AGCO Balanced Scorecard, described as delivered "at the beginning of this fiscal year" as a named performance-measurement milestone. Anti-Money Laundering is named as a standalone risk-management focus area for the first time at this level of prominence (distinct from its prior treatment folded into general igaming compliance).

FY2023-24/2024-25 financial-statement notes (fs-2024, a69280f7b134): Two subsequent/in-year events not visible in the business plans: (1) Bill 124 — the wage-restraint law struck down by the Superior Court of Justice (Nov 29, 2022) — was resolved for AGCO bargaining-unit employees in May 2024 via a negotiated retroactive salary adjustment totalling 6%, landing as a one-time cost spike; (2) the Liquor Retail Expansion to grocery/convenience stores is disclosed as a subsequent event: phased rollout beginning July 18, 2024 (existing licensed grocery stores gain ready-to-drink/large-pack beer), convenience stores no earlier than September 5, 2024, remaining grocery stores no earlier than October 31, 2024 — AGCO began accepting Convenience Store and Grocery Store Licence applications June 17, 2024.

FY2024-25 financial-statement notes (fs-2025, c0db239e6682): The single most significant structural event in the whole read window: "On May 12, 2025, the iGaming Ontario Act, 2024 was proclaimed which creates iGaming Ontario as a stand-alone agency and dissolves the parent-subsidiary relationship between the AGCO and iGaming Ontario." This ends, within this archived record, a four-fiscal-year (2022-2025) subsidiary arrangement under which AGCO billed iGaming Ontario for shared services and consolidated its regulatory-charge recoveries.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • Post-COVID cost-recovery normalization: the annual Province contribution covering AGCO's structural deficit (AGCO cannot run an accumulated surplus/deficit; the Province absorbs the gap every year) fell sharply from $46,490 thousand in FY2022 (fs-2022, 21d1d3be9b93) to $17,186 thousand in FY2023 (fs-2023, e73bbd0da44b), before rising to $21,431 thousand in FY2024 (partly Bill 124-driven) and settling at $17,245 thousand in FY2025 (fs-2025, c0db239e6682) — consistent with the business plans' own narrative of COVID-era cost-recovery shortfalls resolving through 2022-23.
  • Total revenue growth, PS3400-affected: $56,253K (FY2022 actual) → $91,121K (FY2023 actual) → $105,708K (FY2024 restated actual, PS3400-adjusted) → $117,909K (FY2025 actual) (fs-2022 through fs-2025) — growth is real but not fully like-for-like across the 2023-24 accounting-standard change (see above).
  • Total expenses: $102,743K (FY2022) → $108,307K (FY2023) → $127,139K (FY2024, includes the Bill 124 retroactive-wage impact) → $135,154K (FY2025) (same sources).
  • iGaming Ontario cost-recovery growth (pre-separation): AGCO's recoveries from iGaming Ontario under the Shared Resources Agreement climbed every year the subsidiary relationship existed: $7,264K (FY2022) → $9,474K (FY2023) → $11,360K (FY2024) → $13,822K (FY2025) (fs-2022 through fs-2025) — this entire revenue line's future is now uncertain given the May 2025 separation.
  • Headcount growth: AGCO Total staff (excluding OPP secondments/Board/HRAP) grew from 531 (Sept 30, 2022, bp-2022 wait — actually first reported as of Sept 30, 2022 in bp-2023's prior-year table) to 557 (Sept 30, 2023, bp-2023) to 581 (Sept 30, 2024, bp-2024) — consistent, incremental growth matching the backgrounder's independently-cited 581 figure.
  • Bill 124 wage-law resolution, May 2024: 6% retroactive salary adjustment (fs-2024, a69280f7b134), visible in the FY2024 salaries-and-wages line jumping from $74,805K (FY2023 actual) to $88,678K (FY2024 actual) against a $85,418K budget — an above-budget overshoot directly attributable to this one-time retroactive cost.
  • CEO & Registrar change: Tom Mungham signs as CEO & Registrar in fs-2022 and fs-2023; Dr. Karin Schnarr signs in that role in fs-2024 and fs-2025 — a leadership transition occurring sometime between August 2023 and July 2024, not otherwise dated in the documents read.
  • Statutory-name succession (fully resolved by the read documents, not a gap): Alcohol and Gaming Regulation and Public Protection Act, 1996 → amended 2018, renamed Alcohol, Cannabis and Gaming Regulation and Public Protection Act, 1996 (ACGRPPA) → repealed November 29, 2021, replaced by the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario Act, 2019 (AGCO Act), proclaimed into force — stated identically and consistently in fs-2022 through fs-2025's Note 1.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Direct and structural: AGCO is headquartered at 90 Sheppard Ave E, Suite 200, Toronto, ON M2N 0A4 (stated in every business plan and, implicitly, as the address of record for all correspondence read, e.g. bp-2022 0aa9ef462855) — this is a Toronto-headquartered provincial regulator, not merely a province-wide body with incidental Toronto mentions. Its regulatory reach is province-wide (alcohol, cannabis retail, horse racing, gaming across all of Ontario), so day-to-day licensing/compliance activity is not Toronto-specific, but the organization's own operational seat, corporate governance, and (per fs-2022's Note 12) its Ministry-provided leased office space (at 2-6 locations across the province depending on year) are centered in Toronto. The 2024 Liquor Retail Expansion into grocery and convenience stores applies province-wide, including within the City of Toronto, without any Toronto-specific carve-out disclosed in the documents read.
