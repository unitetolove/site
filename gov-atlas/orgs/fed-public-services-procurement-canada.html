Public Services and Procurement Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-public-services-procurement-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Established by the Department of Public Works and Government Services Act, S.C. 1996, c. 16 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-38.2/index.html - link verified, title/citation confirmed, current to 2026-06-14). Section 3(1) creates "a department of the Government of Canada called the Department of Public Works and Government Services" - the Act's legal name; the applied title "Public Services and Procurement Canada" was adopted through separate administrative government action rather than a legislative rename, and does not itself appear in the consolidated statute text (statute, full text at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-38.2/FullText.html). Section 6 gives the Minister authority over acquiring supplies/equipment for departments, providing services including construction, managing federal real property, telecommunications coordination, and translation services (statute, FullText.html; also s.20 Contracts, s.22.1 Procurement Ombudsman per the Act's index, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-38.2/index.html).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Functions as the Government of Canada's central purchasing agent, real-property manager, pay and pension administrator, and linguistic (translation) authority (2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://www.canada.ca/en/public-services-procurement/corporate/transparency/departmental-plan/2026-2027-plan.html, fetched via https://r.jina.ai/[same URL]).

Governance & reporting line

The Governor in Council appoints a Deputy Minister of Public Works and Government Services to hold office during pleasure as the Department's deputy head (statute, s.4(1), FullText.html). As of the 2026-27 Departmental Plan, the responsible minister is the Honourable Joël Lightbound, Minister of Government Transformation, Public Works and Procurement and Québec Lieutenant (2026-27 Departmental Plan, as above). Registry lists org_type as "department" with no listed parent (registry row, registry id fed-public-services-procurement-canada).

Budget scale

~$5.929B total planned spending for FY2026-27, including internal services and all core responsibilities (2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://www.canada.ca/en/public-services-procurement/corporate/transparency/departmental-plan/2026-2027-plan.html).

Institutional history

Established 1996 by the Department of Public Works and Government Services Act (S.C. 1996, c. 16), itself a 1996 consolidation of the former Department of Public Works and the former Department of Supply and Services; the department has operated under the applied/public title "Public Services and Procurement Canada" since 2015, an administrative redesignation rather than a legislative rename (statute, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-38.2/index.html; ⚠️ still being checked - the 1996 predecessor-merger and 2015 redesignation details are widely reported but were not independently confirmed against a primary Order-in-Council text this review).

Backgrounder summary

PSPC was established by the Department of Public Works and Government Services Act, S.C. 1996, c. 16, which creates a Governor-in-Council-appointed department under a Deputy Minister; the applied title "Public Services and Procurement Canada" replaced "Public Works and Government Services Canada" through a 2015 administrative redesignation, not a statutory rename. It functions as the federal government's central purchasing agent, real-property manager, pay and pension administrator, and linguistic (translation) authority, with ~$5.929B in planned FY2026-27 spending. The responsible minister as of the 2026-27 Departmental Plan is the Honourable Joël Lightbound, Minister of Government Transformation, Public Works and Procurement.

Priority evolution

2000-01 through 2015-16 (dp-2001 through dp-2016, nine documents, sha256-12 listed above): No substantive text recoverable — see Residuals & gaps. The one usable fact from this span is metadata, not body text: the document title field changes from "Public Works and Government Services Canada Report on Plans and Priorities 2014-15" (dp-2015, 8768e90a9730) to "Public Services and Procurement Canada Report on Plans and Priorities 2015-16" (dp-2016, 267ccfc77776) — consistent with the backgrounder's account of a 2015 administrative name change from PWGSC to PSPC, though this is drawn from the archived-record title field only, not from any readable plan content. ⚠️ still being checked beyond this single metadata point — no priority, mandate, or budget content from 2001-2020 could be confirmed.

FY2020-21 through FY2023-24 (dp-2021 589e08ee76bf, dp-2022 99da70fef654, dp-2023 fbf7bacf41ae, dp-2024 795c638c16cd): Only table-of-contents and erratum-notice text survived extraction, not the plan bodies. What this confirms: PSPC's five-core-responsibility structure (Purchase of goods and services; Payments and accounting; Property and infrastructure; Government-wide support; Procurement Ombudsman) plus Internal Services was already stable by FY2020-21 and unchanged through FY2023-24. The dp-2024 table of contents (795c638c16cd) is the first to list "Details on transfer payment programs of $5 million or more," "Gender-based analysis plus," and "United Nations 2030 agenda for sustainable development and the sustainable development goals" as distinct supplementary sections — a reporting-framework expansion visible only in the section list, since none of the underlying content survived extraction. Isolated erratum facts recovered: a GHG-reduction target date for PSPC's Crown-owned building portfolio was corrected from March 2021 to March 2030 (dp-2021, 589e08ee76bf); an SME contracting target was revised to "at least 2.5" qualified bidders on complex procurements (dp-2022, 99da70fef654).

FY2024-25 plan ("at a glance," dp-2025, 2152f8d2af9d): First document in the series with real priority narrative. Four named key priorities: (1) procurement modernization via the Contract Modernization Initiative and increasingly digital procurement; (2) service delivery, explicitly tied to resolving outstanding public-service pay issues and advancing a next-generation pay/HR system; (3) housing — new explicit priority, "accelerating the use of surplus federal lands and the conversion of empty office space... into market-based housing"; (4) the Canadian Dental Care Plan — new explicit priority, working with partners on implementation and communications. Also discloses PSPC-specific Budget 2023 "Refocusing Government Spending" reductions: $148,161,000 (FY2024-25), $154,107,000 (FY2025-26), $162,246,000 (FY2026-27 and after). Planned FY2024-25 spending by core responsibility: Purchase of goods and services $181.7M/2,425 FTE; Payments and accounting $767.9M/6,266 FTE; Property and infrastructure $3,347.6M/4,691 FTE; Government-wide support $159.9M/2,706 FTE; Procurement Ombudsman $4.48M/28 FTE.

FY2025-26 plan (dp-2026, 32297ab84bd0): Total planned spending jumps to $7,262.9M, a $1,795M (roughly +33%) increase over the FY2024-25 forecast, attributed to Investment Plan funding for major capital projects (Les Terrasses de la Chaudière modernization and building-envelope rehabilitation, Lester B. Pearson major renovation, the Energy Services Acquisition Program) and increased Next Generation Human Resources and Pay Initiative funding. Property and infrastructure alone is planned at $5,384.7M — about 78% of the five-core-responsibility subtotal. New or newly-explicit priority items not present in dp-2025: the Icebreaker Collaboration Effort Pact (a trilateral Canada-Finland-US partnership on Arctic/polar icebreaker capability); the Canadian Program for Cyber Security Certification (with the Department of National Defence); a formal Policy on Ethical Procurement targeting human trafficking, forced labour and child labour in supply chains; and the Government of Canada Office Portfolio Reduction Plan, quantified here for the first time as "reducing it by up to 50% over 10 years" (a sharper, numeric version of the "housing" priority named in dp-2025). The Laboratories Canada Strategy is named with a specific facility list: a Regulatory and Security Science (RSS) Centre for plant health in Sidney, BC; an RSS main project and a Transportation Safety and Technology Science facility, both in the National Capital Region; a TerraCanada NCR facility; the Atlantic Science Enterprise Centre in Moncton, NB; and a Cultural Heritage Science hub. The Parliamentary Precinct Long Term Vision and Plan is described as shifting to "the next 50-year horizon" and "a campus-wide approach." The Office of the Procurement Ombudsman is renamed "Procurement Ombud" via Order in Council, stated explicitly as a reporting-framework change "to promote gender and linguistic neutrality." Multi-year spending outlook: from $7,262.9M (2025-26) down to $5,724.3M (2026-27) and $4,570.3M (2027-28), with FTEs falling from 19,417 to 15,324 over the same span — concentrated in Payments and accounting (6,315 → 2,411 FTE) as pay-backlog-stabilization and Next Generation Pay/HR funding sunsets. The corporate-information section lists "Year of incorporation / commencement: 1841" — see Residuals & gaps.

FY2024-25 Departmental Results Report (drr-2025, ea33826462cb): Actual FY2024-25 total spending $5,451,590,462 (net expenditure basis) against 18,916 actual FTEs. Actual core-responsibility spending: Purchase of goods and services $227.2M/2,263 FTE (25% over the $181.7M plan); Payments and accounting $883.2M/5,989 FTE; Property and infrastructure $3,775.6M/4,736 FTE (69% of core-responsibility actuals); Government-wide support $167.8M/2,560 FTE; Procurement Ombud $5.36M/33 FTE; Internal services $392.4M. On an accrual basis, total expenses were $7,751,420,323 against a planned $7,281,400,500 — $470M (6.5%) over plan, attributed to Real Property Services and Optional Services Revolving Fund volume growth and additional pay-stabilization/Next Generation HR-Pay funding. Named FY2024-25 achievements: the Office of Supplier Integrity and Compliance (OSIC) was established during the year, described as "enhancing the integrity and oversight of procurement and real property processes"; the Canadian Dental Care Plan claim-processing contract was successfully implemented (plan launched December 2023, claims processing launched May 2024), called "the largest dental care plan in Canadian history"; the Canada Public Land Bank website launched August 2024 with an initial 56 properties (~25,000 potential housing units), growing to 90 properties (~42,000 housing units) by the report date; COVID-19 pandemic-era contract management continued as a residual (not a named forward priority) — over 12.5 million mRNA vaccine doses received via the Pfizer/Moderna Advance Purchase Agreement, N95/surgical mask contracts with Medicom and 3M still active. Defence/marine procurement results: a Future Aircrew Training contract awarded; River-class destroyer (RCD) Batch 1 production-test-module construction begun at Irving Shipbuilding; two contracts awarded for new light/heavy logistics vehicle fleets. Two target misses are reported explicitly: the Indigenous-business procurement participation target (5% of contract value) was not met on an aggregate basis — PSPC attributes this to a $1.5B (27%) increase in its overall procurement budget from three large contract amendments not originally held by Indigenous businesses, while noting ~6% of lower-value contracts (below $3.75M) went to Indigenous businesses; and the percentage of contract value awarded to small and medium businesses fell sharply from 20% (2023-24) to 5% (2024-25) against a 25% target, attributed in part to several high-value defence procurements. Pay-backlog trend improving but still off-target: employees facing potential pay inaccuracies fell from 135,500 (2022-23) to 112,273 (2023-24) to 94,122 (2024-25), against an "at most 88,000" target.

  • Added — Canadian Dental Care Plan support: first named as an explicit priority in dp-2025 (2152f8d2af9d); reported as a flagship completed achievement in drr-2025 (ea33826462cb, claims-processing contract live from May 2024).
  • Added — Office of Supplier Integrity and Compliance (OSIC): new standing institutional structure; first (and only) appearance in drr-2025 (ea33826462cb), reported as established during FY2024-25. Not named anywhere in the full text of dp-2026 (32297ab84bd0, the FY2025-26 forward plan, read in full) — ⚠️ still being checked why a body reported as newly established in the FY2024-25 results report does not appear in the subsequent year's forward-looking plan.
  • Added — reciprocal procurement policy: first named in dp-2025 (2152f8d2af9d) as an emerging priority ("support the implementation of reciprocal procurement policies"); elaborated in dp-2026 (32297ab84bd0) as ongoing work with the Department of Finance and Global Affairs Canada.
  • Added — formal Policy on Ethical Procurement: named as a forward priority in dp-2026 (32297ab84bd0); drr-2025 (ea33826462cb) shows the groundwork (public engagement sessions) predates that framing, having occurred in "early 2024."
  • Added — Icebreaker Collaboration Effort Pact and Canadian Program for Cyber Security Certification: both first (and only) appear in dp-2026 (32297ab84bd0).
  • Renamed — "Procurement Ombudsman" to "Procurement Ombud": disclosed as an Order-in-Council reporting-framework change in dp-2026 (32297ab84bd0); dp-2025 (2152f8d2af9d, the prior year) still uses "Procurement Ombudsman."
  • De-emphasized — COVID-19 pandemic response: present as continuing operational contract management in drr-2025 (ea33826462cb) but absent from the named "key priorities" lists in both dp-2025 and dp-2026 — a legacy wind-down task rather than a forward strategic priority by FY2024-26.
  • Cannot assess — all priorities pre-2021: the 2001-2020 span of the series is unreadable (see Residuals & gaps), so no addition/drop/rename comparison is possible against that era.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • Budget 2023 "Refocusing Government Spending" — PSPC-specific reductions disclosed from dp-2025 onward: $148.2M (FY2024-25), $154.1M (FY2025-26), $162.2M annually from FY2026-27 (2152f8d2af9d; confirmed in drr-2025, ea33826462cb).
  • FY2025-26 planned spending spikes to $7,262.9M — a $1,795M (~33%) jump over the FY2024-25 forecast, driven by major capital-project funding and Next Generation HR/Pay Initiative funding (dp-2026, 32297ab84bd0).
  • Planned multi-year decline follows: $7,262.9M (2025-26) → $5,724.3M (2026-27) → $4,570.3M (2027-28), as one-time capital and pay-stabilization funding sunsets; FTEs fall by 4,093 over the same window, concentrated in Payments and accounting (32297ab84bd0).
  • Property and infrastructure's spending share is rising: 61% of planned core-responsibility spending in FY2024-25 (2152f8d2af9d) → 69% of actual core-responsibility spending in FY2024-25 (ea33826462cb) → 78% of planned core-responsibility spending in FY2025-26 (32297ab84bd0).
  • FY2024-25 actuals ran $470M (6.5%) over the accrual-basis plan — $7,751.4M actual vs. $7,281.4M planned total expenses (ea33826462cb).
  • Office of Supplier Integrity and Compliance established during FY2024-25 — a new standing integrity-oversight body (ea33826462cb); ⚠️ still being checked its absence from the FY2025-26 forward plan text (32297ab84bd0).
  • Procurement Ombud renamed from Ombudsman via Order in Council, disclosed between the dp-2025 and dp-2026 reporting cycles (32297ab84bd0).
  • Two missed FY2024-25 socio-economic targets: Indigenous-business procurement participation (aggregate target 5% of contract value, not met — three large non-Indigenous contract amendments inflated the denominator by $1.5B/27%) and SME contract-value share (fell from 20% to 5% against a 25% target) (ea33826462cb).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

The archived series does not establish any City of Toronto presence for PSPC. PSPC's headquarters, per both dp-2026 and drr-2025 corporate-information sections, is in Gatineau, Quebec (Portage III, 11 Laurier St), across the river from Ottawa — not Ontario. A direct search of all 16 extracted documents for "Toronto" returned zero matches. Three specific Ontario sites are named, all in drr-2025 (ea33826462cb) only: a new TerraCanada facility "in Mississauga, Ontario" (Laboratories Canada Strategy, integrating government science capabilities with academia); completed rehabilitation of the Burlington Canal Lift Bridge in Burlington, Ontario; and an accelerated modular-bridge installation timeline for the LaSalle Causeway in Kingston, Ontario. None of these is in the City of Toronto proper, and none establishes an ongoing PSPC office or operational presence in Toronto. Toronto/GTA relevance from this series is therefore indirect at best — through federally-funded science infrastructure and infrastructure-asset management occurring in the wider Ontario corridor — rather than through any documented Toronto-specific footprint. ⚠️ Do not stretch this into a claim of Toronto presence; the honest finding is near-absence.
