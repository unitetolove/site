Ministry of Infrastructure - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-infrastructure (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Ministry of Infrastructure is continued under the Ministry of Infrastructure Act, 2011, S.O. 2011, c. 9, Sched. 27, confirmed on the Ontario e-Laws consolidated text (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/11m09b; registry row on-ministry-infrastructure). Section 3 provides that the Minister "shall preside over and have charge of the Ministry," and section 7 assigns responsibility for reviewing infrastructure matters, advising on inter-governmental dealings, and coordinating infrastructure development across government (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Under s.7(1)-(4) of the Act, the Ministry acquires, disposes of and manages Government property, designs/constructs/maintains facilities, undertakes research, develops policy and programs, enters agreements for the Crown, makes grants and loans, and issues directives to agencies under the Minister's supervision (Ministry of Infrastructure Act, 2011, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/11m09b). Operationally, the Ministry coordinates a $236 billion provincial infrastructure investment plan spanning schools, hospitals, transit, roads, bridges and water systems; works with Infrastructure Ontario on delivery; administers municipal funding programs including the Ontario Community Infrastructure Fund and Municipal Housing Infrastructure Program; and oversees Waterfront Toronto and government real-estate/surplus-property functions (Ministry of Infrastructure overview page, https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-infrastructure).

Governance & reporting line

As a ministry of the Ontario public service, it is headed by a Minister accountable to the Legislative Assembly under s.2-3 of the Ministry of Infrastructure Act, 2011 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/11m09b), and is the reporting ministry for provincial infrastructure Crown agencies including Infrastructure Ontario (Infrastructure Ontario "Reporting & Governance," https://www.infrastructureontario.ca/en/about-infrastructure-ontario/reporting-governance/).

Budget scale

Ministry planned expenditures for FY2025-26 total $3,674.7 million, comprising $1,346.5 million in operating expense and $2,328.1 million in capital expense (Published Plans and Annual Reports 2025-2026: Ministry of Infrastructure, document id on-ministry-infrastructure-ar-2026, https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-infrastructure).

Institutional history

The Ministry of Infrastructure Act, 2011 states the Ministry "is continued," indicating it predates the 2011 Act; ⚠️ still being checked — the Ministry's original creation date and any predecessor name (e.g. Ministry of Public Infrastructure Renewal) were not independently source-checked this review. The Act has been amended in at least 2015, 2018, 2020, 2023 and 2025, chiefly expanding the Minister's property-management and information-directive powers (Ministry of Infrastructure Act, 2011, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/11m09b).

Ministry of Infrastructure - strategy evolution

2026-08-02 / registry: on-ministry-infrastructure / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: Across its three archived annual reports (2023-24 through 2025-26), the Ministry of Infrastructure's biggest added priority is housing-enabling infrastructure funding — the Housing-Enabling Water Systems Fund (HEWSF, launched Jan 2024) and Municipal Housing Infrastructure Program (MHIP), which grew from a combined ~$1.8B commitment to nearly $2.3B over four years by 2025-26. The quietest structural change is the Ontario Place Corporation's absorption into the ministry itself (2023, ahead of its planned dissolution) alongside the interim Feb 2024 transfer of the Ontario Science Centre into MOI's agency-oversight portfolio — both consolidations tied directly to the Ontario Place rebuild. The single largest load-bearing number is the ministry's total planned spending, which nearly doubled from $2.42B (2020-21 actuals) to $4.69B (2024-25 estimate) before settling to $3.67B in 2025-26. The open question the archived series does not resolve: whether the Ontario Place rebuild — now backstopped by finalized commercial leases with Therme Group Canada and Live Nation Canada — will hit its "world-class, year-round destination" target on the schedule implied by the 2023 Rebuilding Ontario Place Act, or what the total public cost of the project will ultimately be, since no consolidated capital budget for the rebuild itself is disclosed in any of the three reports.

Backgrounder summary

Per the backgrounder, the Ministry of Infrastructure is continued under the Ministry of Infrastructure Act, 2011 (S.O. 2011, c. 9, Sched. 27), with the Minister presiding over the Ministry (s.3) and holding broad powers under s.7 to acquire/dispose/manage Crown property, undertake research, develop policy, make grants and loans, and direct agencies under the Minister's supervision. The Ministry coordinates a (then) $236 billion provincial infrastructure plan, works with Infrastructure Ontario on delivery, administers municipal funding programs (OCIF, MHIP), and oversees Waterfront Toronto and government real-estate functions. FY2025-26 planned expenditure was given as $3,674.7 million ($1,346.5M operating / $2,328.1M capital), sourced to the ar-2026 document — this matches exactly what is read directly in this review (see Series inventory).

Note on structure: each Ontario "Published Plans and Annual Reports" document is a dual-year artifact — forward-looking plans/strategic priorities for the named fiscal year, paired with a results narrative and financials for the prior completed fiscal year. This gives effective narrative coverage of fiscal years 2022-23 through 2024-25, with forward-looking priority statements extending into 2025-26.

Priority evolution

ar-2024 (7ccce8c3c0dc, plans for 2023-24 / results for 2022-23): Ministry vision framed around "smart, targeted infrastructure investments" across schools, hospitals, roads, transit, water/wastewater/stormwater and broadband. Five named program areas: Infrastructure Programs and Projects (incl. AHSIP broadband, OCIF, SPIF, ICIP), Infrastructure Strategy Policy and Research, Government Real Estate (GREP), Transit-Oriented Communities (TOC) & agency oversight, and Ontario Place Redevelopment as its own named program. KPI targets include a 7% asset-renewal backlog ceiling, P3 on-budget/on-time targets (93%/60%), $1.2B deferred-maintenance reduction, and 700,000 homes connected to broadband by Dec 31, 2025. Ontario Place redevelopment described as "approximately enhanced parkland" with 12 acres of new West Island public space named as a concrete deliverable. Ontario Place Corporation and Toronto Waterfront Revitalization Corporation appear as separate financial-consolidation lines.

ar-2025 (82c25690dbf9, plans for 2024-25 / results for 2023-24): Housing-enabling infrastructure emerges as a headline new priority: HEWSF launched January 2024 ($825M over three years, later revised up via Budget 2024's additional $625M) and the new Municipal Housing Infrastructure Program (MHIP, $1B) are both named as first-time programs tied explicitly to the province's 1.5-million-homes-by-2031 target — the first time a KPI outcome (Outcome #1) is framed directly around net new housing units. Ontario Place Corporation's operations are reported "integrated into MOI" in 2023 pending the Corporation's formal dissolution — the first sign of the entity's absorption. The Ontario Science Centre (OSC) appears for the first time as an MOI-overseen agency, transferred on an interim basis in February 2024, explicitly tied to its planned relocation into the Ontario Place rebuild. The 2023 New Deal for Toronto Act / Rebuilding Ontario Place Act, 2023 is cited as new legislative authority accelerating Ontario Place approvals, with the City of Toronto formally conceding provincial authority to advance project approvals.

ar-2026 (3e371b1aaec0, plans for 2025-26 / results for 2024-25): Housing-enabling funding is scaled further — HEWSF now described as "nearly $1.3 billion," MHIP investment specified at $725M, and a 2025 top-up of "an additional $400 million in the immediate term" brings the combined HEWSF+MHIP commitment to "nearly $2.3 billion over four years." A new Housing-Enabling Water Infrastructure lending stream (up to $1B in loans, launched Dec 2024) is added. Ontario Place's commercial partners are named for the first time as executed agreements rather than plans: Therme Group Canada (lease finalized May 2022) and Live Nation Canada (lease finalized May 2024). "Reduce gridlock" language is newly prominent in the ministry's top-line vision statement (absent from ar-2024/ar-2025 phrasing). The Ontario Science Centre appears with a full statutory mandate description (Centennial Centre of Science and Technology Act) rather than the thinner treatment in ar-2025, and its transfer source ministry is now specified as the Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Sport (renamed Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming) — a detail absent from ar-2025. Waterfront Toronto's Port Lands Flood Protection Project is reported reaching substantial completion in 2025, unlocking a newly announced $975M tri-government-funded "next phase" of waterfront work beginning 2025-26 on "Ookwemin Minising (formerly Villiers Island)."

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — Housing-enabling infrastructure as an explicit funding pillar: absent as a distinct category in ar-2024; HEWSF and MHIP both launch/are named starting ar-2025 (82c25690dbf9), scaled further with a new lending stream and top-up funding in ar-2026 (3e371b1aaec0). This is the single largest new-money priority across the series.
  • Added — Ontario Science Centre agency oversight: absent from ar-2024; first appears ar-2025 (82c25690dbf9) as an interim Feb 2024 transfer; fuller statutory/mandate treatment and clearer source-ministry attribution added in ar-2026 (3e371b1aaec0).
  • Added — "Reduce gridlock" as top-line vision language: not present in the ar-2024 or ar-2025 vision statements; appears in ar-2026 (3e371b1aaec0) ministry-overview framing alongside the pre-existing housing/jobs/economy language.
  • Added — Digital twin strategy: first appears ar-2025 (82c25690dbf9) as a $5M 2024-25 allocation "developing options"; ar-2026 (3e371b1aaec0) reports concrete progress — a completed Proof of Concept for the Eglinton Crosstown West Extension LRT project.
  • Quietly absorbed/restructured — Ontario Place Corporation: treated as an arm's-length agency with its own financial consolidation line in ar-2024 (7ccce8c3c0dc); ar-2025 (82c25690dbf9) reports its operations "integrated into MOI" in 2023 pending dissolution, and by ar-2026 (3e371b1aaec0) it no longer appears as a separate consolidation line in the financial tables at all (shows "N/A" against prior-year actuals) — a structural absorption the reports describe factually but do not editorialize about.
  • Renamed — Station Contribution Fee → Transit Station Charge: the same GO Transit Station Funding Act, 2023 municipal funding tool is named "Station Contribution Fee" in ar-2025 (82c25690dbf9) and "Transit Station Charge" in ar-2026 (3e371b1aaec0), with no explanation given for the rename.
  • Quietly dropped — explicit P3 on-time/on-budget percentage KPI targets: ar-2024 (7ccce8c3c0dc) names specific ministry-level KPI targets (93% on-budget / 60% on-time for P3 projects); ar-2025 and ar-2026 continue to report Infrastructure Ontario's own P3 completion statistics (94%/68% and 94%/80% respectively) but frame them as IO agency results rather than restating them as MOI's own forward KPI targets — the ministry's own 2024-25 and 2025-26 KPI tables pivot instead to housing-unit and deferred-maintenance outcome measures.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2022-23 → 2025-26 total planned operating+capital spend: $2.23B (2021-22 actuals, per ar-2024 historic trend table, 7ccce8c3c0dc) → $2.42B (2022-23) → $3.51B (2023-24) → $4.69B (2024-25 estimate, ar-2025, 82c25690dbf9) → $3.67B (2025-26 estimate, ar-2026, 3e371b1aaec0) — a near-doubling over three years followed by a pullback, driven largely by swings in the General Real Estate Portfolio and Infrastructure Partnership Projects & Agency Oversight consolidation lines.
  • January 2024 — HEWSF launched, initially $825M over three years (7ccce8c3c0dc reports the later-revised figure; 82c25690dbf9 gives the $200M FES-2023 baseline plus $625M Budget 2024 top-up breakdown).
  • February 2024 — Ontario Science Centre oversight transferred to MOI on an interim basis (82c25690dbf9; source ministry specified only in 3e371b1aaec0 as Tourism, Culture and Sport/Gaming).
  • 2023, precise date not given in series — New Deal for Toronto Act, 2023 (incl. Rebuilding Ontario Place Act, 2023) passed, with City of Toronto conceding provincial authority to advance Ontario Place approvals (82c25690dbf9).
  • May 2022 / May 2024 — Ontario Place commercial leases finalized with Therme Group Canada and Live Nation Canada respectively; both only disclosed as completed in ar-2026 (3e371b1aaec0), not mentioned as executed in the earlier two reports.
  • December 2024 — Housing-Enabling Water Infrastructure lending stream launched (up to $1B in loans) (3e371b1aaec0).
  • 2025, precise date not given — additional $400M immediate-term top-up to HEWSF/MHIP announced, bringing combined commitment to "nearly $2.3 billion over four years" (3e371b1aaec0).
  • January 2025 — all three levels of government announce the next phase of Waterfront Toronto revitalization ($975M tri-government-funded), timed to the Port Lands Flood Protection Project's 2025 substantial completion (3e371b1aaec0).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This ministry's Toronto/Ontario footprint is direct and extensive across all three documents, not inferred: it directly oversees the Ontario Place redevelopment (Cinesphere/Pod complex repairs, new Ontario Science Centre relocation, Therme Group Canada and Live Nation Canada as named commercial partners), holds legislative agency-oversight responsibility for Infrastructure Ontario, Waterfront Toronto, and (on an interim basis) the Ontario Science Centre, and jointly funds Waterfront Toronto's Port Lands Flood Protection Project and its 2025-26 "next phase" on Ookwemin Minising (formerly Villiers Island) alongside the federal government and City of Toronto (3e371b1aaec0). It also manages Toronto-specific real-estate projects (Macdonald Block Reconstruction Project, "Toronto Office Optimization Plan") and Toronto-area transit-oriented development sites (King-Bathurst, Queen-Spadina, Ontario Line North, Scarborough Subway Extension stations) (3e371b1aaec0). Per the backgrounder's own framing, this ministry is squarely embedded in Toronto capital-project delivery rather than adjacent to it — the archived series substantiates that framing directly rather than requiring inference from boilerplate.
