Special Investigations Unit - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-special-investigations-unit (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Special Investigations Unit (SIU) is "a civilian law enforcement agency, independent of the police, that conducts criminal investigations" arising from interactions between police and civilians (https://www.siu.on.ca/en/what_we_do.php, fetched this review). Its mandate is "to nurture confidence in Ontario's police services and select special constables and peace officers by assuring the public that the actions of these officials resulting in serious injury, death, the discharge of a firearm at a person or an allegation of sexual assault are subjected to rigorous, independent investigations" (https://www.siu.on.ca/en/civilian_oversight.php, fetched this review). The SIU now operates under its own governing statute, the Special Investigations Unit Act, 2019, in force since December 1, 2020, which replaced earlier provisions under section 113 of the Police Services Act and replaced officers' "duty to cooperate" with a mandatory "duty to comply" - breach of which is a provincial offence carrying possible fines or imprisonment (same source, and https://www.siu.on.ca/en/what_we_do.php).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The SIU investigates incidents - not individuals - meeting specific statutory thresholds: death, serious injury, an allegation of sexual assault, or discharge of a firearm at a person, arising from an interaction with police or select special constables and peace officers. Investigations may be triggered by a report from the involved police service, the affected person, or any other person; the Unit's civilian investigators and forensic investigators "gather and assess evidence," after which the Director determines whether the evidence supports a reasonable belief that a criminal offence occurred (https://www.siu.on.ca/en/what_we_do.php, cited above). Where the Director finds sufficient grounds, charges are laid and prosecuted by the Crown Attorney; where not, no criminal charges follow. The SIU's investigative objective is strictly criminal wrongdoing - it does not assess provincial offences or police code-of-conduct/professional-misconduct matters, which are handled by other bodies such as the Office of the Independent Police Review Director (same source). Its jurisdiction covers all Ontario police services (47 services, roughly 23,600 officers) plus special constables employed by the Niagara Parks Commission and peace officers with the Legislative Protective Service under the 2019 Act (same source).

Governance & reporting line

The SIU is headed by a Director; ⚠️ still being checked the exact statutory appointment mechanism - secondary sources describe appointment by the Lieutenant Governor in Council on the recommendation of the Attorney General under s. 5(1) of the Special Investigations Unit Act, 2019, but this was not independently confirmed against the SIU's own site this review. The Unit was "moved from the Solicitor General to the Ministry of the Attorney General in April 1993" specifically "to provide greater separation from the police," and it continues to operate under a Memorandum of Understanding with the Ministry of the Attorney General (SIU-MAG MOU) (https://www.siu.on.ca/en/civilian_oversight.php, cited above). Ontario courts have affirmed that "the SIU was intended to be an independent overseer of the police," with investigations serving "the public as a whole rather than any particular person" (same source). Per project convention, no individual Director or officer names are reproduced here.

Budget scale

For the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, SIU total annual expenditures were $12,201,133 against a year-end budget of $12,278,700. Salaries and Wages accounted for $9,297,227 (76.2%) and Employee Benefits $1,308,600 (10.7%) of spending - roughly 86% of total expenditures were compensation-related - with Investigative Services and Identification Services (forensics) together accounting for roughly 60% of expenditures by function (SIU Annual Report 2024-2025, "2024-2025 Financials," https://www.siu.on.ca/pdfs/siu_annual_report_2024-2025.pdf, registry doc on-special-investigations-unit-ar-2025).

Institutional history

The SIU began operations in August 1990, established following recommendations of the 1989 Race Relations and Policing Task Force, which had called for "an independent investigative body with the power to lay criminal charges when warranted" (https://www.siu.on.ca/en/civilian_oversight.php, cited above). It moved from the Solicitor General's portfolio to the Ministry of the Attorney General in April 1993, and its governing framework was substantially rebuilt by the Special Investigations Unit Act, 2019 (in force December 1, 2020), which gave the Unit its own standalone statute for the first time, expanded its mandate, and stiffened officers' cooperation obligations (same source). Its registered document shelf traces annual reports back to 2002-03 (registry doc on-special-investigations-unit-ar-2003), with a documented publication gap around calendar-year 2020 attributed to a COVID-era realignment of the reporting cycle from calendar-year to fiscal-year format (registry doc on-special-investigations-unit-ar-2020, flagged ⚠️ Still being checked: searched-not-found).
