Pacific Economic Development Canada - backgrounder

Mandate & statutory basis

PacifiCan is not created by its own dedicated statute. Per its own Departmental Plan, it was established by Order in Council P.C. 2021-0828 under the authority of the Western Economic Diversification Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 11 (4th Supp.) (https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development/corporate/transparency/departmental-plans/2026-2027-departmental-plan.html, fetched via r.jina.ai) — that Act's existence and consolidated text (current to 2026-06-14, last amended 2018-06-21) were independently verified at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/W-6.8/index.html (fetched). It was added as a department for Financial Administration Act purposes via Schedule I.1 designation (⚠️ still being checked - the specific FAA Schedule I.1 entry text was not independently pulled up this review; landing pages for the schedule 404'd on fetch).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

PacifiCan is "the dedicated federal economic development agency for British Columbians," promoting growth and diversification of the B.C. economy through business-competitiveness funding, innovation and clean-technology support, Indigenous economic development initiatives, and regional investment/export-advisory programs (https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development.html, fetched via r.jina.ai; PacifiCan 2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development/corporate/transparency/departmental-plans/2026-2027-departmental-plan.html, fetched via r.jina.ai; registry row fed-pacific-economic-development-canada).

Governance & reporting line

As of this review's fetch, the Honourable Gregor Robertson holds the title "Minister responsible for Pacific Economic Development Canada" concurrent with his role as Minister of Housing and Infrastructure (https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development.html, fetched via r.jina.ai); ministerial responsibility for the agency has changed by Order in Council more than once since 2021 (reported transfers in March 2025 and June 2025) - ⚠️ still being checked current assignment against a primary gazette/OIC source. A President serves as deputy head; ⚠️ still being checked - the current President's name was not confirmed this review.

Budget scale

~$180.6M total planned spending for 2026-27 ($180,582,915: $170.9M for the Economic Development in British Columbia program, $9.65M internal services), 181 planned FTEs (PacifiCan 2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development/corporate/transparency/departmental-plans/2026-2027-departmental-plan.html, fetched via r.jina.ai).

Institutional history

Established in 2021 by Order in Council P.C. 2021-0828 under the Western Economic Diversification Act, splitting British Columbia programming out of the former Western Economic Diversification Canada into a standalone Pacific-focused agency (PacifiCan 2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development/corporate/transparency/departmental-plans/2026-2027-departmental-plan.html, fetched via r.jina.ai; enabling Act verified at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/W-6.8/index.html).

Pacific Economic Development Canada - strategy evolution

2026-07-31 (rolling continuation) / registry: fed-pacific-economic-development-canada / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition is a 2026-27 three-part "key priorities" reframe (Invest in B.C.'s Strengths / Supply Canada / Grow Exports) replacing the four-year-old "Quality Jobs/Clusters/Global Businesses/Inclusive Growth" set, alongside new trade-response programs (Regional Tariff Response Initiative, Regional Defence Investment Initiative). The most consequential quiet drop is a Comprehensive Expenditure Review disclosed in dp-2027 that non-renews the Tourism Growth Program, Regional Quantum Initiative, and Regional Homebuilding Innovation Initiative, alongside the four founding-era service "roles" (investor/pathfinder/convenor/advisor) quietly disappearing from dp-2026 onward with no stated retirement. The most load-bearing number is total planned 2026-27 spending of $180,582,915 with 181 FTEs, against CER-driven cuts of $9.8M-$19.8M annually through 2028-29. One open question the record cannot resolve: whether PacifiCan ever tabled a 2021-22 Departmental Plan under its own name, since dp-2023 calls itself the agency's "first" plan, leaving dp-2022's absence ambiguous between a genuine non-existence case and a capture failure.

Backgrounder summary

PacifiCan has no dedicated founding statute; it was established by Order in Council P.C. 2021-0828 under the Western Economic Diversification Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 11 (4th Supp.), splitting British Columbia programming out of the former Western Economic Diversification Canada (WD) into a standalone Pacific-focused agency on August 6, 2021. Its mandate is to promote growth and diversification of the B.C. economy through business-competitiveness funding, innovation/clean-technology support, Indigenous economic development, and regional investment/export advisory programs. It reports through a minister responsible for Pacific Economic Development Canada (a portfolio held concurrently with other ministerial duties, and reassigned more than once since 2021) and a President as deputy head. Planned spending for 2026-27 is ~$180.6M with 181 planned FTEs.

Series inventory

The archived series holds six documents spanning PacifiCan's entire existence as a standalone agency: five annual Departmental Plans (dp-2023 through dp-2027, covering fiscal years 2022-23 through 2026-27) and one Strategic Plan (sp-2026, covering 2026-2028). All six were fetched live from canada.ca and archived successfully; all six were read in full. No pre-2021 rows for this registry id represent a capture gap in the ordinary sense — PacifiCan did not exist as an organization before August 6, 2021 (dp-2023, bdbf95638f54), so departmental plans for fiscal years ending 2021 and earlier would have been filed, if at all, under Western Economic Diversification Canada's own registry id, not this one. See Residuals & gaps for the one edge case (dp-2022) that needs a closer look.

Priority evolution

2022-23 (dp-2023, bdbf95638f54): PacifiCan's own first Departmental Plan (the Minister's foreword calls it "PacifiCan's first Departmental Plan"). Founding-era framing is explicit and prominent: the agency is introduced as "building on Western Economic Diversification Canada's 34-­year legacy," launched August 6, 2021, and organized around four service "roles" — investor, pathfinder, convenor, advisor. Priorities center on standing up the agency itself (new offices in Campbell River, Cranbrook, Fort St. John, Kelowna, Prince George, Prince Rupert, Victoria; a new Surrey headquarters; an Ottawa liaison office) and on delivering five streams of Budget 2021 pandemic-relief funding — the Jobs and Growth Fund (JGF), Tourism Relief Fund (TRF), Canada Community Revitalization Fund (CCRF), Aerospace Regional Recovery Initiative (ARRI), and Major Festivals and Events Support Initiative — alongside core programs REGI (Business Scale-up and Productivity / Regional Innovation Ecosystems streams), CEDD, EDI, and the Western Canada Business Service Network (WCBSN, seven member organizations including a Black Entrepreneurship Program).

2023-24 (dp-2024, b1d8df16223d): First appearance of a named four-part priority set for the year — Quality Jobs, Competitive Industry Clusters, Globally Successful Businesses, Inclusive Growth — under a newly stated agency vision, "enduring prosperity for all British Columbians." The seven new regional offices announced in dp-2023 are now confirmed open. Two new programs launch: the Regional Quantum Initiative (announced Dec 6, 2022, part of the federal National Quantum Strategy) and Rebuilding Lytton (Lytton Homeowner Resilient Rebuild, $6M; Lytton Business Restart, $7.2M), responding to the June 2021 Lytton wildfire. The British Columbia Indigenous Clean Energy Initiative (BCICEI) is named for the first time, with an explicit note that PacifiCan continues a program WD had run since 2016. The four-role framing (investor/pathfinder/convenor/advisor) is retained unchanged.

2024-25 (dp-2025, 6f62dd342ce8): A structural break from the founding-era reporting apparatus: the plan states outright that "PacifiCan's previous departmental plans were built on a departmental results framework (DRF) inherited from the former Western Economic Diversification Canada," and replaces it with a PacifiCan-specific DRF — renaming departmental results ("Businesses are innovative and growing" → "Businesses are growing"; "Communities are economically diversified" → "Communities are growing"; "Businesses invest in the development and commercialization of innovative technologies" → "Businesses are commercializing technology and adopting technology") and swapping most underlying indicators. WCBSN is renamed the Pacific Business Service Network (PBSN); its member list drops the "Black Entrepreneurship Program" line item (though Black Entrepreneurship funding continues to appear elsewhere in later plans). New program: the Business Acceleration Pilot (BizAP, launched Oct 2023 under RIE) and the Tourism Growth Program (TGP, launched Nov 2023, part of the Budget 2023 Federal Tourism Growth Strategy). The four "quality jobs / clusters / global businesses / inclusive growth" goals from dp-2024 persist, reworded slightly as four goals under the same "enduring prosperity" vision. A new Surrey headquarters (Gateway Station Tower) is announced for "fall 2024."

2025-26 (dp-2026, 292d47c87037): The ministerial portfolio changes — the Honourable Gregor Robertson (Minister of Housing and Infrastructure) is now named as minister responsible, replacing the Honourable Harjit S. Sajjan who signed dp-2023 through dp-2025; the institutional head is now named as Naina Sloan, replacing Dylan Jones who is named as President in every prior plan in this series. The Surrey headquarters is reported as opened, dated here to "January 2025" (contrast dp-2025's "fall 2024" — see Residuals & gaps). New program: the Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative (RAII), launched November 2024 as B.C.'s share ($32.2M) of a $200M/5-year Budget 2024 commitment spread across all federal regional development agencies; also new: the Regional Homebuilding Innovation Initiative (RHII), tied to Canada's Housing Plan. "Building one strong Canadian economy" language and tariff-related risk language appear for the first time. The Indigenous Economic Inclusion Framework, launched in the prior fiscal year, enters its stated "second phase." The four-role (investor/pathfinder/convenor/advisor) framing used in dp-2023 through dp-2025 is absent from this plan's text.

2026-27 (dp-2027, 9ce428cf9c0b): A new three-part "key priorities" framing replaces the "quality jobs/clusters/global businesses/inclusive growth" set used since dp-2024: Invest in British Columbia's Strengths, Supply Canada, Grow Exports — identical to the framing in the companion sp-2026 strategic plan. A Comprehensive Expenditure Review (CER) is disclosed, with planned reductions of $9,812,491 (2026-27), $13,132,534 (2027-28), and $19,831,934 (2028-29), and an anticipated cut of ~14 indeterminate FTEs by 2028-29; the Tourism Growth Program, Regional Quantum Initiative, and Regional Homebuilding Innovation Initiative are explicitly not being renewed past their current end dates, and REGI's temporary funding is set to end in 2028-29 with REGI "reprioritized to focus on its core mandate." Two new temporary programs launch in response to trade conditions: the Regional Tariff Response Initiative (RTRI) and the Regional Defence Investment Initiative (RDII, tied to Canada's Defence Industrial Strategy). Total planned 2026-27 spending is confirmed at $180,582,915 with 181 planned FTEs, matching the backgrounder's figure. Robertson and Sloan continue as minister and institutional head. Corporate information now explicitly names the enabling Order in Council, P.C. 2021-0828, alongside the Western Economic Diversification Act.

2026-2028 (sp-2026, 63ce6b49a0b1), "Pacific Prosperity": A short (6K-character), companion strategic-plan document restating the same three dp-2027 priorities — Invest in B.C.'s Strengths, Supply Canada, Grow Exports — with sector-level detail: oceans/agriculture, digital/AI/quantum, advanced materials/manufacturing, clean technologies, critical minerals/energy/trade/logistics, life sciences/biomanufacturing, and shipbuilding/aerospace are named as B.C. strengths PacifiCan will invest around. The founding-era "enduring prosperity" vision language persists but is subtly widened from "for all British Columbians" (dp-2024, dp-2025) to "for British Columbians and Canadians," consistent with the "Supply Canada"/"one strong Canadian economy" reframing introduced in dp-2027. Investment-attraction targets cite major 2026 events (Web Summit, Men's World Cup 2026) and set 2026-27 delivery targets: ~$300M in leveraged project investment, 5,000 jobs created/maintained, 19,000 firms supported, 15%+ revenue growth, and $500M+ in export-sales growth among supported firms.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

PacifiCan's statutory mandate is explicitly regional and Pacific-facing — "the dedicated federal economic development agency for British Columbians" — and every program, office, and target in this six-document series is scoped to British Columbia; this is a geographically distinct federal region from Ontario/Toronto. The only Ontario touchpoint anywhere in the series is administrative: PacifiCan maintains a liaison office in Ottawa, Ontario, named consistently in the "Corporate information" sections of every Departmental Plan from dp-2023 through dp-2027 (bdbf95638f54 through 9ce428cf9c0b) — a federal-government-relations presence, not a program-delivery one, and never described as operating in Toronto specifically. The series does reference genuinely national program design shared across federal regional development agencies — REGI ("a national program delivered by regional development agencies across Canada," dp-2025, 6f62dd342ce8), the National Quantum Strategy, and the Budget 2024 AI-initiative envelope split across all RDAs (dp-2026, 292d47c87037) — but these are federal policy vehicles administered regionally; PacifiCan's own delivery of them is confined to B.C., and the series contains no reference to Ontario's or Toronto's regional development agencies (which carry separate org_ids in this registry). Ontario/Toronto relevance is genuinely none at the level of PacifiCan's own operations — only indirect, through shared national program architecture administered separately by Ontario-focused federal counterpart agencies.
