Council of the College of Kinesiologists of Ontario - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-council-college-kinesiologists-ontario (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The College of Kinesiologists of Ontario (COKO/CKO) regulates the profession of kinesiology under the Kinesiology Act, 2007, S.O. 2007, c. 10, Sched. O, and the Regulated Health Professions Act, 1991, S.O. 1991, c. 18 (RHPA) (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/07k10; https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/272). Its stated mandate is "to protect the public through governance that ensures excellent professional practice of Kinesiologists in Ontario," and its major function is "to administer the Kinesiology Act, 2007 in the public interest" (https://coko.ca/about/what-we-do/; COKO Annual Report 2024-25, Note 1, https://coko.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/COKO-Annual-Report-2024-25-AR_final.pdf). Kinesiology became a regulated health profession in Ontario on April 1, 2013, when COKO was established as the profession's self-governing regulatory college (https://uwaterloo.ca/kinesiology-health-sciences/news/kinesiology-becomes-regulated-health-profession).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

COKO controls entry to the profession, sets standards of practice and quality-assurance requirements, protects the "kinesiologist" title, and investigates and adjudicates complaints and professional-conduct concerns against Registered Kinesiologists (R.Kins) (https://uwaterloo.ca/kinesiology-health-sciences/news/kinesiology-becomes-regulated-health-profession; https://coko.ca/coko-resources/practice-advice-scope-of-practice/). Under the RHPA's controlled-acts framework (s.27(2)), Council has set rules on when a kinesiologist may accept delegation of a controlled act from another regulated health professional (https://coko.ca/coko-resources/practice-advice-scope-of-practice/). For the registration year September 1, 2024-August 31, 2025, COKO regulated 3,000 total registrants (2,546 active, 454 inactive), with 246 new registrants added that year (COKO Annual Report 2024-25, p.10, https://coko.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/COKO-Annual-Report-2024-25-AR_final.pdf).

Governance & reporting line

COKO is governed by a Council under Kinesiology Act, 2007, s.4: 7-9 members elected by College registrants and 6-8 public members appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council (who may not be members of the College or of any other RHPA college/council); appointees are compensated per Schedule A, Level 1 of the Agencies and Appointments Directive, and Council elects a President and Vice-President annually (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/272; https://coko.ca/about/what-we-do/). As a health-professions regulatory college operating under the RHPA framework, COKO is independent in its day-to-day regulatory decisions but accountable to the Ontario Minister of Health for its legislated mandate, and is listed on the province's central Public Appointments Secretariat agencies roster (registry row on-council-college-kinesiologists-ontario, source authority https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agencies-list; https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/272).

Budget scale

For the fiscal year ended August 31, 2025, COKO reported total revenue of $2,400,854 (registration fees $1,927,270; examination fees $242,705; application fees $82,880; jurisprudence fees $39,812; interest income $100,362; other $7,825) against total expenses of $2,111,705 (largest lines: salaries and benefits $1,054,866; examination $185,868; professional fees $161,809; communications and media $157,899; rent $146,726) plus $2,565 in office-relocation costs, producing an excess of revenue over expenses of $286,584 for the year; net assets stood at $1,853,585 at year-end, up from $1,567,001 (COKO Annual Report 2024-25, Statement of Operations and Statement of Changes in Net Assets, p.25-26, https://coko.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/COKO-Annual-Report-2024-25-AR_final.pdf).

Institutional history

COKO was incorporated without share capital as an Ontario not-for-profit organization on June 4, 2007, with its statutory authority set out in the RHPA (COKO Annual Report 2024-25, Note 1, https://coko.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/COKO-Annual-Report-2024-25-AR_final.pdf). A Transitional Council operated in the College's earliest years, evidenced by a registered "Transitional Council COKO Annual Report 2010-2011" (this library's government-document registry row on-council-college-kinesiologists-ontario-ar-2011). Kinesiology became a fully regulated health profession under the RHPA on April 1, 2013, when Ontario became the first jurisdiction in the world to recognize kinesiologists as a self-governing health-care profession, with COKO assuming its role as licensing and standards body (https://uwaterloo.ca/kinesiology-health-sciences/news/kinesiology-becomes-regulated-health-profession). COKO's current strategic plan covers 2025-2030, succeeding a 2019-2022 plan and a 2015-2018 plan (https://coko.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Strategic-Plan-2025-2030.pdf; this library's government-document registry rows on-council-college-kinesiologists-ontario-sp-2022, -sp-2018).
