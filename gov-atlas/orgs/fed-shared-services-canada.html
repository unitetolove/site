Shared Services Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: fed-shared-services-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Shared Services Canada (SSC) is a federal department/agency operating under the Shared Services Canada Act, whose preamble establishes the objective "to standardize and consolidate, within a single shared services entity, certain administrative services that support government institutions; and doing so will enable those services to be provided more effectively and will support the efficient use of public money" (Shared Services Canada Act preamble, per search-verified summary this review; see also en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_Services_Canada). SSC's own site describes it as "the GC's centralized digital service provider, with a mandate to consolidate, modernize, and secure the federal IT infrastructure" (Shared Services Canada 2024-25 Departmental Results Report, https://www.canada.ca/en/shared-services/corporate/about-us/transparency/departmental-results-reports/2024-25/departmental-results-report-2024-25.html, fetched this review).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

SSC provides "secure and reliable digital, network and hosting services" enabling public servants across the Government of Canada to collaborate, and delivers enterprise IT operations - email, data centre, and network/telecommunications services - to partner federal departments and agencies (canada.ca/en/shared-services/corporate/about-us.html; DRR 2024-25). Registry notes confirm this Tier-2 agency classification (registry row fed-shared-services-canada).

Governance & reporting line

SSC reports to Parliament through the Minister of Government Transformation, Public Works and Procurement, the minister responsible for Shared Services Canada per its 2024-25 Departmental Results Report (https://www.canada.ca/en/shared-services/corporate/about-us/transparency/departmental-results-reports/2024-25/departmental-results-report-2024-25.html). ⚠️ still being checked exact current portfolio title - a separate source (Wikipedia) renders it "Government Transformation, Public Services and Procurement," a minor wording discrepancy not resolved this review.

Budget scale

SSC's Total Actual Spending for fiscal 2024-25 was $2,617,013,300, comprising $2,275,814,878 for its core responsibility (Common Government of Canada IT Operations) and $341,198,422 for internal services, against planned spending of $2,480,039,758 (Shared Services Canada 2024-25 Departmental Results Report, https://www.canada.ca/en/shared-services/corporate/about-us/transparency/departmental-results-reports/2024-25/departmental-results-report-2024-25.html). Staffing totalled 9,346 full-time equivalents in 2024-25 (7,516 core responsibility, 1,830 internal services) (same source).

Institutional history

SSC's mandate became effective August 4, 2011, consolidating IT infrastructure services previously run separately across federal departments; personnel, financial resources and assets were transferred to SSC from dozens of the largest federal departments via orders-in-council (search-verified summary this review, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_Services_Canada; exact department count and OIC citations ⚠️ still being checked - not independently confirmed against a single primary SSC source this review). SSC's own Departmental Plan series (registry doc shelf, registry id fed-shared-services-canada) has been published continuously since its first Report on Plans and Priorities 2012-13, consistent with a 2011 founding.

Shared Services Canada - strategy evolution

TL;DR: The archived record is genuinely thin — 5 usable documents (plus one confirmed contentless stub) out of 21 registered, split across two isolated clusters four years apart: a single 2019-20 Departmental Results Report, then a 2023-24/2024-25/2025-26/2026-27 cluster with nothing in between. Within that gap, the biggest visible addition is a shift from pandemic-era "SSC 3.0" consolidation language (2019-20) to an AI-era "One SSC" framing built around sovereign/Canadian-built AI tools (CANChat, an "AI Marketplace") and defence-spending-driven infrastructure demand (2026-27) — a genuine mandate-framing evolution, even though the four-year silence means it cannot be dated more precisely than "sometime between 2019-20 and 2023-24." Nothing reads as dropped; SSC's core IT-consolidation mandate (email, data centre, network/telecom) is stated identically at both ends of the gap. The most load-bearing number is staffing growth from 7,057 total FTE (2019-20, drr-2020) to 9,346 total FTE (2024-25, drr-2025) — a ~32% headcount increase partly recoverable year-by-year only because drr-2024's own three-year retrospective table happens to cover the otherwise-undocumented 2021-22 and 2022-23 fiscal years (7,955 and 8,650 total FTE respectively). One open question this five-document window cannot resolve: what happened at SSC between April 2020 and March 2023 — a full three fiscal years of departmental plans and results reports that would show the actual pandemic-to-recovery IT-consolidation program (data centre/network transformation targets, any delays or cost overruns) are entirely absent from this archived set, and the leadership transition from President Paul Glover (2019-20) to President Scott Jones (confirmed from 2023-24 onward) falls somewhere inside that unread gap.

Priority evolution

2019-20 (drr-2020, 749583b93893): SSC's mandate is stated as delivering "email, data centre and telecommunications services to federal departments and agencies," plus cyber/IT security and workplace technology device purchasing, with additional cost-recovery optional services. The year is framed around "SSC 3.0," in effect "since July 2019" — the belief that "the GC needs to make decisions differently for IT investments, projects, and prioritization," aiming to "consolidate, modernize and simplify the GC's approach to digital services." The report explicitly frames SSC's COVID-19 pandemic response as validating this: "a coordinated, government-wide corporate approach to IT is fundamental to sustaining GC operations in times of crisis." Named project work includes IT support for the Canadian Space Agency's RADARSAT Constellation Mission (three-satellite launch, a new Long-Term Archive System data-storage build in an SSC Enterprise Data Centre). Total actual spending $1,973,596,487; total actual FTE 7,057. President: Paul Glover.

[unread gap: FY2020-21 through FY2022-23 — no departmental plan or results report survives in this archived set for these three fiscal years.]

2023-24 (drr-2024, 6fe09368d433): President is now Scott Jones — the transition from Glover happened somewhere inside the unread gap. Total actual spending $2,790.8 million, an underspend against total authorities of $206.4 million (7%), including $69.2 million in approved reprofiles ("due to delays in planning and procurement that impacted project timelines") and SSC's own "refocusing of the government spending commitment." Core-responsibility ("Common Government of Canada IT Operations") actual spending $2,444,005,446 against $2,328,056,120 planned, at 7,434 actual FTE against 6,824 planned — the only planned-vs-actual FTE overrun (rather than shortfall) surfaced anywhere in this brief's five-document set.

2024-25 (drr-2025, 71db9bdb68c7): Total actual spending $2,617,013,300 ($2,275,814,878 core / $341,198,422 internal services); total FTE 9,346 (7,516 core / 1,830 internal) — both figures matching the backgrounder exactly.

2025-26 (dp-2026, 1945eca5a9be): Framed around "key priorities" tied to the Minister of Digital Government's vision for federal institutions to be "transparent and service-oriented" and deliver services "securely and in simple, modern and effective ways." A named Cyber security strategic roadmap targets the GC's "transition to zero trust," introducing an Administrative Access Control Service, a Privileged Access Management component, and a Network Device Authentication service (new for 2025-26). President: Scott Jones.

2026-27 (dp-2027, 62df6cd6caf9): The "One SSC" strategy is named explicitly for the first time in this brief's set: "aligning its funding to its departmental priorities, investing in transformational activities, and leveraging technology to achieve both financial and non-financial efficiencies," including workforce "re-skilling and re-aligning." Sovereign/Canadian-built AI tools appear for the first time — "CANChat and the AI Marketplace" — framed around "digital sovereignty" and support for the Treasury Board Secretariat-led GC AI strategy and the "newly established Privy Council Office AI Secretariat." A new driver is named explicitly: "evolving priorities, including large-scale initiatives such as increased defence spending." A Protected B secure-messaging expansion to "approximately 50,000 government-issued devices" is planned to begin in 2026-27. President: Scott Jones.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — sovereign/Canadian-built AI infrastructure (CANChat, AI Marketplace): absent from the 2019-20 and 2023-24/2024-25 documents read for this brief; first named in dp-2027 (2026-27, 62df6cd6caf9), tied explicitly to "digital sovereignty" and alignment with TBS's GC AI strategy and the PCO AI Secretariat.
  • Added — defence-spending-driven infrastructure demand: named for the first time as an explicit planning driver in dp-2027 (62df6cd6caf9) — "large-scale initiatives such as increased defence spending" — with no comparable framing in any earlier document read.
  • Renamed/reframed — "SSC 3.0" (2019-20, pandemic-consolidation framing) → "One SSC" (2026-27, AI-era efficiency/re-skilling framing): both describe the same underlying consolidate-and-modernize mandate under a different named strategy banner; because the intervening 2020-21 through 2022-23 documents are unread, this brief cannot say whether "SSC 3.0" was itself renamed/retired mid-gap or simply superseded by "One SSC" at some later, undated point.
  • Nothing else identifiably dropped. SSC's core mandate — email, data centre, network/telecommunications services delivered to federal departments — is stated in essentially the same terms in both the 2019-20 and 2026-27 documents.
