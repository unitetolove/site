People & Equity (City Manager Services) - backgrounder

Backgrounder / this library's government-strategy research Batch 12 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: tor-people-equity (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

People & Equity is not a separate corporation; it is a division of the City of Toronto, itself continued as a corporation with broad interpretive powers under the City of Toronto Act, 2006, S.O. 2006, c. 11, Sched. A, ss.6(1) and 7 ("the City has the capacity, rights, powers and privileges of a natural person") (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06c11, not re-fetched this review, cited per house convention established in prior-batch backgrounders for City of Toronto divisions). No division-specific enabling statute is named in the budget book read for this org.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Per the City's own program summary, People & Equity (P&E) delivers "corporate leadership and strategic Human Resources (HR) advisory services and customized solutions to complex workforce priorities and challenges; manage[s] the employee relations environment; support[s] divisions in embedding reconciliation and equity into all corporate workforce policies and programs; provide[s] a work environment that is equitable, legislatively compliant and free of discrimination and harassment," alongside recruitment, health & safety compliance, and learning and leadership development (2026 Program Summary — City Manager Services, sha256-12 75474e23efd4, p.3). It sits alongside the City Manager's Office, Communications, and the FIFA World Cup 2026 Secretariat as one of the divisions comprising City Manager Services (same source, p.1). This org's registry entry (1 row, 2026 only) has no separately-registered 2025 counterpart, but the same document names a 2025 "People and Equity" line item within the prior year's City Manager's Office budget book (tor-communications's 2025 doc, sha256-12 3d0b211d999c) — see Residuals in the brief for how this is handled.

Governance & reporting line

The division reports through City Manager Services to the City Manager and, ultimately, City Council (2026 Program Summary, sha256-12 75474e23efd4, p.1-3). A Human Rights Office (HRO) operates within P&E, handling complaint resolution, discrimination/harassment investigations, and mandatory human-rights training (same source, p.14, 19). ⚠️ still being checked — the same document's Table 1 footnote states figures are "Adjusted for transfer of IAO and Equity group to Social Development" (p.13); it is not fully clear from the text read whether "Equity group" refers to a sub-function distinct from the "People and Equity" division itself, or how it relates to the division's continued existence and budget line — flagged as a residual rather than resolved.

Budget scale

$59.868M gross / $54.010M net for 2026, essentially flat against $60.949M gross / $53.164M net in 2025 (gross down modestly, net up modestly) (2026 Program Summary, sha256-12 75474e23efd4, p.9-13; 2025 figures from tor-communications's 2025 doc, sha256-12 3d0b211d999c, p.9). No division-specific position count is broken out; City Manager Services as a whole reports 556.0 approved positions for 2026.

Institutional history

The division appears as "People and Equity" in both the 2025 (within "City Manager's Office") and 2026 (within "City Manager Services") program summaries with a stable name, unlike the Communications division in the same cluster which was renamed over the same period (see tor-communications backgrounder). Backgrounder summary

People & Equity is a division of the City of Toronto (no division-specific enabling statute; the City itself operates under the City of Toronto Act, 2006, ss.6(1), 7). It delivers corporate HR advisory services, employee relations, equity/reconciliation integration into workforce policy, health & safety compliance, recruitment/talent acquisition, and learning & development, including a Human Rights Office handling complaint resolution and mandatory training. It sits within the City Manager Services cluster alongside the City Manager's Office, Communications, and the FIFA World Cup 2026 Secretariat. Budget & mandate inflection points

  • $59.868M gross / $54.010M net for 2026 — the second-largest of City Manager Services' four divisions, after the FIFA World Cup 2026 Secretariat's $189.997M gross (itself a one-time event-driven spike) (75474e23efd4, p.13).
  • HRO Service Sustainment: $330K gross/net, 4.0 new permanent positions, rising to a $599K annualized 2027 impact (75474e23efd4, p.8, 19).
