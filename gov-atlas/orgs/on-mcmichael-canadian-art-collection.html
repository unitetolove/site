McMichael Canadian Art Collection - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-mcmichael-canadian-art-collection (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The McMichael Canadian Art Collection is an agency of the Government of Ontario, governed by the McMichael Canadian Art Collection Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.4, as amended (https://mcmichael.com/board-members/). Its financial statements describe it as "an agency of the Government of Ontario assisted through the Ontario Ministry of Tourism, Culture, and Sport" and as "a crown agency under the Income Tax Act (Canada)" exempt from income tax while government support continues (McMichael Fiscal 2024-25 Annual Report, Note to Financial Statements, https://mcmichael.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/McMichael_Fiscal_2024-2025_Annual_Report_EN.pdf). The gallery brands itself "Home to the Art of Canada," with a collecting mandate centred on the Group of Seven, Tom Thomson, Indigenous art, and contemporary Canadian art (https://mcmichael.com).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Collection maintains and grows a permanent art collection, operates a public gallery with rotating exhibitions and education/public programs, and manages specialized holdings including Indigenous art and archival/library materials (https://mcmichael.com; https://mcmichael.com/library-and-archives/). Provincial oversight is exercised through a Memorandum of Understanding and an annual Letter of Direction issued by the Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming, published in both official languages for the 2025-26 fiscal year (https://mcmichael.com/governance-and-reports/).

Governance & reporting line

The Collection's affairs are under the control of a Board of Trustees "appointed according to the [McMichael Canadian Art Collection] Act"; trustees are appointed by the Premier (https://mcmichael.com/board-members/). As of this review the Board comprised 14 voting trustees plus the Executive Director & Chief Curator as an ex officio member and two directors-in-training, led by a Board Chair (current membership at https://mcmichael.com/board-members/), operating through five standing committees: Finance and Audit, Governance and Nominating, Human Resources, Arts Advisory, and Property and Buildings (https://mcmichael.com/board-members/). The Collection reports to the Ontario Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming via its Memorandum of Understanding and annual Letter of Direction (https://mcmichael.com/governance-and-reports/), consistent with its registry classification as a provincial agency (registry row on-mcmichael-canadian-art-collection, source authority https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agencies-list).

Budget scale

For the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, the Collection reported total revenues of $14,012,824, including a Province of Ontario operating grant of $3,328,800 (unchanged from FY2024) plus $734,014 in provincial designated-program funding and $44,985 in federal designated-program funding, for total government revenue of $4,107,799; internally generated revenue totalled $8,558,239, led by a $2,708,825 transfer from the McMichael Canadian Art Foundation, $1,338,888 in special-events revenue, $1,297,822 in collections/exhibitions revenue, $914,949 in retail operations, $785,658 in admissions, and $659,698 in programs and education. Total expenses were $13,019,295 (largest lines: salaries, wages and benefits $5,502,928; curatorial and exhibitions $1,616,944; amortization of capital assets $1,346,786), producing an excess of revenue over expenses of $993,529 for the year, versus a $543,857 deficiency the prior year; unrestricted net assets rose to $1,050,243 from $56,714 (McMichael Fiscal 2024-25 Annual Report, Statement of Operations and Changes in Net Assets, https://mcmichael.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/McMichael_Fiscal_2024-2025_Annual_Report_EN.pdf).

Institutional history

The Collection traces its founding to 1952, when its namesake founders purchased an initial ten acres of land in Kleinburg, Ontario, and began assembling a private art collection that grew to 194 works by 1965 (https://mcmichael.com/mcmichael-history/). In 1965 the founders donated their collection, home and land to the Province of Ontario, and the gallery opened to the public on July 8, 1966 under the name "McMichael Conservation Collection of Art" (same source). Provincial legislation in 1972 incorporated the gallery as a non-profit organization; it now operates under the McMichael Canadian Art Collection Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.4 (McMichael Fiscal 2024-25 Annual Report, Note to Financial Statements, https://mcmichael.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/McMichael_Fiscal_2024-2025_Annual_Report_EN.pdf; https://mcmichael.com/board-members/). Its most recent business plan covers fiscal year 2026 (this library's government-document registry row on-mcmichael-canadian-art-collection-bp-2026, https://mcmichael.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/MCAC-Business-Plan-FY26_ENG.pdf).
