Canadian Statistics Advisory Council - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canadian-statistics-advisory-council (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Canadian Statistics Advisory Council (CSAC) is established under section 8.1 of the consolidated Statistics Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. S-19 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/s-19/page-1.html, fetched and verified, consolidated version current to 2026-06-14). Section 8.1 charges the Council to "advise the Minister and Chief Statistician in a transparent manner on any question that either of them has referred to the Council on the overall quality of the national statistical system" and to "make public an annual report on the state of the national statistical system" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CSAC's statutory role is advisory rather than operational: it reviews questions referred to it by the Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry or the Chief Statistician touching the quality of Canada's national statistical system, and it independently publishes a public annual report assessing that system's state (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/s-19/page-1.html; registry row fed-canadian-statistics-advisory-council). It has no regulatory, enforcement, or data-collection powers of its own.

Governance & reporting line

The Council is capped at "not more than 10 other members appointed by the Governor in Council to hold office during pleasure, including one Chairperson," with the Chief Statistician sitting as an ex officio member; the Chairperson serves a term of up to five years (renewable once for up to three more), other members serve three-year terms (renewable once for up to three more) (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/s-19/page-1.html, fetched). Members advise, and report their annual assessment to, both the Minister and the Chief Statistician (same source).
