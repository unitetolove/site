Canada Lands Company Limited - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canada-lands-company-limited (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

CLCL is a federal Crown corporation listed in Schedule III, Part I of the Financial Administration Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. F-11, confirmed on the consolidated Justice Laws text (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/F-11/page-22.html; registry row fed-canada-lands-company-limited). It was originally incorporated under the Companies Act on March 7, 1956, and continued under Articles of Continuance dated September 19, 1977 under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CLCL Corporate Plan Summary 2025/26-2029/30, https://www.clc-sic.ca/sites/default/files/documents/CLCL%20Corporate%20Plan%20Summary%202025-26%20Final%20EN_FINAL-s.pdf, document id fed-canada-lands-company-limited-bp-2026). Its current mandate — "the commercially oriented, orderly disposition of surplus real properties with best value to the Canadian taxpayer and the holding of certain properties" — was approved by the Government of Canada upon CLCL's 1995 reactivation and re-confirmed by a 2001 Treasury Board review (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

CLCL operates as a self-financing holding company for a group of subsidiaries — Canada Lands Company CLC Limited (CLC), Parc Downsview Park Inc. (PDP), and Old Port of Montreal Corporation Inc. (OPMC) — that carry out real estate development, community redevelopment, and attractions operations, including management of the CN Tower (CLC-SIC "About Us," https://www.clc-sic.ca/about-us; CLCL Corporate Plan Summary 2025/26-2029/30, document id fed-canada-lands-company-limited-bp-2026). Budget 2024 expanded CLCL's role in disposing of federal surplus properties for housing (CLCL Corporate Plan Summary 2025/26-2029/30, document id fed-canada-lands-company-limited-bp-2026).

Governance & reporting line

CLCL reports to Parliament through the Minister of Public Services and Procurement (CLC-SIC "About Us," https://www.clc-sic.ca/about-us). It is governed by a Governor-in-Council-appointed Chair and Board of Directors, with a single employee — the President and CEO — who also serves as President/CEO and a Board member of CLC, PDP and OPMC; the President and CEO's compensation is set by the Governor in Council (CLCL Corporate Plan Summary 2025/26-2029/30, document id fed-canada-lands-company-limited-bp-2026).

Budget scale

The Company forecasts $371.7 million in revenue for FY2024/25 and projects a cumulative $2.4 billion in revenue and $702.4 million in net income before tax over the FY2025/26-2029/30 Plan Period (CLCL Corporate Plan Summary 2025/26-2029/30, document id fed-canada-lands-company-limited-bp-2026).

Institutional history

CLCL was incorporated in 1956 under the Companies Act, continued in 1977 under the Canada Business Corporations Act, and reactivated in 1995 with its current surplus-property-disposition mandate (CLCL Corporate Plan Summary 2025/26-2029/30, document id fed-canada-lands-company-limited-bp-2026).

Priority evolution

Because 27 of the 33 registered documents yielded no report text (see Series inventory and Residuals & gaps), a genuine chronological trace of CLCL's stated priorities is only possible across three real snapshots — Corporate Plan Summaries covering 2019/20-2023/24, 2021/22-2025/26, and 2025/26-2029/30 — plus the boilerplate corporate-history paragraphs (1956 incorporation, 1977 CBCA continuance, 1995 reactivation, 2001 Treasury Board review) that recur verbatim inside those same plans. No annual report anywhere in the 1999-2025 span, and no pre-2018 business plan, could be read. ⚠️ Still being checked: CLCL's stated priorities and mandate emphasis for 1999-2018 are not established by this archived series at all.

2019/20-2023/24 Corporate Plan Summary (bp-2019/bp-2020, 5dd2d0cf7780): CLCL frames itself as an "agent federal Crown corporation" whose mandate is "the commercially oriented, orderly disposition of surplus strategic real properties, optimizing financial and community value, and the holding of certain properties," executed by buying surplus federal property at fair market value and improving/managing/selling it (5dd2d0cf7780). The Company projects $1,923 million in cumulative five-year revenue and $1,383 million in cumulative expenses (5dd2d0cf7780). Four "overarching objectives" are named: government-policy alignment, quality-of-life/economic-development delivery, tourism-attraction stewardship, and — explicitly — continuing partnerships with Indigenous peoples and contributing "to the Government's priorities of community development, including affordable housing" (5dd2d0cf7780). Named short/medium-term strategic issues include the Old Port of Montreal master-plan public consultations, First Nations joint ventures in Vancouver/Ottawa/Halifax, cyber-security investment, and a review of the City of Toronto's Downsview Area Secondary Plan (DASP) triggered by the sale of adjacent Bombardier-owned lands to the Public Sector Pension Investment Board (5dd2d0cf7780). The Governor-in-Council appoints CLCL's Chair/Directors/CEO "upon the recommendation of the Minister of PSPC" (5dd2d0cf7780).

2021/22-2025/26 Corporate Plan Summary (bp-2021/bp-2022, d32c1b85224b): The plan is dominated by the COVID-19 pandemic, declared by the WHO on March 11, 2020; CLCL suspended Attractions operations twice (March 2020, October 2020) and projects an annual operating loss in 2021/22 for the first time in the Company's history, to be funded from existing cash rather than new financing (d32c1b85224b). A new "2.2 Public Policy Role" section is introduced (absent by that name from the 2019/20-2023/24 plan). Objectives narrow to seven numbered items, adding an explicit COVID-19 compliance/recovery-support objective alongside continued property reintegration, Indigenous partnerships, PSPC collaboration projects "in the National Capital Region, Toronto and other opportunities," and attractions-value enhancement (d32c1b85224b). Financial projections drop relative to pre-pandemic framing: $1.7 billion cumulative revenue, ~$500 million income before tax, ~$550 million total contribution to the shareholder including $200 million in dividends, and ~$600 million in capital spending over the five-year period (d32c1b85224b). CLCL reports 12 First Nations agreements across 7 properties, a 10% affordable-housing target for the plan period, and reaffirms the PSPC recommendation channel for Governor-in-Council appointments (d32c1b85224b).

2025/26-2029/30 Corporate Plan Summary (bp-2026, 0ea3f44c3377): Post-COVID, this plan is organized for the first time around a named, three-pillar "Strategic Plan" — Transform Surplus and Underutilized Federal Properties; Create Unique Canadian Experiences; Be a Workplace of Choice — which the text says was "operationalized" via an implementation plan developed by "teams" in 2023 (0ea3f44c3377). Budget 2024 (April 16, 2024) is described as setting "a new course for the Company with an expanded role in the delivery of housing," including deepening partnerships with other Crown corporations such as Canada Post to acquire and redevelop their surplus/underutilized lands, working with custodians (naming the Department of National Defence) to enable housing on active-use sites rather than only decommissioned ones, and accelerating housing/affordable-housing delivery (0ea3f44c3377). CLCL reports having enabled over 11,000 housing units (of which 1,650+ affordable, since 2016) as of October 2024, and projects 45,000 units by 2031 through its own pipeline plus up to 4,000 more through a new Build Canada Homes (BCH) partnership announced in September 2025 (0ea3f44c3377). The affordable-housing target is raised to "a minimum of 20% affordable units" where no municipal requirement exists, up from the 10% target in the prior plan (0ea3f44c3377 vs. d32c1b85224b). A new "Corporate/Shared Services" division is added alongside Real Estate and Attractions, and the Governor-in-Council appointment channel has changed to "the recommendation of the Minister of Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada" — a different minister than the PSPC channel named in both prior plans (0ea3f44c3377 vs. 5dd2d0cf7780, d32c1b85224b). The federal government's September 5, 2025 Buy Canadian Policy is noted as applying to Crown corporations including CLCL, "pending further policy direction" (0ea3f44c3377). Financial projections rise to $2.4 billion cumulative revenue, $700+ million net income before tax, ~$1.0 billion total shareholder contribution including $300 million in dividends, and $940+ million in capital spending (0ea3f44c3377) — matching the backgrounder's figures for this period.

  • Quietly dropped — explicit COVID-19 risk framing: the pandemic dominates d32c1b85224b's entire "Operating Environment" section but is absent from bp-2026's risk list (0ea3f44c3377), which instead foregrounds cybersecurity, geopolitical uncertainty, and climate/aging-infrastructure risk.
  • Renamed/restructured — divisional and regional structure: "BC/Ontario, West, NCR/Atlantic, Québec" real-estate regions (5dd2d0cf7780; d32c1b85224b) become "West, Central, NCR, Québec/Atlantic" in bp-2026 (0ea3f44c3377), and a new "Corporate/Shared Services" division is added alongside Real Estate and Attractions (previously just two divisions, d32c1b85224b).

  • Unchanged throughout: the core 1995 mandate language — "commercially oriented, orderly disposition of surplus real properties with best value to the Canadian taxpayer and the holding of certain properties" — is quoted verbatim in both the earliest and latest readable plans (5dd2d0cf7780; 0ea3f44c3377); the shift over time is in emphasis and delivery tools around that unchanged mandate, not in the mandate text itself.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

CLCL's head office, the CN Tower, and Downsview Park are all located in Toronto across every readable plan in the series (5dd2d0cf7780; d32c1b85224b; 0ea3f44c3377). The Downsview Lands are described in the earliest readable plan as 572 acres (231 hectares) of parkland and development lands, subject to the City of Toronto's Downsview Area Secondary Plan (DASP), with an amendment process triggered by the sale of adjacent Bombardier-owned lands to the Public Sector Pension Investment Board and expected — as of the 2021/22-2025/26 plan — to take "up to five years" (5dd2d0cf7780; d32c1b85224b). Toronto-area real estate sales are quantified across all three plans: approximately $210 million in Toronto-area sales projected over the 2019/20-2023/24 period, with more than half concentrated in the plan's final year (5dd2d0cf7780); and approximately $200 million in Downsview Lands sales specifically projected for the 2025/26-2029/30 period (0ea3f44c3377).
