Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-innovation-science-economic-development-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

ISED operates under the Department of Industry Act, S.C. 1995, c. 1 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/i-9.2/page-1.html, fetched and verified, consolidated version current to 2026-06-14). Section 2: "There is hereby established a department of the Government of Canada called the Department of Industry over which the Minister of Industry, appointed by commission under the Great Seal, shall preside" (same source; the department operates under the applied title Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada). Section 4 gives the Minister's powers over "industry and technology... trade and commerce... science... consumer affairs... corporations and corporate securities; competition and restraint of trade... bankruptcy and insolvency; patents, copyrights, trademarks... telecommunications... investment, small businesses, and tourism," plus regional economic development in Ontario (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

ISED leads the Innovation, Science and Economic Development portfolio across three core responsibilities: Companies, Investment and Growth (business competitiveness, marketplace fairness); Science, Technology, Research and Commercialization (research and tech commercialization); and People, Skills and Communities (broadband access, inclusive entrepreneurship) (https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/planning-performance-reporting/en/departmental-plans/2026-27-departmental-plan, fetched via r.jina.ai; registry row fed-innovation-science-economic-development-canada). It coordinates a portfolio of federal economic-development agencies and regulators.

Governance & reporting line

Led by the Minister of Industry (currently the Honourable Mélanie Joly), with a Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation, a Secretary of State for Small Business and Tourism, and a Secretary of State for Rural Development also assigned to the portfolio; Deputy Minister Philip Jennings heads the department, supported by a Deputy Minister of Economic Development and two Associate Deputy Ministers (https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en, fetched via r.jina.ai). Under the Act the Minister "shall preside" over the Department (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/i-9.2/page-1.html).

Budget scale

~$8.84B total planned spending for 2026-27 ($8,843,046,848; Companies, Investment and Growth $6.65B, Science/Technology/Research/Commercialization $1.14B, People, Skills and Communities $0.85B), 5,908 planned FTEs (ISED 2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/planning-performance-reporting/en/departmental-plans/2026-27-departmental-plan, fetched via r.jina.ai).

Institutional history

Established as the Department of Industry under the Department of Industry Act, S.C. 1995, c. 1 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/i-9.2/page-1.html, fetched); ⚠️ still being checked - the 2015 rebranding/renaming to "Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada" (applied title vs. statutory name) was not independently confirmed against a primary source this review.

Backgrounder summary

ISED operates under the Department of Industry Act, S.C. 1995, c.1, which establishes the Department of Industry (applied title "Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada") and gives the Minister of Industry authority over industry and technology, trade and commerce, science, consumer affairs, corporations, competition, bankruptcy and insolvency, IP, telecommunications, investment, small business and tourism, plus regional economic development in Ontario. As of the 2026-27 Departmental Plan the department is organized around three core responsibilities — Companies, Investment and Growth ($6.65B), Science, Technology, Research and Commercialization ($1.14B), and People, Skills and Communities ($0.85B) — for ~$8.84B total planned spending and 5,908 planned FTEs. Current governance spans a Minister of Industry, a Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation, a Secretary of State for Small Business and Tourism, and a Secretary of State for Rural Development, with Deputy Minister Philip Jennings. The backgrounder flags ⚠️ still being checked on the exact date/mechanism of the "Industry Canada" → "Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada" applied-title rebranding; this archived series does not resolve that question either (see Residuals & gaps).

Priority evolution

2001-2005 (Industry Canada era) — unreadable. All five registered documents from this period (b77befb5e36a, fa3dac2cb024, 654fc1ab1296, e2e675acc4dd, 374126cbf059, 359fced09c85) returned only Library and Archives Canada interstitial notices. No substantive priority language from the Industry Canada era is available in this archived series. 2006-2017 is a registry gap — no documents are registered at all, so the transition from "Industry Canada" to "Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada" cannot be traced from primary sources in this set.

2017-18 (dp-2018 df576da6d2bc; drr-2018 e6ced083e5b2): Earliest readable content. The department reports under the legacy Program Alignment Architecture (PAA): three Strategic Outcomes ("The Canadian marketplace is efficient and competitive"; "Advancements in science and technology, knowledge, and innovation strengthen the Canadian economy"; "Canadian businesses and communities are competitive") delivered through eight Programs. The DP frames the year around implementing "Canada's Innovation Agenda," built on "three priority areas—people, technologies and companies" (df576da6d2bc). By the DRR, published later in the cycle, the umbrella term has shifted to "Canada's Innovation and Skills Plan," organized around four pillars (skills; this library's internal records; program simplification; investment/scale-up) (e6ced083e5b2) — the renaming happens within a single fiscal year, DP to DRR. Three flagship delivery vehicles launch mid-year and are absent from the DP but present in the DRR: the Strategic Innovation Fund (July 2017, $1.26B/5yr, consolidating four legacy R&D programs — the Automotive Innovation Fund, Automotive Supplier Innovation Program, Strategic Aerospace and Defence Initiative, and Technology Demonstration Program), the Innovation Superclusters Initiative (launched May 24, 2017, $950M, five superclusters named Feb 15, 2018 including the Ontario-based Advanced Manufacturing Supercluster), and Innovative Solutions Canada (e6ced083e5b2). Internally, ISED implements "Organizing for Success" restructuring and creates a Portfolio and Intergovernmental Engagement Secretariat (e6ced083e5b2).

2018-19 (dp-2019 6d2ddb44edbb; drr-2019 1eca8cd0ae94): A government-wide Treasury Board "Policy on Results" drives ISED's first major reporting-framework replacement: the PAA's three Strategic Outcomes/eight Programs are formally retired and replaced by three "Core Responsibilities" — People, Skills and Communities; Science, Technology, Research and Commercialization; Companies, Investment and Growth — with a 24-program Program Inventory and an explicit PAA-to-DRF concordance table (6d2ddb44edbb). This three-part core-responsibility structure persists unchanged through every subsequent document in the series. Canada's Intellectual Property Strategy launches April 2018; Canada's Digital Charter follows mid-2018 consultations; the Regional Economic Growth through Innovation (REGI) program launches December 2018; the Women Entrepreneurship Strategy launches, with its Ecosystem Fund (Sept 2018) and Women Entrepreneurship Fund (Oct 2018) (1eca8cd0ae94). The Science, Technology and Innovation Council is replaced by a new Council on Science and Innovation (Order in Council, Feb 25, 2019). The institutional head changes from John Knubley to Simon Kennedy between the DP and DRR, and the Minister's title fluctuates within the DRR itself between "Minister of Innovation, Science and Economic Development" and "Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry" (1eca8cd0ae94) — an unresolved internal inconsistency, not a clean rename (see Residuals & gaps).

2019-20 (dp-2020 a6aaabd3d5ee; drr-2020 7bbabb1771a6): Framework stable. The Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy is named explicitly for the first time by that name (a6aaabd3d5ee). "High-Speed Access for All: Canada's Connectivity Strategy" is announced June 2019, alongside Budget 2019's $1.7B top-up to Connect to Innovate and creation of the new Universal Broadband Fund concept (7bbabb1771a6). The DRR is the first document in the series to mention COVID-19, describing a late-in-year pivot: "At the close of 2019–20, the world was hit by the COVID-19 pandemic... ISED swiftly adjusted its programming" and stood up a cross-functional "COVID-19 Tiger Team" (7bbabb1771a6). The government's Speech-from-the-Throne policy baseline is updated from the 2015 Throne Speech to the 2019 Throne Speech following the October 2019 election, and internal renewal branding shifts from "Innovation 2020" to "ISED Renewal" (7bbabb1771a6).

2020-21 (dp-2021 c0f661e8e03e; drr-2021 5f6cbb313ba8): The starkest single-year contrast in the series. The DP, dated March 2020, contains no COVID-19 language at all and reads as a routine continuation — five superclusters, an Innovation Superclusters Initiative "ending in 2022–23," a 95%-by-2026/100%-by-2030 broadband target (c0f661e8e03e). The DRR for the same fiscal year is dominated by pandemic response: the Canada Emergency Wage Subsidy, Canada Emergency Rent Subsidy, Lockdown Support and Canada Emergency Business Account; a $2B Regional Relief and Recovery Fund delivered with Regional Development Agencies; a Medical Countermeasures Initiative that channelled "nearly $1B" through SIF for vaccine/therapeutic manufacturing (partnering with Moderna and Novavax); and new equity programs — the Black Entrepreneurship Program and the 50-30 Challenge — both launched in direct pandemic-recovery context (5f6cbb313ba8). The Universal Broadband Fund formally launches Nov 9, 2020 with an accelerated $750M top-up. The Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry changes from Navdeep Bains to François-Philippe Champagne mid-year.

2021-22 (dp-2022 cb5f433d7ec2; drr-2022 418c2663afff): Second major reporting-framework overhaul, this time explicit and self-described: "ISED updated its reporting framework for 2021–22 to better align its Departmental Results with the Department's enduring, legislated mandate." A published change table shows five programs (Consumer Affairs, Insolvency, Federal Incorporation, Investment Review, Trade Measurement) consolidated into a new "Marketplace Protection and Promotion" program; the Innovation Superclusters Initiative is folded into a "Business Innovation" program; several other programs are renamed or merged (cb5f433d7ec2). By the DRR, the renaming is confirmed explicitly: "ISED's Global Innovation Clusters (GIC), formerly known as the Innovation Superclusters Initiative" (418c2663afff). The most significant machinery-of-government event of the series occurs here: "Budget 2021 announced that [FedNor] would become a standalone agency as of August 12, 2021," transferring authorities and Northern Ontario programming out of ISED (418c2663afff). The $1.4B Canada Digital Adoption Program launches; the Net Zero Accelerator (a Strategic Innovation Fund stream) scales from an announced $3B (dp-2022) to a described $8B initiative (drr-2022) within the same fiscal year.

2022-23 (dp-2023 63561aa23cfa; drr-2023 d9ec8267ac23): The National Quantum Strategy is named with a $360M/7-year commitment; the Rural Economic Development Strategy and its Centre for Rural Economic Development are transferred in from Infrastructure Canada (63561aa23cfa). FedNor's "Economic Development in Northern Ontario" program is formally removed from ISED's program inventory, completing the FedNor separation begun the prior year. The Canadian Critical Minerals Strategy is launched and named for the first time, tied explicitly to net-zero transition investments including "an agreement with Volkswagen... to build an electric vehicle battery manufacturing plant in Ontario" (d9ec8267ac23). This is also the first document in the series to name a Toronto institution: the Vector Institute in Toronto, alongside Amii (Edmonton) and Mila (Montreal), as one of three national AI institutes receiving Pan-Canadian AI Strategy funding (d9ec8267ac23). Ministerial portfolio fragmentation accelerates: Small Business gains a standalone minister (Rechie Valdez) for the first time, and Tourism and Rural Economic Development are each tied to separate regional-agency responsibilities.

2023-24 (dp-2024 a6b058997ebb; drr-2024 2e6416d6525d): Global Innovation Clusters funding is topped up by $750M through 2028; the Strategic Science Fund (launched Aug 2021) makes its first funding announcements; Competition Act reforms pass (Bill C-56, royal assent Dec 15, 2023; Bill C-59, royal assent June 20, 2024) alongside a $50M price-fixing fine against Canada Bread Company, described as "the highest price-fixing fine imposed by a Canadian court to date" (2e6416d6525d). A $1.5B Critical Minerals Infrastructure Fund launches jointly with Natural Resources Canada. PowerCo (a Volkswagen subsidiary) announces a $7B EV battery plant in St. Thomas, Ontario. On June 4, 2024 — the same day the Auditor General tabled its report on Sustainable Development Technology Canada (see below) — the government announces "a phased transition of SDTC programming to the National Research Council of Canada (NRC) prior to being eventually subsumed under the Canada Innovation Corporation (CIC)," explicitly "to strengthen governance, enhance accountability, and ensure robust conflict of interest practices" (2e6416d6525d).

2024 — Auditor General audit of Sustainable Development Technology Canada (oth-2024, 8b09876fe024): Not an ISED self-report but a direct audit of ISED-portfolio governance, tabled June 4, 2024. The OAG found "significant lapses in Sustainable Development Technology Canada's governance and stewardship of public funds" and that "Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada did not sufficiently monitor the compliance with the contribution agreements" between SDTC and government. Of 58 Start-up/Scale-up/Ecosystem projects examined, 10 (totalling $59M) were found ineligible, with an estimated 1-in-10 of the remaining projects also likely ineligible; 90 board-approval decisions worth nearly $76M were found connected to unfollowed conflict-of-interest policies; and SDTC's board had reduced its statutory member council from 15 members (required by its enabling Act) to 2 (8b09876fe024). Between March 2017 and December 2023, SDTC's board had approved $836M across 226 projects. The audit is the evident trigger, or at minimum the same-day companion event, for ISED's June 4, 2024 transition announcement reported above.

2024-25 (dp-2025 a5375017bcbe; drr-2025 d0056ac3bc0c): AI policy becomes an organizing theme. The Canadian Artificial Intelligence Safety Institute is "launched... in 2024," and ISED announces the Canadian Sovereign AI Compute Strategy with "up to $2 billion to strengthen and increase Canada's AI compute infrastructure," including a December 2024 SIF-run "Artificial Intelligence Compute Challenge... in response to increasing geopolitical threats and supply chain fluctuations" (d0056ac3bc0c). Health Emergency Readiness Canada (HERC) is established in September 2024 as a special operating agency within ISED, building on the Biomanufacturing and Life Sciences Strategy, and facilitates an AstraZeneca $820M investment in Mississauga. A significant machinery-of-government reshuffle appears: "Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry" (Champagne) is replaced by "Minister of Industry" (Mélanie Joly), and a new, previously nonexistent portfolio — "Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation" (Evan Solomon) — is created, alongside a downgrade of the rural-development portfolio from full Minister (Gudie Hutchings) to Secretary of State (Buckley Belanger) (d0056ac3bc0c). Federal "Refocusing Government Spending" austerity reaches ISED for the first time, with permanent reductions reaching $313.7M/year by 2026-27, including cuts to CDAP (-$43.7M) and the SIF envelope (-$141.4M by 2026-27) and a scale-down of Innovative Solutions Canada (-$70M ongoing) (a5375017bcbe).

2025-26 (dp-2026 40fc2fa6da3d, "at a glance"): The department's stated top priority for the first time in the series is explicitly defensive/reactive rather than growth-oriented: "Protect Canadians and the Canadian economy against tariffs imposed by the United States, including the automotive manufacturing industry, by protecting jobs, supporting upskilling workers, and building Canada's domestic supply chain" (40fc2fa6da3d). AI safety is elevated from a program-level initiative (2024-25) to explicit top-line priority language: "Help Canada and Canadians benefit from the opportunities provided by artificial intelligence (AI), including by promoting AI safety." Canada's 2025 G7 presidency is named as a trade/investment priority driver. Sovereign AI compute sub-programs are named individually for the first time: the AI Compute Challenge, AI Compute Access Fund, and AI Sovereign Compute Infrastructure Program. Core responsibilities are now explicitly numbered ("Core responsibility 1/2/3") for the first time in this document set. Total planned spending reaches $8,591,437,915 — roughly triple the department's 2017-18 baseline (~$2.6-2.9B) eight fiscal years earlier.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • May-July 2017 — Innovation Superclusters Initiative ($950M) and Strategic Innovation Fund ($1.26B/5yr) launched, consolidating four legacy R&D programs (e6ced083e5b2).
  • FY2018-19 planning cycle — PAA replaced with Core Responsibilities framework department-wide, per Treasury Board Policy on Results (6d2ddb44edbb).
  • 2020-21 (in-year) — COVID-19 Economic Response Plan drives major in-year spending: Regional Relief and Recovery Fund ($2B), Medical Countermeasures Initiative (~$1B via SIF), CEWS/CERS/Lockdown Support (5f6cbb313ba8); total actual spending falls only modestly to $2.85B (from $3.05B planned) despite the shock, per the same document.
  • Aug 12, 2021 — FedNor spun off as a standalone federal department, the largest single machinery-of-government contraction in the series (418c2663afff).
  • 2021-22 — Net Zero Accelerator scales from an announced $3B to a described $8B within one fiscal year (cb5f433d7ec2; 418c2663afff); Canada Digital Adoption Program launches at $1.4B/four years.
  • Budget 2021 — described in dp-2023 as delivering "over $20B in new funding for ISED and the Portfolio" (63561aa23cfa).
  • 2022-23 — total planned spending jumps from $3.7B (2021-22) to $5.78B (2022-23), a roughly $2B one-year increase, driven by critical-minerals/EV-battery investment announcements (63561aa23cfa).
  • June 4, 2024 — OAG audit of Sustainable Development Technology Canada tabled (8b09876fe024), same-day as the government's announcement of SDTC's phased transition out of ISED to the NRC (2e6416d6525d) — the only governance-scandal-driven mandate change identified in the series.
  • Sept 2024 — Health Emergency Readiness Canada established as a special operating agency within ISED (d0056ac3bc0c).
  • 2024-25 — first appearance of federal "Refocusing Government Spending" austerity inside ISED's own planning documents: permanent reductions reaching $313.7M/year by 2026-27, hitting CDAP, the SIF envelope, and Innovative Solutions Canada (a5375017bcbe; d0056ac3bc0c).
  • 2024-25 (mandate) — creation of a standalone "Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation" (Evan Solomon), the first ISED-portfolio ministerial title dedicated to AI (d0056ac3bc0c).
  • 2025-26 — total planned spending reaches $8.59B, roughly triple the FY2017-18 level, while the #1 stated departmental priority becomes defensive tariff response rather than growth/innovation framing — the sharpest one-line reorientation of stated priorities anywhere in the series (40fc2fa6da3d).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Toronto/GTA relevance in this series is real but consistently secondary and funding-recipient-driven rather than institutional — no document describes an ISED office, facility, or headquarters function located in Toronto or the GTA. The department's own address given in these documents is in Ottawa; its named regional program through most of the series (FedNor, later spun off entirely in 2021) covers Northern Ontario specifically, a distinct region from the GTA. The first identifiable Toronto-specific activity is a 2017 blockchain proof-of-concept "with the government of Ontario and the City of Toronto" to simplify restaurant permitting (the BizPaL Restaurant Bundle pilot), reported in both drr-2018 (e6ced083e5b2) and dp-2019 (6d2ddb44edbb). From 2022-23 onward, the Vector Institute in Toronto recurs repeatedly as one of three federally funded national AI institutes (alongside Amii in Edmonton and Mila in Montreal) under the Pan-Canadian AI Strategy, first named in drr-2023 (d9ec8267ac23) and repeated through dp-2025 and drr-2025. Toronto Metropolitan University appears twice as a funded partner — a work-integrated-learning program (drr-2024, 2e6416d6525d) and an aerospace-sector INSAT recipient (drr-2025, d0056ac3bc0c). The most substantial single Toronto-area capital investment named in the series is AstraZeneca's $820M facility in Mississauga, facilitated through Health Emergency Readiness Canada partnerships (drr-2025, d0056ac3bc0c); drr-2025 also names a small Toronto-based Black Entrepreneurship Loan Fund recipient, Ladies Love Units. Larger Ontario manufacturing investments named in the series — Honda in Alliston, Volkswagen/PowerCo in St. Thomas, NextStar Energy in Windsor, Nova Chemicals in Sarnia, Nokia in Kanata, Burloak Technologies in Burlington — are all outside the GTA proper. As of 2024-25, the Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation (Evan Solomon) also holds the title "Minister responsible for the Federal Economic Development Agency for Southern Ontario" (FedDev Ontario), a distinct agency whose mandate covers the GTA — but FedDev Ontario itself is not part of this archived ISED document series, so its programming cannot be traced here. On this evidence, ISED's Toronto/GTA footprint is real (AI-institute funding, a handful of named company investments, one municipal pilot) but modest and indirect; the series does not establish Toronto as an operational or programmatic centre of gravity for the department.
