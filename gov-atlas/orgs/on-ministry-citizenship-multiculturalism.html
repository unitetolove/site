Ministry of Citizenship and Multiculturalism - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-citizenship-multiculturalism (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The ministry's enabling instrument is the Ministry of Citizenship and Culture Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.18, which continues "a ministry of the public service known in English as the Ministry of Citizenship and Culture" and gives the Minister power to "preside over and have charge of the Ministry" (consolidated text verified at https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m18, s.2-3). The Act's short title has not been amended to match the ministry's current operating name; the ministry now brands itself "Ministry of Citizenship and Multiculturalism" (ontario.ca/page/ministry-citizenship-and-multiculturalism, fetched) — the statutory name lag is common for Ontario ministries, whose day-to-day names shift by order-in-council rather than legislative amendment.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The ministry "leads the government's anti-racism, economic inclusion, cultural heritage and provincial awards initiatives to build an equitable Ontario" (ontario.ca/page/ministry-citizenship-and-multiculturalism, fetched). Concretely it runs the Anti-Racism Directorate and associated grant programs (Anti-Racism Anti-Hate Grant Program, Ontario Anti-Hate Security and Prevention Grant), the Black Youth Action Plan and RAISE entrepreneurship grants, provincial honours administration (including the Order of Ontario), and cultural-heritage conservation functions covering archaeological and built heritage (same source). Its current strategy artifact is the "Annual progress report 2025: Ontario's Anti-Racism Strategic Plan" (registry row on-ministry-citizenship-multiculturalism, strategy_current_url).

Governance & reporting line

As an Ontario ministry, it is headed by a Minister who sits in Executive Council (Cabinet) and is accountable to the Legislative Assembly; the Minister "has power to act for and on behalf of the Ministry" and may delegate to the Deputy Minister (Ministry of Citizenship and Culture Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.18, s.3-4, verified). Freedom-of-information requests route through Ontario's central FOI portal rather than a ministry-specific channel (registry row on-ministry-citizenship-multiculturalism, notes field).

Budget scale

~$92.7M total planned expenditure for 2025-26 ($89.1M operating + $3.6M capital), per the ministry's Published Plans and Annual Report (https://www.ontario.ca/page/published-plans-and-annual-reports-2025-2026-ministry-citizenship-and-multiculturalism, fetched).

Institutional history

The citizenship portfolio traces to a Provincial Secretary and Minister of Citizenship (1961), was reorganized as the Ministry of Citizenship and Culture in 1982 (per the still-standing enabling Act), and cycled through several names since — Citizenship, Culture and Recreation; Citizenship and Immigration; Citizenship, Immigration and International Trade (2014-2016) — before being reconstituted as the Ministry of Citizenship and Multiculturalism in 2021 after a period folded into the Ministry of Children, Community and Social Services. ⚠️ still being checked - this rename sequence is drawn from Wikipedia's ministry history summary, not independently confirmed against a primary Ontario government source this review.

Ministry of Citizenship and Multiculturalism - strategy evolution

TL;DR: The ministry's central strategy artifact is Building a Stronger and More Inclusive Ontario (Aug 2023), a 66-initiative, 14-ministry Anti-Racism Strategic Plan built on four fixed areas of action (economic empowerment; children and youth; population-specific anti-racism/anti-hate; policy and accountability) that has not been restructured since launch — the two subsequent annual progress reports (2024, 2025) report against the same four areas without adding or renaming any. The most notable addition across the archived series is two brand-new statutory honours programs created by name in 2024 (the Murray Whetung Community Service Award Act and the Honouring Veterans Act) alongside a 2025-26 Anti-Racism Directorate operating-budget jump of 86.4% ($22.98M) year-over-year — the largest single line-item swing in the archived financials. Open question: the Anti-Hate Security and Prevention Grant is reported at a flat $12.75M in both 2024-25 and 2025-26 in the ar-2026 document, but the original announcement was framed as a two-year, $25.5M-total commitment ending in 2024-25 — whether the 2025-26 tranche is a renewal, an extension of the same envelope, or a new commitment is not stated in the archived text. ⚠️ still being checked.

Priority evolution

2023 (sp-2023, d29a4dec4f83), "Building a Stronger and More Inclusive Ontario": Launch of the current Anti-Racism Strategic Plan (Aug 24, 2023), replacing the prior strategy after a review process ("more than 300 participants," "over 1,000 online and written submissions"). Structured around four areas of action — economic empowerment; supporting children and youth; population-specific anti-racism and anti-hate initiatives; policy and accountability — delivered via named initiatives from multiple ministries (RAISE and the Anti-Hate Security and Prevention Grant sit under Citizenship and Multiculturalism; most named dollar initiatives belong to partner ministries — Education, Children/Community/Social Services, Labour/Immigration/Training/Skills Development, Health, Solicitor General, Attorney General, Seniors and Accessibility). Total ministry investment stated as "over $132.5 million." A detailed terminology glossary (racism, systemic racism, Islamophobia, antisemitism, intersectionality, etc.) is appended as standard front/back matter (d29a4dec4f83).

2024 (ar-2024, 525f9dbf6698), first annual progress report under the new plan: Confirms the same four areas of action, unchanged. Minister (Michael Ford) and Deputy Minister (Nosa Ero-Brown) messages both frame the report as tracking the August 2023 plan's initial year; the Deputy Minister's message specifically calls out an expansion of the Anti-Hate Security and Prevention Grant as a named enhancement. Reporting scope: "14 ministries" (525f9dbf6698).

2025 (ar-2025, 192d0ca33438), sixth annual report under the Anti-Racism Act reporting requirement (second under the current 2023 plan) — new Minister: Minister attribution changes from Michael Ford to Graham McGregor; Deputy Minister (Ero-Brown) continues. Same four areas of action retained; same "14 ministries" scope claim. Framing shifts noticeably toward economic/competitiveness language ("global uncertainty and economic pressures," "future-ready economy," "protect Ontario") not present in the 2023 or 2024 documents' framing, alongside continued equity language. New for 2025: an explicit note that initiatives outside the four official strategy areas are now also being surfaced in the annual report ("new initiatives... not part of the official Anti-Racism Strategy... help illustrate how ministries across the government are supporting anti-racism and anti-hate efforts outside of the Anti-Racism Strategy") (192d0ca33438).

2025-26 (ar-2026, 096a5ad7edfb), Published Plans and Annual Report — combined forward plan + FY2024-25 retrospective: Ministry structure formalized as three divisions: Anti-Racism Directorate (ARD), Citizenship, Inclusion and Heritage Division (CIHD), Heritage Policy and Programs Division (HPPD). Forward plan for 2025-26 restates the same anti-racism strategic plan and the same named grant programs (AHSP, RAISE, BYAP Economic Empowerment) at flat or near-flat dollar levels versus 2024-25. New material versus the 2023/2024/2025 documents: two brand-new statutory honours programs (Murray Whetung Community Service Award Act, Royal Assent April 2024; Honouring Veterans Act, Royal Assent November 2024), three completed 2024-25 "Lean efficiency reviews" of honours programs (Order of Ontario, Volunteer Service Awards, Amethyst Awards) yielding "41 recommendations" targeted at "at least 30%" efficiency gains, and a heritage-side operational overhaul (archaeological-assessment review timeline cut from 70+ business days to a 40-day target, met "90 per cent of the time" as of March 31, 2025; backlog of 3,000 reports "reduced... by 47 per cent"). The Return of the Ancestors Initiative (Indigenous repatriation program, running since 2014) is reported in both the forward-plan and retrospective sections with concrete 2024-25 progress (106 sets of Ancestors under draft reporting; consultations initiated with nine Indigenous communities for 15 sets) (096a5ad7edfb).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is an Ontario provincial ministry with province-wide scope by definition; Toronto-specific footprint within the archived documents is concentrated in named programs and grantees rather than a head-office-location argument. sp-2023 names a $1.8M grant to the Toronto District School Board specifically to address school violence and support inclusion/engagement, delivered through "culturally responsive and faith-based community agencies" (d29a4dec4f83). The Black Youth Action Plan's Economic Empowerment stream names Toronto Metropolitan University's Digital Media Zone (DMZ) Black Innovation Program as one of six funded initiative streams, both in the ar-2026 forward plan ($16.5M, 2025-26) and retrospective (same figure, 2024-25) (096a5ad7edfb). The Ontario Heritage Trust — consolidated into MCM's financials from 2024-25 Interim onward — operates the Elgin and Winter Garden Theatre Centre, a Toronto heritage property, per the ar-2026 organization chart (096a5ad7edfb). No document in this series names a Toronto-specific anti-racism or heritage initiative beyond these; program funding is otherwise described at the provincial or named-organization level without city breakdowns.
