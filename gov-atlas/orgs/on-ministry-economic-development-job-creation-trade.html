Ministry of Economic Development, Job Creation and Trade - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-economic-development-job-creation-trade (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The ministry's continuation statute is the Ministry of Economic Development and Trade Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.27, consolidated text current to the e-Laws currency date, consolidation period effective April 27, 2021 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m27, fetched and verified via the e-Laws legislation service, section-by-section content confirmed). Section 2 continues the former "Ministry of Industry, Trade and Technology" under the name "Ministry of Economic Development and Trade" (renamed 2006, c. 19, Sched. I, s. 4(3), same source). Section 3 sets statutory objectives: stimulating income opportunities via industry/trade development, supporting employment growth through trade expansion and investment, and advancing the interests of Ontario's private sector (same source). ⚠️ Still being checked: the Act's own text still names the ministry "Ministry of Economic Development and Trade," not the current public name "Ministry of Economic Development, Job Creation and Trade" — the instrument adding "Job Creation" was not located this review.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Beyond s. 3's objectives, the ministry's own page describes supporting business start-up/growth/investment/trade, managing international trade and investment offices, representing Ontario in trade negotiations (CUSMA, CPTPP, CETA), and developing sector strategies (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-economic-development-job-creation-and-trade, fetched directly). Affiliated agencies include Invest Ontario and Venture Ontario (same source).

Governance & reporting line

As a Crown ministry it is structurally organized under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.25, ss. 1-2, which empowers the Lieutenant Governor in Council to appoint ministers and assign ministerial/ministry duties by order in council (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e25, fetched and verified); the M.27 Act sets the ministry's objectives (s. 3) rather than a separate "Minister to have charge" clause (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m27).

Institutional history

Established as "Ministry of Industry, Trade and Technology," continued and renamed "Ministry of Economic Development and Trade" by 2006, c. 19, Sched. I, s. 4(3) — evidence this Act has previously been amended to track ministry renames (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m27, s. 2 text and amendment note, fetched and verified). The further rename to "Ministry of Economic Development, Job Creation and Trade" seen on the ministry's current site is a more recent machinery-of-government change not yet reflected in the Act text located this review — ⚠️ still being checked.

Priority evolution

1998-99 (bp-1999, 631260986fde): Not recoverable — the archived capture is an Internet Archive item-detail/catalog page (title, OCR/scan metadata, download-format list, "SIMILAR ITEMS" widget) for a 15-page business plan titled "Ministry of Economic Development, Trade and Tourism 1998-1999 Business Plan," but contains no plan text itself. The ministry name recorded on this record — "Ministry of Economic Development, Trade and Tourism" — is itself evidence of a still-earlier ministry name/mandate configuration than either the M.27 Act's "Ministry of Economic Development and Trade" or the current "…Job Creation and Trade," but this cannot be developed further from the captured text. ⚠️ still being checked.

2021 (oth-2021, 5b4f65fac00e), Auditor General value-for-money audit of the ENCQOR 5G Program: Documents a specific ministry-administered funding stream, the Jobs and Prosperity Fund, which in 2016-17 approved a proposal from Ericsson, Ciena and Thales for a five-year, $400 million tri-government/tri-vendor 5G pre-commercial test-platform program (Ontario's share $66.7 million), administered on the ministry's behalf by the Ontario Centre of Innovation (OCI) — the same OCI that appears as a standing ministry-supported agency/program-delivery partner in every later annual report in this set. The audit is critical: it found the Transfer Payment Agreement between the ministry and OCI lacked measurable targets for five of nine performance metrics, that only ~130 of a claimed 320 SME-side jobs were actually verified as created/retained (against a 3,000-job program-wide target), that test platforms were significantly underused, and that OCI's fund-allocation and cybersecurity practices needed improvement (5b4f65fac00e). This document is useful evidence of the ministry's program-delivery/oversight model (transfer payment agreements to arm's-length or agency delivery partners, e.g., OCI) recurring across the later annual reports, and as an early, harder-edged external data point against which the annual reports' self-reported achievement narratives can be read.

2022-23 reporting year, described retrospectively in ar-2024 (28dbebab40ab): The ministry's stated vision is "building Ontario's economy for today and for tomorrow through attracting and protecting investments and jobs," organized under five priority areas: Building Ontario's Electric Vehicle Supply Chain; Bringing Manufacturing Jobs Back to Ontario; Attracting Investments to Ontario; Supporting Ontario Businesses and Entrepreneurship; Fostering a Growing Innovation and Technology Ecosystem. Named 2022-23 achievements include ~155 investment projects landed (>$16 billion, 7,300+ jobs created, 20,000 retained), the Volkswagen/PowerCo $7 billion EV battery plant announcement (St. Thomas, March 2023), an Advanced Manufacturing Council established (April 2023), a Life Sciences Council established (April 2023), and Intellectual Property Ontario (IPON) beginning a beta service phase (October 2022) (28dbebab40ab). 2023-24 planned expenditure is reported at $1,309,133,287 total ministry operating expense (Table 1, 28dbebab40ab).

2023-24 reporting year, described in ar-2025 (ba77e50ffa6f): The five-priority framing from ar-2024 contracts to four priority areas: Growing Ontario's Electric Vehicle Supply Chain; Attracting Investments to Ontario; Supporting Ontario Businesses and Entrepreneurship; Building the Future Economy of Ontario. "Bringing Manufacturing Jobs Back to Ontario" and "Fostering a Growing Innovation and Technology Ecosystem" as named, separate priority headings both disappear; their program content (industrial land development, critical technologies, advanced manufacturing) is folded into the remaining four headings rather than dropped substantively. First appearance of formal KPI/target-and-trend reporting structure with named outcome statements (job creation, investment leveraged, businesses/entrepreneurs supported) and multi-year trend/target values through FY2029-30 (ba77e50ffa6f). Named 2023-24 achievements: 57 export business missions ($1.5B potential export sales), five free trade agreements negotiated, 62 investment projects landed with Invest Ontario (>$1.1B, 2,200+ jobs), the Critical Technology Initiatives (CTI) program launched ($107 million to five organizations: Vector Institute, OCI, Ontario Bioscience Innovation Organization, Quantum Valley Ideas Lab, Canadian Cyber Threat Exchange). Ministry total operating expense for 2024-25 planned at $2,686,059,865 (Table 1, ba77e50ffa6f) — roughly double the prior year's figure, driven by the Economic Development, Job Creation and Trade Program vote rising 107.2% year-over-year (Table A1, ba77e50ffa6f).

2024-25 reporting year, described in ar-2026 (26e992d9d9f0): The four-priority structure is retained but reframed with a new lead priority — "Protecting the economy of Ontario" replaces "Building the Future Economy of Ontario" as one of the four headings, explicitly framed around responding to U.S. tariffs: "The Ontario government is taking action to protect workers, businesses, and jobs in the face of U.S. tariffs and continued economic uncertainty, with a plan to build an economy that is more resilient and self-reliant" (26e992d9d9f0). Two new named programs appear under this heading: the Ontario Together Trade Fund (OTTF, $50 million over three years from 2025-26) and the Trade-Impacted Communities Program (TICP, up to $40 million in grants from 2025-26). The April 2025 "Protect Ontario through Free Trade within Canada Act" is named as new legislation enabling mutual recognition of goods/services/workers with reciprocating Canadian jurisdictions, with implementing regulations targeted by end of 2025. Named 2024-25 achievements: development of "Ontario's U.S. Tariff Response Strategy," 15 EV-sector projects landed (>$15 billion, 3,000+ jobs created, 7,000+ retained), the AstraZeneca $820 million Mississauga R&D/HQ relocation investment (January 2025, $16.1 million ministry support via Invest Ontario Fund), the Siemens $150 million Oakville AI-manufacturing R&D centre (March 2025, $7.2 million ministry support). Ministry total operating expense for 2025-26 planned at $2,494,743,665 (Table 1, 26e992d9d9f0) — a modest decrease from the 2024-25 estimate, driven by a 6.7% reduction in the Economic Development, Job Creation and Trade Program vote (Table A1, 26e992d9d9f0), even as Operating Assets nearly tripled (from $141,965,400 to $329,371,600) — consistent with Invest Ontario Fund top-ups being booked as investment/loan assets rather than expense.

  • Added — Ontario Together Trade Fund (OTTF) and Trade-Impacted Communities Program (TICP): both new in ar-2026 (26e992d9d9f0, $50M/3yr and up to $40M respectively, both starting 2025-26), tied to the new "Protecting the economy" priority.
  • Added — formal KPI/trend/target reporting structure: absent as a named framework in ar-2024 (28dbebab40ab, which reports program-level performance measures/outcomes/outputs but not ministry-wide KPI trend tables); first appears in ar-2025 (ba77e50ffa6f) with three outcome-statement KPIs (job creation, investment leveraged, businesses/entrepreneurs supported), each carrying baseline/trend/target values through FY2029-30; retained and extended in ar-2026 (26e992d9d9f0).
  • Added — "Protect Ontario through Free Trade within Canada Act" (April 2025): new interprovincial-trade legislation, first appears ar-2026 (26e992d9d9f0); not present in ar-2024 or ar-2025 (predates their reporting windows).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 2016-17 — Jobs and Prosperity Fund approves the ENCQOR 5G proposal, later becoming a $400M tri-government/tri-vendor program with $66.7M Ontario share (5b4f65fac00e, retrospective description in the 2021 audit).
  • March 2022 — ENCQOR 5G Program agreement concludes; a third-party economic impact study was planned by OCI upon completion (5b4f65fac00e).
  • April 2023 — Advanced Manufacturing Council and Life Sciences Council both established (28dbebab40ab).
  • March 2023 — Volkswagen/PowerCo $7 billion EV battery plant announcement for St. Thomas — described in the series as "the largest electric vehicle-related investment in Canadian history" (28dbebab40ab).
  • 2023-24 to 2024-25 — Ministry total operating expense roughly doubles, from $1,309,133,287 (planned 2023-24, 28dbebab40ab) to $2,686,059,865 (planned 2024-25, ba77e50ffa6f) — a 105.2% increase per the ar-2025 historic-trend table (ba77e50ffa6f).
  • October 2024 — Phase 2 of the Life Sciences Strategy ("Taking Life Sciences to the Next Level") released, an all-of-government $146 million action plan with four comprehensive goals (26e992d9d9f0).
  • 2024 Fall Economic Statement — Advanced Manufacturing Plan released, a 10-year roadmap targeting >$120 billion sector output and 1 million manufacturing-sector jobs by 2035 (26e992d9d9f0).
  • Early 2025 (reported in ar-2026) — Ministry develops "Ontario's U.S. Tariff Response Strategy" in response to U.S. tariff actions; this becomes the organizing frame for the entire 2025-26 published plan (26e992d9d9f0).
  • April 2025 — "Protect Ontario through Free Trade within Canada Act" introduced (26e992d9d9f0).
  • 2025 Budget — additional $600 million allocated to the Invest Ontario Fund; new Ontario Together Trade Fund ($50M/3yr) and Trade-Impacted Communities Program (up to $40M) both launched starting 2025-26 (26e992d9d9f0).
  • 2023-24 to 2025-26 — Ministry total operating expense declines from the 2024-25 peak, with 2025-26 planned at $2,494,743,665, a 6.6% decrease from the prior year's estimate (Table A1, 26e992d9d9f0), even as Operating Assets rise sharply (driven by Invest Ontario Fund and Venture Ontario capital commitments booked as investments).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is a provincial Ontario ministry; the entire archived series is Ontario-scoped by definition, and Toronto/GTA-specific content is frequent and substantive rather than incidental. Toronto Global — the investment-attraction agency for the City of Toronto, Mississauga, Brampton, and the regions of Durham, Halton and York — is a named, ministry-funded transfer-payment recipient in ar-2024 (28dbebab40ab: 2022-23 budget $2.5M, funded by the ministry "since 2017," achieving 27 closed wins, $748,675,664 money invested, 2,565 jobs created) and remains a named investment-attraction organization (alongside Waterloo Region Economic Development Corporation and Invest Ottawa) receiving $10.3 million combined over three years in ar-2026 (26e992d9d9f0). Toronto-specific investments named across the series include: Citibank's Internal Audit Hub in Mississauga (28dbebab40ab); Unilever's Horizon3 AI Lab headquartered in downtown Toronto with manufacturing in Etobicoke and Simcoe (ba77e50ffa6f); AstraZeneca's $820 million Mississauga R&D expansion/HQ relocation (26e992d9d9f0); the ShopHERE small-business e-commerce program, delivered by the Toronto Association of Business Improvement Areas (28dbebab40ab); one of seven OVIN Regional Technology Development Sites is explicitly sited in Toronto, with a ministerial visit named (28dbebab40ab). The ministry's head office/business-planning contact in every annual report is listed with a 647 area code (Toronto), consistent with the ministry being headquartered in Toronto, though no report states this as an explicit fact. ⚠️ Still being checked: no document in this archived set gives the ministry's street address or explicitly names Toronto as its headquarters location.
