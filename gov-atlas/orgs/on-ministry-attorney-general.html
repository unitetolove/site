Ministry of the Attorney General - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: on-ministry-attorney-general (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Ministry is continued by the Ministry of the Attorney General Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.17, consolidated text current to the e-Laws currency date, consolidation period effective July 1, 2019 (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m17, fetched and verified via the e-Laws legislation service, section-by-section content confirmed). Section 2(1) continues "the ministry of the public service known in English as the Ministry of the Attorney General," with the Attorney General presiding over and having charge of it (s. 2(2), same source). Section 5 makes the Attorney General "the Law Officer of the Executive Council," responsible for seeing that public administration is conducted according to law and for superintending matters connected with the administration of justice (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Under s. 5, the Attorney General/Ministry is Law Officer of the Executive Council and superintendent of justice-administration matters (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m17). The ministry's own page describes administering the justice system, coordinating court services, and providing legal counsel to government ministries, agencies and tribunals (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-attorney-general, fetched directly). It oversees eight independent agencies (e.g., Legal Aid Ontario, the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario, the Ontario Human Rights Commission, the Special Investigations Unit) and Tribunals Ontario, which administers twelve specialized tribunals (same source).

Governance & reporting line

The Attorney General presides over and has charge of the Ministry (Act s. 2(2)); a Deputy Attorney General, appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council, serves as deputy head (s. 3) (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m17). As a ministry of the Crown, it is part of the executive government of Ontario, structurally organized under the Executive Council Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.25, which empowers the Lieutenant Governor to appoint ministers and, by order in council, prescribe ministerial and ministry duties (Executive Council Act ss. 1-2, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e25, fetched and verified).

Institutional history

The Ministry traces its continuation to the Ministry of the Attorney General Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.17 (a Revised Statutes of Ontario consolidation of pre-existing law); the Act has been amended, most substantively regarding Ministry employee appointment authority, by 2006, c. 35, Sched. C, s. 80 and 2019, c. 7, Sched. 17, s. 119, with no change recorded to its s. 2 "Ministry continued" naming provision, which still reads "Ministry of the Attorney General" — matching the current public name (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m17, amendment history field, fetched and verified).

Ministry of the Attorney General - strategy evolution

2026-08-01 / registry: on-ministry-attorney-general / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority addition in the recent (2024-2026) record is the Courts Technology and Transformation Program, a wholly new named unit with its own budget line and Assistant Deputy Attorney General, alongside a new Associate Attorney General's Office. The most notable quietly-dropped/changed item is the LAMP Facility Condition Index KPI, redefined from a percentage-of-target metric to a dollar-investment metric after the ministry stated it lacks funding to reach an "optimal" condition target. The most load-bearing number is the roughly 16% rise in ministry-wide operating-and-capital spending between the FY2024-25 estimate (~$1.97B) and the FY2025-26 estimate (~$2.28B). One open question the record cannot resolve: the entire 2000-2020 institutional history is ungrounded because four early Internet Archive captures (2000, 2001, 2002, 2006) are landing-page stubs with no report text, and no documents at all were archived for 2003-2020.

Backgrounder summary

The Ministry is continued by the Ministry of the Attorney General Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.17; the Attorney General is "Law Officer of the Executive Council" and superintends the administration of justice (Act ss. 2, 5), with a Deputy Attorney General as deputy head (s. 3). The ministry administers the justice system, coordinates court services, and provides legal counsel across government, overseeing roughly eight independent agencies (Legal Aid Ontario, the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario, the Ontario Human Rights Commission, the Special Investigations Unit, etc.) and Tribunals Ontario. The backgrounder flagged ministry-wide budget scale as ⚠️ still being checked — not grounded in that pass. This brief's read of the three most recent Published Plans and Annual Reports closes part of that gap: ministry-wide operating-and-capital totals of roughly $1.92B (FY2023-24 estimate), $1.97B (FY2024-25 estimate) and $2.28B (FY2025-26 estimate) are directly grounded (a053e79c8cec; 44a43957eede; 32f56c857e6c), though nothing earlier than FY2020-21 is grounded and the pre-2021 institutional history remains entirely unverified in this series (see Residuals & gaps).

Priority evolution

2000 / 2001 / 2002 / 2006 (bb5b02279dfc; 8cf39081ace4; 1776a76a93b6; 90e701032b45): No institutional content readable — see Series inventory and Residuals & gaps. The only extractable text on these four pages is Internet Archive catalog metadata, not ministry-authored content, so no priority claims are grounded for this quarter-century span.

Dec 2021, Auditor General report "Internet Gaming in Ontario" (b75dda295a07): The earliest grounded institutional content in this series. The report traces AGCO's establishment as regulator in 1998 and OLG's formation in 2000 (merger of the 1975 Ontario Lottery Corporation and 1994 Ontario Casino Corporation), then covers the ministry's Internet-gaming mandate expansion: in Feb 2019 the government directed the Ministry of Finance and the Ministry of the Attorney General jointly to develop an Internet-gaming strategy; the April 2019 Budget announced plans for a competitive regulated market; the Nov 2020 Budget proposed giving AGCO conduct-and-manage authority via a new subsidiary; and on July 6, 2021 iGaming Ontario was created by regulation as a wholly-owned AGCO subsidiary. The Auditor General flagged three concerns — Criminal Code "conduct and manage" legal risk, a governance conflict of interest (AGCO regulating a subsidiary whose object is to generate provincial revenue), and reduced AGCO direct oversight of game-fairness testing — and recommended separating iGaming Ontario's governance from AGCO. The ministry's formal response declined that separation, stating "the benefits of maintaining the status quo outweigh the operational risks" (b75dda295a07). Notably, the ministry's own 2020 value-for-money audit of AGCO had already recommended the same separation the ministry now declined (b75dda295a07).

FY2022-23, read via the ar-2024 filing's appendix (a053e79c8cec): iGaming Ontario's market launched April 1, 2022, and by year one reported 40+ operators across 70 gaming websites, $35.6B in total wagers and $1.4B in gaming revenue — the legal-risk question the Auditor General raised in Dec 2021 (b75dda295a07) was not resolved before launch. The ministry also narrowed its own direct victim-services footprint this year: three victim-services programs were transferred to the Ministry of Children, Community and Social Services in January 2022, followed by six more in April 2022. The criminal case backlog reduction strategy (launched Oct 2021, $72M over two years) continued; the Kenora Justice Centre opened Feb 6, 2023 as the fourth Justice Centre (after London, Sept 2020, and Toronto Downtown East/Northwest, May 2021); and a new 17-storey Ontario Court of Justice — Toronto courthouse opened March 2023, consolidating six OCJ criminal courts.

FY2023-24, read via the ar-2024 plan and the ar-2025 filing's appendix (a053e79c8cec; 44a43957eede): The ministry's "Justice Accelerated" multi-year digital-transformation strategy and the criminal case backlog strategy anchor stated priorities, alongside a new "courthouse of the future" concept piloted at Milton (Halton Region). A $166M investment (July 2023) launched the Courts Digital Transformation (CDT) initiative with Thomson Reuters as delivery vendor. New specialized prosecution capacity was created: the Major Auto Theft Prosecution Response Team ($13.9M/3yr, May 2023) and Intensive Serious Violent Crime Bail Support Teams ($26M/3yr, April 2023, part of a larger $112M/3yr government investment). Legal Aid Ontario began its first tariff-compensation reform in roughly 25 years, phased from October 2023. The $117M Brampton courthouse expansion completed in early 2024. In February 2024 Ontario itself filed a reference with the Court of Appeal asking whether allowing Ontarians to play against players outside Canada was consistent with the Criminal Code — directly engaging the legal-risk question the 2021 Auditor General audit had raised about iGaming Ontario's model (b75dda295a07; 44a43957eede).

FY2024-25, read via the ar-2025 plan and the ar-2026 filing's appendix (44a43957eede; 32f56c857e6c): Court-capacity expansion became an explicit priority: a May 2024 $29M investment funded at least 25 new Ontario Court of Justice judges plus 190 more Crown prosecutors, victim-support and court staff; by the ar-2026 report, 40 new judges had been appointed since May 2024. The government announced a major AGCO mandate expansion — phased alcohol-retail liberalization (grocery, convenience and big-box stores) rolling out August-October 2024, with AGCO receiving added compliance/enforcement funding. Ontario's first Indigenous Learning Centre opened at the Ontario Court of Justice — Toronto in June 2024, jointly operated with the Mississaugas of the Credit First Nation. CDT's first phase was slated for 2025 launch in Toronto (Superior Court of Justice family/civil/small claims/enforcement/divisional, plus Ontario Court of Justice family court).

FY2025-26 plan, read via the ar-2026 filing (32f56c857e6c): The strategic framing shifts to "protecting communities by creating a modern, sustainable justice system." New organizational units appear for the first time in this series: an Associate Attorney General's Office (under Ministry Administration), a standalone "Courts Technology and Transformation Program" with its own budget line and Assistant Deputy Attorney General, a Provincial Gun and Gang Support Unit, and new cybercrime/cryptocurrency prosecution teams. The Intensive Serious Violent Crime Bail Teams and the criminal-backlog hiring are described as being made permanent. Judicial-appointment process legislation is planned (a new pool-based recommendation process for the Judicial Appointments Advisory Committee) alongside up to 10 additional Ontario Court of Justice judges over three years. A "Safer Municipalities Act" is planned to increase penalties for repeat trespass. CDT Phase 2 (Toronto criminal proceedings) is targeted for 2027.

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Added — iGaming Ontario as an AGCO subsidiary (created by regulation July 6, 2021; market launched April 1, 2022): absent before 2021, then a recurring consolidation-adjustment budget line from the ar-2024 filing onward (b75dda295a07; a053e79c8cec).
  • Added — Courts Technology and Transformation Program: a wholly new named ministry program with its own budget line ($12.55M FY2025-26 estimate) and dedicated Assistant Deputy Attorney General, first appearing in ar-2026 (32f56c857e6c); previously court digital work sat inside the Court Services Program.
  • Added — Associate Attorney General's Office: first named under Ministry Administration in ar-2026 (32f56c857e6c), absent from the ar-2024 and ar-2025 program descriptions.
  • Renamed — Office of the Independent Police Review Director → Law Enforcement Complaints Agency: named "Office of the Independent Police Review Director" in the ar-2024 agencies table (a053e79c8cec); by ar-2025 it appears as "Law Enforcement Complaints Agency," with a corresponding new legislative citation to the Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019 (44a43957eede).
  • Added — specialized prosecution units: Major Auto Theft Prosecution Response Team (May 2023) and Intensive Serious Violent Crime Bail Support Teams (April 2023) first appear in the ar-2025 appendix (44a43957eede); a Complex Prosecution Bureau appears in the ar-2025 org chart (44a43957eede); a Provincial Gun and Gang Support Unit and cybercrime/cryptocurrency prosecution teams first appear in the ar-2026 plan (32f56c857e6c).
  • Dropped/transferred — nine victim-services programs, moved from the Ministry to the Ministry of Children, Community and Social Services in two tranches (three in Jan 2022, six in April 2022), narrowing the Victims and Vulnerable Persons Program's direct delivery scope (a053e79c8cec).
  • Redefined — the LAMP Facility Condition Index KPI: tracked as a percentage-of-target metric through ar-2025 (targets of 54.1%/54.9% by 2029) (44a43957eede), then replaced in ar-2026 with a dollar-investment-amount metric (target $515.46M by 2029), with the ministry stating directly that it lacks funding to reach an "optimal" 30% FCI and is tracking affordable investment instead (32f56c857e6c).
  • Made permanent — two time-limited investments: the Oct 2021 criminal-backlog hiring ($72M/2yr) is described in ar-2025 as now "part of the ministry's annual funding" (44a43957eede); the April 2023 Intensive Serious Violent Crime Bail Support Teams ($26M/3yr, time-limited) are described in ar-2026 as being "permanently" established (32f56c857e6c).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is an Ontario provincial ministry with its statutory seat, courts and headquarters functions concentrated substantially in Toronto, so relevance here is direct rather than incidental. Grounded Toronto-specific items across the readable series: a new 17-storey Ontario Court of Justice — Toronto courthouse opened March 2023, consolidating six OCJ criminal courts under one roof (a053e79c8cec); the Toronto Downtown East and Toronto Northwest Justice Centres launched May 2021 (a053e79c8cec); Ontario's first Indigenous Learning Centre opened at the Ontario Court of Justice — Toronto in June 2024, jointly operated with the Mississaugas of the Credit First Nation (32f56c857e6c); the Courts Digital Transformation initiative's first phase is targeted to launch in Toronto in 2025 (Superior Court of Justice family/civil/small claims/enforcement/divisional plus Ontario Court of Justice family court), with Phase 2 (Toronto criminal proceedings) targeted for 2027 (44a43957eede; 32f56c857e6c); the Major Auto Theft Prosecution Response Team is based partly in Toronto, alongside Hamilton, Ottawa and London (44a43957eede); and 2024 court-staffing investment specifically named Toronto Region for recruitment focus (44a43957eede). All three organizational charts read (a053e79c8cec; 44a43957eede; 32f56c857e6c) list "Toronto" as one of seven Court Operations/Crown Operations regional divisions alongside Central East, East, Central West, West, Northeast and Northwest. Separately, the four unreadable Internet Archive landing pages carry an archive.org catalog "Publisher" field reading "[Toronto]: Ministry of the Attorney General" (bb5b02279dfc; 8cf39081ace4; 1776a76a93b6; 90e701032b45) — this is bibliographic catalog metadata, not ministry report content, and is noted here only as a minor corroborating detail, not a substantive institutional claim.
