Sinai Health - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: hosp-sinai-health (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Sinai Health (legal name Sinai Health System) operates as an approved public hospital under the Public Hospitals Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.40, which requires an approved governing board and empowers the Minister of Health to issue binding directives and set funding conditions (https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90p40, verified 2026-07-30). Its own audited financial statements state directly: "Sinai Health System (the Hospital) was formed in 2015 through the voluntary amalgamation of Mount Sinai Hospital (inclusive of the Lunenfeld-Tanenbaum Research Institute) and Hennick Bridgepoint Hospital" (Sinai Health System Audited Financial Statements, year ended March 31, 2026, document id hosp-sinai-health-fs-2026, https://www.sinaihealth.ca/media/28051/download?inline). ⚠️ Still being checked: the specific incorporating instrument for the 2015 amalgamation (e.g., Corporations Act (Ontario) letters patent) was not stated explicitly in the sections of the FS reviewed this review.

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Sinai Health is "a public teaching and research hospital affiliated with the University of Toronto," providing "patient care, teaches healthcare professionals, conducts research and provides chronic care and rehabilitation services" (document id hosp-sinai-health-fs-2026). The network comprises Mount Sinai Hospital, Hennick Bridgepoint Hospital, and system partner Circle of Care (registry row hosp-sinai-health, notes field; https://www.sinaihealth.ca/about-sinai-health, fetched 2026-07-30).

Governance & reporting line

Sinai Health is governed by a Board of Directors; its independent auditor's report is addressed "To the Board of Directors of Sinai Health System" (document id hosp-sinai-health-fs-2026). Per the registry, Ontario Health (on-ontario-health) is the funding/oversight parent for this independent, non-profit hospital corporation. No corporate-wide strategic plan was located this review (registry row hosp-sinai-health, notes field).

Budget scale

~$975.8M total income against ~$1.004B total expenses for the year ended March 31, 2026, a deficiency of income over expenses of $28.5M, of which $731.7M of income came from the Ministry of Health and Ontario Health (Sinai Health System Audited Financial Statements FY2026, document id hosp-sinai-health-fs-2026).

Institutional history

Sinai Health System was formed in 2015 through the voluntary amalgamation of Mount Sinai Hospital and Hennick Bridgepoint Hospital, unifying acute academic care with complex/rehabilitation care under one corporate entity (document id hosp-sinai-health-fs-2026). ⚠️ Still being checked: individual founding histories of the predecessor Mount Sinai and Bridgepoint institutions were not researched this review.

Backgrounder summary

Sinai Health (legal name Sinai Health System) is an approved public hospital under Ontario's Public Hospitals Act, formed in 2015 through the voluntary amalgamation of Mount Sinai Hospital (inclusive of the Lunenfeld-Tanenbaum Research Institute) and Hennick Bridgepoint Hospital, and affiliated with the University of Toronto (hosp-sinai-health-fs-2026, f691f9d006cc). It is funded primarily by the Province of Ontario through the Ministry of Health and Ontario Health, is governed by a Board of Directors, and reports through an independent auditor's report addressed to that Board (hosp-sinai-health-fs-2026, f691f9d006cc). For the year ended March 31, 2026, total income was $975,756 thousand against total expenses of $1,004,209 thousand — a deficiency of income over expenses of $28,453 thousand, of which $731,744 thousand came from the Ministry of Health and Ontario Health combined (hosp-sinai-health-fs-2026, f691f9d006cc).

No income-statement or notes content is available from this doc to characterize the year's priorities.

FY2021: No readable content exists in this archived series (fs-2021 extraction failed). ⚠️ Still being checked: nothing about FY2021 can be grounded to an archived document id in this review; the only FY2021 figures available are the prior-year comparatives embedded in fs-2022 (7d85f6412f07), cited below.

FY2022 (fs-2022, 7d85f6412f07): Total income $751,417 thousand (of which Ministry of Health $499,775 thousand and COVID-19 funding $71,527 thousand, down from $107,911 thousand in FY2021 per the comparative column); total expenses $731,534 thousand; excess of income over expenses $9,809 thousand. The Hospital states its incremental COVID-19 operating expenditures and revenue reduction at $71,527 thousand for the year, plus $415 thousand in COVID-19 capital expenditures, per the Ministry's "COVID-19 Incremental Hospital Expenses Guidance" document (fs-2022, 7d85f6412f07). The Renew Sinai Phase 3A redevelopment project (Mount Sinai Hospital expansion/modernization of emergency department, surgical suites, ICU, ambulatory areas, and a new inpatient unit, under a June 2017 project agreement) is an active, named capital priority, with $37,241 thousand in construction/financing work completed in the year (fs-2022, 7d85f6412f07). In November 2022, the Ontario Superior Court of Justice ruled Bill 124 unconstitutional, triggering union reopener provisions; the Hospital reports it "accrued its best estimate for retroactive wage increases due to the various union groups," with some contracts still in negotiation or arbitration as of the statement date (fs-2023, 9aeae3d4a63f). Renew Sinai Phase 3A construction/financing work completed in the year rose to $48,137 thousand (fs-2023, 9aeae3d4a63f). The Hospital adopted PS 3280 (Asset Retirement Obligations) on a modified retroactive basis, increasing other long-term liabilities and net deficit by $1,549 thousand as of April 1, 2022 (fs-2023, 9aeae3d4a63f).

FY2024 (fs-2024, e0f124c37cfc): Total income $837,509 thousand; Ministry of Health income alone reaches $642,353 thousand (COVID-19 funding is no longer broken out as a separate line — comparative figures were reclassified to fold COVID-19 funding into the Ministry of Health line, per note 16, fs-2024, e0f124c37cfc); total expenses $819,847 thousand; excess of income over expenses $7,129 thousand, returning to surplus. As a subsequent event, in June 2024 the Renew Sinai Phase 3A redevelopment project "reached substantial completion": the Ministry transferred $110,523 thousand to the Hospital and the Hospital paid $100,244 thousand to the redevelopment project's contractor (fs-2024, e0f124c37cfc).

FY2025 (fs-2025, ca3cbfd3c8bc): Total income (per fs-2026's comparative column) $951,881 thousand; Ministry of Health income is now reported as a combined "Ministry of Health and Ontario Health" line at $706,146 thousand (restated comparative in fs-2026, f691f9d006cc) / $686,388 thousand as originally reported in fs-2025 itself (fs-2025, ca3cbfd3c8bc) — see Residuals & gaps for the reporting-line change. Excess of income over expenses fell to $252 thousand, a near-breakeven year. Renew Sinai Phase 3A's remaining redevelopment receivable and long-term redevelopment obligation shrink sharply (redevelopment receivable Phase 3A: $18,165 thousand, down from $112,469 thousand the prior year; long-term redevelopment obligation: $5,339 thousand, down from $122,790 thousand), consistent with the project's June 2024 substantial completion (fs-2025, ca3cbfd3c8bc).

FY2026 (fs-2026, f691f9d006cc): Total income $975,756 thousand; income line renamed "Ministry of Health and Ontario Health" at $731,744 thousand (COVID-19 funding no longer appears as any distinct line anywhere in the statement); total expenses $1,004,209 thousand; a deficiency of income over expenses of $(28,453) thousand — the largest reported shortfall in this archived series. Expense-line categories are also restructured this year: "Salaries and wages" and "Employee benefits" are merged into a single "Salaries and benefits" line ($640,998 thousand), and "Drugs" and "Clinical supplies" are merged into "Drugs and clinical supplies" ($103,903 thousand) (fs-2026, f691f9d006cc).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

Sinai Health's Mount Sinai Hospital campus and Lunenfeld-Tanenbaum Research Institute are affiliated with the University of Toronto, and the Hospital's independent auditor (PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP) is based at "PwC Tower, 18 York Street, Suite 2500, Toronto, Ontario" from fs-2023 onward, with the auditor's report itself signed "Toronto, Ontario" in every year read (fs-2022 through fs-2026; 7d85f6412f07; 9aeae3d4a63f; e0f124c37cfc; ca3cbfd3c8bc; f691f9d006cc). System partner Circle of Care (CoC) is described in fs-2022 (7d85f6412f07) as "a community-based not-for-profit agency whose objectives include providing home care services to individuals across Metropolitan Toronto." Hennick Bridgepoint Hospital, the second amalgamated predecessor institution, operates its own capital-redevelopment obligation (originally the "Bridgepoint Redevelopment Project," later "HBH Redevelopment Project") under a Design-Build-Finance-Maintain agreement dating to a July 2009 Ministry approval (fs-2022, 7d85f6412f07; fs-2023, 9aeae3d4a63f). Unlike the AECL reference brief, this org's archived series is entirely Toronto-based by construction — every document describes a hospital corporation whose sites, funding relationship (Ontario Ministry of Health / Ontario Health), and governance are inherently provincial and municipal rather than federal.
