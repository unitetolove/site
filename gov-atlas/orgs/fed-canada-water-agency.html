Canada Water Agency - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canada-water-agency (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Agency is established by the Canada Water Agency Act, S.C. 2024, c. 15, s. 209, confirmed current to 2026-06-14 on Justice Laws, with section 3 establishing the Agency and last-amended date shown as 2024-10-15 (SI/2024-49) (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-11.001/). The Act states the Agency "is established for the purpose of assisting the Minister in exercising or performing the Minister's powers, duties and functions in relation to fresh water under any Act of Parliament" (Canada Water Agency Act, s. 3, Justice Laws, per duckduckgo.com search excerpt quoting the section; full text not independently re-fetched this review, so this quotation carries ⚠️ still being checked).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Per its 2026-27 Departmental Plan, the Agency's core responsibility is "Freshwater Stewardship": it "improves federal collaboration on fresh water in Canada and develops and coordinates whole-of-government approaches," working with provinces, territories, Indigenous peoples, local authorities and scientists to protect and restore freshwater ecosystems (canada.ca/en/canada-water-agency/corporate/transparency/priorities/departmental-plans/2026-2027.html, fetched via r.jina.ai; canada.ca/en/canada-water-agency.html, fetched via r.jina.ai). ON-TOR relevant given its Great Lakes/water-infrastructure mandate (registry row: fed-canada-water-agency, notes field).

Governance & reporting line

The Agency sits within the Environment and Climate Change Canada portfolio; its responsible minister is the Honourable Julie Dabrusin, Minister of the Environment, Climate Change and Nature, and Minister responsible for the Canada Water Agency, with Mark Fisher serving as President (canada.ca/en/canada-water-agency.html, fetched via r.jina.ai).

Budget scale

Total planned spending for 2026-27 is $81,026,925 ($69.1M for Freshwater Stewardship, $11.9M for Internal Services), supporting 217 planned full-time equivalents; the Agency is also implementing headcount reductions (approximately 13 FTEs by 2028-29) under the government's Comprehensive Expenditure Review (2026-27 Departmental Plan, canada.ca/en/canada-water-agency/corporate/transparency/priorities/departmental-plans/2026-2027.html, fetched via r.jina.ai).

Institutional history

The Agency launched October 15, 2024 as an interim body within Environment and Climate Change Canada and was formally established in statute the same year by the Canada Water Agency Act, S.C. 2024, c. 15, s. 209 (Justice Laws, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-11.001/; 2026-27 Departmental Plan, canada.ca).

Priority evolution

This section describes a founding snapshot, not an evolution — there is no prior document in the archive to compare it against.

The archived document is explicitly labelled the Agency's "inaugural Departmental Plan for 2025-2026" (fed-canada-water-agency-dp-2026, 9175ac6f1119); its own "Summary of changes to reporting framework since last year" states plainly: "The Canada Water Agency was established in October 2024, and as a result, there is no data available for last year" (9175ac6f1119).

The Plan sets out a single core responsibility, Freshwater Stewardship, under which the Agency "improves federal collaboration on fresh water in Canada and develops and coordinates whole-of-government approaches," "advances the protection and restoration of freshwater ecosystems, as informed by science and Indigenous knowledge," and "proactively collaborates on freshwater opportunities and challenges" (9175ac6f1119). This is delivered through five named workstreams: Service to Canadians, Policy Leadership, Cross-Country Collaboration, Water Literacy, and Foundational Science & Data (9175ac6f1119).

Two departmental results anchor the plan, each with dated targets: "Canada has healthy freshwater ecosystems" (targets: reduce phosphorus loads by 25 tonnes/year into Lake Winnipeg and 196 tonnes/year into Lake Erie by March 31, 2033; 100% of Freshwater Ecosystem Initiatives reporting good/fair, stable-or-improving ecosystem-health indicators by March 31, 2033) and "Partners and stakeholders are meaningfully engaged in freshwater stewardship" (target: 60% of partners/stakeholders reporting meaningful engagement, by March 31, 2028) (9175ac6f1119).

The flagship delivery mechanism named is the Freshwater Ecosystem Initiatives (FEIs), covering eight named water bodies — the Great Lakes, Lake Winnipeg, Lake of the Woods, St. Lawrence River, Wolastoq/Saint John River, Fraser River, Mackenzie River, and Lake Simcoe — said to cover roughly 90% of Canada's population and 40% of its surface area (9175ac6f1119). Community-level delivery also runs through EcoAction, with an existing national community-led restoration/protection stream and a new pilot stream (launched March 2025) for freshwater sustainability and innovation/clean-technology piloting (9175ac6f1119).

On policy leadership, the Plan names a review of the Canada Water Act — tied to measures in the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act Action Plan — as underway, with pre-engagement discussions begun with provincial/territorial governments, Indigenous peoples, and other federal departments (9175ac6f1119). It also commits to "anticipatory policy" work using futures/foresight methods on freshwater issues 3 to 100 years out, including collaboration with the Privy Council Office on behavioural science around drought preparedness (9175ac6f1119).

On whole-of-government coordination, the Plan describes the Agency chairing the Federal Freshwater Committee (senior management from 26 federal departments and agencies) and operating the Federal Freshwater Hub, an "experimental approach" embedding freshwater expertise from across government, with departments involved to date including Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, Environment and Climate Change Canada, Fisheries and Oceans Canada, Natural Resources Canada, Statistics Canada, and Indigenous Services Canada (9175ac6f1119). These bodies are named as supporting a National Freshwater Data Strategy (NFDS, led by the Agency) and a National Freshwater Science Agenda (NFSA, led by Environment and Climate Change Canada, with Agency support) (9175ac6f1119).

Internationally, the Plan states the Agency "leads the implementation of the Canada-United States Great Lakes Water Quality Agreement on behalf of the Government of Canada," participates in domestic boards (Prairie Provinces Water Board, Mackenzie River Basin Board) and international boards with the US (International Rainy-Lake of the Woods Watershed Board, Red River Watershed Board, Great Lakes Water Quality Board), and is "leading Canada's input to the 2026 United Nations Water Conference" (9175ac6f1119).

On stakeholder engagement, the Plan commits to finalizing and implementing a Stakeholder Relations Strategy in 2025-26, built around three core goals (raising awareness, informing strategic direction, collaborating on key issues) and four 2025-26 objectives (using engagement resources effectively, establishing a regional presence, leading sector-specific engagement, strengthening strategic partnerships) (9175ac6f1119). It also names the "President's Freshwater Tables" as an existing stakeholder-dialogue mechanism and the "Water One Window" (WOW) as its centralized public web presence for freshwater data, programs and services (9175ac6f1119).

On internal build-out — consistent with an agency in its first full year — the Plan commits to delivering the Agency's first human-resources plan and first financial-management framework, transitioning financial and HR systems from Environment and Climate Change Canada to independent Agency-run systems, and tabling its first Accessibility Plan in June 2026 (9175ac6f1119).

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • October 15, 2024 — Agency inception date, named repeatedly in the document as the point from which its first (partial) fiscal year and all forecasted comparatives run (9175ac6f1119).
  • 2024 — "Year of incorporation / commencement" per the Plan's corporate-information table; enabling instruments named as the Canada Water Agency Act and the Canada Water Act (9175ac6f1119).
  • 2024-25 (partial year, Oct 15, 2024 – Mar 31, 2025) — first forecast financial and FTE data: $48,011,234 total forecast expenditures (Freshwater Stewardship $41,518,001; Internal Services $6,493,233) and 112 total forecast FTEs (86 Freshwater Stewardship; 26 Internal Services) (9175ac6f1119).
  • 2025-26 — first full planned fiscal year: $84,831,831 total planned spending ($72,675,393 Freshwater Stewardship / 165 FTE; $12,156,438 Internal Services / 58 FTE); approved funding of $84,831,831 ($80,893,900 voted, $3,937,931 statutory) (9175ac6f1119).
  • March 2025 — EcoAction pilot stream (freshwater sustainability/innovation) launched (9175ac6f1119).
  • April 1, 2025 — start date for the Agency's work toward the mandatory minimum 5% Indigenous-procurement target, under Phase 3 (44 organizations scheduled to meet/exceed the target by FY2024-25) (9175ac6f1119).
  • 2025-26 → 2026-27 — planned spending and FTEs both step down (total spending $84.8M → $79.8M; total FTE 223 → 221), attributed to the sunset of funding for "some/certain engagement activities" and, on the HR side, to the sunset of freshwater-pillars contributions agreements netted against increased funding for the Fraser, Wolastoq/Saint John, Mackenzie River, and St. Lawrence River basins (9175ac6f1119).
  • June 2026 — first Accessibility Plan to be tabled (9175ac6f1119).
  • A named driver of forecast-to-planned expense and revenue swings between 2024-25 and 2025-26 is the "Randle Reef cost-shared remediation project" (location not stated in this document) (9175ac6f1119).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

The document grounds Ontario relevance directly: two of the eight named Freshwater Ecosystem Initiatives sit wholly or partly in Ontario — the Great Lakes and Lake Simcoe — and the Agency names the Canada-Ontario Agreement on Great Lakes Water Quality and Ecosystem Health as one of its formalized federal-provincial collaboration instruments (9175ac6f1119). The Agency also states it "leads the implementation of the Canada-United States Great Lakes Water Quality Agreement on behalf of the Government of Canada" and participates in the (Canada-US) Great Lakes Water Quality Board (9175ac6f1119). A "Randle Reef cost-shared remediation project" is named as a material driver of the Agency's forecast-to-planned revenue and expense changes between 2024-25 and 2025-26, though the document does not state the project's location (⚠️ still being checked) (9175ac6f1119). The document does not name any Toronto-specific site, office, or program; Ontario/Toronto relevance in this snapshot runs through the Great Lakes/Lake Simcoe basin work and the Canada-Ontario Agreement rather than a documented physical presence in the city.
