Bereavement Authority of Ontario - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-bereavement-authority-ontario (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Bereavement Authority of Ontario (BAO) administers provisions of the Funeral, Burial and Cremation Services Act, 2002 (FBCSA), S.O. 2002, c. 33 (confirmed via CanLII citation, canlii.org/en/on/laws/stat/so-2002-c-33, cross-checked against thebao.ca/legislation this review). "The Bereavement Authority of Ontario (BAO) is a government delegated authority and not-for-profit corporation administering provisions of the Funeral, Burial and Cremation Services Act, 2002 (FBCSA) on behalf of the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery," established as a Delegated Administrative Authority (DAA) on January 16, 2016 under the Safety and Consumer Statutes Administration Act, 1996 (thebao.ca "About the BAO" / "Who We Are," fetched via search and WebFetch this review). "The BAO is responsible for the protection of the public interest" and its mission is "To effectively regulate Ontario's bereavement services by: Using risk-based regulatory tools to promote compliance and inspire confidence in the sector; Using evidence to inform decision making; Remaining responsive to the changing needs of consumers and the sector" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The FBCSA "generally applies to many facets of the bereavement sector, including cemeteries, funeral establishments, transfer services, crematoriums, funeral directors, sales representatives and funeral preplanners" (thebao.ca, fetched via search this review). The BAO licenses establishments and individuals across these categories, maintains a public register, and issues compliance and disciplinary orders through its Registrar. The BAO reports it is "wholly funded by licensee fees (not tax dollars)" rather than general provincial tax revenue (same source).

Governance & reporting line

The BAO is "governed by an independent Board of Directors" (thebao.ca, fetched via search this review); the Registrar issues compliance notices and takes disciplinary actions against licensees. The organization administers the FBCSA on behalf of, and reports to, the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery as its delegating ministry (same source; registry row parent_org field is blank in this library's government-document registry, so no separate parent org row exists in the atlas for that ministry relationship). ⚠️ still being checked - exact Board of Directors member count (search results referencing "10-member Board of Directors" were not independently confirmed against a current BAO primary source this review).

Budget scale

For the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, the BAO reported total revenues of $9,586,296 (cemetery operator license fees $4,711,547; funeral operator license fees $3,727,270; funeral personal license fees $768,989; interest income $132,591; cemetery individual license fees $153,656; funeral exam fees $61,055) against total expenditures of $7,606,414 (salaries and wages $5,026,132; information technology $554,464; occupancy costs $324,135), for an excess of revenues over expenditures of $1,979,882. Total assets stood at $10,270,487 as at March 31, 2025 (BAO Audited Financial Statements FY2024-25, Statement of Operations and Statement of Financial Position, https://thebao.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025-03-31-Bereavement-Authority-of-Ontario-Financial-Statements-FINAL-Signed.pdf, fetched this review).

Institutional history

The BAO was established as a Delegated Administrative Authority on January 16, 2016 under the Safety and Consumer Statutes Administration Act, 1996, taking on administration of the FBCSA (thebao.ca, fetched via search this review). ⚠️ still being checked - the BAO's predecessor regulatory arrangement for Ontario's bereavement sector prior to the 2016 DAA transition (commonly understood to include a preceding Board of Funeral Services under earlier legislation) was not independently confirmed against a primary BAO source this review.

Bereavement Authority of Ontario - strategy evolution

2026-08-05 / registry: on-bereavement-authority-ontario / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: The biggest priority added across the readable 2017-2025 record is the modernization agenda triggered by the Auditor General of Ontario's December 2020 Value-for-Money Audit — 51 action items that drove a new 2022-2025 Strategic Plan, the Ontario Bereavement Information System (OBIS/CRM, live April 2024), the BAO's first non-inflation licensing-fee increase in roughly a decade (effective July 2023), and a near-doubling of inspections (160 → 250, +56.2%) by FY2024-25. The biggest quietly dropped element is the Bereavement Education Committee — the sector-run, OACFP/OFSA-administered continuing-education-approval body — which ar-2024 (cdf5a8d41f3f) states "disbanded at the end of 2022," with the BAO itself assuming direct course-approval authority from January 1, 2023; alongside it, the founding-era mission/vision/values language ("To protect consumers by regulating funeral, burial and cremation services") was fully rewritten around FY2022 into the current risk-based/evidence-informed framing tied to the new Strategic Plan. The most load-bearing number is FY2024-25 total revenue of $9,586,296 against net assets of $8,596,462 and total assets of $10,270,487 (fs-2025, 1b1c5a43e26f) — the highest revenue and net-asset figures in the readable record, more than double the founding-era FY2016-17 net assets of $3,787,946 (ar-2018, 587410051f74). One open question the record cannot resolve: the 2026/27 Business Plan (bp-2027, dcb07cda026c) previews a new three-pillar strategic framework (SP1 Consumer Protection & Engagement / SP2 Operational Excellence / SP3 Evidence-Based Regulation) ahead of the "broadest consultation" the Board announced for fall 2025 (ar-2025, fc9609b57f40) — no archived document confirms this framework was formally ratified as the successor to the 2022-2025 Strategic Plan, because the underlying new Strategic Plan document itself is not part of this archived set.

Priority evolution

FY2016-17 (ar-2017, 05d9d97e13c0), inaugural year: BAO assumed cemetery/crematorium licensing January 16, 2016 and full bereavement-sector responsibility (incl. funeral/transfer) April 1, 2016, under an 8-member interim Board chaired by Tom Wright, with CEO/Registrar Carey Smith. Founding mission: "to ensure consumer protection by regulating funeral, transfer, cemetery and crematorium services in accordance with the FBCSA." Early priorities: database unification (merging legacy Board of Funeral Services and Cemeteries Regulation Unit systems), a new online licence-renewal system, and establishing three statutory Advisory Committees (Funeral & Transfer Service; Cemetery, Crematorium & Municipal; Faith-based).

FY2017-18 (ar-2018, 587410051f74): First strategic-planning session (Oct 2017) produced the BAO's first three-year Strategic Plan. New Discipline and Appeal Committees regulation approved (spring 2018, not yet operational). Trust Unit recovered ~$1.6M in improperly-handled care-and-maintenance money. 140 inspections conducted (up from 42 in FY2016-17).

FY2018-19 (ar-2019, 671242d2bcd8): Board reaches 9 members. Mission/vision/values formalized in current-at-the-time form ("To protect consumers by regulating funeral, burial and cremation services"). Auditor General of Ontario begins reviewing several DAAs, including the BAO (January 2020, reported in retrospect in later documents). Two legacy database systems merged; alkaline-hydrolysis and other novel disposition technologies first identified as an emerging regulatory pressure requiring new guidance.

FY2019-20 (ar-2020, b1b8ceb49bc3): Board reaches full 10-member complement. Mission/vision expanded to explicitly cover "transfer, cremation and alternative disposition services." Alternative Disposition (Hydrolysis) appears for the first time as a distinct licence category (4 licensees). Fiscal year closes with the onset of COVID-19; the Expedited Death Response (EDR) protocol is developed jointly with the Office of the Chief Coroner for Ontario.

FY2020-21 (ar-2021, 9feb79df9c93): Full COVID-19 year — 10-person attendance caps, colour-coded provincial alignment chart, ~$3.9M MGCS grant funding a one-year licence-fee "holiday" for licensees, 15 CEO/Registrar webcasts. December 2020: the Auditor General of Ontario publishes her Value-for-Money Audit of the BAO — 20 recommendations, later operationalized as 51 action items — becoming the central inflection point for the next four fiscal years. One funeral home suspended two weeks (Feb 2021, GTA) for a 60-person visitation breach; described only in aggregate/institutional terms, no names given (source itself does not name the licensee).

FY2021-22 (ar-2022, 8a4c44f8b26c): Board approves the new 2022-2025 Strategic Plan; mission/vision/values fully rewritten (see Priorities added/dropped/renamed). "BAO Interim Business Plan" (2021/22-2022/23) bridges the old five-priority plan and the new four-pillar plan while implementing AG recommendations — 33 of 51 action items (65%) reported complete by fiscal yearend. Discipline and Appeal Committees become operational, with named committee chairs (officeholders: Paul Famula, Discipline; Marilyn Marshall, Appeal). Only two licensees suspended for COVID protocol breaches all pandemic-to-date (one business, one personal licence) — not named.

FY2022-23 (ar-2023, 351a5f330867): 2022-2025 Strategic Plan enters "full implementation mode." Interim CEO/Registrar Jim Cassimatis appointed (Dec 2022) pending a permanent search. Auditor General's Nov 2022 two-year Follow-Up report: 86% of action items implemented or in-process. Bereavement Education Committee (BEC) — the OACFP/OFSA-run continuing-education-approval body — disbands at year-end; BAO assumes direct CE-approval authority effective Jan 1, 2023. CRM (later branded OBIS) development begins in earnest; February 2023: BAO announces first non-inflation licensing-fee increase in roughly a decade, effective July 2023.

FY2023-24 (ar-2024, cdf5a8d41f3f): Jim Cassimatis confirmed as permanent CEO/Registrar (March 2024) after a national search of 70+ applicants. Fee increase takes effect (July 2023). AG action-item completion reported at 78.43% (40/51), rising to 90.19% counting items on-schedule. Governance review completed; eight Board policies updated. Three new directors join (Sept 2023). BAO begins a discussion of the Northwestern Ontario funeral-director shortage as a named policy concern. Ontario Bereavement Information System (OBIS) — replacing a ~20-year-old legacy CRM/licensee portal — approaches launch at fiscal yearend (dates given inconsistently within this document as "April 2" in the Chair's message and "April 30, 2024" in the IT Report; ar-2025 confirms "April 2024," see Residuals).

FY2024-25 (ar-2025, fc9609b57f40): OBIS live from April 2024, its first full year in production. Inspections nearly double (160 → 250, +56.2%) with four new inspectors (team of 8). Full completion of all applicable Auditor-General 2020 recommendations reported. New By-Law No. 3 receives Ministerial approval (Aug 2024), formally adding a defined "Consumer Representative Director" seat to the Board composition (previously an informal/unstated category). BAO Board begins a new Strategic Plan process, with the "broadest consultation it has ever conducted" scheduled for fall 2025 (i.e., after this document's coverage window). A handful of named-institutionally (never named-individual) enforcement actions are disclosed: a Scarborough funeral home licence non-renewal upheld by the Licence Appeal Tribunal; a Niagara-area cemetery operator ordered to stop new sales after commingling Care & Maintenance trust funds; and a fraud conviction of "a former Simcoe funeral establishment owner and director" (12 months house arrest, $386,000 restitution) — described institutionally per the private-individual rule, consistent with how the BAO's own report treats it.

FY2025-26 (bp-2026, 106504040e18) and FY2026-27 (bp-2027, dcb07cda026c), forward-looking business plans: bp-2026 (2025/26 Business Plan) introduces three parallel new-guideline workstreams not present in earlier documents: Natural Organic Reduction (human composting) licensing guidelines, Conditions-of-Licence guidelines, and an Administrative Monetary Penalty (AMP) framework recommendation to government. bp-2027 (2026/27 Business Plan, the most recent document in the archived set) previews a new three-pillar strategic framework — SP1 "Enhance Consumer Protection and Engagement," SP2 "Enable Sector Impact Through Operational Excellence," SP3 "Advance an Evidence-Based Regulatory Approach" — replacing the 2022-2025 plan's four pillars (Risk Based / Evidence Informed / Strong Sector / Forward Looking) ahead of the Board's planned fall-2025 strategic-plan consultation. bp-2027 also discloses a planned office move to 5160 Yonge St (new lease effective June 1, 2026) and a "Registrar's Practice Bulletin" launching in 2026.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • January 16, 2016 / April 1, 2016 — BAO established as a DAA in two stages (cemetery/crematorium, then full sector) under the Safety and Consumer Statutes Administration Act, 1996 (ar-2017, 05d9d97e13c0).
  • December 2020 — Auditor General of Ontario's Value-for-Money Audit of the BAO (part of a 5-DAA review) publishes 20 recommendations, later operationalized into 51 action items (36 BAO-specific, 11 shared with the ministry, 4 shared with other ministries/parties) — the single largest external driver of change across FY2021-FY2025 (ar-2021 through ar-2025).
  • FY2020-21 — ~$3,949,318 one-time MGCS grant funds a full-year licence-fee "holiday" for licensees during the pandemic (fs-2021, 72c83f4535b9).
  • January 1, 2022 — FBCSA regulatory changes: Care & Maintenance Fund minimum establishment deposit raised from $100,000 to $165,000; non-commercial cemetery operators permitted limited capital access under Registrar approval (ar-2022, 8a4c44f8b26c; ar-2023, 351a5f330867).
  • FY2021-22 and FY2022-23 — two consecutive deficit years: net (deficiency) of revenues over expenses of -$1,397,551 (FY2022, fs-2022 e9dc94accde3) and -$594,299 (FY2023, fs-2024 66441bd362b3, comparative column), the only two deficit years in the readable record, explicitly attributed to AG-recommendation implementation costs and CRM development — cash reserves were drawn down to cover the gap ahead of the FY2024 fee increase.
  • July 1, 2023 — First non-inflation licensing-fee increase in roughly a decade takes effect (described as "nearly 10 years" in ar-2023 and "more than 10 years" in ar-2024/ar-2025 — a minor internal wording inconsistency, ⚠️ still being checked exact prior increase date), restoring surpluses from FY2024 onward.

Ontario/Toronto relevance

The BAO's regulatory mandate is explicitly province-wide — it licenses and regulates funeral, cemetery, crematorium, transfer-service and (from FY2019-20) alkaline-hydrolysis operators and sales representatives "across Ontario," with no Toronto-specific carve-out anywhere in the nine annual reports or five plans read. The BAO's head office, however, is consistently disclosed as within the City of Toronto throughout the entire archived record: "100 Sheppard Avenue East, Suite 505" (later "Suite 500 and 505"), described interchangeably as "Toronto, Ontario M2N 6N5" in earlier documents (e.g. ar-2017, 05d9d97e13c0; fs-2018, c764da533df0) and "North York, ON M2N 6N5" in later ones (ar-2020 onward, e.g. fc9609b57f40) — North York has been part of the amalgamated City of Toronto since 1998, so both labels describe the same Toronto address. The current lease runs to May 31, 2026 (fs-2020 through fs-2025), and bp-2027 (dcb07cda026c) discloses a planned relocation, effective June 1, 2026, to 5160 Yonge St — also within North York/Toronto — meaning the BAO's head office has been, and per the most recent forward-looking document will remain, physically located within Toronto for the full readable record. This is office-location relevance only; the province-wide regulatory mandate itself is not Toronto-specific. A handful of Toronto-located outreach events appear incidentally (e.g. the November 2022 rededication ceremony at St. James' Cemetery, Toronto, following reconstruction of a berm holding 5,000 cremated remains, ar-2023 351a5f330867; the Zoomer Show trade-show venue, Toronto, multiple years) — these are general-audience events at a Toronto venue, not evidence of a Toronto-specific program.
