Public Sector Integrity Commissioner of Canada (Office of the) - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-public-sector-integrity-commissioner-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Office of the Public Sector Integrity Commissioner (PSIC) is established under the Public Servants Disclosure Protection Act, S.C. 2005, c. 46, consolidated at laws-lois.justice.gc.ca (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-31.9/, fetched and verified directly). Section 39 establishes the Office of the Public Sector Integrity Commissioner, with a Commissioner and supporting staff empowered to investigate wrongdoing disclosures, handle reprisal complaints, and conduct inquiries affecting the federal public service (same source). PSIC's own corporate page confirms it was "Established in 2007 under the Public Servants Disclosure Protection Act" (https://psic-ispc.gc.ca/en/corporate-information, fetched directly).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

PSIC investigates disclosures of wrongdoing and complaints of reprisal from federal public servants, including complex cases requiring specialized expertise; it renders quasi-judicial decisions in reprisal matters and reports founded cases of wrongdoing to Parliament, alongside recommendations to chief executives on corrective measures (2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://psic-ispc.gc.ca/en/resources/corporate-publications/2026-27/departmental-plan, fetched directly). Complementary to this, the Auditor General has a distinct statutory role investigating wrongdoing disclosures made about PSIC itself (https://www.canada.ca/en/auditor-general/transparency/departmental-plans/2026-27-raison-detre-mandate-role-who-we-are-what-we-do.html).

Governance & reporting line

PSIC "acts as an Agent of Parliament in the public interest to expose major wrongdoing in the federal public sector" (https://psic-ispc.gc.ca/en/corporate-information), placing it among the independent federal watchdog offices rather than under a portfolio minister's direction.

Budget scale

~$8.1 million total planned spending for 2026-27 (including internal services), with 45 planned full-time-equivalent staff (2026-27 Departmental Plan, https://psic-ispc.gc.ca/en/resources/corporate-publications/2026-27/departmental-plan, document id per doc-shelf: annual-report). The plan states existing reference levels are insufficient for the Office's caseload and that it is seeking additional funding, with spending having risen from $6.78M (2023-24 actual) to $8.19M (2025-26 forecast) (same source).

Institutional history

Established in 2007 under the Public Servants Disclosure Protection Act, S.C. 2005, c. 46 (https://psic-ispc.gc.ca/en/corporate-information; https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/p-31.9/); no subsequent renaming or reorganization identified this review ⚠️ still being checked.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • Submissions growth, multi-year, self-reported by the Office: 191 (2020-21) → 188 (2021-22) → 227 (2022-23) → 331 (2023-24) → 437 (2024-25) received; completions rose from 164 to 343 over the same span, described by the Commissioner as "a doubling... in comparison to the relatively stable levels leading to 2022-23" (f312b12a0319).
  • Investigations launched more than doubled from 2022-23 to 2024-25: 18 total (9 disclosure, 9 reprisal) in 2022-23 rising to 37 total (11 disclosure, 26 reprisal) in 2024-25, with reprisal-investigation launches specifically driving the 2024-25 increase (f312b12a0319).
  • General inquiries more than doubled: 220 (2021-22) to 502 (2024-25) (f312b12a0319).
  • Budget did not keep pace with caseload growth — the report states this explicitly and lists specific internal-mitigation steps taken in response (deferred essential non-staff costs, cut discretionary training for a full fiscal year, curtailed investigation travel outside the National Capital Region, eliminated non-mandated services); "regular cycle and off-cycle budget requests were submitted, resulting in some additional funding that fell far short of needs" (f312b12a0319).
  • Proposed budget-governance model change: submit PSIC's proposed budget directly to the Speakers of the Senate and House of Commons for scrutiny and transmission to the Treasury Board President without amendment, "to bring transparency, ensure parliamentary control and shield the process from the possible perception of executive influence" (f312b12a0319) — a mandate-adjacent structural proposal, not yet enacted per this document.
  • One founded case of wrongdoing resulted from the 450 disclosure files processed in 2024-25, with 4 Commissioner recommendations issued and 11 investigations launched (f312b12a0319).
