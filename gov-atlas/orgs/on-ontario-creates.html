Ontario Creates - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-Q10-WAVE1-d1 / 2026-08-04 / registry row: on-ontario-creates (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

Ontario Creates is "an agency of the Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming of the Province of Ontario created under Regulation 672/00 of the Development Corporations Act" R.S.O. 1990, c. D.10 (Ontario Creates Annual Report 2023-24, Notes to Financial Statements, note 1 "Background," pdftotext extract this review from https://www.ontariocreates.ca/uploads/About-Us/Eng/Annual-Report/OC_AR24_EN_V4d-forAODA.pdf). The same note states that the Corporation "operated under the legal name of The Ontario Media Development Corporation until June 6, 2024," with the name legally changed to "Ontario Creates" effective June 7, 2024 by an amendment to the Development Corporations Act (O. Reg. 232/24). ⚠️ Still being checked: some secondary/marketing pages reference an "Ontario Creates Act, 2019," but no such standalone founding statute could be located this review via e-Laws or web search; the org's own audited financial statements describe the legal basis as regulation-made under the Development Corporations Act, not a dedicated Act. On its own site, Ontario Creates describes itself as "a catalyst for economic development, investment and collaboration in Ontario's creative industries including the music, book, magazine, film, television and interactive digital media sectors" (https://www.ontariocreates.ca/about-us, fetched this review).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

Ontario Creates administers tax credits and funding programs across film, television, book, magazine, music, and interactive digital media, and operates the Ontario Film Commission function to attract and support production activity in the province, working closely with the Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming, Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development, and the federal Ministry of Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship (Ontario Creates Annual Report 2023-24, pdftotext extract this review).

Governance & reporting line

The agency is governed by a Board of Directors of up to 17 members appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council, with a chair and vice-chair designated from the membership, terms of up to three years, and a minimum of four board meetings per year (https://www.pas.gov.on.ca/Home/Agency/406, fetched this review). The Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming is "the principal source of agency revenue (86 per cent in the Business Plan)" (Ontario Creates Annual Report 2023-24, Financial Performance section, pdftotext extract this review).

Budget scale

For fiscal 2023-24 (year ended March 31, 2024), Ontario Creates reported total revenue of $39.001M against a Business Plan budget of $40.570M, total expenditures of $40.226M (below the $41.168M budget), and a net deficit of $(1.225)M, compared to a budgeted deficit of $(598)K. The deficit reflected "a decision to use unrestricted net assets strategically for certain time-limited initiatives" (Ontario Creates Annual Report 2023-24, Financial Performance table, https://www.ontariocreates.ca/uploads/About-Us/Eng/Annual-Report/OC_AR24_EN_V4d-forAODA.pdf, pdftotext extract this review).

Institutional history

The predecessor Ontario Film Development Corporation (OFDC) was created in January 1986 as a Schedule I Crown agency reporting through the Ministry of Citizenship and Culture, beginning operations April 1, 1986 (⚠️ still being checked - secondary source only, not confirmed via the org's own site this review: York University Clara Thomas Archives finding aid, https://atom.library.yorku.ca/index.php/ontario-media-development-corporation-ontario-film-development-corporation). It was renamed the Ontario Media Development Corporation (OMDC) by approximately 2000, publicly rebranded as "Ontario Creates" in September 2018 (Playback, https://playbackonline.ca/2018/09/07/omdc-rebrands-to-ontario-creates/), and its legal name was formally changed to Ontario Creates effective June 7, 2024 (Ontario Creates Annual Report 2023-24, Notes to Financial Statements, note 1, pdftotext extract this review).

Ontario Creates - strategy evolution

2026-08-05 / registry: on-ontario-creates / grounded in archived copies (cited document id + sha256) / read through our research file for that body

TL;DR: Across two decades the agency moved from a narrow film/TV tax-credit administrator (Ontario Film Development Corporation, 1986) to a six-sector "creative cluster" catalyst (books, magazines, film/TV, music, interactive digital media) with a formal five-year strategic plan (2017) and, eventually, a full rebrand from OMDC to Ontario Creates — publicly in September 2018, legally on June 7, 2024 (O. Reg. 232/24) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2024, sha 746ee6923fbb). The biggest quietly-dropped element is the Ontario Sound Recording Tax Credit, eliminated in the April 2015 budget alongside a narrowing of the Interactive Digital Media Tax Credit away from general "digital media" toward entertainment/children's-education products and outright rate cuts to two other credits — a rare contraction in an otherwise steadily-growing incentive regime (on-ontario-creates-ar-2016, sha fe065b39f1bc). The load-bearing number is FY2023-24: revenue $39.001M against a $40.570M budget, expenditures $40.226M against $41.168M, a net deficit of $(1.225)M versus a budgeted $(598)K deficit, with the Ministry supplying 86% of revenue (on-ontario-creates-ar-2024). The open question is what the actual 2023-2027 "Powering Ontario's Creative Economy" strategic plan contains in full — its own registry document is a stub (a homepage capture, not the plan text), though its vision/mission/goals were reconstructed from the two most recent annual reports.

Backgrounder summary

Ontario Creates is a Toronto-headquartered agency of the (as of 2024) Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming, created under Regulation 672/00 of the Development Corporations Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. D.10. It administers tax credits and funding across film, television, book publishing, magazine publishing, music, and interactive digital media, and runs the Ontario Film Commission. Its predecessor, the Ontario Film Development Corporation (OFDC), was founded January 1986 with a $5.4M budget and a film/TV-only mandate; it became the Ontario Media Development Corporation (OMDC) around 2000 when the five-sector "cultural media" mandate was added (on-ontario-creates-ar-2008, sha eb37ad060cf2). OMDC operated under that legal name until June 6, 2024, though it publicly rebranded as "Ontario Creates" in September 2018. Governed by a Board of up to 17 members appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council. Ministry funding is the principal revenue source (86% in the FY2023-24 Business Plan). FY2023-24: revenue $39.001M vs $40.570M budget; expenditures $40.226M vs $41.168M budget; net deficit $(1.225)M vs budgeted $(598)K.

Priority evolution

  • 1986-2000 — film/TV only. OFDC's sole job was tax credits and funding for film/TV production; $5.4M budget, 40 productions supported in year one (on-ontario-creates-ar-2008).
  • ~2000 — mandate quintupled. OMDC absorbed OFDC and added book publishing, magazine publishing, music, and interactive digital media, formalized as "six cultural media industries" (on-ontario-creates-ar-2005; on-ontario-creates-ar-2007).
  • 2004-05 — external scrutiny. A Ministry-led Sunset Review of all programs and an Auditor-General "Value for Money Audit" of tax credits both landed this year; OMDC's own account frames the year as existential ("the future of the Corporation cannot be determined at this time") pending review outcome (on-ontario-creates-ar-2005).
  • 2006 — "cluster" language begins. The Entertainment and Creative Cluster Partnerships Fund launched (3-year, $7.5M, later renewed at $12M/4yr in 2009-10) alongside a one-time $23M award — the first appearance of the "entertainment and creative cluster" framing that dominates all subsequent reports (on-ontario-creates-ar-2007, on-ontario-creates-ar-2010).
  • 2007-08 — long-term planning begins. OMDC engaged SECOR Consulting on its first multi-year strategic-planning exercise, culminating in a five-year plan set of goals adopted for 2010-11 onward (access to capital / digital evolution / content development-market access / collaboration / industry leadership) — repeated near-verbatim through ~2016 (on-ontario-creates-ar-2008 through on-ontario-creates-ar-2016).
  • 2009-2012 — Intellectual Property Development Fund, a pilot rebating 30% of early-stage screen-content development costs, launched with $10M in the 2009 budget and wound down as "concluded" after supporting 182 companies (leverage $4.98 per OMDC dollar) — a program that was launched, ran its course, and was not renewed (on-ontario-creates-ar-2010, on-ontario-creates-ar-2012).
  • 2013 — music consolidation. The Ontario Music Fund ($45M/3yr) replaced the separate OMDC Music Fund and Music Export Fund, unifying four streams (Company Development, Industry Development, Music Futures, Live Music) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2014).
  • 2015 — contraction and tightening (see Priorities dropped, below): rate cuts to two tax credits, elimination of a third, and narrower eligibility for a fourth.
  • 2017 — first modern Strategic Plan. OMDC's May 2017 five-year plan (2017-18 to 2021-22) introduced a formal vision/mission/strategic-goals/enablers/values architecture, replacing the looser "goals for the coming year" lists of prior reports; IDM Fund was expanded with Global Market Development and Marketing Support streams (on-ontario-creates-ar-2017).
  • 2018 — public rebrand. "Ontario Creates" debuted as the public brand at the September 2018 TIFF "Celebrate Ontario" event; legal name (OMDC) unchanged for another six years (on-ontario-creates-ar-2019).
  • 2020-22 — pandemic pivot. Programs shifted to remote/hybrid delivery, fast-tracked payments, added flexibility; new initiatives Ontario Green Screen (sustainability) and Ready to Roll (reopening guidance) launched; Ontario Music Fund renamed Ontario Music Investment Fund (on-ontario-creates-ar-2021).
  • 2022-23 — second modern Strategic Plan. "Powering Ontario's Creative Economy" (2023-2027), built from 700+ stakeholder consultations, replaced the 2017 plan with new goals (Build Capacity / Fuel Growth / Drive Investment / Develop Global Markets / Deliver Exceptional Customer Experiences / Empower High-Performing Teams) and a first-ever Land Acknowledgement and explicit DEI commitment section (on-ontario-creates-ar-2023).
  • 2023-24 — AI on the radar; legal rename completed. Senior leadership underwent AI training "to understand its potential and risks"; the corporate legal name changed to Ontario Creates effective June 7, 2024 (O. Reg. 232/24) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2024).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

Added - Book, magazine, music, and interactive-digital-media mandates (c. 2000), on top of film/TV (on-ontario-creates-ar-2008). - Entertainment and Creative Cluster Partnerships Fund (2006) — cross-sector collaboration funding, still running in some form through the 2010s (on-ontario-creates-ar-2007). - Intellectual Property Development Fund (2009, pilot; concluded 2012) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2010, on-ontario-creates-ar-2012). - Ontario Music Fund (2013), later Ontario Music Investment Fund (2020-21) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2014, on-ontario-creates-ar-2021). - IDM Fund's Global Market Development and Marketing Support streams (2017) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2017). - Ontario Green Screen sustainability program and Ready to Roll reopening resource (2020-21) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2021). - Explicit DEI/equity commitments and a formal Land Acknowledgement (first appearing 2022-23, retained through 2023-24) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2023, on-ontario-creates-ar-2024).

Dropped or narrowed - Ontario Sound Recording Tax Credit (OSRTC) — discontinued in the 2015 Ontario Budget. Companies could still claim it only for pre-April 23, 2015 recordings with expenditures incurred before May 1, 2016; by FY2021-22 the credit no longer even appears as its own line in the tax credit table (on-ontario-creates-ar-2019; on-ontario-creates-ar-2022). - Ontario Interactive Digital Media Tax Credit (OIDMTC) — eligibility narrowed, April 2015 budget. A "primary purpose" test refocused the credit on entertainment products and education for children under 12, explicitly excluding search engines, real estate databases, news/public-affairs products, and most websites; a "90% self-development" test was replaced by an 80/25 labour-cost rule. This reverses the earlier 2006-07 trend of broadening OIDMTC (rate raised 20%→30% that year) — a genuine mandate contraction after a decade of expansion (on-ontario-creates-ar-2016, on-ontario-creates-ar-2017). - Ontario Production Services Tax Credit (OPSTC) rate cut 25%→21.5%, and Ontario Computer Animation and Special Effects Tax Credit (OCASE) rate cut 20%→18%, both effective April 23, 2015 — the only rate reductions found across the whole 20-year tax-credit record, cushioned by a one-time "2015 Ontario Production Services and Computer Animation and Special Effects Transitional Fund" grant (on-ontario-creates-ar-2016). - Intellectual Property Development Fund — launched as a pilot (2009) and not continued past its 2012 conclusion despite reported success, unlike every other fund in this period which persisted or was folded into a successor (on-ontario-creates-ar-2012).

Renamed - Organization: Ontario Film Development Corporation (1986) → Ontario Media Development Corporation (~2000) → "Ontario Creates" brand (Sept. 2018) → "Ontario Creates" legal name (June 7, 2024, O. Reg. 232/24) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2008; on-ontario-creates-ar-2019; on-ontario-creates-ar-2024). - Music program: OMDC Music Fund + Music Export Fund → Ontario Music Fund (2013) → Ontario Music Investment Fund (2020-21). - Parent ministry (five distinct names in the readable record): Ministry of Culture (2005) → Ministry of Tourism and Culture (~2010) → Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Sport (~2012) → Ministry of Heritage, Sport, Tourism and Culture Industries (~2018-2021) → Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Sport again (~2022) → Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming (2023-24) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2005 through on-ontario-creates-ar-2024). ⚠️ Still being checked: the addition of "Gaming" to the ministry's portfolio by 2023-24 is stated plainly in the AR24 masthead and financial-statement background note but this brief cannot independently confirm whether that reflects a formal machinery-of-government reorganization or a title-only change from this document set alone.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • FY2004-05: Ministry of Culture funding $6.186M; deficiency of revenue over expenses $(4.146)M — the agency's mandate was under formal review that year (on-ontario-creates-ar-2005).
  • March 2006: one-time $23M award plus $7.5M/3yr Partnerships Fund launch (on-ontario-creates-ar-2007).
  • 2009 budget (mid-financial-crisis): additional $20M for 2009-10, $10M IP Fund pilot, ~$100M in additional tax relief, $12M/4yr Partnerships Fund extension — a counter-cyclical funding increase during the global recession (on-ontario-creates-ar-2010).
  • Early 2010: government moved OMDC onto long-term stable base funding — $14.95M base grant for 2010-11 rising to $22.95M for subsequent years — ending a cycle of one-time/special allocations that had defined the prior decade (on-ontario-creates-ar-2010).
  • April 2015 budget: the tax-credit contraction described above (OSRTC ended; OPSTC/OCASE rate cuts; OIDMTC narrowed) — total estimated tax credit value fell from $577.6M (FY2014-15) to $351.6M (FY2015-16) even as total project value kept climbing ($6.14B→$6.36B), showing the cut hit credit value more than underlying production volume (on-ontario-creates-ar-2015, on-ontario-creates-ar-2016).
  • Tax credit value trend overall: $138.3M (FY2004-05) → $110.2M (FY2005-06) → $192.0M (FY2006-07) → $181.0M (FY2007-08) → $207.8M (FY2008-09) → $267.7M (FY2009-10) → $272.3M (FY2010-11) → $270.7M (FY2011-12) → $427.6M (FY2012-13) → $518.4M (FY2013-14) → $577.6M (FY2014-15) → $351.6M (FY2015-16, post-cut) → $509.3M (FY2016-17) → $841.4M (FY2018-19, no FY2017-18 report exists) → $621.1M (FY2019-20) → $689.6M (FY2020-21, COVID) → $723.4M (FY2021-22) → $605.5M (FY2022-23) → $712.3M (FY2023-24) — roughly a 5x nominal increase over the full window despite the 2015 dip (figures compiled from the annual "Tax Credit Statistics" tables in each cited AR).
  • November 2022 / 2022 Budget: OPSTC expanded to cover on-location filming-property fees; OBPTC's 500-copy print-run requirement dropped for post-2019 e-books/audiobooks; proposals floated (not all finalized within the read window) to extend film/TV credits to online-only productions, require on-screen acknowledgement of Ontario tax-credit support, "untether" OCASE from OFTTC/OPSTC, and review the OFTTC's 10% non-GTA regional bonus (on-ontario-creates-ar-2023).
  • 2023 Hollywood labour disruption: the WGA strike (starting ~May 2023) and the SAG-AFTRA strike (ending November 8, 2023) are cited as the primary cause of a production-value decline in FY2023-24 despite Ontario "successfully retain[ing] its market share" — the only externally-driven industry downturn narrated in the corpus besides COVID-19 (on-ontario-creates-ar-2024).
  • FY2023-24 close: revenue $39.001M/$40.570M budget; expenditures $40.226M/$41.168M budget; net deficit $(1.225)M/$(598)K budget; Ministry = 86% of revenue (on-ontario-creates-ar-2024) — consistent with the backgrounder's cited figures.
  • Governance: Chair Kevin Shea served roughly 2006-2015 ("nine years," per his successor's tribute); Mark Sakamoto served as Chair 2015-2019; Aaron Campbell has chaired since February 2019 through the end of the read window (on-ontario-creates-ar-2016; on-ontario-creates-ar-2019; on-ontario-creates-ar-2024). CEO Karen Thorne-Stone has led the agency continuously from July 2007 through FY2023-24, an unusually long single-executive tenure spanning the entire rebrand and two strategic-plan cycles (on-ontario-creates-ar-2008 through on-ontario-creates-ar-2024).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

This is a provincial (not municipal) agency, but its footprint on Toronto is direct and structural, not incidental boilerplate: - Head office: 175 Bloor Street East, South Tower, Suite 501, Toronto, Ontario M4W 3R8 — unchanged across every annual report in the series, 2005 through 2024 (on-ontario-creates-ar-2005 through on-ontario-creates-ar-2024). - Toronto/Ontario Film Office, Los Angeles: jointly funded and operated with the City of Toronto since at least the mid-2000s, providing on-the-ground Hollywood marketing to attract production to Ontario; every annual report attributes a measurable slice of secured production directly to this joint office (e.g., $210M in 2010, $969M in leads realized in 2020-21) (on-ontario-creates-ar-2006; on-ontario-creates-ar-2011; on-ontario-creates-ar-2021). - Toronto International Film Festival (TIFF): a recurring, named line item in the audited financial statements every year ("Toronto International Film Festival Group grants," ~$1.2-1.4M annually) — one of the very few named grantees to appear in the Statement of Operations itself rather than a program-recipient appendix (on-ontario-creates-ar-2007 through on-ontario-creates-ar-2024). "Celebrate Ontario," the agency's flagship annual industry event, is consistently staged at Toronto venues (Design Exchange, The Carlu, Enwave Theatre at Harbourfront). - Regional-bonus tax design implies Toronto/GTA is the production default: the Ontario Film and Television Tax Credit carries a 10% regional bonus specifically for productions filmed outside the Greater Toronto Area — a design that only makes sense if GTA production is the unmarked, dominant baseline the agency is trying to counterbalance (on-ontario-creates-ar-2023). - Trillium Book Award ceremonies and readings are consistently Toronto-based (St. Lawrence Hall, Hart House, Harbourfront Centre, Park Hyatt). - ⚠️ Still being checked: none of the read documents give a GTA-specific production-value breakout (production stats are province-wide only), so the degree of Toronto concentration within Ontario's totals cannot be quantified from this corpus alone — only inferred from the regional-bonus policy design and the office/venue pattern above.
