Superintendent of Financial Institutions Canada (Office of the) - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-superintendent-financial-institutions-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

OSFI is established under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 18 (3rd Supp.), Part I (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/o-2.7/page-1.html, fetched via r.jina.ai after direct laws-lois fetch timed out; consolidated version current to 2026-06-14). Section 3.1: "The purpose of this Act is to ensure that financial institutions and pension plans are regulated by an office of the Government of Canada so as to contribute to public confidence in the Canadian financial system." Section 4 establishes the Office, presided over by the Minister of Finance, to supervise financial institutions' soundness and legal compliance and pension plans' funding/regulatory compliance, protecting depositors, policyholders and plan members (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

OSFI regulates and supervises more than 400 federally regulated financial institutions and roughly 1,200 pension plans as "an independent agency of the Government of Canada" (https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en, fetched via r.jina.ai; registry row fed-superintendent-financial-institutions-canada). Its statutory functions span prudential regulation/supervision of banks, insurers and trust companies, actuarial services for federal pension and benefit plans, and pension-plan oversight under the Act (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/o-2.7/page-1.html, fetched via r.jina.ai).

Governance & reporting line

The Office is "presided over" by the Minister of Finance under the Act, with a Superintendent of Financial Institutions appointed by the Governor in Council as deputy head for a seven-year term (removable for cause, reappointable, with temporary-replacement provisions for absence/vacancy up to six months) (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions Act s. 5, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/o-2.7/page-1.html, fetched via r.jina.ai). ⚠️ still being checked - the name of the current Superintendent was not confirmed this review (osfi-bsif.gc.ca leadership pages did not load).

Budget scale

~$351.3M total planned spending for 2025-26 (Regulation/Supervision $195.8M, Actuarial Services $14.0M, Internal Services $141.6M), 1,303 planned FTEs; more than 99% cost-recovered via industry assessments ($349.99M statutory/cost-recovered funding vs. $1.32M voted parliamentary appropriation for public-sector actuarial services) (OSFI 2025-26 Departmental Plan, https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/about-osfi/reports-publications/2025-26-Departmental-Plan, fetched via r.jina.ai).

Institutional history

Established under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 18 (3rd Supp.) (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/o-2.7/page-1.html, fetched via r.jina.ai); ⚠️ still being checked - predecessor institutions (the separate offices of the Inspector General of Banks and the Department of Insurance, merged to form OSFI in 1987) not independently confirmed against a primary source this review.

Backgrounder summary

OSFI is established under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 18 (3rd Supp.), with the purpose (s. 3.1) of ensuring financial institutions and pension plans are regulated by a Government of Canada office "so as to contribute to public confidence in the Canadian financial system." The Office is presided over by the Minister of Finance (s. 4), with a Superintendent appointed by the Governor in Council for a seven-year term (s. 5). OSFI regulates and supervises "more than 400 federally regulated financial institutions and roughly 1,200 pension plans." Budget scale per the backgrounder: ~$351.3M total planned spending for 2025-26 (Regulation/Supervision $195.8M, Actuarial Services $14.0M, Internal Services $141.6M), 1,303 planned FTEs, more than 99% cost-recovered ($349.99M statutory vs. $1.32M voted appropriation). The backgrounder flagged two ⚠️ still being checked items: the name of the current Superintendent, and the 1987 predecessor-institution merger story. Both are resolved by the archived series itself (see Priority evolution and Budget & mandate inflection points below): the archived documents independently confirm Peter Routledge as Superintendent from June 2021 through the most recent archived Departmental Plan (dp-2026, dated June 17, 2025), and confirm OSFI "was established on July 2, 1987... through the merger of its two predecessor agencies – the Department of Insurance and the Office of the Inspector General of Banks" (ar-2021, 95b5d7e8f63b; ar-2022, 33c779430da3; ar-2023, a2b0da554825; ar-2024, e941ee9997cc). The backgrounder's budget figures ($351.3M total; $195.8M / $14.0M / $141.6M by core responsibility; 1,303 FTEs; $349.99M statutory vs. $1.32M voted) are likewise an exact match to dp-2026's own tables (93ae4e637109) — no year-mismatch discrepancy of the kind found in other org briefs in this series.

Priority evolution

FY2020-21, under outgoing Superintendent Jeremy Rudin (ar-2021, 95b5d7e8f63b): OSFI reports progress against the 2019-2022 Strategic Plan (released March 2019), organized around four goals: (1) FRFI/FRPP preparedness and resilience to financial risk, in normal conditions and in the next stress event; (2) FRFI/FRPP resilience to non-financial risks before they harm financial condition; (3) improved agility and operational effectiveness; (4) preserving Canadians' and industry support/cooperation. Each goal carries named sub-priorities — e.g., Goal 1 includes "apply a more risk-based and principles-based approach to regulation and supervision," Goal 2 includes "continue to develop our regulatory and supervisory approaches to technology risks, including digitization, cloud computing, modelling and cyber security," Goal 3 includes "better leverage our data assets by improving data management and analytics." Concrete FY2020-21 deliverables cited include a January 2021 discussion paper on prudential considerations regarding climate change, a September 2020 technology risk discussion paper, and continued work of a "Culture External Advisory Committee." Total costs were $201.3M (up $11.5M/6.1%); FTEs reached 867 (up 9.3%), explicitly tied to Strategic Plan implementation. As of March 31, 2021, OSFI employed approximately 860 people across offices in Ottawa, Montréal, Toronto and Vancouver.

June 2021 — leadership transition (ar-2021, 95b5d7e8f63b; ar-2022, 33c779430da3): Peter Routledge is appointed Superintendent, succeeding Jeremy Rudin, who is thanked in ar-2022 "for leading our organization with integrity and distinction over the past seven years."

FY2021-22 — close of the 2019-22 Strategic Plan and the pivot to transformation (ar-2022, 33c779430da3): The report frames itself as the completion of the 2019-2022 Strategic Plan while pivoting focus toward "the risks on OSFI's horizon" — the digitalization of money, intensifying climate-related prudential risk, and pandemic aftermath. In May 2021, a Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion Unit is created. On December 20, 2021, OSFI releases "A Blueprint for OSFI's Transformation 2022-25," built on three foundational elements: refocusing mandate delivery toward public-confidence contribution, expanding/fortifying risk management capabilities and risk appetite, and enhancing culture "to thrive in increasing uncertainty." In March 2022, OSFI announces organizational-structure changes to align with the new strategy, quoting Superintendent Routledge: "OSFI has an impressive record of accomplishment in helping to keep Canada's financial system resilient. To sustain that record of service, we must be prepared and well positioned to not only react, but also adapt urgently to an [intensifying] risk environment." Total costs $212.5M (up 5.6%); FTEs 920 (up 6.1%).

April 22, 2022 — the 2022-2025 Strategic Plan (ar-2022, 33c779430da3; ar-2023, a2b0da554825; dp-2023, 9b45979d92ad): Building on the Blueprint, the plan sets "six priority initiatives" — Culture; Risk Strategy and Governance; Strategic Stakeholder and Partner Engagement; Policy Innovation; Supervisory Framework; and Data Management and Analytics. ar-2023 records that implementing these initiatives was projected to grow OSFI's budget from $212.5M (year ended March 31, 2022) to approximately $290M by March 31, 2025 — a projected 31.8% three-year growth. dp-2023 (FY2022-23 planning) describes an ambition for "OSFI to become a global leader in prudential supervision," including an "expanded and dedicated team for climate risk that builds out the work of OSFI's climate risk working group."

FY2022-23 (ar-2023, a2b0da554825; dp-2023, 9b45979d92ad): The first full year of Blueprint/Strategic-Plan implementation. Total costs $242.8M (up 14.3%); FTEs 1,024 (up 11.3%). A "Culture, Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion unit" is established as part of a three-year plan; OSFI publicly defines its risk appetite, supported by a new enterprise risk management (ERM) function.

June 22, 2023 — Bill C-47 expands OSFI's mandate (ar-2024, e941ee9997cc; dp-2025, 5b8874b19dd0; dp-2026, 93ae4e637109): Parliament passes the Budget Implementation Act (Bill C-47), stemming from Budget 2023, adding integrity-and-security duties to OSFI's mandate. Effective January 1, 2024, federally regulated institutions must have and adhere to "adequate policies and procedures to protect themselves from threats to their integrity and security, including foreign interference," which OSFI must examine and report on annually to the Minister of Finance. A new National Security Sector (NSS) and Integrity and Security Risk Division (ISRD) are established within OSFI in 2023-24 (dp-2026, 93ae4e637109) — the NSS providing intelligence/advice on threats, the ISRD embedding integrity-and-security expectations into supervisory work.

FY2023-24 (ar-2024, e941ee9997cc): OSFI states its 2022-25 Blueprint and Strategic Plan "was fulfilled a year earlier than expected." An enterprise risk management vision, mandate, and risk-appetite statement are articulated; a 2024-27 Human Capital Strategy is developed from internal consultation. On March 26-27, 2024, OSFI hosts the first International Financial Regulators Chief Risk Officer Roundtable in Toronto. Total costs $311.7M (up 28.4%, "largely due to... expanded mandate"); FTEs 1,315 (up 28.4%).

April 1, 2024 — the 2024-27 Strategic Plan (ar-2024, e941ee9997cc): A new set of six strategic priorities takes effect: Expanded mandate – Integrity and security regime; Supervisory renewal; Culture initiatives; Data management and analytics; Critical functions; and Operational resilience. The report states plainly: "Our organization has evolved over the past few years in so many ways – in capacity, mandate, and strategic priorities, supporting the need for a new forward-looking plan."

FY2024-25 (dp-2025, 5b8874b19dd0; ar-2025, d3e6a453bed0 — partial): dp-2025 describes standing up the NSS/ISRD machinery in detail — new National Security unit, new integrity-and-security guidance/frameworks, new supervisory expectations for non-financial risk, and processes to apply "expanded intervention responsibilities." A new Supervisory Framework takes effect April 1, 2024. The "Quality of Life Framework for Canada" linkage ("Good Governance" domain / "Confidence in institutions" indicator) appears for the first time in this cycle. Planned spending $321,937,861; planned FTEs 1,298. ar-2025's only available content — a landing page, not the full report — carries a Superintendent's letter (dated October 7, 2025) describing a "rapidly evolving global risk landscape," strengthened "operational resilience," and deepening relationships "with government and industry partners to address emerging national security issues... including state-sponsored cyberattacks or foreign interference."

FY2025-26 (dp-2026, 93ae4e637109): The listed minister changes from the Honourable Chrystia Freeland (Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Finance, through dp-2025) to the Honourable François-Philippe Champagne (Minister of Finance and National Revenue) — a federal department rename, not an OSFI-specific mandate change, mirroring the same-cycle rename seen elsewhere in this brief series. Continued focus on "Delivering on Bill C-47" and the integrity/security mandate; two temporary resource increases are named — bolstering cyber infrastructure/defense/response, and modestly reinforcing supervisory capacity — alongside continued cost discipline (a further $6.3M/$8.8M reduction from "refocusing government spending," per Budget 2023). Planned spending $351,314,899 ($349,991,000 statutory + $1,323,899 voted); planned FTEs 1,303. A footnote to the FTE table states the FTE-calculation methodology was revised in this plan "to eliminate past vacancy assumptions," meaning FTE figures before and after this document are not strictly comparable on a like-for-like basis.
