Canada Employment Insurance Commission - backgrounder

Backgrounder / GOV-STRAT-EVOL-d1 Wave 1 / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-canada-employment-insurance-commission (research/atlas/government_orgs.csv) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Commission is continued under Part 3, section 20, of the Department of Employment and Social Development Act, S.C. 2005, c. 34, confirmed current to 2026-06-14 on Justice Laws (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/H-5.7/). It also administers functions set out in the Employment Insurance Act, S.C. 1996, c. 23, likewise confirmed current to 2026-06-14 (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-5.6/). Section 20 states the Commission "is continued. It consists of four commissioners" (Department of Employment and Social Development Act, s. 20, Justice Laws).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

The Commission is responsible for annually monitoring and assessing the EI program, including evaluating the impact and effectiveness of employment benefits, and for EI policy review, financial transparency and premium rate-setting, and oversight of the EI appeals process (canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/corporate/portfolio/ei-commission.html, fetched via r.jina.ai). It operates as a tripartite body with representation from government, business and labour, and canada.ca states the Commission "has existed for more than 75 years" in some institutional form (same source). CEIC has no dedicated corporate site of its own — it is hosted on canada.ca and, per this registry's tracking, publishes an annual EI Monitoring and Assessment Report but no forward-looking corporate/strategic plan (registry row: fed-canada-employment-insurance-commission, notes field).

Governance & reporting line

The four commissioners are the Deputy Minister of Employment and Social Development (Chairperson), an Associate Deputy Minister of Employment and Social Development (Vice-Chairperson), a worker-representative commissioner, and an employer-representative commissioner, with the latter two appointed by the Governor in Council after consultation with worker and employer organizations respectively (Department of Employment and Social Development Act, s. 20, Justice Laws). The two government seats tie its governance directly to Employment and Social Development Canada's deputy-ministerial leadership (same source).

Institutional history

The Commission traces its institutional lineage through more than 75 years of unemployment/employment insurance administration (canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/corporate/portfolio/ei-commission.html) and is continued in its present four-member form under Part 3 of the Department of Employment and Social Development Act, S.C. 2005, c. 34, s. 20 (Justice Laws, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/H-5.7/).

Backgrounder summary

The Canada Employment Insurance Commission (CEIC) is continued under Part 3, section 20, of the Department of Employment and Social Development Act, S.C. 2005, c. 34, and administers functions under the Employment Insurance Act, S.C. 1996, c. 23 (both confirmed current to 2026-06-14 on Justice Laws). Section 20 states the Commission "is continued. It consists of four commissioners." It is a tripartite body — the Deputy Minister of Employment and Social Development (Chairperson), an Associate Deputy Minister (Vice-Chairperson), a worker-representative commissioner, and an employer-representative commissioner — responsible for annually monitoring and assessing the EI program, EI policy review, financial transparency and premium rate-setting, and oversight of the EI appeals process. CEIC has no dedicated corporate site (hosted on canada.ca) and, per this registry's tracking, publishes an annual EI Monitoring and Assessment Report but no forward-looking corporate/strategic plan. Canada.ca states the Commission "has existed for more than 75 years" in some institutional form. Budget scale is flagged ⚠️ VERIFY in the backgrounder: no standalone CEIC operating-budget figure separate from ESDC/EI program administration was located.

FY2016-17 / ar-2017 (fbb96eb4a202), transmittal letter dated March 2018: the 21st annual EI Monitoring and Assessment Report. Chairperson Louise Levonian, Commissioner for Employers Judith Andrew, and Commissioner for Workers Pierre Laliberté transmit the report to the Hon. Jean-Yves Duclos, Minister of Families, Children and Social Development, from an Employment and Social Development Canada address in Gatineau, QC. Stated focus: "the responsiveness of the EI program in a period marked by improving economic and labour market conditions at the national level but characterized by regional disparities as the downturn in global commodity prices continued to weigh on commodity-based regions."

FY2017-18 / ar-2018 (5c4214a0ca0a), transmittal letter dated March 2019: the 22nd annual report. New Chairperson Graham Flack replaces Levonian; Andrew and Laliberté continue as Employer/Worker commissioners. Same Minister addressed (Duclos, Families, Children and Social Development), now at a House of Commons, Ottawa ON address. Stated focus shifts to "a period characterized by strong economic growth and a relatively low national unemployment rate" — the opposite economic framing from the prior year's letter.

FY2018-19 through FY2023-24 / ar-2019 through ar-2024 (f33abeb8189b, b7437c83be9f, 2e3eb412b6b1, ea7876cc8242, 1e2b837bd7b9, 75d79471e28b): for these six consecutive report years, only the report's landing page / table of contents was captured — no transmittal letter, no chapter body, no commissioner names, no data. All six retain the same skeleton: Chapter 1 (labour market context), Chapter 2 (EI Part I income benefits), Chapter 3 (EI Part II Employment Benefits and Support Measures), Chapter 4 (program administration), plus seven annexes — indicating structural continuity of the report format across the period. No priority-level or program-level substantive claim can be grounded for these six years from what was actually read; only minor wording drift in chapter titles is visible (see Priorities added, dropped, renamed).

FY2024-25 / ar-2025 (5875eba16ea7), "Introduction" page: the only archived document in the series with substantive content beyond a transmittal letter or table of contents. It restates CEIC's "legislated mandate to monitor and assess the EI program," its four-member/three-voting-member composition with the ESDC Deputy Minister as Chairperson and Senior Associate Deputy Minister as Vice-Chairperson (voting only when acting for the Chair), and states that "since 2017, the CEIC has been responsible for setting EI premium rates using a 7-year-break-even mechanism." It documents several concrete, dated program changes in force or piloted this fiscal year: Budget 2024's extension of the temporary seasonal EI measure to October 2026 (up to 5 additional weeks of regular benefits, 45-week maximum, across 13 EI regions); a June 2024 legislative change creating a new EI adoption benefit (up to 15 shareable weeks, plus a Canada Labour Code job-protected leave), not yet in force pending a Governor-in-Council order; a February 2025 pilot project giving a one-time 300-insurable-hour credit to EI claimants displaced by 2024 wildfire evacuations in Jasper, Alberta, and Bunibonibee Cree Nation, Manitoba (claims established July 21, 2024 to July 19, 2025); and a March 2025 pilot project responding to tariff-driven economic disruption — waiving the one-week waiting period, suspending standard treatment of severance pay, adding 20 weeks of benefits for long-tenured workers, and adjusting regional unemployment rates upward by 1 percentage point (capped at 13.1%, floored at 7.1%) for claims beginning April 6 to October 11, 2025 — alongside expanded Work-Sharing provisions (broadened eligibility, maximum duration extended from 38 to 76 weeks, cooling-off period waived).
