Toronto Atmospheric Fund - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: tor-toronto-atmospheric-fund (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

The Toronto Atmospheric Fund (TAF, branded "The Atmospheric Fund") is a non-share-capital corporation continued by private provincial legislation, the Toronto Atmospheric Fund Act, 2005 (Bill Pr15, S.O. 2004, c. Pr6; Royal Assent text verified at https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-38/session-1/bill-pr15, via the Legislative Assembly of Ontario's bill page since this private act is not carried on the consolidated e-Laws statute index). Section 2(1) of the Act states: "The Toronto Atmospheric Fund is continued as a corporation without share capital under the same name and is composed of the members of its board of directors" (same source).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

TAF funds and delivers climate and clean-air programming, originally for the City of Toronto and, following a 2016 provincial endowment, expanded to the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area (registry row tor-toronto-atmospheric-fund; TAF Act 2005, s.2-3). Its object, per the Act's preamble, is to "continue the Fund" following the 2005 restructuring that also split off the former Foundation as the separate Clean Air Partnership (https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-38/session-1/bill-pr15).

Governance & reporting line

Under s.3(1) of the TAF Act, 2005, Toronto City Council appoints the members of TAF's board of directors, who serve at City Council's pleasure, and Council sets policies governing the board's structure and procedures (https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-38/session-1/bill-pr15). TAF is listed by the City of Toronto as one of its agencies and corporations (https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/accountability-operations-customer-service/city-administration/city-managers-office/agencies-corporations/agencies/toronto-atmospheric-fund/; registry row tor-toronto-atmospheric-fund).

Budget scale

TAF's consolidated total assets were $103,417,938 at December 31, 2024, up from $97,812,440 at December 31, 2023 (Toronto Atmospheric Fund Consolidated Financial Statements, December 31, 2024, Statement of Financial Position, https://taf.ca/custom/uploads/2025/05/Toronto-Atmospheric-Fund-2024-FS.pdf). Combined fund balances (Toronto Fund, Ontario Fund and Canada/Endowment Fund) were approximately $37.2 million at that date, per the same audited statements' Statement of Changes in Fund Balances.

Institutional history

Toronto City Council established the Toronto Atmospheric Fund in 1991, endowing it with roughly $23 million from the sale of surplus City property, and it was continued and restructured (with its former Foundation renamed the Clean Air Partnership) under the Toronto Atmospheric Fund Act, 2005; a $17 million Ontario endowment in 2016 and a $40 million federal endowment in 2020 expanded its mandate regionally (https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-38/session-1/bill-pr15; registry row tor-toronto-atmospheric-fund).

TAF is a non-share-capital corporation continued under the Toronto Atmospheric Fund Act, 2005, funding climate/clean-air programming originally for Toronto and, since a 2016 provincial endowment, the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area (GTHA). City Council appoints its board. Total assets reached ~$103.4M at Dec 31, 2024. The backgrounder dates TAF's 1991 founding to a ~$23M endowment from surplus City property sale.

Priority evolution

2017-2019 statements frame TAF's aim as positioning "the City of Toronto to achieve reductions in air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions, and ... also to position the GTHA to achieve significant reductions in greenhouse gas emissions" (fs-2017, 136e86604f9e, Note 1). From FY2020 onward this two-tier city/GTHA framing is replaced by a single unified sentence — "to innovate, demonstrate, de-risk and advance social, financial, policy and technological opportunities to achieve reductions in air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions" — unchanged through FY2025 (fs-2020, dac4769d735a, Note 1; fs-2025, 9b5903f8773c, Note 1).

The budget's program-delivery split ("Strategic programs" vs. "New and committed grants") is stable across all nine years, but its scale grew roughly 3x: total annual budget rose from $3,708K (FY2017, 136e86604f9e) to $12,224K (FY2025, 9b5903f8773c); the "Strategic programs" line alone grew from $1,814K to $8,984K over the same span (same cites).

Within direct investments, TAF's flagship Energy Savings Performance Agreement (ESPA) retrofit-financing product peaked at 9 active projects (fs-2018, 90e2b94aa776, Note 7; also fs-2019, fs-2020) and declined to 4 by FY2025 (fs-2025, 9b5903f8773c, Note 6). Over the same span, "Investments in private funds and private entities" grew from $633,348 at cost (fs-2017, 136e86604f9e, Note 6) to $13,974,853 (fs-2025, 9b5903f8773c, Note 5) — a diversification from single-building retrofit loans toward a portfolio of cleantech/infrastructure private funds (Greensoil, InvestEco, IFM Net Zero Infrastructure, CC&L Infrastructure, etc.).

Priorities added, dropped, renamed

  • Renamed/generalized: the explicit "City of Toronto" + "GTHA" dual-geography aim statement (2017-2019) was folded into one ungeographied statement from FY2020 (fs-2017 vs fs-2020, cited above) — descriptive wording only; the statements do not explain the change.
  • Added: an "alternative investments" investment-policy category (cap raised to 15%) replaced the narrower "private equities up to 5%" category between the FY2021 and FY2022 statements (fs-2021, a527e8f9e320, Note 5 vs. fs-2022, 88493feb8964, Note 5).
  • Added: a project-management / fee-for-service delivery mode — a $5.8M Toronto Community Housing Corporation ("TCHC") "Project" services agreement entered in 2023, first disclosed as a standalone "Related party transactions" note in fs-2024 (476db2a2836e, Note 15).
  • Added: a third-party loan-guarantee instrument, new in FY2025 (a "Guarantees" accounting policy, Note 2(m), and a $3.5M guarantee, Note 18) — not present in any prior year's notes (fs-2025, 9b5903f8773c).
  • De-emphasized, not dropped: ESPA retrofit financing continues to be described in every year's notes through FY2025 but shrinks steadily in active-project count (9→4) while private-fund investment grows roughly 20x in dollar terms (cites above) — the statements never label this a policy pivot; it is this brief's inference from the numbers.

Budget & mandate inflection points

  • 1992 (per financial-statement Note 1, all years) / 1991 (per backgrounder): founding City endowment, stated as $23,000,000 (fs-2019, c8c874f3254c, Note 1; fs-2023, cffaf668288b, Note 1). ⚠️ The incorporation year is inconsistent between sources — see Residuals.
  • Nov 2016: $17,000,000 Ontario endowment, expanding TAF's scope to the GTHA (fs-2017, 136e86604f9e, Note 1).
  • Sept 2020: $38,000,000 FCM/Government-of-Canada endowment plus a separate $2,000,000 operating fund (fs-2020, dac4769d735a, Note 1 and Note 12).
  • 2022: TAF Relationship Framework re-approved by City Council, replacing the 2013 framework cited in every prior filing (fs-2017, 136e86604f9e, Note 1 vs. fs-2023, cffaf668288b, Note 1).
  • 2023: change of accounting policy from the restricted-fund method to the deferral method (with 2022 comparatives restated), coinciding with a change of auditor from Welch LLP to Grant Thornton LLP (fs-2023, cffaf668288b, Note 2 and Independent Auditor's Report).
  • 2025: subsequent-events note (dated April 27, 2026) discloses the FY2025 loan guarantee is now in default and under legal dispute — the newest live item in the archived series (fs-2025, 9b5903f8773c, Note 19).

Ontario/Toronto relevance

TAF is a City of Toronto agency/corporation in its own right: it is listed by the City among its agencies and corporations, its board is appointed by and serves at the pleasure of Toronto City Council (TAF Act 2005, s.3(1), per backgrounder), and it was created by City Council itself in 1991 out of proceeds from a surplus City property sale (backgrounder; fs-2019, c8c874f3254c, Note 1). Its relevance is direct rather than indirect, but its geographic scope is not Toronto-only: the archived financial statements' own Note 1 describes the mandate as originally serving "the City of Toronto" before a 2016 Ontario endowment expanded it to also serve "the GTHA" (fs-2017, 136e86604f9e, Note 1), and from FY2020 the stated aim drops city/region-specific language entirely in favour of an ungeographied climate/air-quality mission (fs-2020, dac4769d735a, Note 1). So TAF today is best described as a Toronto-founded, Toronto-Council-governed climate-finance corporation whose funded programming now reaches beyond city limits across the GTHA, rather than a body whose mandate is scoped strictly to Toronto residents.
