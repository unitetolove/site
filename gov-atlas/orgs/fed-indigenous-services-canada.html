Indigenous Services Canada - backgrounder

Backgrounder / 2026-07-30 / registry row: fed-indigenous-services-canada (this library's government-document registry) / lens file for this org's series briefs

Mandate & statutory basis

ISC is established under the Department of Indigenous Services Act, S.C. 2019, c. 29, s. 336 (chapter designation I-7.88), consolidated text verified at https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-7.88/. Section 3 provides "there is established a department of the Government of Canada, called the Department of Indigenous Services, over which the Minister presides" (same source). Section 6(1) gives the Minister authority over "the provision of services to Indigenous individuals who, and Indigenous governing bodies that, are eligible to receive those services under an Act of Parliament or a program of the Government of Canada," and s.6(2) enumerates child and family services, education, health, social development, economic development, housing, infrastructure, emergency management and governance among the Minister's areas (same source). The annual statute confirms this Act, enacted as s.336 of the Budget Implementation Act, 2019, No. 1, "establishes the Department of Indigenous Services and confers on the Minister of Indigenous Services various responsibilities relating to the provision of services to Indigenous individuals" (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/AnnualStatutes/2019_29/FullText.html).

Roles, responsibilities & scope

ISC organizes its work under one core responsibility, "Indigenous Well-Being and Self-Determination," spanning six service areas - health, children and families, education, infrastructure and environments, economic development, and governance (2025-26 Departmental Plan, document id fed-indigenous-services-canada-dp-2026, https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1731612400516/1731612419549). Its stated aim is to support First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples "to improve well-being and advance self-determination" (same source; statute s.6).

Governance & reporting line

ISC reports to the Minister of Indigenous Services under s.3 of the Act (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-7.88/; 2025-26 Departmental Plan, document id fed-indigenous-services-canada-dp-2026). Per the registry, ISC's open-data organization identifier is shared with the legacy Aboriginal Affairs and Northern Development Canada (AANC) file (registry row fed-indigenous-services-canada, notes field).

Budget scale

~$25.0B total planned spending for 2025-26 (6,251 planned FTEs), per the department's 2025-26 Departmental Plan (document id fed-indigenous-services-canada-dp-2026, https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1731612400516/1731612419549).

Institutional history

ISC was established by statute effective 2019 under the Department of Indigenous Services Act, enacted via s.336 of the Budget Implementation Act, 2019, No. 1, S.C. 2019, c. 29, concurrently with the separate creation of Crown-Indigenous Relations and Northern Affairs Canada as the two successor departments to the former Indigenous and Northern Affairs Canada portfolio (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/AnnualStatutes/2019_29/FullText.html; https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-7.88/).

Backgrounder summary

ISC is a federal department established under the Department of Indigenous Services Act, S.C. 2019, c. 29, s. 336, enacted as part of the Budget Implementation Act, 2019, No. 1 — created concurrently with Crown-Indigenous Relations and Northern Affairs Canada (CIRNA) as the two successor departments to the former Indigenous and Northern Affairs Canada portfolio. Section 6 of the Act gives the Minister of Indigenous Services authority over service delivery to Indigenous individuals and Indigenous governing bodies eligible under an Act of Parliament or federal program, naming child and family services, education, health, social development, economic development, housing, infrastructure, emergency management and governance among covered areas. ISC organizes its current work under one core responsibility, "Indigenous Well-Being and Self-Determination," spanning six service areas (health, children and families, education, infrastructure and environments, economic development, governance), per its 2025-26 Departmental Plan. The department reports to the Minister of Indigenous Services and, per that same Departmental Plan, plans roughly $25.0B in total spending and 6,251 FTEs for FY2025-26. Per the registry, ISC's open-data organization identifier is shared with the legacy Aboriginal Affairs and Northern Development Canada (AANC) file.
