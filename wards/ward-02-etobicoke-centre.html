Ward 02 — Etobicoke Centre

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

Etobicoke Centre occupies the historic core of the former City of Etobicoke, in Toronto's west end. It runs from the Humber River and Dixon Road in the north and east, through established postwar subdivisions such as Eringate-Centennial-West Deane, down to Highway 427 and Dundas Street West in the south, with the Region of Peel forming its entire western edge. It's home to Toronto's second-largest park, Centennial Park — but its southeastern corner sits beside one of the city's biggest active redevelopments: the former Six Points highway interchange at Bloor Street and Kipling Avenue, once known locally as "Spaghetti Junction," has been rebuilt into a new mixed-use district, and construction is underway on a roughly 475,000–508,000-sq-ft Etobicoke Civic Centre a short walk from Kipling subway station. Notably, as this brief details below, the flagship Bloor-Kipling redevelopment blocks and the new civic centre itself are attributed by the City's own project database to the neighbouring ward, Etobicoke–Lakeshore (Ward 3) — not to Etobicoke Centre, despite the shared place name. Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (per Elections Ontario's boundary description, reproduced on Wikipedia): the ward begins at the city's western limit and Highway 401, runs east along Highway 401 to Highway 427, east along Highway 427 and Eglinton Avenue West to Martin Grove Road, north on Martin Grove to Dixon Road, east along Dixon Road to the Humber River, southeasterly along the Humber to Dundas Street West, southwesterly along Dundas to the Canadian Pacific Railway, south along the railway to Mimico Creek, northwesterly along Mimico Creek to Kipling Avenue, south along Kipling Avenue to Bloor Street West, west along Bloor to Highway 427, south along Highway 427 to Dundas Street West, and west along Dundas back to the city's western limit. In plain terms: the west boundary is the municipal border with the Region of Peel; the east boundary is the Humber River; the north boundary runs along Dixon Road (east) and Eglinton Avenue West (west); and the south boundary runs along Dundas Street West, Kipling Avenue, Bloor Street West and Highway 427 in a stepped line through the ward's southeast corner. Community council: Etobicoke and York. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_2_Etobicoke_Centre, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/944c-CityPlanning-Map-Ward-2.pdf (City of Toronto ward map), accessed 2026-08-14]

A cited source (Wikipedia's neighbourhood article, sourced in turn to the Toronto Public Library) explicitly ties the Eringate-Centennial-West Deane neighbourhood — bounded by Centennial Park to the west, Eglinton Avenue West to the north, Martin Grove Road to the east and Rathburn Road to the south, and encompassing the West Deane Park and Centennial Park areas — to this ward and its councillor. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eringate-Centennial-West_Deane, accessed 2026-08-14] Other names in general local use for parts of the ward's interior include Cloverdale and Eatonville (both near the Dundas Street West/Highway 427 corridor); this brief did not find a single source stating a complete, formal neighbourhood list for the ward, consistent with the plan's project-wide note that no formal neighbourhood-to-ward crosswalk is on file (plan §3) — so no such list is asserted here beyond what individual cited sources themselves state.

A boundary-precision finding worth flagging for the profile fold-in: applying this ward's own legal description literally, Kipling Avenue and then Bloor Street West form the ward's boundary through its southeastern corner — meaning the ward sits on the *west/north* side of that line, while the area immediately *east of Kipling Avenue* and *south of Bloor Street West* (where the Six Points/Bloor-Kipling redevelopment and the new Etobicoke Civic Centre are being built) falls in the neighbouring ward. This is confirmed directly by CreateTO's own project database, which lists "Ward: Etobicoke-Lakeshore" and "Councillor: Amber Morley" for every Bloor-Kipling development block, including the block housing the new civic centre. [Source: https://createto.ca/projects/bloor-kipling, accessed 2026-08-14] The City's own promotional and consultation materials nonetheless use "Etobicoke Centre" as a place name for this immediate area (e.g., photo captions reading "Etobicoke Centre roadway construction" on the Six Points Interchange page), which could otherwise be misread as meaning the electoral ward. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/get-involved/public-consultations/infrastructure-projects/six-points-interchange-reconfiguration/, accessed 2026-08-14] This brief could not find a source stating definitively which side of the Kipling Avenue/Bloor Street West line the Kipling subway/GO/bus station structures themselves fall on (see §8).

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Active City construction and infrastructure work, ward-wide (2025-2026). The City's own live Ward 2 profile page lists numerous current or recently active projects, dated as recently as June 2026: local road resurfacing under the Council-approved 2026 Capital Works Program (pre-construction notice dated January 21, 2026); Basement Flooding Protection Program (BFPP) sanitary/storm sewer upgrades on Kipling Avenue and other local roads (multiple notices through April 2026, including construction impacts near Dixon Grove Junior Middle School and Wincott Park); similar BFPP work on Appleby Road/Martin Grove Road (through May 2026), on Beckwith Road/Gentian Drive/Wellesworth Drive (through April 2026), and on Chapman Road (through March 2026); Eglinton Avenue West and Russell Road pedestrian-safety and infrastructure improvements (multi-year project, updated April 2025); Eglinton Avenue West bridge-pier rehabilitation over the Humber River (2025); Humber Creek restoration west of Scarlett Road, Phase 2 (construction update February 2026); Dundas Street West and Neilson Drive watermain replacement (construction notice January 2026); and watermain-relining work on The Kingsway and on Harrop Avenue (March 2026). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-2-etobicoke-centre/, accessed 2026-08-14, page dated "modified June 4, 2026"]
  • Six Points Interchange reconfiguration — completed, reshaping the ward's southeast corner. Following more than a decade of planning, the City demolished the former Six Points highway interchange ("Spaghetti Junction") at Bloor/Dundas/Kipling; major construction ran March 2017 to substantial completion in October 2020, with a formal completion celebration and street-naming ceremony November 26, 2021. The project realigned Dundas Street West, extended Bloor Street West, regraded Kipling Avenue, added protected bike lanes, and unlocked roughly 18 acres of City-owned land for redevelopment. Three new streets were named through public consultation: Adobigok Pathway, Biindagen Trail, and Jerry Howarth Drive (the last honouring the longtime Toronto Blue Jays radio broadcaster). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/get-involved/public-consultations/infrastructure-projects/six-points-interchange-reconfiguration/, accessed 2026-08-14] As noted in §2, the redevelopment blocks themselves (including the new Etobicoke Civic Centre) are attributed to the neighbouring Ward 3, not Ward 2 — reported here because the project is immediately adjacent to the ward and was the subject of the reconfiguration that affects Ward-2-side approaches (Kipling Avenue, Bloor Street West).
  • High-rise development pressure along the Dundas Street West corridor, inside the ward. Per this ward's own boundary (§2), Dundas Street West between Highway 427 and Kipling Avenue runs through the ward's interior, not its edge. UrbanToronto's coverage of a January 2026 rezoning application at 5500 Dundas Street West (Eatonville area, near Billingham Road/Paulart Drive, about 800m southwest of Kipling Station) describes this stretch as being reshaped by "a series of large-scale projects... between Cloverdale Mall and the Kipling area," including: a 13-storey proposal at 251 The West Mall; the Cloverdale Mall redevelopment (on hold, envisioned at up to nine buildings, six to 39 storeys); 5509 Dundas Street West (proposed 22 storeys); the nine-tower Pinnacle Etobicoke (under construction, 25-43 storeys) and its proposed Phase 2 ("Aspen at Pinnacle Etobicoke," 40 and 44 storeys); The Kip District Phase 3 (proposed 36 and 37 storeys); and 5359 Dundas Street West (proposed 50 storeys). The 5500 Dundas Street West application itself proposes 560 residential units (53 studio, 285 one-bedroom, 149 two-bedroom, 73 three-bedroom) across two towers (14 and 16 storeys) with ground-floor retail, on a site currently occupied by a single-storey car dealership. [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2026/01/residential-complex-proposed-west-kipling-station-dundas.60149, accessed 2026-08-14] A separately reported 45-storey rental tower at 4 Beamish Drive (WZMH Architects, for LiUNA, Fengate Asset Management and The Hi-Rise Group) was still being revised as of July 2026; this brief could not fetch that article's full text (see §8) and notes it only via a search-result summary — Beamish Drive is one of the streets named in the Six Points reconfiguration itself, so this project likely sits on the Ward 3 side of the Kipling/Bloor line, not confirmed. [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2026/07/latest-plans-further-revise-45-storey-rental-tower-near-kipling-station.61407, accessed 2026-08-14, via search summary only — direct fetch unsuccessful]
  • Citywide monthly council-approval roundups (UrbanToronto) note Etobicoke-York activity through 2026 — for example, a "City Council July, 2026: Etobicoke-York and North York Quick Approvals" roundup describing ten development sites approved across Etobicoke, York and North York that month, including Queensway multi-phase plans, mixed-use towers, affordable housing, a retirement residence and transit-area planning — but this brief could not isolate which specific approvals, if any, were Ward-2-specific from the available summary (direct article fetch was unsuccessful; see §8). [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2026/08/city-council-july-2026-etobicoke-and-north-york-quick-approvals.61480, accessed 2026-08-14, via search summary only]

Transit & development

  • TTC Line 2 (Bloor-Danforth) — Kipling Station. Kipling is the western terminus of Line 2, opened November 21, 1980. It sits at Bloor Street West and Kipling Avenue — directly on this ward's southeastern boundary line as legally described (§2). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipling_station, accessed 2026-08-14] Islington Station, one stop east on Line 2, is well east of Kipling Avenue and, per this ward's boundary, is not associated with Ward 2 — the two stations are adjacent stops on the same subway line, not a "subway interchange" in the sense of two intersecting lines. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_2_Etobicoke_Centre, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Kipling Transit Hub (Metrolinx). A completed (May 2022) integration of GO Transit (Milton line), MiWay, and TTC subway/bus service at Kipling, comprising: a new bus terminal (14 bus bays), a renovated GO station building and upgraded GO platform with accessible boarding, underground tunnels linking the bus terminal to the TTC station, a pedestrian bridge with elevators over the rail tracks, a signalized intersection at Acorn Avenue, and 90 bicycle-parking spaces. Metrolinx describes it as connecting riders to the Milton GO line, 10+ bus routes, GO, TTC and MiWay, plus express bus access to Pearson Airport. [Source: https://www.metrolinx.com/en/projects-and-programs/milton-line-go-expansion/what-were-building/kipling-transit-hub, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Etobicoke Civic Centre (adjacent to, not inside, Ward 2 — see §2/§3). Under construction at the Bloor/Kipling corner (addressed variously as 3745 and 3755 Bloor Street West across sources); groundbreaking April 10, 2024; four buildings of 7, 10, 13 and 16 storeys designed by Henning Larsen Architects and Adamson Associates Architects for CreateTO, built by Multiplex. Construction status "Under Construction" as of August 14, 2026; the Toronto Public Library's own renovations page states design and approvals are complete, with construction beginning 2024 and projected completion in 2028. The complex will include municipal offices, a council chamber, a ~27,211-sq-ft TPL branch (up to 100,000-item collection), a community recreation centre with pools, a childcare centre, a Toronto Public Health clinic (dental and breastfeeding services), and a near-zero-emissions district energy plant serving the whole Bloor-Kipling precinct. [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/database/projects/etobicoke-civic-centre.26549, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-celebrates-start-of-construction-of-the-new-state-of-the-art-etobicoke-civic-centre/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://tpl.ca/about-the-library/renovations/new-etobicoke-civic-centre-branch/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Bloor-Kipling (Six Points) housing blocks (adjacent, Ward 3 per CreateTO). Five of the seven Six Points-precinct blocks are residential: together they are planned to deliver roughly 2,700 homes (~900 affordable rental). 5207 Dundas Street West (Block 1) is under construction, delivering 725 rental homes including 218 affordable; 3725 Bloor Street West (Block 3) is at the development-concept stage for ~698 rental homes (30% affordable); 970 Kipling Avenue (Block 5) is in pre-construction for 800+ homes. [Source: https://createto.ca/projects/bloor-kipling, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Development inside Ward 2's interior. The Dundas Street West corridor between Highway 427 and Kipling Avenue (Eatonville/Cloverdale area) is the ward's most active development stretch identified — see §3 for the specific projects. Bus routes serving parts of the ward, per Toronto Public Library branch-access notes, include TTC 32 (Eglinton Avenue), TTC 37, TTC 111, TTC 112, and TTC 50, several of which connect to Kipling or Islington subway stations. [Source: https://tpl.ca/locations/ri/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://tpl.ca/locations/ea/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://tpl.ca/locations/EB/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Eglinton Crosstown West Extension (Metrolinx, future planning). Metrolinx lists this 9.2 km westward extension of the Eglinton Crosstown LRT into Etobicoke and Mississauga among its active project pages; Eglinton Avenue West forms part of this ward's northern boundary (§2), so the corridor is geographically adjacent to the ward, but this brief could not confirm specific Ward-2 station or alignment detail (see §8). [Source: https://www.metrolinx.com/en/projects-and-programs/eglinton-crosstown-west-extension, accessed 2026-08-14, page content not independently confirmed beyond project listing — see §8]

Conseil scolaire Viamonde is the one board fully resolved, exact, for every ward. See the tiered breakdown and this ward’s precision note in “What else is on your ballot” above.
  • Libraries and community/recreation centres are now geospatially joined to this ward’s boundary (see “What’s live here, by the numbers” under Local commons above — complete citywide datasets for both layers).
