Ward 07 — Humber River-Black Creek

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

Humber River-Black Creek sits in Toronto's northwest, in the old municipality of North York, bounded by the Humber River to the west, Keele Street to the east, Steeles Avenue to the north, and a jagged southern line running along Black Creek, Sheppard Avenue West, Jane Street and Highway 401. Its best-known area is Jane and Finch, long a focus of City-led neighbourhood planning — the Jane Finch Secondary Plan and Community Development Plan took legal effect in most of the area in 2025, and Toronto's newest light-rail line, Line 6 Finch West, opened along Finch Avenue West through the ward in December 2025. The ward also touches York University's western edge and includes the Black Creek Pioneer Village heritage site near Jane Street and Steeles Avenue. It is one of the city's more actively redeveloping wards: an independent development tracker counted three dozen residential and institutional projects underway or recently completed here as of late 2023, several timed to the new transit line.

Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (per the province's 2018 ward redistricting, reproduced on Wikipedia): the ward commences at the intersection of the city's northerly limit with Keele Street; runs south along Keele to Grandravine Drive; generally west along Grandravine to Black Creek; generally southeast along Black Creek to Sheppard Avenue West; west along Sheppard to Jane Street; south along Jane to Highway 401; west along Highway 401 to the Humber River; then generally northwest along the Humber River back to the city's northerly limit. Community council: North York. The current ward is an amalgamation of the western section of the old (pre-2018) Ward 7 York West and the eastern section of the old Ward 8 York West. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_7_Humber_River%E2%80%94Black_Creek, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/8d0d-CityPlanning-Map-Ward-7.pdf (City of Toronto ward map), accessed 2026-08-14]

Named neighbourhoods commonly associated with the ward, per source: Humber Summit, Humbermede, Humberlea, York University Heights, Jane and Finch, and the extreme western part of Downsview. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_7_Humber_River%E2%80%94Black_Creek, accessed 2026-08-14] Within the "Jane and Finch" area itself, a separate source distinguishes two sub-areas: the portion from Finch north to Steeles is commonly called Black Creek, while the portion from Finch south to Sheppard is commonly called Glenfield-Jane Heights — this is the source's own stated distinction, not a formal ward-neighbourhood mapping this project asserts. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_and_Finch, accessed 2026-08-14]

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Jane Finch Secondary Plan and Community Development Plan. City Council adopted the Jane Finch Secondary Plan (Official Plan Amendment 633) and a companion Jane Finch Community Development Plan in June 2024, following the multi-year Jane Finch Initiative planning study — the second City-directed study (after Keele Finch Plus) of focus areas along Finch Avenue West ahead of the Finch West LRT's opening. The Secondary Plan has been in force and effect since February 3, 2025, except for the property at 2775 Jane Street and the "Norfinch District," both subject to an ongoing appeal at the Ontario Land Tribunal (case OLT-24-000833). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/planning-studies-initiatives/jane-finch-initiative-planning-study/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2024/ey/bgrd/backgroundfile-243793.pdf, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • 2775 Jane Street, current appeal-area development. The City passed Zoning By-law 471-2026 on May 21, 2026, permitting a 27-storey infill residential building north of the existing, retained 17-storey apartment building at 2775 Jane Street — this specific site remains one of the two areas still under OLT appeal noted above. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/ey/bgrd/backgroundfile-264707.pdf, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://secure.toronto.ca/nm/api/individual/notice/7503.do, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Community Development Plan implementation. Per the City's own report, the Community Development Plan's actions are meant to be implemented over a 10-year period with evaluations and updates every five years; the City has sought to establish a "Jane Finch Resourcing Table" to help coordinate implementation. In March 2024 the area's Community Advisory Committee transitioned into a Community Partnership Circle and a Residents' Leadership Circle as part of that implementation structure. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2024/ec/bgrd/backgroundfile-242910.pdf, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.downsviewadvocate.ca/news/jane-and-finch-secondary-plan-and-community-development-plan-set-for-completion-at-community-city-council, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Jane-Finch Community Action Plan (2026). As of June 2026, a Jane-Finch Community Action Plan — described as a shared vision shaped by residents, grassroots groups, service providers and institutional partners, and led locally by the Jane/Finch Centre — is reported to build on the area's earlier Community Benefits Framework and the Community Development Plan above. [Source: https://www.unitedwaygt.org/issues/how-jane-and-finch-is-building-community-solutions-that-last/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Jane Finch Mall (jfm+) redevelopment. The mall, owned and operated by Brad-Jay Investments Ltd. since 1968, has been approved by the City (August 2024) for a phased, mixed-use redevelopment into seven mixed-use blocks with roughly a dozen new mid- to high-rise buildings and more than 4,600 new residential units; the approval is described as a milestone, not a finish line. In June 2025, Brad-Jay entered a partnership with developer Hullmark, which acquired a minority interest and will act as Master Development Manager. Phase A is planned on the site's existing parking lots, with the mall itself largely retained in this first phase. [Source: https://storeys.com/jane-finch-mall-redevelopment-towers/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://jfmplus.ca/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Active City capital works, 2026. The City's own Ward 7 profile page lists a live set of construction notices within the ward: local road resurfacing under the Council-approved 2026 Capital Works Program (notice dated January 21, 2026); Grandravine Drive (over Black Creek) culvert replacement (construction update dated May 19, 2026); Jane Street (over Black Creek) bridge rehabilitation (pre-construction Feb. 2025, construction notice March 2025); York Gate Boulevard road resurfacing and bikeway construction (update dated June 17, 2026); Sentinel Road reconstruction, watermain installation and a raised cycle track (notices/updates 2024–2025); Secroft Crescent and Fletcherdon Crescent watermain replacement (pre-construction March 2026, deferred June 2026); Steeles Avenue West and Jane Street watermain repair (April 2025); and a multi-year Keele Street-corridor transmission main/pumping-station/storm-sewer upgrade (notices/updates 2022–2025). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-7-humber-river-black-creek/, accessed 2026-08-14, page itself dated "modified June 23, 2026"]

Transit & development

TTC service. Line 6 Finch West LRT opened December 7, 2025: 11 km, 18 stops, running along Finch Avenue West from Finch West Station (at Keele Street, the ward's eastern boundary) to Humber College Station near Highway 427. Peak headways run roughly every 6½ minutes (Mon–Fri, 7–10am and 3–7pm); full service now runs until 1am daily as of March 15, 2026. The line connects to TTC Line 1, roughly 30 TTC bus routes, and regional carriers (York Region Transit, MiWay, and others). [Source: https://www.ttc.ca/riding-the-ttc/Line-6-Finch-West, accessed 2026-08-14] The line's 18 stops, in order, are: Finch West, Sentinel, Tobermory, Driftwood, Jane and Finch, Norfinch Oakdale, Signet Arrow, Emery, Milvan Rumike, Duncanwoods, Pearldale, Rowntree Mills, Mount Olive, Stevenson, Albion, Martin Grove, Westmore, and Humber College. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_6_Finch_West, accessed 2026-08-14] A ward-specific development report (see below) directly ties the Sentinel, Norfinch Oakdale, and Emery stops to projects sited within Ward 7; Tobermory and Driftwood sit immediately beside, respectively, the York Woods library branch and the Driftwood Community Recreation Centre, both themselves inside the ward (§5) — the stops west of Emery are not independently confirmed as inside Ward 7 (see §8; Ward 1 Etobicoke North's own research brief treats Humber College Station, the line's western terminus, as inside that ward, not this one).

Line 1 subway. The Line 1 Yonge-University extension (opened December 17, 2017) runs six stations, 8.6 km, from Sheppard West Station through York University to Vaughan Metropolitan Centre, including York University Station and Pioneer Village Station — named after the nearby Black Creek Pioneer Village heritage site — near the ward's northwest. Finch West Station, at Keele Street and Finch Avenue West, sits on the ward's eastern boundary and now also serves as the eastern terminus of Line 6. [Source: https://dailyhive.com/toronto/ttc-line-1-extension-information-2017, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Village_station, accessed 2026-08-14]

Development pattern. UrbanToronto's Ward 7 Custom Location Report (dated September 2023 — historical texture, not a current count) tracked 36 projects in various stages: 24 pre-construction, 2 under construction, 10 recently completed, totalling 13,129 tracked new dwellings, with an average building height of 45.2 m (max 154.84 m) and an average Floor Space Index of 2.59 across the ward. Notable named projects: the Humber River Long Term Care Home (2111 Finch Ave W, 320 long-term-care beds, near the Norfinch Oakdale LRT stop); Casa Emery Village (2345 Finch Ave W, a 10-tower multi-phase project by Medallion Corporation totalling 2,237 units — three towers complete as of the report — near the Emery stop); and 470 Sentinel Road (Ranee Management/Kirkor Architects, five infill buildings adding 1,720 units on an existing tower-in-the-park site, near the Sentinel stop). [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2023/09/utpro-instant-reports-torontos-ward-7-humber-river-black-creek-hotbed-diverse.53774, accessed 2026-08-14] This sits alongside the Jane Finch Secondary Plan / OPA 633 land-use framework and Community Development Plan described in §3, both explicitly framed by the City as growth-management ahead of and around the new LRT line.
