Ward 22 — Scarborough-Agincourt

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

Scarborough-Agincourt sits in the middle of Scarborough, bounded by Steeles Avenue East, Highway 401, Victoria Park Avenue, and Midland Avenue — a fully built-out, largely residential ward with no subway or LRT line of its own, but with Agincourt GO station near its centre. Its biggest current civic story is redevelopment near that station: the 26-acre Agincourt Mall site and the six-parcel Cowdray Court lands next to it are both moving through City approvals toward thousands of new homes. The ward's population is heavily immigrant and multilingual — Mandarin and Cantonese are its two most-common non-official home languages — a mix reflected in its Sheppard Avenue retail strip and in the closure, within the past three years, of both the ward's longtime English-language community paper and a Chinese-language daily whose Toronto office was historically based in Agincourt.

Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (per the ward's Wikipedia article, citing the Elections Ontario legal description, reproduced there verbatim): the ward is bounded by Steeles Avenue East to the north, Highway 401 to the south, Victoria Park Avenue to the west, and Midland Avenue to the east. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_22_Scarborough%E2%80%94Agincourt, accessed 2026-08-14]

Named neighbourhoods commonly associated with the ward, per that same source: L'Amoreaux, Tam O'Shanter, and part of Agincourt. [Source: same as above]

  • L'Amoreaux — bounded by Victoria Park Avenue (west), McNicoll Avenue (north), Kennedy Road (east), and Huntingwood Drive (south) — a box entirely inside the ward's own boundary box. Named after Josue L'Amoreaux, a French Huguenot loyalist who settled the area in 1816. Bridlewood Mall (at Warden and Finch Avenues) is the neighbourhood's largest retail building. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Amoreaux, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Tam O'Shanter-Sullivan — bounded by Huntingwood Drive (north), Kennedy Road (east), Highway 401 (south), and Victoria Park Avenue/Pharmacy Avenue (west); includes the Tam O'Shanter community (east of Warden Avenue) and Sullivan (west of Warden). This Wikipedia article states the neighbourhood "is sometimes included as part of the neighbourhood of Agincourt," and that Agincourt Mall itself is located within Tam O'Shanter. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tam_O%27Shanter-Sullivan, accessed 2026-08-14]

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Cowdray Court redevelopment (20, 40, 50, 70, 80 and 100 Cowdray Court). A Zoning By-law Amendment and Draft Plan of Subdivision for six parcels totalling roughly 6.6 hectares (currently four commercial buildings) was adopted by City Council on February 4, 2026, after the Scarborough Community Council recommended approval on January 13, 2026. The approved proposal permits 2,157 dwelling units in towers ranging from 23 to 41 storeys, plus 2,238 square metres of retail space, on lands within the Agincourt Secondary Plan (Site and Area Specific Policy 4). Conditions include an on-site parkland dedication of at least 6,224 square metres, expansion of the existing Collingwood Park, and a new north-south road intended eventually to connect Village Green Square to Sheppard Avenue East. [Source: https://secure.toronto.ca/council/agenda-item.do?item=2026.SC28.3, accessed 2026-08-14; background report: https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/sc/bgrd/backgroundfile-261231.pdf, accessed 2026-08-14 (referenced within the agenda-item record, not independently opened as a PDF)]
  • Agincourt Mall redevelopment (a separate site, 3850 Sheppard Ave. E., roughly 26 acres, at the northeast corner of Kennedy Road and Sheppard Avenue East, near Agincourt GO station). Developer NADG's own property page (accessed 2026-08-14) describes approvals "secured" for roughly 4.4 million square feet of new development, buildings up to 450 feet/43 storeys, and a build-out of up to 4,372 residential units, 258,300 sq. ft. of ground-floor retail, 107,600 sq. ft. of office space, and 23,680 sq. ft. of community-amenity space. A May 2023 press report described NADG as then advancing a Phase One Site Plan Application (two towers, 26 and 42 storeys, 659 units, replacing an existing Walmart) — this brief found no more recent (2024–2026) public confirmation of that specific application's approval status. [Source: https://nadg.com/property/reinvent-agincourt-mall/, accessed 2026-08-14; https://storeys.com/agincourt-mall-redevelopment-scarborough/, accessed 2026-08-14 (published 2023-05-11); title/existence of https://canada.constructconnect.com/dcn/news/projects/2023/05/agincourt-mall-redevelopment-goes-to-site-plan confirmed via search only, not independently fetched]
  • 40 Cowdray Court towers (Gemterra Developments). A separate 2023 press report describes Markham-based Gemterra Developments seeking approval for two towers (41 and 26 storeys, 813 units total) at 40 Cowdray Court, part of a multi-phase "Agincourt Centre" plan. This address is among the six parcels named in the February 2026 Council approval above; this brief could not independently confirm from the sources gathered whether the two are the same application at a later stage, an overlapping-but-distinct block, or under different ownership — flagged for re-verification (see §8). [Source: https://storeys.com/40-cowdray-court-scarborough-towers/, accessed 2026-08-14 (published 2023-08-23)]
  • Major Transit Station Area (MTSA) zoning. Ward 22 is explicitly named among the wards covered by the City's Scarborough-district Major Transit Station Area zoning-update maps (Agincourt GO is one of 120 city-wide Protected Major Transit Station Areas the Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing approved in August 2025; as of the page's last update, June 30, 2026, the City was still drafting the implementing zoning by-law and maps, with additional time needed to assess the effects of Bill 98, and no Council date yet set). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/planning-studies-initiatives/zoning-for-major-transit-station-areas/, accessed 2026-08-14; Scarborough-district map naming Ward 22: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/8efc-city-planning-zoning-for-major-transit-station-areas-scarborough.pdf, URL confirmed directly on the page above, PDF contents not independently opened]
  • Ongoing City capital works (infrastructure, not private development). The City's own Ward 22 profile page (last modified July 15, 2026) lists an active slate of projects: a 2026 local road-resurfacing program; creek-culvert rehabilitation/replacement on Birchmount Road (two separate culverts), Collingsbrook Boulevard, Huntingwood Drive, L'Amoreaux Drive, and Timberbank Boulevard; a Finch Hydro Corridor multi-use trail (in construction since 2024, with a further update dated April 2026); Huntingwood Drive road rehabilitation with bike-lane updates; Midland Avenue watermain replacement, road resurfacing, and road-safety upgrades; Sheppard Avenue watermain replacement; a Kennedy Road watermain relining; and a "priority sanitary infrastructure protection" project at L'Amoreaux Park (notice dated January 5, 2026). Transit & development

  • TTC: no subway or LRT line was found to run within Ward 22's own boundaries in the sources checked — this is inferred from the ward's legal boundary description plus the routing described for the (cancelled) Sheppard East LRT and the planned Line 4 Sheppard extension (below), not a verified point-in-polygon check. The Sheppard East Village BIA, whose territory falls partly within the ward, describes its bus service on Markham, McCowan, Brimley, Midland and Sheppard as linking to "the Sheppard, Yonge-University and Bloor-Danforth Subways" — i.e., to subway stations outside its own (and the ward's) footprint. [Source: https://www.toronto-bia.com/find-a-bia/bias/sheppard-east-village/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • GO: Agincourt GO Station (Stouffville line), which opened in 1982 on the site of an earlier rail station dating to 1871, sits on Sheppard Avenue East just east of Kennedy Road, inside the ward. A 2018–2023 rebuild (new station building, second platform, two pedestrian tunnels, expanded 340-space parking lot) was completed by Metrolinx in September 2023 as part of Regional Express Rail/GO Expansion work on the Stouffville corridor. TTC's 85 Sheppard East, 985 Sheppard East Express, and 904 Sheppard-Kennedy bus routes connect the station to Don Mills subway station to the west and Scarborough Centre station to the east. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agincourt_GO_Station, accessed 2026-08-14; https://www.metrolinx.com/en/projects-and-programs/stouffville-line-go-expansion/what-were-building/agincourt, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Sheppard East LRT / Line 4 extension (historical/adjacent context, not confirmed inside the ward). A light-rail line along Sheppard Avenue East was first proposed in 2007, partly built (an underpass at the GO tracks near Agincourt GO opened in 2012), then effectively shelved; in April 2019 the Ontario government announced it would instead extend the Line 4 Sheppard subway east to McCowan Road — which sits outside Ward 22's eastern boundary at Midland Avenue — "at some unspecified time in the future." No sources checked confirmed a current construction timeline for that extension. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheppard_East_LRT, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Development: the ward's largest current development activity clusters around the Agincourt GO/Sheppard-Kennedy node — the Agincourt Mall redevelopment and the Cowdray Court lands (§3) — both explicitly described in their own planning documents as transit-oriented, walkable redevelopment near the GO station and both within the Agincourt Secondary Plan area.
  • Business improvement area: the Sheppard East Village BIA (formed 2007) covers businesses on and near Sheppard Avenue East from Midland Avenue to Markham Road, including part of the Agincourt commercial district; its own page describes "many Chinese shops and popular restaurants" alongside businesses serving European, South Asian, Filipino, and other communities. [Source: https://www.toronto-bia.com/find-a-bia/bias/sheppard-east-village/, accessed 2026-08-14]

Local media landscape

  • Scarborough Mirror — a Metroland Media weekly that covered Scarborough communities including Agincourt South- Malvern West and Agincourt North, published 1962–2023. It ceased publication on September 15, 2023, when Metroland's parent Nordstar sought bankruptcy protection and immediately ended print publication of 70 weekly newspapers across Ontario, including the Scarborough Mirror, without severance for the roughly 605 employees affected. [Source: https://beachmetro.com/2023/09/19/in-my-opinion-closure-of-metrolands-weekly-newspapers-shows-that-bad-ownership-is-the-biggest-threat-facing-canadian-journalism/, accessed 2026-08-14; title/coverage-area description of https://muckrack.com/media-outlet/scarborough-mirror confirmed via search only, not independently fetched]
  • Ming Pao Daily News (Canada) — a Chinese-language daily whose Toronto editorial office was historically based in Agincourt (its Toronto/"Eastern" edition launched in 1993). Reported as Canada's last Chinese-language daily newspaper, it ceased publication on January 18, 2026. [Source: Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Pao_Daily_News_(Canada), accessed 2026-08-14; closure date corroborated via search results citing CBC News and CP24 reporting — the CBC article (https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/chinese-language-newspaper-ming-pao-ceasing-operations-january-2026-9.7043431) was attempted directly but returned no readable content; the CP24 article title/date was confirmed via search only, not independently fetched]
  • Sing Tao Daily (Canada) — a Chinese-language paper also serving the Toronto Chinese community; ceased its Toronto (and Vancouver/Calgary) print editions on August 28, 2022, in a pivot to digital, and continues to publish online. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sing_Tao_Daily_(Canada), accessed 2026-08-14]
  • No dedicated, currently-publishing ward-specific news outlet was identified. The ward's historical English-language community paper (Scarborough Mirror) and one of its historical Chinese-language dailies (Ming Pao) have both ceased operating within the past three years; see gaps below for what wasn't checked.
