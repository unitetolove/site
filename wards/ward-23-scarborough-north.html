Ward 23 — Scarborough North

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

Scarborough North sits in the city's northeast corner, bordered by Steeles Avenue (the Markham boundary) to the north, Highway 401 to the south, Midland Avenue to the west, and Neilson Road to the east. It's a low-rise, largely residential suburban ward whose named neighbourhoods — Malvern, Milliken, Morningside Heights, and the eastern half of Agincourt — have no subway or LRT line of their own and are served entirely by TTC and GO bus/rail connections at the ward's edges. The ward is currently the site of two connected, ward-specific planning stories: a City-approved pilot allowing five- and six-unit "multiplex" housing on typical single-family lots (a first for Toronto, unique to this ward), and a long-running private redevelopment proposal to rebuild the aging Malvern Town Centre mall into a mixed-use, multi-tower neighbourhood.

Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (Elections Ontario boundary text, reproduced on Wikipedia): commencing at the intersection of the northerly limit of the City with the Rouge River; thence generally southerly along the river to the electric power transmission line; thence westerly along that transmission line to Morningside Avenue; thence southerly along Morningside Avenue to Neilson Road; thence generally southerly along Neilson Road to Highway No. 401; thence westerly along the highway to Midland Avenue; thence northerly along Midland Avenue to the northerly limit of the City; thence easterly along that limit back to the point of commencement. In plain terms: Steeles Avenue East to the north (the boundary with Markham), Highway 401 to the south, Midland Avenue to the west, and Neilson Road/the Rouge River corridor to the east. Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Ward 23 Multiplex Study (fiveplex/sixplex pilot). On February 5, 2025, City Council adopted an Official Plan Amendment and Zoning By-law Amendment permitting multiplexes with up to six dwelling units on residentially-zoned (RD/RS/RM/RT) lots, ward-wide — a permission that exists only in Ward 23, not city-wide. The amendments are in effect; no appeals were received. The City's own study found roughly 61% of Ward 23's residential lots could physically accommodate at least a sixplex, and staff linked the pilot to the ward's population decline limiting support for local retail/services. A city-wide sixplex study, informed by this ward's pilot, was to be delivered to Council by Q4 2025. A community consultation on October 3, 2024 found less than half of attendees supportive of the by-law change as surveyed. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/planning-studies-initiatives/ward-23-multiplex-study/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://storeys.com/fiveplexes-sixplexes-scarborough-north/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Malvern Town Centre redevelopment. Davpart Inc. submitted an Official Plan and Zoning By-law Amendment application on June 28, 2022 to redevelop the Malvern Town Centre mall (Tapscott Road/Neilson Road) into a mixed-use community through a phased, 10-phase master plan; the first phase proposes two towers of 39 and 32 storeys above retail podium space, with 877 residential units. The applicant appealed the proposed Official Plan/Zoning By-law Amendment to the Ontario Land Tribunal on June 26 (case number OLT-25-000513). A City-hosted Community Consultation Meeting was held November 2, 2023, and the ward's councillor hosted a separate town hall on the proposal September 20, 2023. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/planning-studies-initiatives/malvern-town-centre-proposal-planning-framework/, accessed 2026-08-14] A community-facing description elsewhere puts the eventual build-out at 13 towers ranging 25–42 storeys across the full site. [Source: https://www.mymalvern.ca/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • MyMalvern community-wealth-building initiative. A community planning initiative led by the Toronto Community Benefits Network (with the Native Child and Family Centre), engaging Malvern residents, businesses, and community groups to identify priorities and co-create a vision for Malvern's future and the Town Centre redevelopment. The City's 2026 budget continued $80,000 in funding for this initiative, added as one of 18 Council-directed service enhancements at the February 10, 2026 final budget vote. [Source: https://www.mymalvern.ca/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.socialplanningtoronto.org/final_2026_city_budget_makes_targeted_investments_in_key_services, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Community-led illegal-dumping cleanups, Malvern. A Malvern-based youth/community advocacy group ("42 Voices") has organized weekly volunteer cleanups (Sundays, 10:30 a.m., meeting at Malvern Town Centre) since spring 2026 to address illegal dumping of garbage in local parks, ravines, and stormwater channels; the group is now also planning a discussion at Malvern Library on the area's history of dumping and environmental neglect. [Source: https://torontoobserver.ca/2026/07/20/42-voices-malvern/, accessed 2026-07-20 (article date), accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Multi-year capital works, ward-wide. The City's own Ward 23 profile page lists active construction/infrastructure notices: Invergordon Avenue culvert rehabilitation (over East Highland Creek; notices May 2025–Nov. 2025); Milner Avenue culvert rehabilitation (same creek, same notice window); Milner Drive watermain cleaning/relining (Jan.–July 2024); Midland Avenue watermain replacement, road resurfacing and safety upgrades from Steeles Ave. E. to south of McNicoll Ave. (notices May–June 2026); Purcell Square/Passmore Avenue/Copthorne Avenue watermain replacement and road resurfacing (notices Aug. 2025–June 2026, spanning Wards 22/23); Select Avenue new sidewalk and road resurfacing (notices March–June 2026); Steeles Avenue East (Midland Ave. to McCowan Rd.) road reconstruction, resurfacing and new bikeway construction (notices March 2024–March 2025); and a 2026 local-road-resurfacing program (pre-construction notice dated March 3, 2026). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-23-scarborough-north/, accessed 2026-08-14, page itself dated "modified June 19, 2026"]

Transit & development

No subway or LRT line runs within Ward 23's boundaries; the ward is served entirely by TTC bus routes and, at its southwest edge, the Milliken GO Station (Stouffville line, GO Transit/Metrolinx) — though this brief could not confirm the station's exact address (39 Redlea Avenue) sits inside rather than just outside the ward's western Midland Avenue boundary; see §8. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milliken_GO_Station, accessed 2026-08-14] TTC bus routes reported as serving the ward, per the ward councillor's constituency-office website (facts stated below are TTC service/route facts as described there; this brief did not independently cross-verify each route/date against a TTC service-change bulletin — see §8): the 903 Kennedy–SCC Express (launched September 2023, runs on Midland and Kennedy with dedicated lanes and signal priority); the 904 Sheppard–Kennedy Express (launched September 2024); the 334 Eglinton East (launched February 2026, part of the Line 5 Eglinton surface bus network); and the 830 Middlefield (launched February 2026, described as designed for school travel within Scarborough North). The same source describes service increases on routes 129 McCowan North, 38 Highland Creek, 131 Nugget, 939 Finch Express, 902 Markham Road Express, 39 Finch East, 53 Steeles East, 57 Midland, 21 Brimley, 85 Sheppard East, and 43 Kennedy. [Source: https://www.jamaalmyers.com/ttc-in-ward-23, accessed 2026-08-14] Development: the ward's most significant live planning activity is residential — the Ward 23 Multiplex Study (ward-wide low-rise intensification, §3) and the Malvern Town Centre master-plan redevelopment (large-scale, multi-tower, §3) — rather than a single dominant transit-infrastructure project of the kind found in some other wards.

Networks & local commons

As of August 17, 2026, this project's public organization registry maps 114 organizations directly to Ward 23 (Scarborough North) — up from the 87 this brief stated the previous day. A dedicated dork-library sweep completed the same day ran a full category-by-category search across the ward's own named areas (Malvern, Milliken, Morningside Heights, and the eastern half of Agincourt only — the historic Agincourt/Milliken cores west of Midland Avenue are a different ward, and seven plausible-looking finds on that side of the line were independently confirmed outside the boundary and excluded) and against its top five home languages besides English and French (Cantonese, Mandarin, Tamil, Tagalog and Urdu). Nineteen further organizations and groups were newly added — residents' associations, a housing co-op, congregations, food-security programs, an arts collective, and a community-wealth-building initiative among them, spanning faith, food systems, mutual aid, environment, and ethnocultural categories — and eight already-registered Ward 23 organizations, found again independently by this same sweep, had their location corrected on the record: several carried the wrong ward entirely (a cluster of Malvern churches had been tagged to Scarborough-Rouge Park), others a generic "Toronto" tag or a ward label the site's own classifier doesn't recognize without the literal word "Ward." Among the corrections is the Malvern Family Resource Centre, a major multi-service hub (EarlyON, youth, a 55+ seniors' centre, newcomer settlement, survivor support) that had never carried a ward-specific tag at all. The remaining 87 organizations behind today's total are principally bulk City/CRA register rows (early-years childcare, K-12 parent councils) captured registry-wide rather than ward-by-ward; 8 of the 114 carry `verified-live` status, the rest `seed-web-verified` or, for two thinly-sourced finds, `seed-unverified` pending a fuller confirmation pass. This sweep also found real gaps worth stating plainly: no BIA exists anywhere in the ward (checked against the City's own BIA list), no union local could be pinned inside the Tapscott Road employment corridor, and — despite a cricket-heavy South Asian population share — no movement/play club or rec league was independently confirmed as ward-based.
