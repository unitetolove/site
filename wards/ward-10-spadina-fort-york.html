Ward 10 — Spadina-Fort York

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

Spadina-Fort York is Toronto's fastest-built downtown-west waterfront ward — Liberty Village, CityPlace, Fort York, King West, and the Entertainment District sit alongside older neighbourhoods like Kensington Market (part), Chinatown, Alexandra Park, and West Queen West. It's dense, high-rise, and still growing: the ward carries an unusually long list of live City infrastructure work (watermain, sewer, road, and Gardiner Expressway repairs) plus four planned Ontario Line stations and, as of mid-2026, a newly announced large-scale redevelopment of the downtown rail corridor (the "Toronto Rail Yards"/Rail Deck lands).

Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (Elections Ontario boundary text, per Wikipedia): commences at Ossington Avenue & Dundas Street West, east along Dundas to Bay Street, south along Bay to Front Street West, northeast along Front to Yonge Street, south along Yonge to The Esplanade, east along The Esplanade to Berkeley Street, east in a straight line to Mill Street & Parliament Street, east along Mill Street to the Don River, south along the river to the Keating Channel, then southwesterly along the channel. In plain terms: spans roughly from Liberty Village/Ossington in the west to the Distillery District area in the east, and from the waterfront/Toronto Islands north to Trinity Bellwoods, Chinatown, and City Hall. Named areas commonly associated with the ward: Alexandra Park, Chinatown (part), CityPlace, Corktown, Distillery District, Entertainment District, Fort York, King West, Liberty Village, Ossington, St. Lawrence (part), West Queen West. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_10_Spadina%E2%80%94Fort_York, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.ausmamalik.ca/ward10, accessed 2026-08-14]

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Toronto Rail Yards / Rail Deck lands redevelopment. In June 2026, Fengate Asset Management (with LiUNA) announced a large mixed-use development proposal above the active rail corridor between Bathurst Street and Spadina Avenue, near the ward's southern edge — described as bringing nearly 4,000 homes, ~85,000 sq. m of office space, retail, childcare, and over two acres of park space, beginning with a six-acre deck over the rail yard and a roughed-in connection to a planned Spadina-Front GO Station. This sits on/near the long-discussed Rail Deck Park lands; the City's Executive Committee has separately continued to weigh acquiring adjacent air-rights parcels for public park space, with negotiations reported as ongoing since 2018 without resolution. [Source: https://www.blogto.com/real-estate-toronto/2026/06/rail-deck-park-development-toronto/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/rail-deck-district-park-condo-development-1.6846841, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Active City capital works, 2026. The City's own Ward 10 profile page lists an unusually long set of live or recently completed construction notices spanning the ward — watermain replacement on Beverley, Emily, Harbour, Widmer, and Wolseley Streets; sanitary/storm sewer work on Fraser Avenue, Freeland Street/Lake Shore Blvd E., and Princess Street; road reconstruction/resurfacing on Draper, Euclid, Hagerman, and Markham Streets; TTC streetcar track renewal on King Street West (at Dufferin and between Dufferin/Shaw); a "Fleet Street TTC Transit Hub Installation" community notice (dated May 8, 2026); University Avenue watermain/cycle-track work shared with Ward 11 (notice dated July 21, 2026, with a deferral notice dated July 27, 2026); and F.G. Gardiner Expressway bridge and soffit repairs near Bay/Yonge and Grand Magazine/York/Cherry (notices dated February 2026). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-10-spadina-fort-york/, accessed 2026-08-14, page dated "modified July 27, 2026"]
  • Ontario Line construction. Four planned Ontario Line stations sit within/adjacent to the ward — Exhibition, King/Bathurst, Queen/Spadina, and Osgoode (University). Station advance work and tunnelling are described as beginning in 2026; Toronto Hydro is relocating underground electrical infrastructure (Fleet Street, Fort York Blvd., Bremner Blvd. corridor) to prepare for a Metrolinx substation at Exhibition Place. [Source: https://www.ausmamalik.ca/ontarioline, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/te/bgrd/backgroundfile-261289.pdf (Ontario Line Construction Update, Q4 2025), accessed 2026-08-14]

Transit & development

TTC: the ward is served by Line 1 (Yonge-University) at its eastern/northern edge (e.g. St. Andrew, Osgoode, Queen's Park-area stations sit on or near the boundary) and multiple streetcar routes (504 King, 501/301 Queen, 509/510 Harbourfront/Spadina) run through the ward's core; exact station-to-ward assignment not independently verified against the boundary polygon — named as general context, not a formal join. Major transit project: four planned Ontario Line stations (§3). Development: the largest single item found this research is the Rail Deck/Toronto Rail Yards proposal (§3); the ward's own City construction-notice page also shows sustained, ward-wide watermain/sewer/road renewal activity consistent with its high-density, largely post-2000 built form (CityPlace, Liberty Village, King West towers). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/transit-in-toronto/transit-expansion/ontario-line/, accessed 2026-08-14]

What else is on your ballot

Every Toronto voter marks three races on October 26, 2026: this ward’s councillor (above), the citywide mayor, and one school trustee.

  • Mayor (citywide — the same race for every ward) — 42 candidates registered as of 2026-08-14, identical treatment: the mayoral race card.
  • School trustee — one seat, for whichever of Toronto’s four school boards matches your school-support designation. Trustee-district boundaries do not in general line up with the 25 council wards; here is what this project has established for THIS ward, tiered by how confidently each is sourced:
    • Conseil scolaire Viamonde (French public) — Toronto-Centre (Secteur 2, Annexe CENTRAL) (race card) — confirmed exact from the board’s own official regulatory determination document (Détermination et répartition des conseillers scolaires, Conseil scolaire Viamonde, March 2026, Annexe C).
    • TCDSB — not established for this ward: no reliable source pairs it to a specific TCDSB ward (a named gap, not a guess).
    • CSC MonAvenir (French Catholic) — this ward falls in one of MonAvenir’s two Toronto régions (Toronto-Est or Toronto-Ouest); the board has not published a ward-level boundary document, so which région applies here is not resolved — a region-level statement, not a guess at which one.
    • Toronto District School Board (TDSB) — local reporting places this ward in TDSB Ward 5 — not yet verified against an official board document (race card); see this ward’s precision note below.
    39 TDSB and 21 TCDSB trustee candidates are registered citywide as of 2026-08-14. Whatever the tiers above show, MyVote is the authoritative resolver for your own address — check it before you vote: MyVote.
