Parkdale-High Park sits in Toronto's west end, running from the Humber River in the west to a rail-line/Dufferin Street boundary in the east, and from a CP rail line south of St. Clair Avenue down to the Lake Ontario shoreline. It combines the lakefront neighbourhoods of Parkdale and Sunnyside with High Park — the ward's signature green space — and the residential streets of Roncesvalles, Swansea, Runnymede–Bloor West Village, the Junction Area, and Lambton Baby Point/Old Mill. Five TTC Line 2 subway stations (Keele, High Park, Runnymede, Jane, and Old Mill) sit within the ward, and the 501 Queen and 504 King streetcars run along its southern edge. One concrete foothold on what makes this ward distinct: multi-tenant ("rooming") housing has been a defining local policy question for decades — a 2017 community land-trust study counted roughly 198 rooming houses (about 2,715 individual dwelling rooms) in Parkdale alone, more units than Toronto Community Housing owned in the same study area, and the City's first-ever citywide licensing framework for this housing type only took effect March 31, 2024.

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Multi-tenant ("rooming") housing — a long-running Parkdale-specific policy thread. A May 2017 study by the Parkdale Neighbourhood Land Trust ("No Room for Unkept Promises") found 198 rooming houses in Parkdale with an estimated 2,715 dwelling rooms — more than double the number of units Toronto Community Housing owned in the same study boundaries — and documented 28 rooming houses lost to conversion/upscaling in the prior decade, displacing an estimated 347 people, with a further 59 (housing an estimated 818 people) assessed at the time as at imminent risk. [Source: https://pnlt.ca/learn/research/parkdale-rooming-house-study, accessed 2026-08-14] Separately, the City helped fund the purchase of a Parkdale rooming house into community-land-trust ownership under a 99-year affordability agreement. [Source: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/city-helps-purchase-rooming-house-amid-destructive-housing-boom-in-parkdale-1.5118708, accessed 2026-08-14] The City's first citywide licensing framework for multi-tenant houses — standardizing rules that previously varied by former-municipality zoning — was adopted by Council in December 2022 and took effect March 31, 2024; it applies uniformly across all 25 wards, this one included. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/housing-shelter/rental-housing-rights-information/multi-tenant-rooming-houses/new-framework-for-multi-tenant-rooming-houses/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Encampments. Citywide, the number of encampments on City property fell from 283 in March 2025 to 84 as of March 15, 2026, per city data reported by CBC; this is a citywide figure, not ward-specific. [Source: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/number-of-encampments-decreased-past-year-homelessness-9.7182350, accessed 2026-08-14] Within this ward, a makeshift shelter at Marilyn Bell Park (at the south end of Parkdale, on the ward's Lake Ontario shoreline) was cleared by City staff in summer 2024. [Source: https://thelocal.to/toronto-encampment-homeless-park/, accessed 2026-08-14] Encampment Support Network Parkdale (ESN Parkdale), a community-organized outreach group, states it conducts ongoing outreach in encampments, shelter hotels, and social housing in the area. [Source: https://www.esnparkdale.com/, accessed 2026-08-14] Reported neutrally per source; no named individual is characterized here.
  • Roncesvalles and Dundas West Built Form Study. The City is (per its own study page, last modified September 27, 2024) undertaking a built-form review of Roncesvalles Avenue (Queen Street West to Boustead Avenue) and Dundas Street West (Boustead Avenue to Sorauren Avenue), initiated following a November 3, 2015 request from Toronto and East York Community Council, in response to development pressure in the area; stated outcomes include built-form/urban-design guidelines, a heritage-resource assessment, potential streetscape improvements, and possible Zoning/Official Plan amendments. No more recent (2025-2026) public status update for this specific study was found — flagged, not assumed complete or lapsed. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/planning-studies-initiatives/roncesvalles-and-dundas-west-study/overview-roncesvalles-and-dundas-west-study/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Development beside High Park subway station. City Council accepted a settlement offer for a 19-storey mixed-use, mostly-residential tower at 1930-1938 Bloor Street West, directly across from High Park and adjacent to High Park Station — 144 total units (132 new condominium units plus 12 replacement rental units), an increase from an earlier 17-storey proposal for the same site. [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2025/02/settlement-offer-increases-tower-19-storeys-across-high-park.57975, accessed 2026-08-14]

Keele, High Park, Runnymede, Jane, and Old Mill stations all fall within Ward 4 (Old Mill and the neighbourhoods Jane station serves — Bloor West Village, Swansea, Runnymede, Old Mill, Baby Point — are Ward 4 neighbourhoods per §2 above).   • Streetcars. The 501 Queen and 504 King routes both run through the ward's southern neighbourhoods (Parkdale, Roncesvalles). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/501_Queen, accessed 2026-08-14] The multi-year King-Queen-Queensway-Roncesvalles (KQQR) intersection reconfiguration (City construction notices on file from 2020-2021) reopened this five-way intersection to streetcars; per the Roncesvalles Village BIA, the 504 King streetcar returned to Roncesvalles Avenue on May 7 after a year-long diversion once KQQR reopened (the BIA post does not state the year; the City's own Ward 4 construction-notice record for this project ends in an October 2021 update, consistent with a 2022 reopening — flagged as inferred, not confirmed). [Source: https://roncesvallesvillage.ca/504-streetcar/, accessed 2026-08-14] A separate, newer project: starting November 1, 2026, 504/304 King streetcars are scheduled to be consolidated onto one branch (Dundas West Station to Broadview Station), with westbound service diverting via Queen Street West between Spadina Avenue and Roncesvalles Avenue, and 504C/304C replacement buses running Sunnyside Loop to the Distillery District, for watermain and track-renewal work on King Street; the diversion is expected to continue into early summer 2027. [Source: https://stevemunro.ca/2026/07/06/king-west-construction-plans-2026-27/, accessed 2026-08-14]

Get involved — from thirty seconds to a year

Everything on this page is published so that anyone who lives here can use it: there is no membership, no gate and nothing to belong to. If you want to do something with it, the doors below start at thirty seconds and stop wherever you like — every one of them is optional, every one of them is free to copy or fork, and none of them asks who you vote for.

  • The ladder — what you can do today: thirty seconds, an hour, an evening, a week, a season, a year. Every rung a real door, every rung optional.
  • The Harvest — add three lines to one public record: a vision for 2030, a complaint about what is broken, or a neighbour worth naming.
  • Add your name to the count — a name and a way to reach you is the whole first ask; everything after that is optional.
  • The mobilization framework — for anyone who already leads a room and wants that room, and every room like it, to act together. Free, forkable, evidence-based, nothing to join.
  • The plan — one year, all-out — what this city could do in a single year, month by month, with every number labelled as a target rather than a promise.
  • The conversation kits — free, open kits for running your own conversation about Toronto’s future, at a kitchen table or in a hall.
  • Mutual aid — how neighbours already help each other across Toronto, what a block circle is, and how to start one where you live.
  • The letters — written to the people this city keeps writing about instead of to. Read one; pass it to someone it is about.

This is a neighbours’ project rather than a campaign or an organization: it publishes tools, data and evidence, and leaves every decision to the person reading. Nothing here endorses a candidate or a party, and nothing here needs our permission to be used.
