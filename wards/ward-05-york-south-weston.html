Ward 05 — York South-Weston

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

York South-Weston sits in Toronto's northwest, largely coextensive with the former City of York, bounded by the Humber River, Highway 401, a CN rail line and a CP rail line. Its core neighbourhoods are Weston, Mount Dennis and Rockcliffe-Smythe. The ward just gained a major transit milestone: Line 5 Eglinton's western terminus opened at Mount Dennis on February 8, 2026, built into the site of the historic Kodak Heights factory lands, alongside a new GO Transit/UP Express station that opened there in November 2025. A few kilometres north, Weston's own long-serving GO/UP Express station has just finished a construction upgrade, and a wave of highrise residential proposals — several exceeding 40 storeys — is now moving through the planning process around it.

Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (Elections Ontario boundary description, reproduced on Wikipedia): commencing at the intersection of the Humber River with Highway 401; thence easterly along the highway to the GO Transit railway east of Connie Street; thence southerly along that railway to Rogers Road; thence westerly along Rogers Road to Old Weston Road; thence southeasterly along Old Weston Road to Lavender Road; thence westerly along Lavender Road to Keele Street; thence southerly along Keele Street to the GO Transit railway; thence southeasterly along that railway to the Canadian Pacific Railway; thence generally westerly along the CPR to the Humber River; thence generally northerly along the river back to the point of commencement. In short form, the ward is bordered west by the Humber River, east by a Canadian National Railway track, north by Highway 401, and south by a Canadian Pacific Railway track. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_5_York_South%E2%80%94Weston, accessed 2026-08-14]

The ward is largely made up of the former City of York (pre-1998 amalgamation) and sits within the Etobicoke and York Community Council area. The current Ward 5 boundary dates to the province's 2018 25-ward redraw and combines the western section of the old (pre-2018) Ward 11 with the eastern section of the old Ward 12; that 25-ward structure was upheld by the Supreme Court of Canada in 2021. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_5_York_South%E2%80%94Weston, accessed 2026-08-14]

Named neighbourhoods, per source: Weston and Mount Dennis are both directly documented as Ward 5 neighbourhoods on their own Wikipedia pages, as is Rockcliffe–Smythe, whose page states plainly "It is in Ward 5 (York-South Weston)." [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Dennis_station, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockcliffe%E2%80%93Smythe, accessed 2026-08-14] Secondary/aggregator sources (e.g. Wikidata's summary record for the ward) also name Amesbury and Keelesdale as ward neighbourhoods; this brief did not independently verify those two against a primary boundary source and states them at lower confidence than the three above — flagged in §8, not asserted as equally certain.

This brief did not find a citable list naming which specific wards border Ward 5 (i.e., what is on the other side of the Humber River, the 401, and the two rail lines) — not attempted, see §8. As the template instructs, no formal neighbourhood-to-ward crosswalk is asserted here beyond what individual sources themselves state (per the plan §3, that spine is not on file project-wide).

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Eglinton Crosstown Line 5 — Mount Dennis western terminus opened. After decades of planning (an earlier, 1990s-era "York Centre" subway proposal for the same corridor was shelved in 1995), Line 5 Eglinton's Mount Dennis station opened for GO Transit, TTC bus and UP Express service on November 16, 2025, and for Line 5 LRT service on February 8, 2026 — making Mount Dennis the line's western terminus. The station is built into and around Kodak Building 9, a 1939 landmark from the former Eastman Kodak "Kodak Heights" factory site (operated 1918–2006), which was moved 60 metres and back again during construction to preserve it as the station's third entrance. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Dennis_station, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Weston GO/UP Express Station — construction complete, service upgrade pending signalling. Metrolinx states construction on Weston GO Station's platform, track and pedestrian-tunnel upgrades (part of the Kitchener Line GO Expansion program) is now complete, but service changes will not take effect until new rail signalling is in place. [Source: https://www.metrolinx.com/en/projects-and-programs/kitchener-line-go-expansion/what-were-building/weston-go-station, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Weston Village intensification wave. A resubmitted proposal for 1705 Weston Road (at Victoria Avenue East, ~80m from Weston GO/UP Express station's south entrance) now calls for two 43-storey towers (996 units total, up from ~549 in the prior 2024 application), following a single-tower version approved in July 2025. UrbanToronto's report names several other towers at various stages nearby in Weston Village: 1798–1812 Weston Road (36 storeys), Hickory Tree Towers (38 storeys), 16 Wilby Crescent (39 storeys), 1821 Weston Road and 1828–1844 Weston Road (45 storeys each), and the Weston Park redevelopment (45 and 50 storeys). [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2026/04/two-43-storey-towers-proposed-expanded-plan-near-weston-go.60885, accessed 2026-08-14] The Weston Village BIA's own page corroborates the pattern in general terms, noting "a revitalized GO train service and new station" and that "several redevelopments are in the works for Weston to bring new spaces and density to the neighbourhood." [Source: https://www.toronto-bia.com/find-a-bia/bias/weston-village/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Mount Dennis Early Learning and Child Care Centre. A new 19,000-sq-ft, 98-space child care centre opened at 1234 Weston Rd — described by the City as its largest City-run child care centre and first net-zero-emissions building (geothermal heat pump, 264 rooftop solar panels, triple-glazed windows, no natural-gas connection). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-officially-celebrates-largest-city-run-child-care-centre-and-first-net-zero-building/, dated November 17, 2025, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • "The Dennis" purpose-built rental project, Mount Dennis. A rental housing project on Locust Street broke ground under the City's Purpose-built Rental Housing Incentives stream: 448 rental homes including 89 affordable units, with City support (deferred development charges, a property-tax reduction, and a 40-year property-tax/fee exemption on the affordable units) valued at roughly $29 million. Developer partners named: LiUNA Pension Fund of Central and Eastern Canada, Fengate Asset Management, and The Hi-Rise Group. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-fast-tracks-purpose-built-rentals-breaks-ground-on-448-rental-homes/, dated September 3, 2025, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Active City capital works, 2026 (representative sample). The ward's own City of Toronto profile page lists many dated, in-progress infrastructure notices; a representative sample: a Black Creek Drive bridge-over-Black Creek rehabilitation (construction-update notice dated February 9, 2026); Runnymede Road resurfacing (construction notice April 10, 2026, update May 1, 2026); and the Fairbank-Silverthorn Basement Flooding Protection Program's local collector storm-sewer phase 2 (multiple notices, most recently a Woodborough Park construction notice dated April 11, 2025). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-5-york-south-weston/, accessed 2026-08-14, page itself dated "modified August 4, 2026"]
  • Mount Dennis BIA "Small Green Business Incubator Hub." A local economic-development initiative offering six spaces for entrepreneurs "to launch and grow their ventures in Toronto's next major transit hub," tied explicitly by the BIA to the new Mount Dennis station opening. [Source: https://www.mountdennisbia.ca/news/, accessed 2026-08-14]

Transit & development

  • Line 5 Eglinton (LRT). 19 km, Mount Dennis (western terminus, in this ward) to Kennedy Station. Service hours Monday–Saturday 5:30 a.m.–1:20 a.m., Sunday 7:30 a.m.–1:20 a.m. (plus an overnight 334 Eglinton bus). Connects to 68 bus routes, Line 1 Yonge-University at Cedarvale/Eglinton stations, Line 2 Bloor-Danforth at Kennedy, and to GO Transit's Kitchener Line and UP Express at Mount Dennis station itself. A one-way end-to-end trip runs approximately 52–54 minutes. [Source: https://www.ttc.ca/riding-the-ttc/line-5-eglinton, accessed 2026-08-14] New and changed bus routes tied to the February 8, 2026 Line 5 opening include new routes 18 Caledonia, 27 Jane South, 34 Eglinton, 151 Leslie North, 158 Trethewey (Trethewey Drive runs through the ward's Rockcliffe-Smythe area) and 334 Eglinton (overnight), plus service changes on existing routes including 32 Eglinton West, 35 Jane, 71 Runnymede and 89 Weston. [Source: https://www.ttc.ca/riding-the-ttc/line-5-eglinton, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Eglinton Crosstown West Extension (future). A planned 9.2 km westward extension from Mount Dennis Station to Renforth Drive (Etobicoke/Mississauga), mostly underground, with up to 7 new stations on that segment (up to 3 more possible if a further Pearson Airport link proceeds). Metrolinx's own project page states construction is underway, with the most recent listed milestone being "station excavation begins," dated July 2026; that page does not state a public opening year as of this access. [Source: https://www.metrolinx.com/en/projects-and-programs/eglinton-crosstown-west-extension, accessed 2026-08-14] (A separate secondary source encountered in research cited a 2031 target for this extension; this brief does not treat that as confirmed, since Metrolinx's own current project page does not state it — see §8.)
  • Weston GO Station / UP Express. Located at Weston Road and Lawrence Avenue West, served by GO Transit's Kitchener Line and the Union Pearson Express, with direct connections to Toronto Pearson International Airport and Union Station. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14] Construction on platform, track and pedestrian-tunnel upgrades is complete; full service benefits await new signalling (see §3).
  • TTC bus routes serving the ward (per neighbourhood-level sources, not an exhaustive ward-wide list): 32 Eglinton West, 35 Jane, 52 Lawrence West, 59 Maple Leaf, 71 Runnymede, 73 Royal York, 79 Scarlett, and 89 Weston, plus the new 158 Trethewey and 27 Jane South added with the February 2026 Line 5 opening; a 161 Rogers Road loop is also named for the Rockcliffe-Smythe area. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockcliffe%E2%80%93Smythe, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.ttc.ca/riding-the-ttc/line-5-eglinton, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Development/planning context. The City's 2021 "Picture Mount Dennis Community Services and Facilities Study" is a planning study inventorying the area's recreation and library facilities and anticipating growth pressure from the Crosstown's opening; this brief cites it as a named planning document rather than reproducing its facility counts, which this research did not independently verify by direct fetch. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/9435-CityPlanning-PictureMountDennis-Study.pdf, accessed 2026-08-14 (URL located via search; document itself not separately fetched — see §8)] The Weston Village towers cluster described in §3 sits within what UrbanToronto's reporting calls the "Weston Major Transit Station Area," reflecting City intensification policy tied to the GO/UP Express station. [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2026/04/two-43-storey-towers-proposed-expanded-plan-near-weston-go.60885, accessed 2026-08-14]

Local commons

  • Mount Dennis Branch, Toronto Public Library — 1123 Weston Road (Toronto, ON M6N 3S3), at the southeast corner of Weston Road and Eglinton Avenue West, beside the Church of St. Mary and St. Martha. Closed for major renovation in October 2011, reopened March 2013, and won the 2013 Toronto Urban Design Award (Award of Excellence, Public Buildings in Context). Wheelchair accessible, free Wi-Fi, Youth Hub. [Source: https://tpl.ca/locations/MD, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Weston Branch, Toronto Public Library — 2 King Street (Toronto, ON M9N 1K9), at the northeast corner of King Street and Weston Road. Opened December 29, 1914 as a Carnegie-funded Free Public Library (Lindsay and Brydon, Architects); designated of historic and architectural value under the Ontario Heritage Act in 1978; celebrated its centennial in 2014. Wheelchair accessible, free Wi-Fi, local history collection, Youth Hub. [Source: https://tpl.ca/locations/WE, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • York Community Centre — opened 2017; double gymnasium, running/walking track, six-lane 25-metre indoor training pool, indoor leisure pool, two multi-purpose rooms, weight/aerobic/dance rooms. Described in its source as located "to the east of" the Rockcliffe-Smythe neighbourhood, which is itself confirmed within Ward 5 — this brief flags, rather than asserts, that the centre's precise location falls inside the ward polygon, since "to the east of" a neighbourhood is not itself a ward-boundary statement (same posture as the Ward 1 pilot's treatment of Humber College Station). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockcliffe%E2%80%93Smythe, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Smythe Park Recreation and Community Centre — within Rockcliffe-Smythe (confirmed Ward 5), built on the site of a former gravel pit operated by Conn Smythe in the 1920s; the pit closed after World War II and the surrounding land became residential, retaining the Smythe name. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockcliffe%E2%80%93Smythe, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Weston Lions Park / Weston Lions Recreation Arena — south of Lawrence Avenue West in Weston; 22.13 acres (89,600 m²), with fields for football, rugby union, soccer, tennis and baseball plus an ice rink, swimming pool, basketball court and skatepark. The arena (proposed on the former Weston Fairgrounds) opened in 1949, with a pool added in 1959; it currently hosts Weston Minor Hockey and the Weston Dodgers. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Cruickshank Park — in the Humber River valley in Weston, with paved bicycle paths connecting south along the Humber toward Lake Ontario (linking to the wider Martin Goodman Trail network, per a separate UP Express source describing the same trail corridor near Weston GO). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2026/04/two-43-storey-towers-proposed-expanded-plan-near-weston-go.60885, accessed 2026-08-14]

[Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockcliffe%E2%80%93Smythe, accessed 2026-08-14]

What else is on your ballot

Every Toronto voter marks three races on October 26, 2026: this ward’s councillor (above), the citywide mayor, and one school trustee.

  • Mayor (citywide — the same race for every ward) — 42 candidates registered as of 2026-08-14, identical treatment: the mayoral race card.
  • School trustee — one seat, for whichever of Toronto’s four school boards matches your school-support designation. Trustee-district boundaries do not in general line up with the 25 council wards; here is what this project has established for THIS ward, tiered by how confidently each is sourced:

    • TDSB — not established for this ward: no reliable source pairs it to a specific TDSB ward (a named gap, not a guess).

  • Amesbury and Keelesdale as ward neighbourhoods rest on a secondary/aggregator source (Wikidata's summary record) rather than a primary boundary document independently checked — lower confidence than Weston, Mount Dennis, and Rockcliffe-Smythe, which are each confirmed on their own sourced Wikipedia pages.
  • York Community Centre's precise location relative to the Ward 5 polygon is not confirmed — its only sourced description ("to the east of" Rockcliffe-Smythe) does not itself establish which side of the ward line it falls on.
  • St. Clair–Old Weston GO/UP Express station, a Metrolinx project under construction nearby (St. Clair Avenue West and Old Weston Road, in the Stockyards area), was surfaced during research; this brief could not confirm from available sources whether that site falls inside Ward 5 or an adjacent ward, and excludes it rather than guessing.
  • Eglinton Crosstown West Extension opening date — Metrolinx's own current project page states construction is underway (most recent listed milestone: station excavation begins, July 2026) but does not commit to a public opening year; a separate secondary source found in research suggested 2031, which this brief does not treat as confirmed given that discrepancy.
  • "Picture Mount Dennis Community Services and Facilities Study" — located and cited by URL (§4) but its detailed facility-count content was not independently verified by direct fetch; any figures from it should be re-checked before being treated as current.
  • Full 2026 development-application pipeline for the ward, beyond the specific named projects in §3/§4, was not pulled — would need the City's Application Information Centre or a fresh ward-level UrbanToronto report, not attempted.
  • WebSearch tool budget — this research's WebSearch tool reached a session-wide call-count limit (shared across parallel ward-research lanes) partway through the research. This was a hard call-count cap, not a connectivity outage, so the template's 60-second-retry protocol did not apply; remaining research was completed via direct `web_fetch` of specific, named pages rather than new searches, and every item that could not be independently confirmed after that point is listed as a gap above rather than guessed or filled from memory.
  • Formal point-in-polygon commons join — not attempted, per the plan's own standing scope note (§6 of the plan).
  • No current, ward-specific, neutral "live civic issue" beyond the development/transit/infrastructure items in §3 was found; older public-safety-bulletin-style posts on WestonWeb (dated 2021) were not treated as current civic texture given their age.
  • A full address-to-trustee-district crosswalk is not complete for every board (school board trustee races do not share the 25-ward boundary map): for this ward, TDSB carries not established and TCDSB is narrowed to one of two TCDSB wards, not resolved further; CSC MonAvenir is resolved only to a region (Toronto-Est or Toronto-Ouest), never a ward, for every ward citywide.
