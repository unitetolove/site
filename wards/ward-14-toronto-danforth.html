Toronto-Danforth runs from the Don River east to Coxwell Avenue, and from the lake north to Taylor Creek — covering Riverdale, Greektown/the Danforth, Riverside, East Chinatown (Broadview & Gerrard), and part of Leslieville. It's a well-established, mostly low-rise residential ward, anchored by Riverdale Park and Withrow Park, with sustained City road/watermain/bridge renewal work as its main current civic-infrastructure story rather than any single large redevelopment.

The race & the 2022 baseline

  • Office: Councillor, Ward 14 - Toronto-Danforth
  • Seats: 1
  • Incumbent: Paula Fletcher — running in 2026: unknown — open seat: unknown

2022 result: Paula Fletcher won by 66.8 percentage points over the runner-up — a landslide, not a close call. Source: City of Toronto official 2022 election results, as_of 2026-08-16.

Susan Chapelle

Registered 2026-06-01 · Website: https://www.electsusanchapelle.com

Susan Chapelle is a candidate for Toronto City Council, Ward 14 (Toronto-Danforth), registered on June 1, 2026. Her campaign site describes her as a former two-term elected municipal councillor with a career spanning healthcare, research, technology, labour and business, and a small-business owner in Broadview Danforth. Her platform, 'Community, Opportunity, Action,' calls for growth paired with supporting infrastructure such as parks, schools and healthcare access; support for local business and job opportunity; cleaner and safer neighbourhoods, including improved transit safety; and stronger public services, including waste collection and stormwater capacity. Her site refers respectfully to incumbent Paula Fletcher's tenure.

Jason Stevens

Registered 2026-05-29 · Website: https://www.votejasons.com/

Jason Stevens is a candidate for Toronto City Council, Ward 14 (Toronto-Danforth), registered on May 29, 2026. His campaign site describes him as a long-time Toronto-Danforth resident who states he has served the community 'since my youth.' His platform proposes freezing property taxes to a 2 percent increase, building shelters where appropriate, supporting local businesses, and opposing Billy Bishop Airport expansion, which he states could save the city over $2.2 billion combined with challenging Ontario's Special Purpose Account rules. He also proposes a weekly resident newsletter and describes his campaign as 'rooted in being a responsive, engaged councillor' who brings constituent concerns forward.

Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (Elections Ontario boundary text, per Wikipedia): commences at Highway 401 & Leslie Street, south along Leslie to Eglinton Avenue East, west along Eglinton to the Don River West Branch, southeasterly along that branch to Overlea Boulevard, east along Overlea to Don Mills Road, south along Don Mills Road to the Don River East Branch, southwesterly along the branch and the Don River to Pottery Road, along Pottery Road to Bayview Avenue, north/northwest along Bayview to the CPR line, southwest along the railway to the Beltline Trail in Moore Park Ravine, northwesterly along the trail to Mount Pleasant Cemetery's southerly boundary, west along that boundary to Mount Pleasant Road, north to Broadway Avenue, west to Yonge Street, north to Highway 401, then northeasterly/easterly back to the point of commencement. In plain terms (per the same source): bordered south by Lake Ontario/Toronto Harbour, east by Coxwell Avenue/Coxwell Boulevard, north by Taylor Creek and the Don River East Branch, west by the Don River. Named areas commonly associated with the ward: Riverdale (North and South), Greektown/the Danforth, Riverside ("Queen Broadview Village"), East Chinatown (Broadview & Gerrard), the Studio District, Blake-Jones/"The Pocket," and part of Leslieville. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_14_Toronto%E2%80%94Danforth, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14]

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Active City capital works, 2026. The City's own Ward 14 profile page lists a substantial set of live/recent construction notices — road reconstruction on Greenwood Avenue (with a winter shutdown notice dated November 2025 and an update dated March 2026) and pedestrian-safety improvements on Browning Avenue (notice dated July 2026); watermain/sanitary work on Eastern Avenue (temporary road closures as recent as June 2026) and Wolfrey Avenue; multiple bridge projects — a Don Valley Parkway pedestrian bridge over Royal Drive, a concrete crib wall rehabilitation at Chester Hill Road, a Millwood Road (Leaside) bridge safety-barrier installation (pre-construction notice May 2026), and rehabilitation of the Gerrard/Dundas/Riverdale pedestrian bridges over the Don; and continuing work on the North Toronto Treatment Plant outfall/Don River watercourse restoration (construction update dated July 2026). A general 2026 local road-resurfacing notice for the ward was issued in January 2026. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-14-toronto-danforth/, accessed 2026-08-14, page dated "modified July 31, 2026"]
  • South Riverdale industrial-lands redevelopment (dated context). Per Wikipedia's Riverdale neighbourhood article, the former Lever Brothers/Unilever soap-factory lands in South Riverdale — occupied 1890s to 2009 and since demolished — are described as awaiting redevelopment for commercial/industrial use under Cadillac Fairview ownership; this is background context from an encyclopedia source, not independently confirmed against a current City planning application. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14]

Transit & development

TTC: the ward is served by Line 2 (Bloor-Danforth) at Broadview, Chester, Pape, Donlands, and Greenwood stations running through its core along the Danforth; exact station-to-ward assignment not independently verified against the boundary polygon — named as general context, not a formal join. No major new rapid-transit capital project (comparable to an Ontario Line station or LRT opening) was identified for this ward — the ward's transit infrastructure is the established Line 2 corridor plus the Greenwood Subway Yard (a TTC operations facility within the ward). Development: the largest single item found this research is the former Unilever soap- factory lands redevelopment named in §3; a full current-year development-application count was not pulled. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14]

Local commons

  • Riverdale Branch, Toronto Public Library — 370 Broadview Ave. (at Gerrard St. E.), opened 1910 as one of three Carnegie-funded branch buildings; large Chinese-language collection (established 1973) reflecting the adjacent East Chinatown; a heritage-listed building (Toronto Historical Board plaque, 2006). [Source: https://tpl.ca/locations/RD/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Ralph Thornton Community Centre — 765 Queen St. E., in South Riverdale; a City of Toronto agency and Association-of-Community-Centres member, also housing the Queen/Saulter branch of the Toronto Public Library, the South Riverdale Child-Parent Centre, and Don Valley Community Legal Services; located in a former 1913 federal post office building (Postal Station G). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Thornton_Community_Centre, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Riverdale Park (running track, three baseball diamonds, skating rink, public pool, tennis courts) and Withrow Park (off-leash dog area, ice rink, two baseball diamonds, soccer field) — the ward's two largest parks, both commonly associated with the neighbourhood per the cited source. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14]

What else is on your ballot

Every Toronto voter marks three races on October 26, 2026: this ward’s councillor (above), the citywide mayor, and one school trustee.

  • Mayor (citywide — the same race for every ward) — 42 candidates registered as of 2026-08-14, identical treatment: the mayoral race card.
  • School trustee — one seat, for whichever of Toronto’s four school boards matches your school-support designation. Trustee-district boundaries do not in general line up with the 25 council wards; here is what this project has established for THIS ward, tiered by how confidently each is sourced:
    • Conseil scolaire Viamonde (French public) — Toronto-Centre (Secteur 2, Annexe CENTRAL) (race card) — confirmed exact from the board’s own official regulatory determination document (Détermination et répartition des conseillers scolaires, Conseil scolaire Viamonde, March 2026, Annexe C).
    • TCDSB — not established for this ward: no reliable source pairs it to a specific TCDSB ward (a named gap, not a guess).
    • CSC MonAvenir (French Catholic) — this ward falls in one of MonAvenir’s two Toronto régions (Toronto-Est or Toronto-Ouest); the board has not published a ward-level boundary document, so which région applies here is not resolved — a region-level statement, not a guess at which one.
    • Toronto District School Board (TDSB) — local reporting places this ward in TDSB Ward 5 — not yet verified against an official board document (race card); see this ward’s precision note below.
    39 TDSB and 21 TCDSB trustee candidates are registered citywide as of 2026-08-14. Whatever the tiers above show, MyVote is the authoritative resolver for your own address — check it before you vote: MyVote.

Get involved — from thirty seconds to a year

Everything on this page is published so that anyone who lives here can use it: there is no membership, no gate and nothing to belong to. If you want to do something with it, the doors below start at thirty seconds and stop wherever you like — every one of them is optional, every one of them is free to copy or fork, and none of them asks who you vote for.

  • The ladder — what you can do today: thirty seconds, an hour, an evening, a week, a season, a year. Every rung a real door, every rung optional.
  • The Harvest — add three lines to one public record: a vision for 2030, a complaint about what is broken, or a neighbour worth naming.
  • Add your name to the count — a name and a way to reach you is the whole first ask; everything after that is optional.
  • The mobilization framework — for anyone who already leads a room and wants that room, and every room like it, to act together. Free, forkable, evidence-based, nothing to join.
  • The plan — one year, all-out — what this city could do in a single year, month by month, with every number labelled as a target rather than a promise.
  • The conversation kits — free, open kits for running your own conversation about Toronto’s future, at a kitchen table or in a hall.
  • Mutual aid — how neighbours already help each other across Toronto, what a block circle is, and how to start one where you live.
  • The letters — written to the people this city keeps writing about instead of to. Read one; pass it to someone it is about.

This is a neighbours’ project rather than a campaign or an organization: it publishes tools, data and evidence, and leaves every decision to the person reading. Nothing here endorses a candidate or a party, and nothing here needs our permission to be used.
