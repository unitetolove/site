Ward 19 — Beaches-East York

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

Beaches-East York runs along the lake in Toronto's east end — the Beach(es) neighbourhood, Upper Beaches/East End-Danforth, Woodbine Corridor, East Danforth, and part of Old East York. It's a lower-rise, largely residential ward with a historic Carnegie library on Kew Gardens, a subway line along its northern edge, and a GO station midway through a multi-year relocation and rebuild.

The race & the 2022 baseline

  • Office: Councillor, Ward 19 - Beaches-East York
  • Seats: 1
  • Incumbent: Brad Bradford — running in 2026: no — open seat: yes

2022 result: Brad Bradford won by 32.0 percentage points over the runner-up — a landslide, not a close call. Citywide 2022 turnout was 29.7% (per-ward turnout is not compiled in this dataset, so this figure is citywide, not this ward’s own). Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (Elections Ontario boundary text, reproduced on Wikipedia): starts at Victoria Park Ave. & Sunrise Ave., west along Sunrise to the Don River East Branch, along that branch to Taylor Creek, east along the creek to Coxwell Blvd., south on Coxwell to Lake Shore Blvd. E., then a line to Ashbridge's Bay, south along the bay, due south to the city's southern limit (the lake), northeast along that limit, then north on Victoria Park Ave. back to the start. In plain terms: Lake Ontario to the south, Coxwell Avenue on the west, Sunrise Avenue to the north, Victoria Park Avenue on the east. Community council: Toronto and East York. Named neighbourhoods/areas: Upper Beaches (city's "East End-Danforth," with the Woodbine Avenue-to-Coxwell portion called "Woodbine Corridor"), East Danforth (Danforth Village), Old East York. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_19_Beaches%E2%80%94East_York, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/8c9d-CityPlanning-Map-Ward-19.pdf (City of Toronto ward map), accessed 2026-08-14]

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Danforth GO Station relocation and rebuild. Metrolinx is relocating the Danforth GO station roughly 500m east and rebuilding it to accommodate the Lakeshore East GO expansion. Early works began September 2025 and were slated to run to spring 2026; full construction was to start fall 2026, with completion targeted for 2029. Metrolinx held public information sessions on the plans in October 2025. [Source: https://beachmetro.com/2025/10/02/metrolinx-information-sessions-on-future-plans-for-danforth-go-station-set-for-oct-2-and-9/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Major Transit Station Area (MTSA) zoning. The City has published zoning-for-Major-Transit-Station-Areas planning material specific to Ward 19 (part of the citywide MTSA framework tied to stations like Main Street and the Danforth GO corridor), a live planning-policy thread relevant to future density near those stations. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/8e22-city-planning-zoning-for-major-transit-station-areas-toronto-east-york-Ward-19.pdf, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Growth/development, ward-wide (dated context). An October 2023 UrbanToronto ward-level development report characterized Ward 19 as seeing growth activity "enhancing the east end"; treat as historical texture pending a fresh pull, not a current count. [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2023/10/utpro-instant-reports-growth-ward-19-beaches-east-york-enhancing-east-end.54089, accessed 2026-08-14 — title/existence confirmed via search, full text not independently re-fetched this pass]
  • Active City capital works, 2026. The City's own Ward 19 profile page lists a substantial, multi-year Basement Flooding Protection Program along St. Clair Avenue East, Westview Boulevard, and local roads (Phases 1-4, notices running from February 2025 into May 2026), plus Danforth Avenue watermain/sewer upgrades (Main St. to Victoria Park Ave., notices running December 2025-June 2026), Victoria Park Avenue watermain/ sewer replacement and bridge rehabilitation, and Woodbine Avenue bridge rehabilitation under the Metrolinx rail corridor. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-19-beaches-east-york/, accessed 2026-08-14, page itself dated "modified June 8, 2026"]

Transit & development

Subway: TTC Line 2 (Bloor-Danforth) runs along/near the ward's northern edge; Main Street Station sits just north of Danforth Avenue in the ward's western section (exact station-to-ward assignment not independently verified against the boundary polygon this pass). GO: the Lakeshore East line's Danforth GO station is mid-relocation/rebuild (§3), tied to the broader Lakeshore East expansion. Development: current live activity skews toward transit-adjacent infrastructure (Danforth GO, MTSA zoning) and basement-flooding/watermain capital works rather than a single large private redevelopment site, per the City's own construction-notice page.

What else is on your ballot

Every Toronto voter marks three races on October 26, 2026: this ward’s councillor (above), the citywide mayor, and one school trustee.

  • Mayor (citywide — the same race for every ward) — 43 candidates registered as of 2026-08-14, identical treatment: the mayoral race card.
  • School trustee — one seat, for whichever of Toronto’s four school boards (Toronto District School Board, Toronto Catholic District School Board, Conseil scolaire catholique MonAvenir, Conseil scolaire Viamonde) matches your school-support designation. Trustee-district boundaries do not in general line up with the 25 council wards, and a verified address-to-trustee-district crosswalk is not on file for this ward — a named gap, not a guess (see below). 39 TDSB and 21 TCDSB trustee candidates are registered citywide as of 2026-08-14; find your own designation and voting place at MyVote.
