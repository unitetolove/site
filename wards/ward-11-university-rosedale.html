Ward 11 — University-Rosedale

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

University-Rosedale runs from the University of Toronto's St. George campus and the Annex, through Yorkville and Harbord Village, to Rosedale and part of Moore Park — an area mixing dense student/rental housing, the Discovery District's research institutions, and some of Toronto's wealthiest low-rise residential streets. Its biggest current civic story is a $50-million donation from the Weston Family Foundation, approved by City Council in 2026, to fund a major revitalization of Queen's Park North.

Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (per Wikipedia, reproducing the Elections Ontario boundary text): begins at College Street & Bay Street, north along Bay to Charles Street West, east along Charles to Yonge Street, south along Yonge to Charles Street East, east to Mount Pleasant Road, north to Bloor Street East, east to Sherbourne Street North, north to Rosedale Valley Road, generally east to the Don River, generally north along the Don to Pottery Road, then along Pottery Road to Bayview Avenue, generally north/northwest along Bayview to the CPR line, continuing along further boundary features. Named areas commonly associated with the ward: the Annex, Harbord Village, Yorkville, Seaton Village, Little Italy (part), Rosedale, and the Discovery District (University of Toronto St. George campus and adjoining research institutions). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_11_University%E2%80%94Rosedale, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_District, accessed 2026-08-14]

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Queen's Park North revitalization ($50M Weston Family donation). In March 2026 City Council authorized staff to negotiate a donation agreement with the Weston family (~$50M capital, plus an estimated $43M over 20 years — roughly $2.15M/year — for maintenance, operations, and programming, paid to the University of Toronto, which owns the land) to fund a major revitalization of Queen's Park North. The concept plan — which preserves the park's historic tree canopy while adding food services, washrooms, year-round programming, and Indigenous placekeeping — went to the Infrastructure and Environment Committee June 10, 2026, and City Council adopted it June 25, 2026. The ward's own councillor sponsored the enabling report and requested a councillor-chaired advisory board for the project. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-welcomes-proposed-donation-to-support-revitalization-of-queens-park-north/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/queen-s-park-north-weston-family-9.7121260, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/ex/comm/communicationfile-205777.pdf, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Active City capital works, 2026. The City's own Ward 11 profile page lists an extensive set of live/recent construction notices — watermain replacement on Bedford Road, Cecil Street, Clinton Street, Howland Avenue, Palmerston Avenue, Pears Avenue, and Rowanwood Avenue (transmission main); the Harbord/Hoskin/Huron corridor watermain, sewer, resurfacing, and bike-lane project (long-running, with construction-impact notices into late 2025); road reconstruction on Boswell Avenue and Grenville St./Dr. Emily Stowe Way; a Governor's Road bridge rehabilitation over Moore Park Ravine; a Don Valley Parkway ramp rehabilitation over Bayview Avenue; and a Rosehill Pumping Station noise-attenuation panel installation (notice dated April 2026). A general 2026 local road-resurfacing notice for the ward was also issued in January 2026. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-11-university-rosedale/, accessed 2026-08-14, page dated "modified June 1, 2026"]

Major capital projects: none of the scale of an Ontario Line station were found for this ward this pass; the largest current item is the Queen's Park North revitalization (§3), a public-realm/park project rather than a transit project. Development: City planning activity found this pass concentrates on Queen's Park North and sustained watermain/road infrastructure renewal (Harbord corridor, Rowanwood, Bedford) rather than a single large private redevelopment site. What else is on your ballot

Every Toronto voter marks three races on October 26, 2026: this ward’s councillor (above), the citywide mayor, and one school trustee.

  • Mayor (citywide — the same race for every ward) — 43 candidates registered as of 2026-08-14, identical treatment: the mayoral race card.
  • School trustee — one seat, for whichever of Toronto’s four school boards (Toronto District School Board, Toronto Catholic District School Board, Conseil scolaire catholique MonAvenir, Conseil scolaire Viamonde) matches your school-support designation. Trustee-district boundaries do not in general line up with the 25 council wards, and a verified address-to-trustee-district crosswalk is not on file for this ward — a named gap, not a guess (see below). 39 TDSB and 21 TCDSB trustee candidates are registered citywide as of 2026-08-14; find your own designation and voting place at MyVote.
