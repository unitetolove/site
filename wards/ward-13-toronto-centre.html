Ward 13 — Toronto Centre

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

Toronto Centre is the heart of downtown — Regent Park (Canada's first social-housing development, now mid redevelopment), St. James Town (one of Canada's most densely populated neighbourhoods), Cabbagetown, the Church-Wellesley Village, St. Lawrence, the Eaton Centre, and part of the financial district all sit inside this ward. It's dense, fast-growing, and carries more live City infrastructure work — watermain, sewer, bridge, streetscape — than almost any other ward on file, plus a major transit project (the Ontario Line's East Harbour station) landing at its eastern edge.

  • Seats: 1
  • Incumbent: Chris Moise — running in 2026: yes — open seat: no

2022 result: Chris Moise won by 30.2 percentage points over the runner-up — a landslide, not a close call. Source: City of Toronto official 2022 election results, as_of 2026-08-14.

Walied Khogali Ali is a candidate for Toronto City Council, Ward 13 (Toronto Centre), registered on May 1, 2026. Geography & boundaries

Legally defined (Elections Ontario boundary text, reproduced on Wikipedia): starts at Sherbourne St. North & Bloor St. East, north to Rosedale Valley Road, east to the Don River, south along the Don to Mill Street, west to Parliament Street, west to The Esplanade/Berkeley, along The Esplanade to Yonge, north to Front St. W., southwest to Bay, north to Dundas W., east to Yonge, north to College, west to Bay, north to Charles St. W., east to Yonge, south to Charles St. E., east to Mount Pleasant Road, northeast to Bloor, east back to Sherbourne. In plain terms: bounded roughly by Bay/College/Yonge/Dundas on the west, The Esplanade on the south, the Don River on the east, and Rosedale Valley Road/Bloor/Mount Pleasant/Charles on the north. Community council: Toronto and East York. Named areas: Regent Park, St. James Town, Cabbagetown, Church and Wellesley, St. Lawrence, Garden District, Toronto Metropolitan University, the Eaton Centre, part of the financial district (east side of Bay St.). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_13_Toronto_Centre, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/947e-CityPlanning-Map-Ward-13.pdf (City of Toronto ward map), accessed 2026-08-14]

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • East Harbour / Ontario Line. East Harbour, on the ward's eastern edge (Leslieville/Riverside border area), is planned as one of Toronto's largest single employment developments — roughly 60 acres, ~1.3M sq ft of office space, with potential to employ up to 50,000 people at full build-out. An Ontario Line station is planned on-site, connecting to downtown and, via a Science Centre interchange, to the Eglinton Crosstown. The East Harbour Transit Hub is targeted to open 2028 (GO Transit) and 2031 (Ontario Line). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Harbour_Transit_Hub, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2022/ex/bgrd/backgroundfile-223065.pdf (City staff report on East Harbour Transit-Oriented Communities), accessed 2026-08-14]
  • St. Lawrence library relocation. The Toronto Public Library's St. Lawrence branch (171 Front St. E.) is relocating; the City confirmed in December 2025 it purchased a heritage building at 339 Queen St. E. for a new branch targeted to open 2028, after an earlier plan to co-locate at 125 The Esplanade (inside the rebuilt St. Lawrence Market North) proved too complex/costly. Some neighbourhood pushback was reported over the new site being nearly 1 km from the current one. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/news/toronto-public-librarys-st-lawrence-branch-to-relocate-to-new-30000-square-foot-location-on-the-esplanade/, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://www.blogto.com/city/2025/12/toronto-neighbourhood-library-branch-1-km-away/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Mixed-use development, ward-wide (dated context). A June 2023 UrbanToronto ward-level development report characterized Ward 13 as having significant mixed-use development activity underway; treat as historical texture pending a fresh pull, not a current count. [Source: https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2023/06/utpro-instant-reports-mixed-use-development-going-big-ward-13-toronto-centre.52895, accessed 2026-08-14 — page title/existence confirmed via search, full text not independently re-fetched]
  • Active City capital works, 2026. The City's own Ward 13 profile page lists an unusually dense set of live 2026 construction notices — watermain replacement and road resurfacing on Berkeley, Bowman/Sackville, Ontario Street, Scott Street, Grosvenor/Queen's Park Crescent, and Yonge Street (Melinda to Wellington); preliminary design work on Church/Wellesley and Monteith Street infrastructure; and a Sherbourne Street bridge rehabilitation over Rosedale Valley Road (notice dated September 2025). Several notices carry 2026 dates as recent as July 2026. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-13-toronto-centre/, accessed 2026-08-14, page itself dated "modified July 31, 2026"]

Transit & development

Subway: the ward is served by parts of TTC Line 1 (Yonge-University, e.g. College, Wellesley, Bloor-Yonge stations sit on/near its boundary) and Line 2 (Bloor-Danforth, e.g. Castle Frank near the eastern edge); exact station-to-ward assignment not independently verified against the boundary polygon — named as general context, not a formal join. Major transit project: the Ontario Line's East Harbour station (§3), plus the existing GO/Union Station corridor at the ward's southwest edge. Development: City planning activity is notably concentrated around East Harbour (employment lands) and the St. Lawrence/Waterfront area (see library relocation, §3) rather than one single site.

What else is on your ballot

Every Toronto voter marks three races on October 26, 2026: this ward’s councillor (above), the citywide mayor, and one school trustee.

  • Mayor (citywide — the same race for every ward) — 42 candidates registered as of 2026-08-14, identical treatment: the mayoral race card.
  • School trustee — one seat, for whichever of Toronto’s four school boards matches your school-support designation. Trustee-district boundaries do not in general line up with the 25 council wards; here is what this project has established for THIS ward, tiered by how confidently each is sourced:
    • Conseil scolaire Viamonde (French public) — Toronto-Centre (Secteur 2, Annexe CENTRAL) (race card) — confirmed exact from the board’s own official regulatory determination document (Détermination et répartition des conseillers scolaires, Conseil scolaire Viamonde, March 2026, Annexe C).
    • TCDSB — not established for this ward: no reliable source pairs it to a specific TCDSB ward (a named gap, not a guess).
    • CSC MonAvenir (French Catholic) — this ward falls in one of MonAvenir’s two Toronto régions (Toronto-Est or Toronto-Ouest); the board has not published a ward-level boundary document, so which région applies here is not resolved — a region-level statement, not a guess at which one.
    • Toronto District School Board (TDSB) — local reporting places this ward in TDSB Ward 7 — not yet verified against an official board document (race card); see this ward’s precision note below.
    39 TDSB and 21 TCDSB trustee candidates are registered citywide as of 2026-08-14. Whatever the tiers above show, MyVote is the authoritative resolver for your own address — check it before you vote: MyVote.

This ward’s trustee-crosswalk precision note: TDSB CONTESTED: CP24 (2026-04-28, dedicated ward-map article) names Toronto Centre in Ward7; Beach Metro (2026-05-14) separately describes a new Ward5 built from 'former Toronto-Danforth + former Toronto Centre + former Spadina-Fort York.' One city ward cannot sit in two TDSB wards, so this is a genuine unresolved conflict between two secondary sources, not a guess - see receipts. Provisional value (Ward7) chosen because CP24's article is a dedicated, single-purpose ward-boundary piece using current city-ward names throughout, versus Beach Metro's incidental use of pre-2026 TDSB ward labels. TCDSB: NOT ESTABLISHED. Viamonde exact. MonAvenir not disambiguated.
