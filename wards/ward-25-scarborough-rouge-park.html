Ward 25 — Scarborough-Rouge Park

Reading this in real time: every registrant figure below — who’s filed, incumbency, candidate counts — is a snapshot as of 2026-08-14, from the City Clerk’s candidate feeds. Nominations close Friday, August 21, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. — this page re-pulls and re-stamps every registrant figure after that date. Geography, transit, development and census figures are structural facts that don’t move at nomination close and are dated separately, section by section.

Scarborough-Rouge Park is Toronto's southeastern-most ward, its boundary tracing the Rouge River almost the whole way around — north, east and along part of the lakeshore — to the city's edge with Pickering and Durham Region. It takes in West Rouge, Port Union and Highland Creek, is served by GO Transit's Lakeshore East line at Rouge Hill GO Station, and holds a large share of Toronto's portion of Rouge National Urban Park, Canada's first national urban park — currently mid-way through a $21-million visitor centre build and a multi-year beach and marsh restoration.

Geography & boundaries

Legal boundary (Elections Ontario legal description, reproduced on Wikipedia; the 25 provincial and 25 municipal wards have shared these same boundaries since the *Better Local Government Act, 2018* took effect December 1, 2018): commencing at the intersection of the Rouge River with the city's northerly limit; thence easterly, generally southerly and generally southwesterly along the city's northerly, easterly and southerly limits (i.e., along the Rouge River/Pickering-Durham boundary and then west along the Lake Ontario shoreline) to the southerly production of Morningside Avenue; thence north along that production and Morningside Avenue to Highway 401; thence west along the highway to Neilson Road; thence generally north along Neilson Road to Morningside Avenue; thence north along Morningside Avenue to an electric power transmission line; thence east along that transmission line to the Rouge River; thence generally north along the river back to the point of commencement. In plain terms: the Rouge River and the city limit form the ward's northern and eastern edge nearly end to end; Morningside Avenue and Neilson Road form most of its western edge; and only the stretch of lakeshore east of Morningside Avenue's southward line (roughly West Rouge/Port Union) is this ward's shoreline — the shoreline further west (Guildwood, West Hill's waterfront) belongs to the neighbouring ward. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_25_Scarborough%E2%80%94Rouge_Park, accessed 2026-08-14]

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Rouge National Urban Park's new visitor, learning and community centre. Following a $21-million contract award announced January 2025, construction officially began on the park's new visitor, learning and community centre in 2025, sited near the Toronto Zoo; the building is billed as an "iconic gateway" into the park with immersive Indigenous, natural, cultural and agricultural exhibits, and includes an Indigenous Participation Plan tied to the ten First Nations of the park's First Nations Advisory Circle. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/parks-canada/news/2025/01/government-of-canada-announces-intent-to-significantly-expand-rouge-national-urban-park.html, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/rouge/info/retrospective-review/2025, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Rouge Beach Improvements Project. Parks Canada awarded a $9.47-million contract in fall 2025 to restore wetland and forest habitat, build a 2.3-km accessible trail linking the lakeshore to Mast Trail, add a new washroom at the upper beach parking area, and re-naturalize the lower parking lot; Rouge Beach and Marsh are closed for construction with reopening anticipated mid-2027. As of this park page's own note, the Zoo Road Day Use Area and Rouge Beach and Marsh remain closed to visitors. [Source: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/rouge/info/retrospective-review/2025, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/rouge, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Pickering Lands transfer. In January 2025 the federal government announced its intent to transfer Transport Canada's Pickering Lands (high-conservation-value lands originally assembled for a never-built airport) to Parks Canada for inclusion in Rouge National Urban Park, following consultation with First Nations and the public; the lands sit north of Highway 407 in Durham, outside this ward, but the announcement and its 10th-anniversary framing were explicitly tied to the park that spans this ward. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/parks-canada/news/2025/01/government-of-canada-announces-intent-to-significantly-expand-rouge-national-urban-park.html, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Hemlock woolly adelgid detected. In 2025 Parks Canada confirmed this invasive, aphid-like insect (a threat to eastern hemlock stands) inside Rouge National Urban Park, and closed unauthorized trails in affected areas while monitoring the infestation's spread. [Source: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/rouge/info/retrospective-review/2025, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Active City of Toronto capital works, per the ward's own City profile page. The City's Ward 25 page (dated "modified June 4, 2026") lists a substantial, multi-year slate of infrastructure work specific to this ward: local road resurfacing under the 2026 Capital Works Program (pre-construction notice dated March 2026); a power plant demolition, electrical equipment installation and site restoration at Beare Hill Park (notices May 2024–March 2025); culvert rehabilitation/replacement over several ward watercourses — Beechgrove Drive (over West Hill Watercourse), Lawson Road (over Centennial Creek), Lawrence Avenue East (over Adams Creek), and Plug Hat Road (over Little Rouge Creek); bridge rehabilitation on Lawrence Avenue (over Highland Creek); an emergency closure and reopening of Stott's Bridge on Twyn Rivers Drive over the Rouge River (July–November 2024); sanitary forcemain replacement on Greyabbey Trail, Island Road, and Lawrence Avenue East; watermain cleaning/relining on Morningside Avenue and Sewells Road; road reconstruction and green infrastructure on Orchard Park, West Hill Drive and Zaph Avenue (pre-construction notices dated March 2026); ditch regrading/road reconstruction on East Avenue and Island Road (May 2026); and stream rehabilitation on Morningside Creek south of the Casebridge outfall (May–June 2026). Separately, sediment removal and maintenance at the Mattamy Rouge Stormwater Management Pond (explicitly noted as "outside Rouge National Urban Park") recurred in both 2024 and 2025. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-25-scarborough-rouge-park/, accessed 2026-08-14, page itself dated "modified June 4, 2026"]

Transit & development

  • GO Transit — Lakeshore East line. Rouge Hill GO Station sits on the Lake Ontario shore in West Rouge, inside this ward; travelling east, it is the last Toronto station before the line crosses into Durham Region. It is a major commuter transfer point with large parking lots and is served locally by TTC route 38 (see below), plus GO Transit bus route 51 and Durham Region Transit route 103. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouge_Hill_GO_Station, accessed 2026-08-14] The Lakeshore East line crosses the Rouge River — this ward's own eastern boundary — on a heritage rail bridge built around 1905 (a listed provincial heritage property with stone masonry end piers and a steel deck truss); Metrolinx completed a life-extension rehabilitation of the bridge in late 2022 rather than replace it, owing to its heritage status. Separately, the Lakeshore East Rail Corridor Expansion project (part of GO Expansion/Regional Express Rail) is preparing the wider corridor for a fourth track and electrification, though the specific construction tasks listed for that project (bridge widenings, retaining walls, the East Harbour Transit Hub) are concentrated well west of this ward, between the Don River and Warden Avenue. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeshore_East_line, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Guildwood GO Station — a caution, not a claim. Some general Scarborough-area transit summaries mention Guildwood GO Station near this ward; per the boundary analysis in §2, Guildwood sits in the neighbourhood of the same name, which this brief does not confirm as inside Ward 25 (it reads as adjacent Ward 24 Scarborough–Guildwood territory) — flagged here so it is not mistakenly counted as this ward's own station. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hill,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • TTC bus service. Per the Highland Creek neighbourhood's own sourcing (a tertiary source, not independently cross-checked against TTC's own route maps — see §8): route 38 Highland Creek is named for and runs through the neighbourhood, operating seven days a week between Scarborough Centre station and Rouge Hill GO Station along Ellesmere Road, Military Trail, Lawson Road and Port Union Road; routes 12 Kingston Road, 54/954 Lawrence East, 85 Sheppard East, 86 Scarborough, 95/995 York Mills, 116 Morningside, 905 Eglinton East Express and 986 Scarborough Express also serve parts of the neighbourhood. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Creek,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouge_Hill_GO_Station, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Eglinton East LRT — boundary-adjacent, not confirmed inside the ward. In May 2023 the City advanced a stand-alone light-rail line (reviving the earlier, Rob-Ford-cancelled Scarborough Malvern LRT under a new name) running from Kennedy Station via Eglinton Avenue, Kingston Road, Morningside Avenue and Sheppard Avenue toward Malvern. Morningside Avenue is this ward's own western boundary for part of its length, making the project directly boundary-adjacent; this brief does not assert the line itself runs inside the ward. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvern,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Development. No 2026 private-development-application pipeline for this ward was pulled (the City's Application Information Centre was not queried — see §8); the confirmed, ward-attributed capital-works activity in §3 skews toward watercourse infrastructure (bridges/culverts on the Rouge's tributary creeks), sanitary/water main renewal, and road reconstruction rather than a single large private redevelopment site.

Local commons

  • Highland Creek Branch, Toronto Public Library — 3550 Ellesmere Road, at Morrish Road, inside the Highland Creek neighbourhood (§2). Traces to a Mechanics' Institute library formed in 1889; became Highland Creek Public Library in 1895; joined the Scarborough Township public library board in 1955; moved to its present building in 1994 and won an Ontario Library Association award for it in 1996; became a Toronto Public Library branch with 1998 amalgamation. [Source: https://tpl.ca/locations/hc/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Rouge National Urban Park — the ward's largest single commons feature. Free and open to the public 365 days a year (parking lots open 7:30 am–9 pm daily); spans roughly 79 km² across the Greater Toronto Area, with West Rouge's own Wikipedia entry noting the neighbourhood "is also home to a portion of the Rouge National Urban Park," including the mouth of the Rouge River. [Source: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/rouge, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Rouge, accessed 2026-08-14]

[Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Creek,_Toronto, accessed 2026-08-14, citing https://www.toronto.ca/data/parks/prd/facilities/complex/2773/index.html]
