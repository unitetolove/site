Davenport sits in Toronto's west end, and is the only ward that spans both sides of the Dupont rail corridor — linking denser, condo-lined streets near Bloor and Ossington in the south to bungalow-lined hills around Fairbank, near Eglinton Avenue, in the north. It takes in Junction Triangle, Bloordale, Bloorcourt, Brockton Village, Dufferin Grove, Corso Italia, and Oakwood-Vaughan, among other named neighbourhoods. [Source: https://thelocal.to/ward-9-profile-toronto-city-council-election-davenport/, accessed 2026-08-14] The ward's biggest concrete civic project right now is the rebuild of Wallace Emerson Community Recreation Centre, park and child-care centre at Dufferin and Dupont — construction has been underway since 2022 and the recreation centre itself is scheduled to finish in fall 2026. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/construction-new-facilities/improvements-expansion-redevelopment/wallace-emerson-park-and-community-centre/, accessed 2026-08-14]

Geography & boundaries

Legal boundary (Elections Ontario's boundary description, as reproduced on Wikipedia): the ward runs from the intersection of Eglinton Avenue West and Dufferin Street, south along Dufferin to Rogers Road, east along Rogers Road to Oakwood Avenue, south along Oakwood to Holland Park Avenue, east to Winona Drive, south to Davenport Road, west along Davenport Road to Ossington Avenue, south to Dundas Street West, west to Dovercourt Road, south along Dovercourt (and its production) to the GO Transit railway, northwesterly along that railway to the southerly production of Keele Street, north along Keele to Lavender Road, east to Old Weston Road, northwest along Old Weston Road to Rogers Road, east along Rogers Road to the GO Transit railway east of Blackthorn Avenue, then north along that railway back to Eglinton Avenue West and east to the point of commencement. In plain terms (per the same source): the ward's north side borders CN railway tracks, Eglinton Avenue and Dufferin Street; its south and west sides border CP railway tracks; its east side borders Winona Drive, Ossington Avenue, Dundas Street and Davenport Road. Community council: Toronto and East York. Davenport is an amalgamation of the pre-2018 Ward 17 (northern section), pre-2018 Ward 18 (southern section) and part of the western edge of the pre-2018 Ward 19; it was first contested in the 2018 election. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_9_Davenport, accessed 2026-08-14]

Named neighbourhoods. Per Wikipedia's Ward 9 article: Fairbank, Oakwood-Vaughan, St. Clair Gardens, Corso Italia, Dovercourt Village, Bloordale Village, Bloorcourt Village, Brockton Village, the Junction Triangle, and the western part of Little Portugal (Rua Açores). [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_9_Davenport, accessed 2026-08-14] Per the sitting councillor's own "Our Ward" page (a source with an interest in the description but useful as a second data point): Little Portugal, Regal Heights, Junction Triangle, Brockton Village, Fairbank Village, Bloorcourt, Bloordale, and Dufferin Grove. [Source: https://www.alejandrabravo.ca/ward, accessed 2026-08-14] The two lists overlap substantially but are not identical (e.g., "Regal Heights" and "Dufferin Grove" appear only on the councillor page; "Oakwood-Vaughan," "St. Clair Gardens" and "Corso Italia" appear only on Wikipedia) — both are reported here rather than resolved into one list, per this template's instruction not to assert a formal neighbourhood-to-ward mapping beyond what a source itself states.

A correction to a common assumption, checked: the Stockyards District — the large mixed-use redevelopment site at St. Clair Avenue West and Keele Street (former Ontario Stock Yards land) — is not in Ward 9. It falls in Ward 5 York South-Weston, represented by a different councillor; coverage of the site's redevelopment identifies it as Ward 5. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/945a-CityPlanning-Map-Ward-5.pdf, accessed 2026-08-14] This tracks with Ward 9's own legal boundary above, which runs north along Keele Street (not west of it) in that stretch of the ward — the Stockyards site, centred west of Keele, sits outside the line. Flagged here because the two areas are geographically close and easy to conflate.

Civic texture — what’s live here

  • Wallace Emerson Park, Community Recreation Centre & Child Care Centre — the ward's largest active capital project. Part of the City's "Reimagine Galleria Master Plan" (the former 1970s Galleria Mall site at Dufferin and Dupont), this rebuild has been under construction since June 2022. The new community recreation centre will be roughly 89,500 sq. ft. — about double the size of the previous facility — with a 62-space child care centre, a full-size gym with a running track, dance/aerobics studios, a six-lane 25-metre pool and leisure pool. The City's own project page states the community recreation centre's construction is scheduled to be complete in fall 2026, with facility set-up and program migration running fall 2026 into winter 2027 (about three months). The adjoining park will grow from 2.66 ha (6.58 acres) to 3.16 ha (7.81 acres), delivered in two phases; phase one includes a BMX/skateboard park, a skating trail, a multipurpose sports/shinny pad, a splash pad and new landscaping, with partial park closures during phase-one construction (the west-side basketball court, drinking fountain, BMX park and the walkway connection to Floyd Street are named as unavailable during that phase). [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/construction-new-facilities/improvements-expansion-redevelopment/wallace-emerson-park-and-community-centre/, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • Bloor GO/UP Express–to–Dundas West subway pedestrian tunnel, under construction after a decade-long delay. A 50-metre underground connection between Bloor GO/UP Express station and the TTC's Dundas West subway station (Line 2) was first announced around 2015 but wasn't formally contracted until 2023; construction began in 2024 and was continuing as of June 2025, with elevator shafts and staircases underway and major excavation still to come at that point. Metrolinx had not given an official opening date as of that report, though a transit advocate cited an unofficial spring/summer 2026 target. Currently, riders transfer by walking 5–8 minutes along Bloor and Dundas streets; the tunnel is expected to cut that to about two minutes. [Source: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/up-express-ttc-tunnel-1.7563769, accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloor_GO_Station, accessed 2026-08-14]
  • New underground connection planned between Dufferin subway station and a 33-storey development at Bloor/Dufferin. By January 2024, the TTC had reached agreement with a developer to build, maintain and own a new accessible, underground station connection from a proposed 33-storey mixed-use building at the southwest corner of Bloor Street West and Dufferin Street (90 Croatia Street) into Dufferin station; the connection was expected to open in 2026. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dufferin_station, accessed 2026-08-14, citing a TTC board report dated 2024-01-25]
  • Active City of Toronto capital works and bridge-rehabilitation notices, ward-wide. The City's own Ward 9 profile page lists a long, currently-live set of construction notices, including: Dupont Street (under Metrolinx rail) bridge rehabilitation (construction update dated August 2025, second update dated November 2025); St. Clair Avenue West (under Metrolinx rail) bridge rehabilitation; Brock Avenue (under Metrolinx rail) bridge rehabilitation, including a special overnight-work notice dated January 2026; Dufferin Street (Bloor to Geary) road reconstruction and resurfacing (multiple 2024–2025 notices); a new sanitary sewer replacement on St. Clair Avenue West (pre-construction notice dated March 2026); and a new laneway reconstruction (Habibi Laneway/North Norman West Caledonia Laneway, pre-construction notice dated April 2026) — the most recent items on the page as of this access date. The page also lists the multi-phase Basement Flooding Protection Program (Fairbank-Silverthorn storm system), with construction notices for individual streets (Earlscourt Avenue, Earlsdale Avenue, Summit/Gilbert Avenues, Harvie/Redhill/Eglinton Avenue West, Oakwood Avenue, Rowntree/Chambers Avenues) dated across 2025. [Source: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-9-davenport/, accessed 2026-08-14, page itself last modified 2026-04-07]

Transit & development

TTC. Line 2 Bloor–Danforth subway stations serve the ward's Bloor Street spine: Lansdowne station (691 Lansdowne Avenue, on the edge of Bloordale Village, in the Dovercourt-Wallace Emerson-Junction area) and Dufferin station (at Dufferin/Bloor, near Dufferin Grove Park and the Bloor–Gladstone library branch) are both within or immediately adjacent to the ward per the named-neighbourhood sourcing above; Dundas West station sits just outside/at the ward's western edge. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansdowne_station_(Toronto), accessed 2026-08-14] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dufferin_station, accessed 2026-08-14] Bus routes named in nearby-neighbourhood sourcing include the 47 Lansdowne, 29/929 Dufferin, and 18 Caledonia. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonia-Fairbanks, accessed 2026-08-14]

GO Transit / UP Express. Bloor GO Station, on the Kitchener line and Union Pearson Express, sits just north of Bloor Street between Lansdowne Avenue and Dundas Street West — within the Junction Triangle area. It is one of the few GO stations with no car parking, but has direct bicycle-path access via the West Toronto Railpath (built on a former CPR right-of-way). Ridership was around 200/day before UP Express opened; Metrolinx has projected roughly 2,000 riders/day by 2031. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloor_GO_Station, accessed 2026-08-14] See §3 above for the in-progress pedestrian tunnel to Dundas West subway station.

Line 5 Eglinton Crosstown (LRT), ward-edge note. The Fairbank neighbourhood's nearest Line 5 stations are named as Caledonia, Fairbank and Oakwood. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonia-Fairbanks, accessed 2026-08-14] This brief cannot confirm precisely how much of the Fairbank/Oakwood-Vaughan area named in §2 falls inside Ward 9's polygon versus a neighbouring ward — see §8.

Development pattern. Beyond the two named projects in §3 (Wallace Emerson's mixed-use master plan context, and the Dufferin/Bloor 33-storey tower feeding a new subway connection), this research did not locate a current, ward-specific development-application tracker or count (the equivalent of the dated UrbanToronto "instant report" used for other wards in this project was not found live for Ward 9 — see §8).

Get involved — from thirty seconds to a year

Everything on this page is published so that anyone who lives here can use it: there is no membership, no gate and nothing to belong to. If you want to do something with it, the doors below start at thirty seconds and stop wherever you like — every one of them is optional, every one of them is free to copy or fork, and none of them asks who you vote for.

  • The ladder — what you can do today: thirty seconds, an hour, an evening, a week, a season, a year. Every rung a real door, every rung optional.
  • The Harvest — add three lines to one public record: a vision for 2030, a complaint about what is broken, or a neighbour worth naming.
  • Add your name to the count — a name and a way to reach you is the whole first ask; everything after that is optional.
  • The mobilization framework — for anyone who already leads a room and wants that room, and every room like it, to act together. Free, forkable, evidence-based, nothing to join.
  • The plan — one year, all-out — what this city could do in a single year, month by month, with every number labelled as a target rather than a promise.
  • The conversation kits — free, open kits for running your own conversation about Toronto’s future, at a kitchen table or in a hall.
  • Mutual aid — how neighbours already help each other across Toronto, what a block circle is, and how to start one where you live.
  • The letters — written to the people this city keeps writing about instead of to. Read one; pass it to someone it is about.

This is a neighbours’ project rather than a campaign or an organization: it publishes tools, data and evidence, and leaves every decision to the person reading. Nothing here endorses a candidate or a party, and nothing here needs our permission to be used.

  • The formal neighbourhood-to-ward geospatial join (named in the plan §6) was not built or run; every neighbourhood/commons association above is reported only as far as a cited source itself states it, per this template's own instruction.
  • A full address-to-trustee-district crosswalk is not complete for every board (school board trustee races do not share the 25-ward boundary map): for this ward, TDSB carries not established and TCDSB is not established; CSC MonAvenir is resolved only to a region (Toronto-Est or Toronto-Ouest), never a ward, for every ward citywide.
