The connector season

Every real room is held together by a membrane — the shared history, language, and trust that make it a room at all. Membranes are why rooms work. They’re also why good ideas move so slowly between them: trust does not broadcast.

Mass channels can’t fix this. A post reaches everyone and convinces no one. The highest-bandwidth thing that ever crosses a membrane is a person. So the distribution layer of this whole strategy is not a channel — it’s a role: the connector, one person who moves room to room, only ever at a room’s own invitation, to listen, to answer anything, and to carry — with permission — what the last room taught them.

If you want to run a connector season in your own city or your own network, here is the pattern:

Invitation-in, never outreach-armed. The connector convenes nothing and holds no stage. A standing public page describes the offer: who can invite, what a visit looks like, what’s asked of the room (nothing). The room hosts, sets the terms, keeps its own name on its own gathering. A personal note to someone you genuinely know inside a room may let them know the offer exists — the personal relationship is itself the membrane being crossed — but only the room’s invitation books anything. This inverts outreach into consent, which is the only register in which a congregation, a union local, a campus club, and a private dining club can all say yes.

A role, not a personality. No follower channel, no self-promotion, no face required on any page. Credibility arrives the honest way: through the visit, and through real shared tribe membership — sport, food, faith, trade, travel, craft, the homeland, the street. A connector worth inviting doesn’t have to claim standing; in some real way they’re already one of you.

Rhythm, stated honestly. A serious season is bounded — about ninety days. A handful of rooms a day, clustered by geography so travel never eats the listening. Weekends run lighter and gentler on purpose: those are the days spiritual communities and weekend-native groups gather, and they cost more to hold well. Alongside the physical circuit, a standing residency in the city’s text channels, where one person can hold many conversations asynchronously and stay reachable after the season ends. Burnout rules apply to connectors first, not last.

The room owns the record. If a room wants its visit recorded, the room records it. Release is decided at booking from four plain classes — no recording · kept in the room (the default) · public after a delay · public now (rare, explicit). Nothing goes public without two keys turned, the room’s and the speaker’s; either key can downgrade at any time; neither can upgrade alone. Audio before video, always, by design: voice travels before face.

Designed to end. The season closes and the role dissolves. What remains is the point: rooms that now know each other, paths a neighbour can walk without any connector involved. Any room can name its own ambassador to its neighbouring rooms tomorrow, with this page as the starter kit. The measure of a connector is the measure of every steward in this design: how fast they become unnecessary.

Bring a connector, or be one

If you lead a room and want a visit — or want to be your network’s own connector — the offer and the kit are free. Start here · the kits.
