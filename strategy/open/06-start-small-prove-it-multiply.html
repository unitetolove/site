Start small, prove it, multiply

Everything in this strategy obeys one growth grammar, and once you see it you can run it on anything: start at the smallest version that’s real, prove it with a receipt, then multiply the pattern — never the institution.

Start small means trust-sized. Coordination that runs on people actually knowing each other has a real ceiling — roughly five in full trust, fifteen as a close team, fifty as a working community, a hundred and fifty who still know each other’s names. Toronto’s unit is the block circle: ten or so households on a stretch of street, meeting because they chose to, doing whatever their street needs. No circle answers to another. When a unit outgrows its ceiling, it splits or federates through a shared kit and a shared symbol — it never merges into a mass, because a mass can’t run on trust and immediately needs the bureaucracy this whole design exists to avoid.

Prove it means a public receipt. One block’s tool library, photographed and counted. One clean-up morning with before-and-after on the record. One dinner that harvested three visions and a complaint that got fixed. The receipt does two jobs: it keeps us honest, and it’s the seed crystal — the thing the next street over can point at and say “we could do that.” Nothing recruits like a proof. This is why the calendar lands one visible proof every single week of the sprint: the proofs are the drumbeat; the conversations are the song.

Multiply means fork, not franchise. When a pattern works, the move is never “open a chapter” — it’s “publish the kit.” A franchise needs permission, brand control, reporting up. A fork needs a copy. Every kit, framework, and playbook here is free to copy, change, and redistribute without asking; a room that rebuilds a kit to fit itself is the success condition, not drift. This is also the capture-proofing: nothing this open can be taken over, because the moment someone tries, everyone else forks the parts that still work.

The deeper pattern comes from the design framework this movement runs on — a system for learning from nature and from everything humanity has studied about how living systems stay alive. Its relevant laws here: feed the edges, where systems actually grow; let evidence rewire structure, so the pattern improves with each fork; and when something crosses a scale threshold, substitute the mechanism — a group of five, fifty, and fifteen hundred are not the same unit grown larger, and pretending otherwise is how movements die of their own success. The framework is the operating system; this movement is its first application — which means the movement itself is a fork waiting to happen, on purpose.

And multiplication has a direction: outward, as a gift. The scenario’s final act isn’t Toronto winning anything — it’s the blueprints leaving: translated, versioned, carried by diaspora communities to the places they love, each on their own calendar. A solution proven in one city’s backyard becomes another city’s starting point, carried by someone who loves both ends of the journey. Demonstration first, export second. Take care of your own backyard, then hand over the tools.

So: don’t start a movement. Start a block. Prove one small thing in public. Publish the kit. The multiplying was never going to be your job — that’s what the crowd is for.

One conversation on your street or in your room, this month. Take a kit · see the framework behind all of this · be counted.
