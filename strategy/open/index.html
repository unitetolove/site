The Open Strategy — read the playbook, then take it

Most movements keep their strategy in a drawer. Here is ours, in the open, on purpose.

The reasoning is simple. This whole effort rests on a claim: that a city can coordinate itself in the open — no membership, no headquarters, no secrets, receipts for everything. A strategy that only worked while hidden would disprove that claim all by itself. So publishing the strategy isn’t a risk we’re managing. It’s the argument, made honestly. If these methods only work with surprise on their side, they were never going to hold a city together anyway. And if they work in daylight — copy them.

What stays private is short and principled: people (nobody’s personal information, nobody’s private conversation), safety-sensitive data, and the person who started this — who writes as the founding steward, is designing their own exit, and matters less every month by design.

The set

  1. How to convene without an organization — the core method: equip rooms that already exist; never build a stage.
  2. The connector season — how ideas cross between rooms without a broadcast machine: one person, invited in.
  3. Records, not endorsements — the election architecture: every candidate asked identically, every answer published, nobody ranked, nobody anointed.
  4. The publishing discipline — receipts, corrections, and why the author’s name is the one thing withheld.
  5. A year, school-shaped — why the calendar starts in September and treats voting day as the mid-sprint scoreboard.
  6. Start small, prove it, multiply — the replication grammar: one block, one proof, then fork.
  7. What gets decided before 2030 — the stakes, itemized and sourced, so the biggest claim in this project has to earn itself.
  8. Somewhere near you votes this fall — for everyone outside Toronto: your city, this method, this October.
  9. What’s proven and what isn’t — the status ledger: which parts of this machine have run, which are live but young, which are still designs.
  10. How to tell the real thing — seven checkable tells of a genuine implementation, for when something wearing this costume shows up near you. Run them on us first.

One idea runs through all ten. The framework behind this movement is a design system — patterns for how living things, including cities, stay alive: guard the slow stores, feed the edges, build so the picture can be checked, design your own exit. The movement is that framework pointed at one city, once. The framework is the operating system; Toronto is the first application. Yours could be the second. Read the mapping in full: the framework behind this plan. And the reasoning behind the year this set describes lives at the plan for the plan; our own sharing rules are published too, at how we share.

Everything here is free to copy, change, and run without asking. That’s not a license note; it’s the design. Nothing this open can be captured — the moment someone tries, everyone else takes the parts that still work and rebuilds the rest.
