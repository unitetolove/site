SIMULATED. Everything on this page concerns a simulation of deliberation by AI agents. No real residents were surveyed, and nothing here measures actual public opinion. No results from the system’s one full run are published anywhere on this site — our own review found the prompts colored the deliberations, and results publish only after a redesigned re-run clears our validity bar.

What would 1,600 Torontonians decide together?

Citizens' assemblies — random residents, real evidence, structured deliberation — have a strong record of producing decisions ordinary politics can't reach. Before anyone convenes a real one, we built something unusual: a complete simulation of a Toronto citizens' assembly, with 1,600 AI agents statistically raked to the city's real census — every ward, every age band, every income group — deliberating in rounds, clustering opinions, and reading expert briefings.

This is a simulation. No real residents were surveyed. It cannot tell you what Torontonians think. What it can do is test the machinery of a mass assembly — the deliberation formats, the briefing designs, the consensus measures — cheaply and in the open, before anyone spends public money or public trust on the real thing.

Why there are no results on this page

We ran the full system once, at 1,600-agent scale. We are not publishing those results — and the reason is the point of this whole site: our own review found the prompts we used colored the deliberations. A simulation whose questions lean is a push-poll with extra steps. Rather than publish impressive-looking numbers we can't stand behind, we're redesigning the prompt layer with neutral framing and a pre-registered analysis plan, fixing three known validity issues our own methods review documents, and re-running the system. Results publish when they survive that bar, and not before.

What's here now

  • The blueprint — what convening a real Toronto citizens' assembly would take: selection, staffing, briefing design, cost.
  • Methods and limits — how agent generation, deliberation, and opinion clustering work, and the three open validity issues we have not solved, stated plainly.
  • The engine — the simulator itself: census-raked agents for all 25 wards and 158 neighbourhoods, a 95-assertion test suite, and a browser control panel. Code and methods documentation open per our commons.

What's next

Prompt redesign → validity fixes → re-run → results, with the run's design published before the run so you can check whether we moved the goalposts. If you work on deliberation, opinion modeling, or survey methodology and see a flaw we missed, the corrections door is open — that's how this site works.
