The Harvest

One record for one Toronto. Three million of us hold the answers between us — what this city should become, what’s broken, and who is already quietly fixing it. The Harvest collects those answers, three lines at a time, from dinner tables, block circles, classrooms, congregations, assemblies — and from you, right now, in about thirty seconds.

Three lines, each optional — fill at least one: a vision (“Toronto in 2030 should ___”), a complaint (what’s broken — cross-streets optional, never an address), a nomination (a neighbourhood superhero). Everything joins the living record below, in aggregate.

Never collected, anywhere on this page: a nominee’s contact info, attendee names, a full street address, a full postal code. Cross-streets only, FSA only. Every submission returns a delete token that removes exactly that submission, whenever you want.

The thirty seconds
I have more than a line. An expert opinion, a research report, or a longer futures statement — depth, welcomed on its own track; it joins the record as its own "contributions" line.

Ran a dinner, an after-service circle, a classroom, an assembly? Set up the room once (kit type + headcount — a number, never a name list), then type in each person's sheet-line through the loop below, at your own pace.

The record is collecting, and the counter is held. Counts publish once a set number of real conversations has happened — no fake numbers, no pre-seeding, ever. The flip, when it comes, is a real public moment: totals, where the conversations happened, week by week, and the threshold itself, all disclosed together. What you add today is in the record and counts toward it.

The record publishes in aggregate only — counts, never anyone’s raw words. Individual visions and complaints are published only through a separate, consent-checked step, never automatically.

On paper somewhere? Paper is the record too

Nothing is lost by starting on paper. Every kit ships with a printed harvest sheet. Run your gathering, pass the sheet, keep it. Then, whenever you’re ready: pick “I’m entering a room’s harvest” above, set up the room, photograph the sheet if you like (it stays a private working copy, never published, deleted on a schedule), and type the lines in — yourself, or hand the photo to someone you trust to type. A sheet typed in Tuesday counts exactly like a form filled Friday night.

Want to run a conversation of your own? Everything you need is in the kits — dinners, after-service circles, block circles, story evenings, debates, assemblies, classrooms.
