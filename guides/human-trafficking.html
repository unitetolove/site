Human Trafficking

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if it helps someone who is being or has been trafficked — for sex or for labour — and is free and reachable in Toronto.

The one thing this whole guide exists to tell you

You do not have to go to the police to get legal status in Canada as a trafficking survivor. Immigration Canada's own guidance says it in these exact words:

"Applicants are not required to provide a law enforcement endorsement or report to substantiate their application and may self-identify to IRCC as a victim of human trafficking... The absence of a law enforcement endorsement or report is not a valid reason to refuse an application."

Most people in this situation believe the only way out runs through a police report — and that belief is often exactly what keeps them where they are. It isn't true. Telling your own story, directly to Immigration Canada, is enough on its own. The permit this unlocks — the Temporary Resident Permit for Victims of Human Trafficking — is fee-exempt and comes with federal health coverage. Full details are under "If you have no immigration status," below. If you take one thing from this page, take that.

Right now, and safely

These two lines are staffed to take a call from someone in the middle of being trafficked, right now, today — and neither one is a police line.

Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline Call 1-833-900-1010, free, confidential, 24/7/365, or use the chat at canadianhumantraffickinghotline.ca. Connects you to a network of 1,000+ frontline partners for emergency/transitional housing, mental health counselling, health care and legal aid. Accessible via Bell Relay Service (TTY or IP Relay) for Deaf and hard-of-hearing callers, and via text chat for anyone who prefers not to or cannot speak. No police involvement: the hotline's own words are that it is "not part of the police or the government," and separately that it is "not a government entity, nor... an immigration, investigative or law enforcement agency." No documents required. Immigration status: explicitly no barrier — the hotline's own page states everyone is welcome "regardless of age, gender or citizenship status" and that it serves people "of all genders, sexual orientations, immigration status, abilities and backgrounds." Free.

Victim Support Line (Ontario) Call 1-888-579-2888 (Greater Toronto Area direct line: 416-314-2447), free, 24/7, in most languages spoken in Ontario, or use online chat with 211 Ontario, Monday–Friday 7am–9pm. This is Ontario's own government referral line, named explicitly on the province's anti-human-trafficking page as a service survivors can be referred to — it connects you to counselling, the emergency funding described under "Legal help and compensation" below, and other supports. ontario.ca — Connect with supports for survivors of violence This is a government referral and counselling line, not a police line. Its own page does not state a policy on police involvement or immigration status one way or the other, so this guide does not claim either. No documents required. Free.

If you have no immigration status

The Temporary Resident Permit for Victims of Human Trafficking

Regularizes your status in Canada if you're a trafficking survivor who is currently out of status (and covers your dependants). An initial permit lasts at least 12 months when IRCC completes an in-depth assessment of your situation, or up to 12 months on a faster preliminary assessment when an in-depth review isn't yet possible (for example because the case is urgent, or you're part of a group identified at the same time) — either way, it's renewable for up to 3 years on a later application. Once you hold it, you can apply for a fee-exempt work and/or study permit, and you get health-care coverage through the federal Interim Federal Health Program if you're not otherwise insured. It covers both labour and sex trafficking under one set of criteria — being controlled by force or threat of force, or coerced into exploitative work (including forced labour, sexual exploitation, slavery, or organ removal). It is not a path to permanent residence on its own. canada.ca — TRP considerations for victims of human trafficking No police involvement: self-identification to IRCC is explicitly sufficient — see the quote above. Immigration status: this permit exists specifically for people with no current status; that's who it's for. Applications are processed by IRCC's Vulnerable Persons Unit (5343 Dundas Street West, Suite 105, Toronto ON M9B 6K5) — this is a mailing address, not a walk-in office — see FCJ Refugee Centre, below, for help preparing an application in person. Free (application, work permit, study permit and biometrics fees are all waived).

If you still hold a work permit, but your employer is the one hurting you

Open Work Permit for Vulnerable Workers A fee-exempt work permit ("there are no fees for this work permit," in IRCC's own words) that lets you leave an abusive employer and work for almost any other employer, without needing a new job offer first, while your immigration status stays valid. It's for someone who currently holds a valid employer-specific work permit, or who applied to extend one before it expired and is still waiting on a decision (unlike the TRP above, which is for someone already out of status). IRCC's own eligibility page defines "abuse" broadly: physical, sexual, financial or psychological abuse, or reprisal — and its listed examples are squarely labour-trafficking patterns: being forced into unsafe work, unsafe or unsanitary employer-provided housing, having your movement controlled, having pay stolen or withheld, or being threatened. canada.ca — Open work permit for vulnerable workers, eligibility · canada.ca — How to apply Police involvement: not required — IRCC's own application guidance lists a police or Canada Border Services Agency report as just one of several optional types of supporting evidence (alongside a sworn statement from you alone, a letter from a support organization, pay stubs, photos, or witness testimony); no police contact is mandatory to apply. Immigration status: for a worker who currently holds, or recently held, a valid employer-specific work permit — not for someone with no status at all. You must apply from inside Canada. Free.

For help understanding either of these and preparing an application, see FCJ Refugee Centre, under "If you're being exploited at work," below — its own page states it helps people apply for both.

If you're being exploited at work

State plainly: this is the thinnest part of what Toronto currently offers. Across this whole guide, only 4 of 15 entries are labour-trafficking-specific — the rest are general-purpose or oriented toward sex trafficking — and there is no Toronto shelter built specifically for adult labour-trafficking survivors, unlike the dedicated doors that exist for sex-trafficking and youth survivors elsewhere on this page. If you're trapped in agriculture, construction, domestic or care work, cleaning, or restaurant work, the doors below are real and working, but you will have to skip past several entries built for a different situation to find them.

Temporary Foreign Worker Program Tip Line Call 1-866-602-9448, free, confidential, 24/7 by phone (live agents in over 200 languages, Monday–Friday 6:30am–8pm ET), or report anytime online. The page's own listed examples of reportable abuse are classic forced-labour signs: being threatened or bullied, having your immigration status threatened, not being allowed to leave your worksite or housing, or someone holding your passport or documents. Anyone can report — a worker, a co-worker, a consulate, an advocacy group — on someone else's behalf. canada.ca — Report abuse of a temporary foreign worker No police involvement: this goes to Service Canada, not police, and the page states plainly "you do not have to give your name, phone number or LMIA/work permit number to make a report." Safe without immigration status: no status check is required to report on someone. Free.

Employment Standards Act / EPFNA Claim A free claim to Ontario's Ministry of Labour when an employer or recruiter has broken the Employment Standards Act or the Employment Protection for Foreign Nationals Act (EPFNA). EPFNA specifically covers foreign nationals working through a program like the Temporary Foreign Worker Program or Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program, and its own listed violations are labour- trafficking precursors: a recruiter charging fees, an employer charging hiring costs, or a recruiter or employer holding onto your passport. Filing a claim is free. ESA claims have a 2-year filing window; EPFNA claims get 3.5 years, specifically because migrant workers often can't come forward right away. Call the Employment Standards Information Centre at 416-326-7160 (toll-free in Ontario: 1-800-531-5551, TTY 1-866-567-8893) for help identifying your claim, or file online, by fax, or by mail. ontario.ca — Filing a claim under the Employment Standards Act No police involvement: this is an administrative labour-standards process — no report to police is required or implied anywhere on this page. Immigration status: not stated whether a fully undocumented worker (as opposed to one holding TFW/SAWP status) can file — not assumed either way. Free.

Migrant Workers Alliance for Change (MWAC) A member-led organizing and advocacy group for migrant farm workers, care workers, international students and other migrants. Its own site says plainly: "If you are a migrant or undocumented person, but are not a student, care worker, or farm worker, we can help you too." Connects migrants to each other, runs know-your-rights organizing, and does policy advocacy — it is a peer-organizing body, not a caseworker or crisis line. migrantworkersalliance.org No police involvement: not stated on the page. Safe without immigration status: explicit — see the quote above. No phone number or office address is published on the provider's own site; contact is by web form or social media. Free.

FCJ Refugee Centre — Anti-Human Trafficking Programs Free, holistic support for trafficked people covering both labour and sex trafficking: referrals to counselling, food security and legal support; help applying for the Open Work Permit for Vulnerable Workers and the Temporary Resident Permit above; orientation on labour rights and the signs of exploitation; plus three dedicated projects — the Migrant Workers Mobile Program, the Migrant Women's Counter Human Trafficking Alliance, and the Youth Alliance Against Human Trafficking (ages 14–24, see "If you're a young person," below). fcjrefugeecentre.org 208 Oakwood Ave. General office line 416-469-9754; the Migrant Workers Mobile Program itself is reached by WhatsApp at 647-971-2153, not the general office line. Safe without immigration status: explicit — the page's own words are "we offer support to people already in Canada" and "FCJ Refugee Centre does not work with agents or representatives, and all our services are free of charge." No police statement was found on the page either way. Free.

Somewhere to live

Aurora House A 7-bed transitional shelter in East Toronto for people who have experienced trafficking or exploitation, with a particular focus on women (16+) with young children and expectant mothers. aurorahouse.ca Immigration status: not stated on the page either way. Police involvement: not stated. Worth knowing before you rely on this: Aurora House's usual phone and email contact channels have shown a "technical difficulties" notice across multiple checks (2026-07-27, and reconfirmed again at this wave's 2026-08-01 seal check), directing people instead to email aurorahouse.admin@gmail.com. The organization itself remains active, not closed — its site describes marking 10 years of operation in 2026, runs an active fundraising campaign, and lists a mailing address (1774 Queen St East, Toronto, ON M4L 1G7) and a second email (aurorahouse@mnlct.org) via its operator, MNLC (Mennonite New Life Centre of Toronto) — so this reads as a contact-channel outage, not a sign the organization has shut down. Treat the phone/usual-email route as unreachable or unreliable right now, not a confirmed working door — if you can't get through, call the Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline or Victim Support Line above for another way in. Free.

Covenant House Toronto — trafficking-specific components Covenant House's 24/7 youth crisis shelter (96 beds total) includes 2 beds designated specifically for trafficking and exploitation survivors, plus a dedicated anti-human-trafficking team reachable at 1-800-435-7308 or (416) 593-4849, or by email at endht@covenanthouse.ca, for inquiries related to shelter and support services. For ages 16–24. (Covenant House's broader youth-homelessness shelter, drop-in and health services are a general youth resource, not trafficking-specific — see Guide 07, Housing, for its full entry.) covenanthousetoronto.ca Walk-in, no documents required. Immigration status not stated on the page. Police involvement not stated. The anti-trafficking team's own contact hours are Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–6 p.m. — not 24/7 — the crisis shelter itself remains staffed for walk-ins 24/7, and the national Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline above is a 24/7 phone alternative outside those hours. Free.

What this guide could not find: an adult, general-population, or labour-trafficking-specific shelter beyond these two. Aurora House's own page states the reason plainly rather than hiding it: Canada is facing "a critical shortage of housing and support services for exploited women," and "the demand for services far exceeds the available resources." There is no confirmed overflow placement if Aurora House's 7 beds are full — the Victim Support Line and the Victim Services Directory (ovss.findhelp.ca, reached via that line) are the best available routing, not a guaranteed bed.

Legal help and compensation

Free Legal Support for Survivors of Human Trafficking A free lawyer from a Ministry of the Attorney General team, for a survivor or potential victim of human trafficking of any age in Ontario (or a child's guardian applying on their behalf): help completing a restraining-order application, and representation at the hearing at any Ontario Court of Justice. The order can last up to 3 years, renewable, and only applies while the trafficker is in Ontario. Reached by calling the Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline above, which refers you to a lawyer. ontario.ca — Free legal support for survivors of human trafficking No police involvement: explicit — the page's own words are "a criminal case is not needed to apply for a restraining order against a trafficker." Immigration status: the page states this is for survivors "of all ages in Ontario," including if the trafficking happened outside Ontario, and does not state an immigration-status requirement. Free.

Victim Quick Response Program+ (VQRP+) Short-term emergency funding for victims of crime — including human trafficking, named explicitly on Ontario's own program page — plus their immediate family and witnesses, to cover essential costs right after a crime: emergency home-safety changes, basic necessities, travel, crime-scene clean-up, short-term counselling, and costs related to serious injury. This is the current emergency-funding program for this situation in Ontario. Available to applicants with no other financial means (for example, no private insurance) where no other publicly funded program covers the cost. Reached through a local Victim Crisis Assistance Ontario provider, found via the Victim Support Line above or the Victim Services Directory (ovss.findhelp.ca). ontario.ca — Connect with supports for survivors of violence Police involvement and immigration status: neither is stated on the page. Free to apply.

General legal or counselling support for survivors of violence who are not specifically trafficking survivors, and general victim-of-crime compensation questions, are outside this guide's selection rule — this guide only carries what's trafficking-specific.

If you're a young person

Boost Child & Youth Advocacy Centre (ages 11–18) A Toronto centre coordinating support for young survivors of sex trafficking and abuse: specialized mental-health support for girls 14–18, family counselling, three-tiered housing (crisis, intermediate, transitional), advocacy and case management. Say this plainly: this is not a no-police door. Boost's own homepage describes its model as supporting victims "all under one roof, from reporting the abuse to police, medical exam, therapy, court prep and support" — it is Toronto's police-and-Children's-Aid-partnered response centre. boostforkids.org Call 1-855-424-1100, 245 Eglinton Ave E, 2nd Floor. Police involvement: yes, explicitly — police and Children's Aid Society of Toronto are core to the model, disclosed here so you know before you call. Immigration status not stated. Free.

Elspeth Heyworth Centre for Women — Connect Young (ages 10–17) A prevention and peer-support program: group therapy and support, storytelling, life-skills and educational workshops, direct contact with survivors of lived experience, help understanding how traffickers use technology to target young people, and a safety-planning toolkit. Also engages parents who are struggling to communicate with their kids, to help prevent the vulnerability before it starts. ehcw.ca Contact Yougeita Jagdesh, 647-500-2978, yougeita@ehcw.ca; main office 2350 Finch Ave W Unit D, 416-663-2978. Police involvement: no statement is made on the page either way — unlike Boost above, which is explicit about its police-partnered model, this program's own page simply doesn't address the question, so this guide does not claim it either way. Immigration status not stated. Free.

For 14- to 24-year-olds specifically, FCJ Refugee Centre's Youth Alliance Against Human Trafficking (see "If you're being exploited at work," above) is a third door built for this age range.

If you're Indigenous

Indigenous Anti-Human Trafficking Liaison (IAHTL) Program A province-wide program from the Ontario Native Women's Association, delivered in Toronto through the Native Women's Resource Centre of Toronto. Connects Indigenous people looking to exit a trafficking situation with culturally appropriate, trauma-informed, wraparound support: survivor-led strategies, systems navigation, mentorship, and — in the program's own words — "safer spaces for survivors." It was, again in its own words, "created by and for Indigenous people in order to end Human Trafficking in our communities," shaped with guidance from survivors including youth and Two-Spirit people. onwa.ca — Anti-Human Trafficking General ONWA contact: 1-800-667-0816. Police involvement and immigration status: neither is stated on the program's own page. Open to any age — the program explicitly engages youth and Two-Spirit survivors alongside adults. Free.

What this guide could not find

  • No Toronto shelter built specifically for adult labour-trafficking survivors. Aurora House and Covenant House both exist and both help — but neither is built around a labour-trafficking situation specifically, and nothing equivalent for that situation was found.
  • The three government-funded residences for trafficked children and youth that Ontario's own provider list names (in Durham, Toronto, and Kenora) cannot be named or linked here — Ontario deliberately withholds the operator's identity on its public page, for safety, and directs all inquiries through the Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline above instead.
  • Strides Toronto and Native Child and Family Services of Toronto are both named on Ontario's government list as delivering trafficking-related services, but neither organization's own page states this in its own words — so neither is cited here as a trafficking-specific door. This may be closed by a future, more targeted check of their program pages rather than their homepages.
  • The Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline's often-repeated "200+ languages" figure is not printed in this guide. It's widely restated across the internet, including on other government pages about this same hotline. This seal pass found it currently restated on two of the hotline's own subpages (Contact; Who are we?) but not on the literal homepage. Blank beats fabricated: this guide states the hotline's confirmed facts (24/7/365, confidential, free, not law enforcement, immigration-status no-barrier, 1,000+ partner network) and leaves the language-count claim out pending confirmation.
  • No confirmed overflow shelter exists once Aurora House's 7 beds are full. The sector's own words (quoted above, under "Somewhere to live") describe a real, acknowledged shortage — not a gap in this guide's research.

Not in this guide — cross-guide boundary

One fact, one home:

  • Sex workers who are not being trafficked — Sex Workers' Safety & Health, one pointer, not duplicated. That guide's own hard selection rule exists specifically so a sex worker isn't defaulted into being treated as a trafficking victim — the same reason this guide doesn't reach into its territory.
  • Newcomer settlement and refugee-claimant services generally (not trafficking-specific) — Newcomers & Refugee Claimants, pointer only.
  • A crisis tonight that isn't specifically about trafficking — Guide 01: In Crisis Now, pointer only.
  • Emergency shelter and the housing system generally — Guide 07: Housing, pointer only, except for the trafficking-specific components of Covenant House and Aurora House cited above.
