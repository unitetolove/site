Jobs & Employment Services

Part of the Free Toronto guide fleet — a plain-language set of guides pulled from the Free Toronto Resource Atlas, our full 765-entry directory of free and low-cost Toronto resources. Every link on this page was checked directly against the provider's own page, most recently on 2026-07-30. Neutral civic reference information — not affiliated with any campaign, candidate, or advocacy organization.

This guide is about getting hired. Every entry below helps a Toronto resident find or get a job at no cost — job-search help, employment counselling, resume and interview support, placement programs, sector-specific hiring doors, and employment services built for a particular group.

This guide is not about getting qualified. Free training, credential/certification programs, academic upgrading, pre-apprenticeship training, tech bootcamps and skills-grant funding (Better Jobs Ontario, Ontario Bridge Training, NPower Canada, Pre-Apprenticeship Training, Hammer Heads, TDSB Adult Credit, freeCodeCamp, Microsoft Learn, and similar) live in a dedicated guide — not here. If a program's core offering is a course, a certificate, or a training grant, it's there, not here, even when the same agency also runs an employment-services desk covered by an entry below.

Not here either: - Campus career services and career closets — covered per-campus in the student guides for each Toronto school. This guide cites only off-campus, general-public doors. - ESL and workplace-language training — a separate guide, still in preparation. - Volunteering — a separate guide, still in preparation. - Income benefits (Ontario Works, ODSP) — see Claim Everything. Nothing below replaces income support; it's about earning your own income instead. As of March 1, 2025, per the City's own page, the Toronto Employment & Social Services office network no longer runs its own separate employment-services function — that transferred to Employment Ontario (cited below). TESS offices are now Ontario Works casework/administration offices; if that's what you need, see Claim Everything. - Multi-service settlement agencies where employment is one of several bundled strands, not a named service — this guide cites agencies whose named, distinct service is employment, not every multi-service centre that happens to offer it as a folded-in strand. Working Women Community Centre and Scarborough Women's Centre are excluded on that basis: what they offer is a resource/referral page or a training course, not a placement service.

Start here: general doors anyone can walk into

These are free to anyone in Toronto. Several route through phone or online contact rather than a guaranteed walk-in — checked against each provider's own page below, since a "walk in any time" claim and a "call or book first" reality are different doors.

Employment Ontario (Toronto Region Network) A locator/portal to roughly 80 independently-run partner agencies across Toronto, York, Peel and Ottawa — several of which (JVS, WoodGreen, ACCES, YMCA, Times Change, The Career Foundation, CCRW) are cited in their own right below. Free one-on-one employment counselling, job search planning, resume/interview coaching, and referrals to skills training are delivered through whichever partner agency you're matched to. eoworks.ca Who it's for: anyone, Toronto/York/Peel/Ottawa. Call 1-888-353-8140 or use the online location finder; no documents required. The provider's own site makes no blanket walk-in claim — access model (walk-in vs. appointment) depends on which partner agency you end up at.

JVS Toronto Free employment counselling, job search workshops, and no-cost recruitment services for employers — plus dedicated youth, newcomer and disability employment streams under the same roof (RiseAbility for developmental disabilities; EMET for the Jewish community). jvstoronto.org Who it's for: anyone, Toronto/Markham/GTA. Contact via the online form or call a specific location (Toronto Centre: 416-929-4331, Mon–Fri 9am–5pm) or the general line (416-787-1151); no documents required. The core service is open to all — the RiseAbility and EMET streams are community-specific add-ons within it.

YMCA of Greater Toronto Employment Services Free employment services: resume/interview help, job matching, technology/skills workshops, and workplace-training programs. ymcagta.org — employment services Who it's for: anyone, Toronto. Book an appointment online — the provider's own page states "book your appointment now" via a form, not a walk-in model. Toronto-region offices: Etobicoke 416-741-8714, North York 416-635-9622, Scarborough (Milner) 416-609-9622, Scarborough (Tapscott) 416-335-5490. No documents required.

WoodGreen Employment Services Free career counselling, resume/interview workshops, and skills-training employment programs (e.g., plumbing pre-apprenticeship). woodgreen.org — employment Who it's for: anyone, Toronto. Call Integrated Employment Services (416-405-5254) or submit an online form to be matched with an Employment Consultant; no documents required. Separately calendared workshops/events (via Eventbrite) are closer to drop-in. Absorbed the former "JobStart" brand — same service under WoodGreen's name now.

The Career Foundation Job search assistance and pre-employment skill development (interview coaching, resume/cover letter writing, career exploration) at 5 full-suite employment centres across Etobicoke, Central Toronto, North York and Scarborough, with specialized streams for youth, women, people with disabilities, newcomers and trades-seekers built into the same intake. careerfoundation.com Who it's for: anyone, Toronto. Call 1-855-223-8231 or email jobsupport@careerfoundation.org, or register online at careerfoundation.com/register; no documents required.

Canada Job Bank Free federal job-search engine, resume builder, job-matching/alerts, and labour-market information. jobbank.gc.ca Who it's for: anyone, Canada-wide. Free self-serve tool online — no counsellor, no appointment, no login required to search; this is a search engine, not a staffed service, so pair it with one of the agencies above if you want a person's help.

If you're a newcomer to Canada

ACCES Employment Free job search support, bridging/sector-specific programs, mentoring, and internationally trained professional programs, at 7 GTA locations (Toronto, North York, Scarborough, Markham, Mississauga, Newmarket, Brampton) and online. accesemployment.ca Who it's for: newcomers, Toronto/GTA. Call 416-921-1800 or register for a scheduled Intake & Orientation Session at your nearest location; no documents required. The provider's own page does not state a walk-in model — sessions are scheduled.

Kababayan Multicultural Centre — Employment Services Free one-on-one career counselling, job search assistance, resume/cover letter guidance, interview preparation, professional networking, and employer connections — plus a free one-on-one resume-critique service. kababayan.org/employment-services Who it's for: anyone, regardless of immigration status — the provider's own FAQ states "all of our services are completely free" and open to "permanent residents, refugees, international students, temporary foreign workers, and citizens." Call (416) 532-3888 (1313 Queen St W) or (416) 633-5056 (540 Finch Ave W), or register online. Employment is its own named service line here, not folded into a general bundle.

Arab Community Centre of Toronto (ACCT) — Employment Named employment programs including Career Café and E3 (Engage Excel Empower); ACCT is also one of the community agencies delivering Toronto Youth Job Corps (see below) out of its Etobicoke office. acctonline.ca/employment Who it's for: newcomers of all backgrounds — serving "newcomers of all cultures, religions, and ethnicities since 1973," not Arab-community-exclusive. Etobicoke: 180 Norseman St, Suite 109, 416-231-7746. Employment is its own named service line.

Afghan Women's Organization (AWO) — Employment Services A named, distinct Employment Services program alongside AWO's broader settlement work — despite the org's name, it serves newcomer families broadly, not only Afghans or only women. afghanwomen.org — employment services Who it's for: newly arrived immigrants and convention refugees — the org's settlement program serves "over 100 newly arrived landed immigrants and convention refugees every month." Locations: Don Mills/Head Office 416-422-2225, Scarborough 416-266-1777, Mississauga 905-279-3679; general line 416-588-3585. Free childminding available at all locations for clients.

Newcomer Women's Services Toronto (NEW) — Employment Services 1:1 career coaching, resume makeovers, mock interviews, employment webinars, and paid pre-employment training for at-risk youth (ages 15–29). newcomerwomen.org/employment-programs Who it's for: despite the org's name, this specific service is explicitly "open to all women, men and youth" per the provider's own page. Register online first, then call 647-224-1371 or email employment@newcomerwomen.org. Employment is its own named service line with its own contact channel.

Canadian Information Centre for International Credentials (CICIC) Free guidance and directories to help internationally trained professionals get academic and occupational credentials recognized for work, study or immigration in Canada. cicic.ca Who it's for: internationally trained professionals, Canada-wide. Call 416-962-9725. Free; no documents required.

World Education Services (WES) — Canada Verification and evaluation of academic credentials earned outside Canada, trusted by IRCC for immigration applications and by employers/licensing bodies. wes.org/ca Who it's for: internationally trained professionals, Canada-wide. Not free. For an employment-application evaluation: Document-by-Document is C$133 (Basic) or C$175 (ICAP, recommended); Course-by-Course is C$242 or C$284. Delivery adds C$14–$97; 13% HST applies on top; all fees subject to change (see wes.org/ca/evaluations-and-fees for current processing times). Included, and priced honestly rather than dropped, because it's the IRCC-trusted, effectively required credential-recognition step for many skilled-immigration and professional-licensing pathways — a labelled purchase, not a free service.

Honest gap: no entry exists for the well-known TRIEC Mentoring Partnership, Achev, Career Edge or Windmill Microlending — all prominent Toronto programs specifically for internationally trained professionals (mentorship matching and micro-loans for licensing costs, respectively). A reader who has already found ACCES and WES will likely go looking for these next and not find them here.

If you're a woman

Times Change Women's Employment Service Free career counselling, job search/computer training, career planning, and job matching for women, operating since 1974. timeschange.org Who it's for: women, Toronto. The Resource Area keeps posted walk-in hours (Mon–Thu 10am–4pm, Fri 10am–2:30pm); the core counselling program requires registering online first, then attending a recurring "Getting Started Orientation" session (held most weeks). Call 416-927-1900. No documents required. (A separate paid career-counselling stream exists for women who are already employed — that's not this service.)

Dress For Success Toronto Free professional work attire (Boutique Experience), career coaching, headshots, and career/ connection days. dressforsuccesstoronto.org Who it's for: women, including trans, non-binary, gender non-conforming and Two-Spirit people, GTA. Referral required — call 416-901-6022 or ask your employment counsellor to refer you; the provider's own site did not clarify a self-referral path on this pass, so treat referral-required as the safer assumption until confirmed by phone.

North York Women's Centre — STEPS to Work Program A 9-week program combining one-on-one support and weekly group workshops to identify employment goals, work options, and gender-based workplace barriers, plus legal-education sessions on workplace rights (disability accommodations, harassment, etc.). nywc.org/programs — STEPS to Work Who it's for: women and gender-diverse adults, Toronto. Runs in cohorts, not rolling admission — the cohort live on the provider's page as of this check ran July 2–August 27, 2026, so intake for that specific cohort is already closed. Complete the Program Inquiry Form, call (416) 781-0479, or email program@nywc.org to be scheduled for the next cohort. Employment is its own named program here.

If you're young

First Work — GPS for Your Career Free AI-powered career-exploration platform for Ontario youth: career recommendations, a skills roadmap, and job matching. firstwork.org/gps-for-your-career Who it's for: youth 15–29, Ontario. Free online tool — no counsellor, no appointment. Funded by the Ontario Ministry of Labour, Skills Development and Training.

Toronto Youth Job Corps (TYJC) Free-to-join, full-time paid employment-preparation program: 5 weeks of group employment-skills training plus up to 8 weeks placed with an employer for paid work experience, case management and job-search support. toronto.ca — youth job seekers Who it's for: ages 15–30 (this guide's own scope covers 13–17 within that at the 15–17 overlap); must be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or protected person legally entitled to work, resident of Toronto. Delivered through community agencies — referral required; ID needed. The City's own page names two intake agencies with published numbers: West Scarborough Neighbourhood Community Centre (416-615-1049) and Arab Community Centre of Toronto (416-231-7746) — the program runs through four agencies total per that page, but only these two publish direct numbers there.

Honest gap: the same City hub page names three related programs — Toronto Youth Partnerships & Employment (TYPE, ages 16–29, contact type@toronto.ca), Youth Employment Partnerships (YEP, ages 15–29, contact Mikael.khalam@toronto.ca), and ArtWorksTO (ages 18–29, IBPOC/2SLGBTQ+ creative industries) — that are not separately rowed in the atlas. A youth reader who doesn't fit TYJC's band has real doors named right next to it that this guide can't yet cite on their own terms.

If you're Indigenous

Miziwe Biik Aboriginal Employment and Training Free one-on-one Employment Advisors, plus post-secondary/apprenticeship funding for books, supplies and transportation, targeted wage subsidies for employers, and self-employment grants, for unemployed/underemployed Indigenous people in the GTA. miziwebiik.com Who it's for: unemployed/underemployed Indigenous people, GTA. Self-register online (no third-party referral needed) — client status expires annually (April 1); 433 Cherry St. The Employment Advisor service is the direct job-search door; the same program also funds training and apprenticeship costs, which is adjacent but not itself this guide's subject.

If you have a disability

Canadian Council on Rehabilitation and Work (CCRW) Free 1-on-1 employment services for job seekers with disabilities: goal-setting, coaching, resume support, employer connections, and an Untapped Talent job board. ccrw.org Who it's for: job seekers with disabilities, Canada-wide (Toronto office: 1867 Yonge St, Suite 906). Call 416-260-3060 or 1-800-664-0925, or use the online contact form.

Canadian Hearing Services — Employment Programs Two named programs: Workforce Literacy (reading/writing, resume and interview help, digital skills, workplace training) and Newcomer Employment Counselling (resume/interview/job-search coaching, job coaching), delivered in-person in Peel Region and/or virtually. chs.ca — employment programs Who it's for: Workforce Literacy requires Deaf status specifically (not hard-of-hearing), 18+, living in Ontario with a valid SIN. Newcomer Employment Counselling requires Deaf or hard-of-hearing status plus permanent resident, convention refugee, or protected-person status. Call 1-866-518-0000 ext. 4048 (TTY 1-877-215-9530) or email literacytraining@chs.ca / settlement@chs.ca. The organization's general line, 1-866-518-0000, is confirmed correct on the provider's own Contact page — an earlier "1-886" figure traced to a metadata typo, not a second real number.

March of Dimes Canada — Employment Services On-the-job training, coaching, and job search/placement support with a wide range of employers for persons with physical disabilities. modcemploymentservices.ca Who it's for: people with physical disabilities, GTA. Call 1-833-612-6632. The provider's own program page could not be read by our verification method (it returned empty content on repeated fetch attempts across three separate lanes, consistent with a JS-rendered app our tool can't parse) — corroborated instead via the parent marchofdimes.ca site, which links to it as a live current service, and via the Employment Ontario partner directory, which lists "March of Dimes Canada" as a current service provider. Cost and document requirements remain unconfirmed — ask directly before assuming this is free.

Spinal Cord Injury Ontario — Accessible Employment Services Occupational Therapist / Rehabilitation Engineer assessment of adaptive technology needs plus job-seeker services for adults with spinal cord injuries or other mobility disabilities. sciontario.org Who it's for: adults 16+ with spinal cord injuries or other mobility disabilities, GTA-based (some virtual workshops province-wide). Start with a New Client Referral consultation — free, per the provider's own page. Call 416-422-5644 or 1-877-422-1112.

Project Work Free ongoing job-search and job-retention counselling, plus a walk-in Employment Resource Program (computers, internet, fax, resume help); also runs ReachingHIRE (an 8-week training and job-placement program specifically for Ontario Works recipients with intellectual disabilities/learning challenges) and a 29-week Supported Employment Program (employment-prep workshops, occupational skills assessment, 1:1 job search help, job coaching). projectwork.ca/employment-programs Who it's for: adults 18+ with intellectual disabilities or who need extra support finding entry-level work, Toronto (250 Merton St, Suite 204). Referral required — via ReachingHIRE intake or an Ontario Works caseworker. No public phone number for self-referral found on the provider's own page, confirmed again on this pass — still an open gap.

Honest gap: the province's own dedicated ODSP-linked employment-support stream (Ontario Disability Employment Support Program) has no entry in the atlas — everything in this section is a nonprofit agency, not the government program itself.

If you're coming out of incarceration, addiction recovery, or a long period out of work

Elizabeth Fry Toronto — Record Suspension (Pardon) Program Free one-on-one assistance completing and submitting a Record Suspension (pardon) application to the Parole Board of Canada, including eligibility screening and document help, plus coverage of the application processing fee once your application is successfully submitted. efrytoronto.org — record suspension Who it's for: anyone with a Canadian criminal record, Toronto (215 Wellesley St E). Call 416-924-3708 ext. 400 (toll-free 1-855-924-3708) or email recordsuspensions@efrytoronto.org. This is not a job-search service — it's help clearing the record that often blocks hiring in the first place. Elizabeth Fry Toronto's broader mandate centres women/non-binary people in conflict with the law, but this specific program page doesn't state a gender restriction; confirm current intake criteria by phone before relying on it.

Oasis Movement — Addiction Recovery Employment Services Two named programs: the Pre-Employment Services Program (4 weeks of life-skills and pre-employment programming, Mon–Thu 10am–12:30pm, for people 30+ days clean and sober) and the Employment Placement Program (job search and placement support once ready). oasismovement.org Who it's for: people in recovery from drug and alcohol addiction, Toronto (921 Danforth Ave). Call 416-461-7739. Employment services are also reachable via the related site oasisemployment.com. The Pre-Employment stream specifically requires 30+ days clean and sober.

Honest gap, and the most consequential one in this guide: no entry in the atlas is a direct job-search or employer-partnership program built specifically for people leaving incarceration. The only atlas-backed resource for this reader is a pardon-application service — genuinely useful, but it removes a barrier rather than finding you a job. A "second-chance hiring" employer-partnership program, if one operates in Toronto, is not currently represented here. This is a concrete gap for future research.

Specific sectors that hire and train

Helmets to Hardhats Canada Free program connecting Veterans, Reservists, senior cadets/Junior Canadian Rangers and CAF family members to apprenticeships and careers in the unionized construction trades, plus free courses and career resources. helmetstohardhats.ca Who it's for: Veterans, Reservists, cadets/Junior Canadian Rangers, and CAF family members, Canada-wide. Register online at app.helmetstohardhats.ca (confirmed live); no documents stated as required.

Honest gap: beyond the military-community trades door above, the atlas has no general sector-specific hiring program for other high-hiring-volume sectors (warehouse/logistics, retail, hospitality, tech). A Hospitality Workers Training Centre entry exists in the atlas but its own description centres a training curriculum rather than job placement, so it belongs with a dedicated guide, not here.

The practical stuff: a resume phone number, interview clothes, a way to get there

TextNow Free real Canadian phone number with unlimited calling and texting over WiFi. textnow.com Who it's for: anyone with WiFi access, Canada-wide. Free download. The number, calling and texting are WiFi-only — TextNow's own coverage page confirms its paid cellular/eSIM plans are U.S.-only (Puerto Rico excluded) with no Canadian carrier partner — so this works well as a resume phone number wherever you have WiFi, not as a mobile line on the go.

Fair Pass Transit Discount Program 36% off TTC adult single-ride PRESTO fares, continuing but subject to annual City Council funding approval (not guaranteed indefinitely). toronto.ca — transit discount Who it's for: low-income Toronto residents ages 20–64, under an income threshold (as of Feb 2026: $22,691/yr for 1 person, $45,383/yr for 4 people). This is an income test, not an OW/ODSP-recipient test — confirmed directly on the provider's own page: if you're on OW, ODSP, or the Toronto Children's Services Child Care Fee Subsidy, you can skip submitting income documents and use your program ID instead, but you do not need to be on any of those programs to qualify. Apply online, call the Human Services Integration Office at 416-338-8888 (also supports phone applications, interpretation, and paper applications), or book a limited-space in-person appointment at Regent Park Community Centre, Keele Community Hub, or Scarborough Civic Centre, 9am–4pm.

TPL Complimentary PRESTO Card Initiative Free physical PRESTO card (normally $4) for people who don't have one, while supplies last, at 9 Toronto Public Library branches. tpl.ca — complimentary PRESTO card Who it's for: anyone without a PRESTO card, Toronto — while supplies last, at Barbara Frum, City Hall, Jane/Sheppard, Lillian H. Smith, Maria A. Shchuka, Northern District, Richview, Sanderson, and York Woods branches (all nine confirmed still listed as participating on the provider's own page). Walk in; no documents required. Call Answerline at 416-397-5981 for current stock. Most useful if you have no fixed address or no other way to get a fare card, so you can physically get to an interview.

See the Women section above for free interview clothing (Dress For Success Toronto) — not repeated here.

Honest gap: no general-population, non-campus, non-gender-specific free interview-clothing program exists in the atlas. Dress For Success Toronto serves women and gender-diverse people specifically; the campus career closets are student-gated. A cisgender man off-campus looking for free interview clothes has no atlas door.
