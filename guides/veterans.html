Veterans

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if a veteran, a still-serving CAF or RCMP member, a reservist, or the family/survivor of any of these can use it because of that service connection — it is reachable from Toronto, and it is free or explicitly labelled subsidized. A general service that a veteran merely happens to qualify for (a general low-income computer program, a general disability-device subsidy) does not belong here. Employment and training services are scoped out on purpose — they belong to Guide 12 Jobs and Employment and Guide 13 Free Training and Credentials; this guide carries only a pointer to the one veteran-specific door in that space.

"Veteran" is not one status, and this guide does not treat it as one. It covers Canadian Armed Forces veterans, RCMP veterans, still-serving members and reservists, and — just as importantly — the families and survivors of all of the above, several of whose doors are open in their own right, not only through the person who served. Two ways to get this wrong:

  • A spouse or survivor who assumes a program is veteran-only, and never applies, is the silent loss. Where a door below is open to family, this guide says so in the entry itself, not in a preamble you might skip.
  • An RCMP veteran sent to a CAF-only program, or vice versa, wastes a hard phone call. This guide never treats "military" and "RCMP" as interchangeable — peer support, transit, and ID all differ between them, and each entry states exactly which one it serves.

Every entry carries its access model exactly as its row states it — several of these doors are application-and-assessment gated, not walk-in, and this guide does not soften that.

If you don't know where to start

VAC Benefits Navigator Free online tool: answer questions about your situation and it suggests which Veterans Affairs Canada benefits and services you may be eligible for. Answers are for reference, not a formal decision. veterans.gc.ca — Benefits Navigator Veterans. No documents required. Free.

For any general VAC question, the toll-free line printed on the Veterans Emergency Fund entry below (1-866-522-2122) is Veterans Affairs Canada's own general contact number, not limited to that one program.

Money right now

Two different funds appear below for two different situations. Use the one that matches yours — not the other one — and note that neither requires Legion membership.

Veterans Emergency Fund (VAC) Free, non-taxable emergency grant of up to $2,500 per Veteran household per fiscal year for food, clothing, uninsured medical/dental costs, or costs needed to keep your shelter — for any kind of financial emergency, not homelessness-specific (this cap can be exceeded in a genuinely exceptional circumstance, per VAC's own FAQ). A decision is issued within two business days of a complete application. Call 1-866-522-2122, or apply online or by mail. veterans.gc.ca — Veterans Emergency Fund · veterans.gc.ca — Veterans Emergency Fund FAQs Veterans, AND — in their own right — a current spouse/common-law partner, a survivor (defined as living with the Veteran/CAF member at time of death), or an orphan of a deceased Veteran/CAF member may apply directly. RCMP does not qualify for this fund: VAC's own FAQ states plainly that VAC has no agreement with the RCMP to provide the Veterans Emergency Fund to current or former RCMP members, and directs them to the Legion's financial assistance instead, immediately below (a current or former RCMP member who is also a CAF Veteran still qualifies through that CAF service). Proof of identity if you're a first-time VAC applicant, plus documentation of the emergency (invoices, quotes, notices). Free.

Royal Canadian Legion — Homeless Veterans Assistance ("Operation: Leave the Streets Behind") This tier is specifically for homelessness or near-homelessness. Free emergency help for homeless or near-homeless Veterans: first/last month's rent, furniture and food vouchers, rental assistance to prevent eviction, dental treatment, transport to therapy, eyeglasses and more, via the Legion's Joe Sweeney Fund. Access is through a local Legion Branch, an Outreach Worker, or a shelter — not a direct application. Ontario Command: 1-888-207-0939. on.legion.ca — Homeless Veterans Assistance Homeless or near-homeless Veterans, Ontario. The provider's own page states this for Veterans; it does not separately state RCMP or family eligibility the way the general fund below does. Referral via a branch, worker, or shelter, not a walk-in or online application. Free.

Royal Canadian Legion — Benevolent Fund (Poppy Fund) General Financial Assistance This tier is for any other financial distress — not homelessness-specific: food, heating, clothing, prescription medication, medical appliances and equipment, essential home repairs, or emergency shelter. Apply with the Benevolent Fund Application form through a local Legion Branch or your Provincial Command Service Officer. Ontario Command: 1-877-534-4666, veteransservices@legion.ca. The Legion's own page states plainly: "Legion services for Veterans and their families are always free and you do not need to be a Legion member to access these services." legion.ca — Financial Assistance CAF (serving or retired), Reserve Force, RCMP (serving or retired), Merchant Navy Veterans with campaign stars/decorations, or Commonwealth/Allied Veterans residing in Canada — plus their eligible dependants. Free; downloadable application form.

Income and ongoing benefits

Income Replacement Benefit (IRB) Taxable monthly benefit that tops up your total income to at least 90% of your gross pre-release military salary (until age 65) while you participate in VAC's Rehabilitation Services and Vocational Assistance Program, or if you have a Diminished Earning Capacity. You can earn up to $20,000/year in employment income before it offsets the benefit dollar-for-dollar above that threshold. Apply via My VAC Account or mail-in form VAC-2502. veterans.gc.ca — Income Replacement Benefit Veterans enrolled in VAC's Rehabilitation program, under 65. Also open in its own right to survivors/orphans of a CAF member or Veteran who died before 65 of a service-related cause (or while receiving IRB). Free (this is a benefit paid to you, not a purchase).

Canadian Forces Income Support (CFIS) Tax-free monthly benefit for low-income CAF Veterans whose Income Replacement Benefit ended within the last six months, whose household income is low (measured by Statistics Canada's formula), and who are looking for work or in Career Transition Services. Apply via My VAC Account or mail-in form VAC-613. veterans.gc.ca — Canadian Forces Income Support Low-income Veterans whose IRB recently ended. Survivors or orphans of a deceased CAF Veteran who was receiving CFIS continue to receive it. Distinct from IRB above — this is the door for once IRB has already ended, not a substitute for it. Free.

Somewhere to live

VETS Canada (Veterans Emergency Transition Services) Free emergency support for veterans in crisis: help with groceries, rent/utilities, homelessness prevention, and referrals to housing, delivered by a network of volunteers (mostly ex-military/ RCMP) across Canada including Toronto. Emergency line 1-888-228-3871, volunteer@vetscanada.org. vetscanada.org Veterans in crisis, Canada-wide. The provider's own page describes the service as being for veterans; volunteers are drawn from the ex-military/RCMP community, but the page does not itself state RCMP or family as separately eligible recipient categories the way some other doors in this guide do. Free.

Good Shepherd Ministries — Homeless Veterans Program (Toronto) Free, Toronto-based housing-focused case management for homeless Veterans: a Veterans Housing Navigation team, a peer support worker, help finding housing, addictions support, life- and social-skills support, and connections to community resources and training/employment services. Delivered in partnership with the Royal Canadian Legion and Veterans Affairs Canada. Call 416-869-3619. goodshepherd.ca — Homeless Veterans Services Former CAF member (Regular or Reserve, honourably discharged) or former RCMP member, homeless or at risk of homelessness, Toronto. As fetched, the provider's own page does not state family/ dependant eligibility — read this strictly as serving the Veteran or former RCMP member. Free.

If a Legion emergency fund is what you need instead of housing case management, see the Homeless Veterans Assistance fund (Joe Sweeney Fund) under "Money right now," above — the two are complementary, not duplicates.

Long-term and complex medical care

Sunnybrook Veterans Centre Canada's largest dedicated veteran care facility: long-term and complex continuing care, plus day programs, for Canadian war and modern-day Veterans. Admission is exclusively through a Veterans Affairs Canada application. sunnybrook.ca — Veterans Centre Veterans, Toronto, referral-required (via VAC application). Residents pay a co-payment determined by the Veteran and VAC — not flatly free; laundry and basic cable are included, phone is billed separately. Mental health and peer support

This is the one place the CAF/RCMP distinction matters most. OSISS and SOSI are two separate peer-support networks, run by two different organizations, for two different service populations — neither substitutes for the other.

VAC Assistance Service (24/7 mental health support line) Free, confidential psychological support (1–20 hours per issue) with a mental health professional, by phone 24/7/365 or online chat weekdays 8am–7:30pm ET. No need to be a VAC client, no application — usually an appointment within 5 working days. Call 1-800-268-7708 (TTY 1-800-567-5803). veterans.gc.ca — Talk to Someone CAF Veterans, former RCMP members, their families (children to 21, or 25 if full-time students), and caregivers. Canada-wide. Free.

OSI Clinics Network — Greater Toronto Satellite Site Free in-person and virtual assessment, treatment and support for operational stress injuries (PTSD and other service-related mental health issues), from psychiatrists, psychologists, social workers and mental health nurses. Greater Toronto Satellite Site, 5000 Yonge St, Suite 1401, Toronto. No application needed once referred — contact VAC or a case manager for the referral. Call 519-685-4292 x45860 or 1-888-237-6967. veterans.gc.ca — OSI Clinics CAF Veterans and current/former RCMP, plus family members, Toronto. Referral-required. Free.

OSISS — Operational Stress Injury Social Support (CAF/military) Free national peer-support network connecting CAF members and Veterans and their family members who are experiencing an operational stress injury with a trained peer support coordinator, one-on- one or in a group. Family support has existed within OSISS since 2005. Toll-free 1-800-883-6094, OSISS-SSBSO@forces.gc.ca. veterans.gc.ca — Talk to Someone Who Can Relate · cfmws.ca — OSISS CAF member or Veteran with an operational stress injury, or a family member of one. This network is for CAF/military only — it does NOT cover RCMP. See SOSI, next, for the RCMP equivalent. Canada-wide. Free. VAC's own page above now functions as a joint "Peer support" hub that routes to OSISS's day-to-day operational home at cfmws.ca (Canadian Forces Morale and Welfare Services) rather than hosting OSISS's program details itself — both the phone number and email above are confirmed current on the cfmws.ca page directly.

SOSI — Support for Operational Stress Injuries (RCMP) Free confidential peer and social support for RCMP employees of all categories and RCMP Veterans living with an operational stress injury, from trained SOSI Coordinators and Peer Supporters with lived experience. Focused on rebuilding a healthy social network, not clinical treatment. Email rcmp.sosi-sbso.grc@rcmp-grc.gc.ca. rcmp.ca — Mental Health, Wellbeing and Support RCMP Veteran or serving employee. As fetched, this specific program is not stated to extend to family — the RCMP's separate Peer to Peer (P2P) program, on the same page (p2p-spp@rcmp-grc.gc.ca), is the one explicitly stated to cover "all RCMP employees, veterans and families," so a family member should ask for P2P, not SOSI. Canada-wide. Free.

Wounded Warriors Canada Free trauma-focused mental health programs for Veterans, first responders and their families: Trauma Resiliency Program, Couples Overcoming PTSD Everyday (COPE), Spousal Resiliency Program, Surviving Family Program, and a clinician directory. woundedwarriors.ca Veterans and their families, Canada-wide. Apply via woundedwarriors.ca/clinical-services or the Clinician Directory (warriorshealth.ca/directory). Free. The provider's own site now frames its work around "Trauma Exposed Professionals/Organizations/Families" rather than the older "Veterans and first responders" language — Veterans/CAF remain one explicitly named covered category alongside police, fire, paramedics, corrections, security and search-and-rescue personnel, so who this guide says it serves is unchanged; only the organization's own vocabulary has shifted.

Soldier On Free adaptive sport, recreation and physical-activity programming — equipment grants, activities, Invictus-style events — to help ill or injured serving CAF members and Veterans adapt and recover. Register online, then connect with a regional coordinator. soldieron.ca Ill or injured serving CAF members and Veterans, Canada-wide. No documents stated. Free. Listed here as a recovery-adjacent complement to the clinical mental-health doors above, not a substitute for them.

Help fighting a decision

Two free routes exist to appeal a VAC decision. They are not the same thing, and you can use either one, or both.

Bureau of Pensions Advocates (BPA) Free legal representation from lawyers employed within Veterans Affairs Canada itself: they review a disability-benefit decision, help prepare a departmental-review submission with new evidence, and represent you at a Veterans Review and Appeal Board hearing. VAC may cover travel costs to attend in person. Call toll-free 1-877-228-2250. veterans.gc.ca — How to Appeal Veterans appealing a VAC disability-benefit decision — also eligible survivors/dependants pursuing their own decision. Canada-wide. Free. These are VAC's own in-house lawyers — a different kind of help than the Legion's Service Officers, below, who are not lawyers.

Royal Canadian Legion Ontario Command — Service Officer Network Free assistance from trained (non-lawyer) Legion Service Officers helping Veterans, former RCMP members and families complete and file VAC disability and pension claim applications. Itinerant visits to local Legion branches (2–3 per year); Toronto-area Veterans fall under the Aurora Service Office district: 905-841-7999 or toll-free 1-888-207-0939. Legion membership not required. on.legion.ca — Service Officers Veterans, former RCMP members and families, Ontario. Referral-required (via the Service Officer network / itinerant branch visits). Free. A free, non-lawyer alternative or complement to BPA, above — VAC's own "How to Appeal" page names both as free options; use one or both.

Family and survivors

Caregiver Recognition Benefit (CRB) $1,264.25/month, tax-free (current rate effective January 1, 2026; the amount rises yearly with the Consumer Price Index — do not treat this figure as fixed), paid directly to an unpaid informal caregiver — often, but not necessarily, a family member — of a Veteran who has an approved VAC Disability Award or Pain and Suffering Compensation and needs daily supervision or assistance. The caregiver must be 18+, unpaid, and the Veteran must not be a permanent resident of a nursing home or long-term care facility. The Veteran (or their Power of Attorney) submits the joint application. veterans.gc.ca — Caregiver Recognition Benefit · veterans.gc.ca — current rates Paid to the Veteran's caregiver, very often a spouse or adult child, Canada-wide. Free (this is a benefit paid to the caregiver, not a purchase). The provider's own eligibility list is explicitly CAF-specific ("you are a former Canadian Armed Forces member") — this benefit is not stated as available where the person receiving care is an RCMP veteran rather than a CAF one.

If you are a spouse, common-law partner, survivor, orphan, or caregiver, several doors listed elsewhere in this guide are open to you directly, in your own right — not only through the Veteran:

  • Veterans Emergency Fund (Money right now, above) — a current spouse/common-law partner, a survivor, or an orphan may apply directly (RCMP survivors of a CAF-only claim are subject to the same RCMP exclusion noted on that entry).

  • Income Replacement Benefit and Canadian Forces Income Support (Income and ongoing benefits, above) — survivors/orphans can newly qualify or continue receiving these in specific circumstances tied to how and when the Veteran died.

  • Bureau of Pensions Advocates and the Legion Service Officer Network (Help fighting a decision, above) — an eligible survivor or dependant pursuing their own benefit decision can use either.

  • Legion Benevolent/Poppy Fund (Money right now, above) — dependants of a qualifying CAF/RCMP/ Reserve/Merchant Navy veteran are explicitly eligible, not only the veteran.

  • VAC Assistance Service, OSI Clinics, and OSISS (Mental health and peer support, above) — family members can use each of these directly; OSISS has included dedicated family peer support since 2005.

  • Wounded Warriors Canada (Mental health and peer support, above) — its Spousal Resiliency Program and Surviving Family Program are built for family members specifically.

Getting around and getting ID

Free year-round GO Transit for Veterans and CAF members Free year-round travel on all GO Transit trains and buses across the GTHA, by presenting a Veteran's Service Card, Canadian Military Service ID, or Temporary National Defence ID Card when boarding. PRESTO card holders can register the special fare type and combine it with One Fare transfers to local transit agencies including the TTC. In effect since March 1, 2025. metrolinx.com — Free GO Transit for Veterans and CAF Members CAF veteran or serving member (Regular or Reserve), GTA. RCMP is not stated as eligible on Metrolinx's own page — do not assume this benefit extends to RCMP veterans. Free.

Veteran's Service Card (free veteran ID) Free, non-expiring photo ID card (the NDI 75 Veteran's Service Card), issued by National Defence to any former CAF member honourably released after completing basic training. Used to access some third-party veteran discounts, free Parks Canada admission, and the GO Transit free-fare program above. Apply online or by mail; if your documents are lost, a free Library and Archives Canada ATIP "Proof of Service" request (about 30 days) can help. canada.ca — Veteran's Service Card CAF veteran (Regular Force, Primary Reserve, COATS, or Canadian Rangers) honourably released after basic training. Explicitly CAF-only — NOT issued to RCMP veterans, who have a separate identification system; do not conflate the two. Free. Not required for any VAC benefit.

Not in this guide — cross-guide boundary

One fact, one home. Employment and training services are scoped out of this guide on purpose, even where they are veteran-specific:

  • Helmets to Hardhats Canada — free program connecting Veterans, Reservists, cadets/Junior Canadian Rangers and CAF family members to unionized skilled-trades apprenticeships. This is a genuine veteran-specific door, but it is an employment program, so its full entry lives in Guide 12 Jobs and Employment, not here.

Honest gaps

  • RCMP Veterans' Association Toronto Division's membership cost is still unconfirmed on any fetchable public page, even after this verification pass tried three of the organization's own pages today (2026-07-31): the Toronto Division's Membership page, its Join page, and the national parent association's member portal. The Division's own Join page confirms exactly who may apply — a former RCMP member, RCMP Reserve, RCMP Auxiliary, or former federal/municipal employee who worked with the RCMP, excluding anyone who left due to discreditable conduct — and gives a live contact (membership@rcmpvetstoronto.ca), but the actual dues figure lives behind a members-only "how to access your fees" page. It still could not be minted as a row without guessing at a number; a direct phone or email confirmation is still the right next step.
  • The City of Toronto's own role in veteran homelessness funding could not be confirmed on a toronto.ca page. It very likely refers residents to Good Shepherd Ministries in practice, but this guide relies on Good Shepherd and VETS Canada as the two confirmed access points rather than implying the City itself runs a public-facing intake.
  • Toronto-specific recreation for veterans beyond Soldier On is thin. No dedicated Toronto veterans' sports league or drop-in was found. Legion branch-level "community events and activities for military and RCMP families" are referenced on legion.ca, but no Toronto-specific branch page could be independently verified — treat this as a soft lead, not a standing door.
  • Merchant Navy veterans appear only inside the Legion Benevolent Fund's eligibility list (FT-18706, above); no Merchant-Navy-specific door exists in this atlas.
  • RCMP veterans have no equivalent to the GO Transit free fare, the Veteran's Service Card, or the Caregiver Recognition Benefit. All three were reconfirmed CAF-specific on their own provider pages this verification pass (2026-07-31).
  • Sunnybrook Veterans Centre's page still could not be fetched. This seal pass tried the cited page again plus a second, different Sunnybrook veterans-program page today; neither returned usable text.
