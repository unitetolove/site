Learn Free: ESL to University

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if it teaches the reader something — a language, literacy, a school credential, an academic subject, or a general skill — at no cost, or at an explicitly-labelled cost, and is available to a Toronto resident. Vocational credentialing and trades training belong to Free Training & Credentials (fleet #10, being built this same wave) — pointer only, never duplicated here. Job-search help, resume/interview coaching, and employment counselling belong to Guide 12 Jobs & Employment — pointer only. Services gated to a specific campus's own enrolled students belong to Guide 04 and its per-campus guides — pointer only.

This guide is organized by where the reader actually is, not by which agency runs the program: learning English (and the status distinction that decides which door you can use); reading, writing and math for adults; finishing high school as an adult; studying an academic subject for free; and help for a young person still in school. For every entry, the access model is stated plainly — walk-in, assessment/intake interview, online registration, or referral — because language and upgrading programs almost always gate enrolment behind an assessment, and that is a real fact a reader needs before showing up.

A note on cost, honestly: every entry in this guide is free outright, including TCDSB's Adult ESL and Get SET programs — a genuine correction from v0.1, where both sat under a cost-unknown label because no fee could be found anywhere on either program's own detail page across two research lanes and this guide's first seal attempt. This seal pass found the missing piece on a TCDSB page none of those attempts had reached: the board's own Adult Education Program landing page ("For Learners"), which states plainly, for the program as a whole, "Free in-person and fully online courses for adults" — directly above the list naming ESL and LINC, French as a Second Language, and Get SET as "our programs." The board's own Course Finder tool independently confirms the English-specific piece: "Find free English courses for adult immigrants." Neither correction rests on an inference from the programs' public funding — both rest on the board's own plain-language statement that its courses are free. One exception, added in v0.3: Canada Learning Code's workshops are stated by the provider itself as "low to no cost," not free outright — see "Free books and general-skills workshops" below, where that entry's cost is labelled exactly as the provider states it rather than rounded up to free.

Learning English

The single most important thing to know before picking a door: which English program you can enrol in depends on your immigration status, and the two main doors are not interchangeable. LINC, the federally funded program, is the one most people have heard of — but it is the narrower door. General (non-LINC) ESL through a school board is open more broadly, including to citizens and refugee claimants whom LINC excludes. Read each eligibility tag below carefully before you go.

LINC — Language Instruction for Newcomers to Canada (Toronto delivery) Free English-language classes (literacy through Level 6, part-time/full-time/evening/weekend), with free Care for Newcomer Children and eligibility-based transportation assistance, delivered citywide by TDSB, COSTI, YWCA Toronto, TNO, LEF, My Neighbourhood Services and others. tdsb.on.ca — LINC Who it's for: federally funded (IRCC) — settled, not a hedge. TDSB's own Requirements page states the criteria as a positive eligibility list, fetched 2026-07-31: "Be a Permanent Resident, a Protected Person, a Convention Refugee. Be at least 18 years old. Not be registered in a CLIC program. Take a test at a Language Assessment Centre." TCDSB's own ESL-and-LINC page confirms the identical rule even more plainly, same day: "Permanent residents and convention refugees, 18 years or older, are eligible. (LINC courses are not open to Naturalized Canadian citizens and refugee claimants)." Not open to naturalized citizens, not to a refugee claim still in progress, not to a temporary-resident permit holder. If LINC's door is closed to you for one of those reasons, TCDSB's own (non-LINC) Adult ESL program below is broader and takes you. Access step, corrected this pass: you must take a placement test at a Language Assessment Centre before you can register — this is not a same-day walk-in-and-enrol program, though once assessed, in-person registration itself is done by visiting your chosen site's classroom with ID in hand. Toronto. Free.

Toronto Catholic District School Board (TCDSB) — Adult ESL (non-LINC) ESL classes (Canadian Language Benchmarks 1–8) at West/Central/East Toronto sites, in person and online, funded by the Ontario Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development. tcdsb.org — ESL and LINC Who it's for: broader than LINC at the same board — open to naturalized Canadian citizens, permanent residents, refugee claimants, and convention refugees, 18+, plus domestic workers admitted under the Live-in Caregiver program (not open to visitors — the board's own page draws that line). Requires a valid YMCA language assessment before enrolling, plus picture ID and Ontario residency for the full term; register in person at your chosen site (first-come- first-served, waitlisted if full). Cost: settled this pass. v0.1 published this as cost-unknown after no fee could be found on this program's own page across two research lanes and a first seal lane. This seal pass found the missing piece on TCDSB's own Adult Education Program landing page ("For Learners"), fetched 2026-07-31, which states plainly: "Free in-person and fully online courses for adults" — directly above the list naming ESL and LINC as one of "our programs." The board's own Course Finder tool independently confirms the English-specific piece: "Find free English courses for adult immigrants." Free.

Occupation-specific Language Training (OSLT) Free sector-specific workplace-communication/language courses (business, health sciences, construction trades, technology, human services) at Ontario public colleges, for newcomers already at CLB 5+. co-oslt.org Who it's for: newcomers with CLB 5+ — permanent residents, convention refugees/protected persons, plus a Gaza-crisis temporary-resident carve-out (valid through March 31, 2027, per the provider's own page) — Ontario-wide. Referral-required intake. Time-sensitive, reconfirmed 2026-07-31: the provider's own homepage today still states "As of September 2026, IRCC will no longer fund the delivery of OSLT" — unchanged from this guide's original finding, and now about five weeks away. Still enrolling as of this guide's date — confirm the program is still running before counting on it past that date. Free.

Not here: English support built specifically for a campus's own enrolled international students lives in Guide 04 and its per-campus guides, not here.

Reading, writing and math for adults

These programs raise a specific skill — reading, writing, math, digital literacy — rather than aiming at English fluency or a diploma specifically.

United for Literacy (formerly Frontier College) — Toronto Adult Literacy & Learning Programs Free literacy, numeracy, digital-skills and credential-preparation programs for adult learners, delivered in person (community partner sites including Scadding Court Community Centre, Regent Park Community Health Centre, and Warden Woods Community Centre) and online, plus small-group tutoring/conversation circles for newcomers and learning support for adults with developmental disabilities. unitedforliteracy.ca — Ontario Who it's for: any adult (18+) resident of Toronto — no immigration-status gate found on either of the provider's own pages fetched. Registration is via the website; use the site's own location filter (set to Toronto) to find current class times, since the program locator did not return live listings on a static fetch. Free.

Ontario Get SET (Skills, Education and Training) — Literacy and Basic Skills Program Free province-wide program (formerly Literacy and Basic Skills/LBS) to build reading, writing, math, digital and other essential skills — including preparing to complete the OSSD or enter college — delivered through roughly 185 Employment Ontario service providers, in-class, online, or blended, part- or full-time, evening/daytime/weekend. ontario.ca — adult learning literacy program Who it's for: Ontario residents 18+, assessed as needing skills below grade-12 level; no immigration-status restriction stated. This is the entry door: request a service-provider appointment through the Employment Ontario Citizen Portal (linked from this page) and you'll be matched to a local provider — in Toronto, that includes school-board deliveries like TCDSB's Get SET (below). Free — "This program is free," per the provider's own page.

Toronto Catholic District School Board (TCDSB) — Get SET (Get Skills, Education and Training) Academic-upgrading classes (reading, writing, math, computer skills) tailored to the learner's needs, at West and East Toronto sites. tcdsb.org — Get SET Who it's for: TCDSB's own page names "Adult immigrants and Canadian-born citizens" as equally eligible — this is not a newcomer-only program despite its newcomer-services setting, so don't self-exclude as a citizen without a diploma. Requires Ontario residency, a SIN, legal ability to work in Ontario, and intermediate spoken English. Register by emailing the program coordinator; an interview and written assessment follow before placement. Cost: settled this pass, on the same evidence as the ESL entry above — TCDSB's Adult Education Program describes itself board-wide, on its own "For Learners" page fetched 2026-07-31, as "Free in-person and fully online courses for adults," directly above the list that names Get SET as one of "our programs." Free.

Finishing high school as an adult

The single most valuable thing in this guide, stated plainly: the GED no longer exists in Canada. Pearson VUE stopped administering it after March 2024. Ontario's replacement, the Canadian Adult Education Credential (CAEC), is administered solely by TVO's Independent Learning Centre and costs $100 to write (the figure and year as the atlas's own research found it, stated as-is rather than rounded or updated) — financial help exists only at a caseworker's discretion "if you receive Ontario Works," not as a guaranteed subsidy. If you are searching for "free GED Toronto," the program you're picturing is gone, and the genuinely free route to an equivalent credential for an adult without a diploma is PLAR, below. (A GED already earned before March 2024 remains valid; partial-GED credit still counts toward the CAEC until May 2027.) Reverified 2026-07-31 against ontario.ca's own CAEC page and TVO ILC's own CAEC page, both fetched fresh today: every figure above is exactly as those two pages currently state it — "It costs $100 to register for the test" (ontario.ca) / "It costs $100 to register for all 5 CAEC tests" (ilc.org), the CAEC formally replaced the GED on May 3, 2024, and partial-GED credit is accepted "until May 2027" per both pages. No figure was rounded, updated, or carried forward from memory — each was read today. This headline stands unchanged.

Prior Learning Assessment and Recognition (PLAR) for Mature Students A free assessment, accessed through the adult and continuing education department of an Ontario school board (e.g., TDSB, TCDSB), that evaluates your prior learning, work experience, and life experience for academic credits toward completing the Ontario Secondary School Diploma (OSSD) faster. ontario.ca — CAEC page (PLAR section) Who it's for: any adult mature student, Ontario-wide, accessed through your local school board's continuing-education department. Referral-required (through the board). Free — "the assessment is free," per Ontario's own page, reconfirmed 2026-07-31.

TDSB Adult Credit Programs / Continuing Education Free adult secondary-credit (OSSD) courses, academic upgrading, ESL, and skills-development programs for adult learners through TDSB continuing education — Toronto's free adult-credit/OSSD route (not a "GED-equivalent" — see the headline above for why that framing is out of date). tdsb.on.ca — Continuing Education, Adult Learners Who it's for: any adult resident of Toronto. Walk in; no documents stated as required. Free.

Studying an academic subject for free

Self-directed study, university auditing, and free online courses — for a reader who wants to learn a subject for its own sake, not necessarily toward a credential.

University of Toronto — Free Course Auditing for Seniors (60+) Seniors 60+ (as of January 1 of the current year) may audit U of T undergraduate courses — attending lectures and discussion, with no assignments, tests, exams, or certificate issued — without being charged the audit fee the general public pays (40% of tuition per course). nmc.utoronto.ca — audit courses Who it's for: seniors 60+ as of January 1. Requires the instructor's written permission, proof of age, and available seat space — registered degree students have priority. Strengthened this pass: now confirmed on two departments' own pages, not one. Near & Middle Eastern Civilizations and the Department for the Study of Religion both state, in near-identical wording fetched 2026-07-31: "Students registered in the University[...], senior citizens, and employees of the University will not be charged an audit fee," and the Religion page cites "the University of Toronto's policy on auditing courses" as its authority. This lane read that underlying university-wide document directly — the Governing Council's "Policy on Auditing of Courses" (adopted October 20, 1994) — which confirms the base rule (registered students and University employees pay no audit fee; all other auditors pay a minimum of 40% of tuition) but whose own Fees section, as fetched today, does not name senior citizens by name as a distinct exempt category. The senior waiver is therefore corroborated identically across two independent departments and tied by both to the same university-wide policy, but no single university-wide document was found today that states the senior carve-out in so many words — confirm the waiver with your own department before enrolling, since day-to-day implementation may vary. Policies genuinely differ by institution and campus: this is the one confirmed free door. Two look-alikes to avoid confusing with this one, found/reconfirmed this pass: U of T Mississauga's own "Senior Citizen Student" pathway is a different, NOT-free program (full academic fees plus a $26 application fee, no formal audit option); and Woodsworth College's own "65+ Program" — new finding this pass — is also different and NOT free: it requires age 65+ (not 60+), full tuition fees (only incidental fees are waived), a $25 application fee, and is a credit-bearing non-degree registration route, not an audit. No claim is made here about York University's audit fees, since no first-party York page was read. Toronto Metropolitan University's own 2026-2027 "Fees, Tuition and Policies" calendar page, checked again this pass, confirms course auditing exists (subject to teaching-department approval) but does not itself publish a fee figure or a senior exemption — that number remains unfetched after two attempts, so still no claim is made about TMU.

eCampusOntario Open Library Free, openly-licensed textbooks and course materials (1,900+ resources) for Ontario college/ university students, replacing commercial textbook purchases. openlibrary.ecampusontario.ca Who it's for: anyone — the materials are openly licensed and freely accessible online, though the library is built with Ontario college/university students in mind. No documents required. Free.

Coursera Financial Aid (Degree Programs) Financial-resource pathways (employer tuition reimbursement info, scholarships/grants directory, prior-learning credit) specifically for Coursera bachelor's/master's degree programs. coursera.org — financial resources Who it's for: prospective Coursera degree students, anywhere online. This page covers degree-program financial-resource guidance only — the commonly-cited "financial aid" that waives fees for individual Coursera courses/Professional Certificates is a separate, per-course application feature not confirmed on this page; don't assume it extends beyond degree programs based on this row.

LinkedIn Learning & TPL eLearning Free unlimited access to thousands of LinkedIn Learning courses, plus Gale Courses and O'Reilly Learning, with a TPL card, usable from anywhere. tpl.ca/elearning Who it's for: any Toronto Public Library cardholder, usable anywhere online. Free.

Indigenous Canada (University of Alberta, via Coursera) A free-to-audit 12-module online course from the University of Alberta's Faculty of Native Studies covering Indigenous worldviews and storytelling, the fur trade, treaties, the Indian Act and residential schools, urbanization, self-determination, land and title, gender, and contemporary Indigenous art and social movements (~21 hours total). coursera.org/learn/indigenous-canada Who it's for: anyone, anywhere — not Toronto-specific, included as a strong general-public learning resource. Free to audit (all lectures, readings, and quizzes); only the optional shareable certificate costs money.

Free books and general-skills workshops

New in v0.3. Two self-directed doors that don't fit neatly under a credential or a language — a free book-exchange network for anyone who just wants something to read, and a workshop series that teaches tech skills at low or no cost.

Little Free Library — Toronto Network A free take-a-book-leave-a-book exchange: informally stewarded wooden boxes scattered across Toronto neighbourhoods, no membership, no library card, and no due date. Not a single organization — a crowd-stewarded network of individually registered boxes, findable through the official interactive world map (search by address, or browse near your current location); no single canonical Toronto-only page exists, only the global map. Coverage and box condition vary block to block. littlefreelibrary.org/map Who it's for: anyone, Toronto. Free — no card, no registration, no due date.

Canada Learning Code — Workshops & Events In-person and live-online workshops teaching coding, digital art, game-making, AI and other tech skills, explicitly welcoming people from low-income communities, newcomers, and groups underrepresented in tech — Toronto sessions listed on a searchable events map. Cost, stated exactly as the provider states it rather than rounded up to free: the provider's own page describes its workshops as offered "at low to no cost" — treat this as pay-what-you-can/sliding scale and check the price on the specific session before registering, not as a blanket free program. Following a 2025–26 merger, workshop registration now happens through a third-party platform ("Ampere"), not directly on canadalearningcode.ca. canadalearningcode.ca/events Who it's for: anyone, with named priority for cis/trans women, non-binary people, Black, Indigenous and racialized people, newcomers, and low-income communities; a national org with Toronto sessions among its listings. Low to no cost — not confirmed free for every session.

Help for a young person still in school

Toronto Public Library Homework Help (Brainfuse) Free online tutoring for K-12 students in core subjects plus writing/French support, and separately, resume help and citizenship exam prep for adults, via any TPL card. tpl.ca/resource/brainfuse Who it's for: any Toronto resident with a TPL card (free to obtain) and PIN. Live tutors available extended hours. Free.

Schoolhouse.world Free live, Zoom-based peer tutoring: homework help, official SAT bootcamps run in partnership with the College Board, college-admissions workshops, and cross-cultural "Dialogues" sessions. schoolhouse.world Who it's for: students, anywhere online — not Toronto-specific or age-gated on the provider's own page. Free ("100% free"), confirmed with 205,000+ learners and 506,000+ sessions cited on-site.

Pathways to Education — Toronto Free weekly after-school tutoring (aligned to the student's actual school lesson plans), summer language lessons, loaner technology, mentoring, and financial support (bus fare, food support, post-secondary scholarships) for high-school students. pathwaystoeducation.ca/the-issue Who it's for: high-school students (grades 9–12) living specifically in Lawrence Heights, Regent Park, Rexdale, or Scarborough Village — not citywide, regardless of income elsewhere in Toronto. Referral-required. Free. Reconfirmed 2026-07-31 via a fresh fetch of the provider's own "The Impact" page, which lists all four Toronto sites by name and founding year exactly as this entry states them (Regent Park since 2001; Lawrence Heights and Rexdale since 2007; Scarborough Village since 2009) — no fifth Toronto site has been added since v0.1.
