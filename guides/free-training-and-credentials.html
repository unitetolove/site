Free Training & Credentials

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if it gives the reader a skill, a certification, or a credential — at no cost, or at an explicitly-labelled subsidized cost — and it's available to Toronto residents. A program that helps you find a job but doesn't teach or certify you in anything belongs to Guide 12 Jobs & Employment, not here. A service that names its cost as free when the provider's own page doesn't say that, or that hides a real fee inside a friendlier word, doesn't belong here either.

A reader here is choosing how to spend the next several months of their life. This guide is organized so you can tell apart, at a glance:

  1. Skilled trades / pre-apprenticeship — cohort-based, application-gated, often with WHMIS, First Aid and other certifications bundled in.
  2. Bridging for internationally-trained professionals — credential recognition and sector-specific bridge training.
  3. Sector-specific staffed training (tech, hospitality) — application-gated but not a trade.
  4. Free online credentialing you can start today — self-paced, no gate beyond an internet connection (or a library card).
  5. Funding that pays for training elsewhere — a grant, not a course. Don't confuse the two.

For every cohort-based program, this guide states what the provider's own page says about duration and whether intake is currently open — an expired cohort is the most common way this class of guide goes stale. Where a program's next cohort is unstated or its published cohort has passed, this guide says so plainly rather than inferring a next start date from a pattern.

Not here: - Help finding a job with no training component — resume help, interview coaching, job matching, employer connections, sector hiring doors, and identity-specific employment services — live in Guide 12 Jobs & Employment (fleet #9). If an agency below also runs a jobs-counselling desk, that desk is Guide 12's territory, not repeated here. - Academic upgrading, ESL, workplace-language training, and general self-directed learning — live in the Learn Free guide (fleet #11), being built this same wave. TDSB's adult-credit/ OSSD program and Occupation-specific Language Training (OSLT) are pointed there, not cited here — see the boundary note below. - Campus-student-only programs — live in Guide 04 Student Survival and the per-campus guides (04A–04H).

1. Skilled trades and pre-apprenticeship

These are cohort-based programs with an application or referral step — not walk-in classes.

Skills for Change — Trade Spark: Electrical Foundations Free 12-week in-class pre-apprenticeship training in Electrician - Construction and Maintenance (plus 1 week of Industrial Mechanic Millwright exposure), including WHMIS, First Aid/CPR and Working at Heights certifications, integrated remedial math support, and an 8–12 week paid work placement at least minimum wage. Training is in-person at Humber Polytechnic's Centre for Trades and Technology, 110 Carrier Drive, Etobicoke. skillsforchange.org/trade-spark Who it's for: any Ontario resident 18+, legally entitled to work in Canada, with a valid SIN — open to any Ontarian meeting those terms, not newcomer-only, despite the program's outreach targeting youth, women, newcomers and other under-represented groups. The provider's own FAQ does not list a high-school diploma or GED as a requirement. Phone 416-658-3101; register at qrto.org/mAx69K. Intake status (reconfirmed 2026-07-31): the cohort runs May 18, 2026 – March 5, 2027, full-time, mandatory attendance — that cohort is already underway and no next-intake date is published. Call before assuming a spot is open; ask about joining a waitlist or the next intake.

Pre-Apprenticeship Training Program (Ontario) Free trade-readiness training to help prepare entrants for the skilled-trades apprenticeship system. eopg.labour.gov.on.ca Who it's for: any Ontario resident; no documents required per the row. This is a partner-facing overview page, not a direct-apply page, reconfirmed 2026-07-31 — the province delivers this program through third-party training organizations (Hammer Heads below is one). Apply through a delivery agent or an Employment Ontario caseworker (see Guide 12), not through this page directly. No cohort dates are published on this hub page.

Hammer Heads Multi-trades training with direct linkage to registered construction-trade apprenticeships (plumbers, electricians, ironworkers, sprinkler fitters, glaziers, and more) for under-resourced and Indigenous youth in Toronto. hammerheadsprogram.com Who it's for: under-resourced youth and Indigenous youth, Toronto. Referral-required; no documents stated. Confirmed 2026-07-31 from the provider's own FAQ: the program runs approximately 6 intakes a year (12 weeks each) — call before assuming a specific start date, but this is a rolling program, not a single annual cohort. Training itself is unpaid, though the provider notes possible Social Services financial/transportation assistance for eligible participants. Training happens across 25 affiliated GTA sites, with transportation provided from two TTC-friendly pickup points; the program's own office is at 2088 Weston Road, Toronto. Phone 416-449-5115.

Honest gap: Skills for Change's second pre-apprenticeship program, Road Ready (Automotive Service Technician / Auto Body and Collision Damage Repair), is genuinely free and well-built — but it is not included here. Its training site is Mohawk College in Hamilton, not Toronto, and the cohort published on the provider's page (June 22–August 21, 2026) remains underway as of this seal (2026-07-31) with no next-intake date published anywhere the row cites. Publishing it now, with no confirmed future start date, would read as an active door when it may not be one for months. See the seal flags in this lane's report for the full reasoning and the choice made.

2. Bridging for internationally-trained professionals

Ontario Bridge Training Program Sector-specific bridge training for internationally trained professionals across roughly 35 approved Toronto-area programs (2025–2027 cycle), often paired with the Ontario Bridging Participant Assistance Program bursary. Achēv's PMCP and HICAP, cited by name below, are two named Toronto-area delivery agencies operating under this umbrella. ontario.ca — Ontario Bridge Training Program Who it's for: newcomers with permanent-resident status, Ontario. Referral-required; ID needed. This is a hub-level program covering many approved providers, not a single course — start with one of the two named agencies below, or ask an Employment Ontario caseworker (Guide 12) which of the 35 approved programs matches your field. Confirmed ACTIVE 2026-07-31: the 2025–2027 approved-provider list is live on the provider's own page (last updated 2025-11-04) and names both Achēv PMCP and HICAP directly — the earlier secondary "on hold" claim does not match the provider's own current page.

Achēv — Project Management in Construction Program (PMCP) Tuition-free 14-week training in project management for internationally trained construction professionals: project planning methodologies, cost estimation/budgeting, risk management, and procurement, plus a 4–8 week job placement, TRIEC Mentoring Partnership access, and alumni networking. achev.ca/service/pmcp Who it's for: Permanent Residents, Canadian Citizens, Approved Provincial Nominees, CUAET visa-holders, or refugee claimants with an open work permit, Ontario. Needs international education (diploma/degree/certificate) in construction or a related field, 1–3 years professional construction/engineering/supervisory experience, and CLB 6+. Phone (437) 453-5175, email pmcp@achev.ca. Reconfirmed 2026-07-31: no cohort start date is published on the provider's own page — call or email to ask about the next intake before assuming a rolling start.

Achēv — Healthcare Integration and Career Advancement Program (HICAP) 26-week bridge-training program combining online academic training from Toronto Metropolitan University (Health Services Management or Health Project Management stream) with employment coaching, job matching for non-licensed healthcare administration roles, and mentorship. Graduates receive a TMU transcript and Recognition of Completion. achev.ca/service/hicap Who it's for: internationally educated healthcare professionals (Permanent Resident, Convention Refugee, Canadian Citizen, or otherwise legally eligible to work), Ontario. Needs CLB 7, degree/diploma/certificate in healthcare from your home country, 1+ year of healthcare experience there, and — a genuine exclusion worth flagging since Better Jobs Ontario is cited below in this same guide — you must NOT already be receiving Better Jobs Ontario or OSAP funding. This program carries a real $300 fee. The provider's own page, reconfirmed word-for-word on 2026-07-31, states the fee "will be reimbursed upon program eligibility approval" — that is, reimbursement happens only after you're approved, not automatically and not up front. Do not read this program as free, and do not read it as simply "reimbursed" without that condition. Achēv office for this program: 90 Burnhamthorpe Rd W, Suite 1404, Mississauga (GTA-wide by phone/online: 437-451-8227, HICAP@achev.ca). No cohort start date is published in this row — ask directly.

Not published here: a third Achēv program in this same family, Employment Pathways in Canada – Health Careers (EPIC-HC), exists and trains internationally trained health professionals — but its own provider page, rechecked 2026-07-31, still states no participant fee anywhere, and "subsidized" in the atlas row was an inference from government funding rather than a confirmed figure. Its cost remains recorded as unconfirmed and it is not citable here until a fee statement is obtained directly from the provider. See the honest gaps below.

See Guide 12 Jobs & Employment for the Canadian Information Centre for International Credentials (CICIC, free) and World Education Services (WES, a paid but IRCC-trusted credential evaluation) — both are credential-recognition services rather than training programs, and are already cited there in full; not repeated here.

3. Sector-specific staffed training (tech and hospitality)

Application-gated, staffed programs that aren't trades and aren't newcomer-bridging — open more broadly to Ontario residents who meet an income or sector test.

NPower Canada Free tech-training programs (Junior IT Analyst, AI & Data Analytics, Network Security Professional) with IT certifications and job-placement support. npowercanada.ca/programs Who it's for: Ontario residents 18+ with household income under $105,000 (or individual under $50,000), CLB 7 English, and unemployed/underemployed — provider states plainly there is "never a fee," reconfirmed 2026-07-31. Apply directly online: application → pre-screen/virtual interview → offer; no third-party referral needed. Proof of income required. The provider's own eligibility list also states applicants must hold "a high school diploma, GED, CAEC or equivalent" — read the GED reference as stale (the GED was discontinued in Canada after March 2024; see the guide-wide note in "Not here either") and lead with the Canadian Adult Education Credential (CAEC) or a high school diploma instead. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis — apply online to learn the current timeline.

Hospitality Workers Training Centre Flexible self-paced and instructor-led training for hospitality-sector careers, funded as an Employment Ontario project. hwtc.ca Who it's for: adults 18+ with internet/device access, described on the provider's site as serving "across Ontario's hospitality and service sector." Its office address, confirmed 2026-07-31, is 116 Industry St., York, Ontario, postal code M6M 4L8 — M6M is the former City of York, part of the City of Toronto since the 1998 amalgamation, not a separate municipality. The page still doesn't state whether the Service Workers POWER Centre itself runs in-person sessions at that address or purely by phone/online — confirm the delivery format by phone before counting on an in-person seat. Call 1-888-777-4982. Reconfirmed 2026-07-31: no disability-specific case-manager stream is named on the POWER Centre page, matching this row's "none" elig_program setting. Info sessions recur roughly weekly (confirmed dates through August 2026) — treat these as evidence of an ongoing, rolling enrolment cycle rather than a single cohort with a fixed close date.

4. Free online credentialing you can start today

No application, no interview, no gate beyond an internet connection (or, for the TPL entry, a free library card).

freeCodeCamp Free self-paced coding curriculum and certifications (web development, data analysis, and more). freecodecamp.org Who it's for: anyone, anywhere online. No documents, no cost — reconfirmed 2026-07-31. Fully self-directed — no live instructor or staff support, so pair it with a staffed program above if you want coaching alongside the curriculum.

Microsoft Learn Free interactive training modules and certification learning paths for Microsoft technologies (Azure, AI, and more). learn.microsoft.com/training Who it's for: anyone, anywhere online. No documents, no cost — reconfirmed 2026-07-31. The training modules are free, but the certification exams themselves typically carry a separate fee not covered by this row — confirm exam pricing on Microsoft's own site before assuming the whole path is free end to end.

LinkedIn Learning & TPL eLearning Free unlimited access to thousands of LinkedIn Learning courses, plus Gale Courses and O'Reilly Learning, with a Toronto Public Library card, usable from anywhere. tpl.ca/elearning Who it's for: anyone with a TPL card (free to get). No cost beyond having a card; no other documents. Usable anywhere online, not limited to Toronto once you have the card. Reconfirmed 2026-07-31: LinkedIn Learning, Gale Courses and O'Reilly Learning are all still listed live on the provider's eLearning page.

5. Funding that pays for training elsewhere

This is a grant, not a course — it pays for training you take somewhere else. Don't file it as if it were a class.

Better Jobs Ontario (formerly Second Career) Skills-training grant covering tuition, books, transportation and a living allowance — up to $28,000 for programs one year or less, up to $35,000 for programs up to two years — for unemployed/underemployed Ontarians. ontario.ca/page/better-jobs-ontario Who it's for: any Ontario resident who is unemployed or underemployed (including those receiving EI, Ontario Works or ODSP); proof of income required. You apply through an Employment Ontario service-provider caseworker, not directly — see Guide 12 Jobs & Employment's "Start here" section for the caseworker agencies (Employment Ontario, JVS, YMCA, WoodGreen, ACCES, The Career Foundation) that can start this application; this guide doesn't repeat that list. The $28,000 / $35,000 figures were reconfirmed unchanged on the provider's own page on 2026-07-31 (page last updated 2026-02-04); the earlier "currently being updated" notice tied to January 2026 guideline changes no longer appears — these figures can be relied on as current.

If you're Indigenous

Miziwe Biik Aboriginal Employment and Training — training and apprenticeship funding Community-specific — this door is for Indigenous people in the GTA. Beyond the Employment Advisor service covered in Guide 12, the same program funds post-secondary and apprenticeship costs (books, supplies, transportation) and runs its own skilled-trades training programs for unemployed/underemployed Indigenous people. miziwebiik.com Who it's for: Indigenous people who are unemployed or underemployed, GTA. Self-register online (no third-party referral needed); client status expires annually on April 1 — reconfirmed 2026-07-31, the registration window is not currently closed, but confirm before relying on an April-dated status. 433 Cherry St, Toronto. Confirmed 2026-07-31, currently registering: a Concrete Formwork Program (4 weeks, in-class, WHMIS/Working at Heights/mentorship included) begins August 24, 2026, and a Painter's Program (6 weeks, residential/commercial painting) is open for registration with a start date still to be announced. A separate, partner-run Electrical Pre-Apprenticeship Training Program for Indigenous Women (with ALFDC/CUSW) is in an interest-exploration phase on the provider's page, not yet a confirmed running cohort — treat it as a door to ask about, not a scheduled start.

Not here either

  • Job-search help, resume/interview coaching, employer connections, and identity-specific employment services — including CCRW (disability), Helmets to Hardhats (military community, a placement/connection service into registered apprenticeships rather than a training curriculum of its own), and every agency in Guide 12's "Start here" list — live in Guide 12 Jobs & Employment. Not re-served here.
  • Academic upgrading (finishing high school as an adult), ESL, and occupation-specific workplace-language training — including TDSB Adult Credit Programs and Occupation-specific Language Training (OSLT) — belong to the Learn Free guide (fleet #11), not here. If a program on this page lists "a high school diploma or GED" in its entry requirements, read that as the provider's own wording, not as current fact: the GED was discontinued in Canada after March 2024 and no longer exists here. Several provider pages have not caught up — this wave's seal check confirmed NPower Canada's and Skills for Change's Road Ready page still use "GED" in their eligibility text. The free route to a high-school credential is in the Learn Free guide — ask the program directly what it will accept (a Canadian Adult Education Credential, or CAEC, is the current equivalent) before you assume you are ineligible.
  • General makerspace/equipment access (e.g., TPL Digital Innovation Hubs) — its core offering is space and equipment, not a training curriculum with a defined skill or credential outcome; not included here.
