Student Survival

If you're a post-secondary student in the Toronto area trying to make ends meet — food, money, health, legal trouble, or just finding the free stuff your tuition already half-pays for — this page is for you. Entries are grouped by campus where the resource is campus-specific, and marked clearly when something is open to any student or any Toronto resident, not just yours. Nothing here requires you to already know the right office to ask.

A repeated honesty note before you start: a resource run by one school's student union is almost never open to students at a different school. Where an entry below says "University of Toronto students only," "TMU students only," or "York students only," that is not a formality — front-desk staff will check. If your school isn't listed for a given need, check the "anyone can use this" sections first, then ask your own students' union what its equivalent is.

I need food this week

Campus food banks first — they're built for exactly this, and none of them shame you for showing up. General-population food banks near your campus are listed after, honestly labelled as not student-specific — anyone can use them, students included.

UTSU Food Bank (University of Toronto) Free, year-round campus food bank for any U of T student facing food insecurity — single students and students with families alike. Summer 2026 hours: Fridays 11am–3pm, running through Aug 28, 2026 (Room 136, Student Commons, 230 College St, Huron St entrance) — Fall/Winter 2026-27 schedule not yet posted; confirm before relying on this outside summer. Register for a free Daily Bread Client ID first, then book an appointment via Calendly (opens Wednesdays 9:30am for the following week). Bring a T-card or copy of your current timetable, plus reusable bags. utsu.ca/food-bank Who it's for: University of Toronto students only. Appointment required — not a walk-in.

Good Food Centre (Toronto Metropolitan University) Free campus food bank for any TMU student facing any level of food insecurity. Same-day in-person registration available, or book an intake appointment. Hours Mon/Wed/Thu 12–3pm, Tue 2–4pm. A separate site note references "appointment-only after 3:00 PM" in summer — confirm this applies to Good Food Centre before a late visit. SCC B-03A (basement), 55 Gould St. Phone: (416) 979-5000 ext. 552362. yourtmsu.ca/services/food Who it's for: TMU students only. Walk in same-day, or by appointment.

YFS Food Support Centre (York University) Free non-perishable food and basic-need items for York University students who can no longer afford them. Hours are posted only as an image on the org's page and could not be confirmed as text; document requirements aren't stated — confirm both when you reach out. Reach out by email or phone to arrange support — 005 Second Student Centre, Keele campus, 416-736-2100 ext. 44861, foodcentre@yfs.ca. yfs.ca/fsc Who it's for: York University students only. Email or phone first — not a pure walk-in.

Free YFS Grocery Shuttle (York University) Free shuttle bus looping between York's Keele campus and a FreshCo grocery store (Bathurst & Steeles) on select fall/winter semester evenings, 6–9pm. Pickup points: Calumet Loop, Second Student Centre lot, Winters Bus Loop. Not running right now — this is a fall/winter-semester- evening service; 2026/27 dates are not yet posted. Honesty flag, reconfirmed 2026-07-30: real and has run in prior semesters, but the current-year schedule still isn't posted — the provider's own page still says "check back later for the official dates of 2026/2027." Confirm current dates before planning around it. yfs.ca/groceryshuttle Who it's for: York University students only. Seasonal — fall/winter semester evenings only.

Not student-specific, but worth knowing about if your campus food bank is closed, out of range, or not enough

These are general-population food resources, open to anyone in Toronto — not run by any school, and staff won't ask if you're enrolled anywhere. Listed here because students living on tight budgets near downtown/east-end campuses use them heavily in practice.

Community Fridges Toronto (CFTO) 24/7 self-serve public fridges and pantries across the city — take what you need, leave what you can. No registration or check-in of any kind. communityfridgesto.org Who it's for: anyone, Toronto. Walk in any time — no documents, no immigration-status check.

Daily Bread Food Bank Central hub routing to 200+ food programs at 129 member agencies across Toronto, via a Find-a-Food-Bank locator. One member food bank visit per week, network-wide — no ID required for a single person; ID is needed only when registering other household members. Phone: 416-203-0050 ext 1. dailybread.ca — programs by location Who it's for: income-qualifying households, Toronto. Walk in.

Toronto Lawyers Feed the Hungry Free hot meals — upwards of 1,200 a week — from the Osgoode Hall cafeteria (130 Queen St W). Tuesday and Wednesday dinner 5:00pm, Thursday breakfast 6:45am, Sunday brunch 10:00am. Dine-in or takeout, no preconditions to participate. lso.ca — hunger relief Who it's for: anyone, Toronto. Walk in.

Yonge Street Mission — Food (Meals & Food Bank) Runs a youth-focused program (YSM Evergreen) offering wrap-around care and support, including food, for Toronto's street-involved youth aged 16–24 — a fit if you're a younger student in crisis. Separately, a biweekly food-bank component at the YSM Davis Centre (270 Gerrard St E) provides 3–5 days' worth of groceries, by appointment only (416-929-9614 x3200), Tue–Fri 10am–12pm and 1–3pm, closed Mon/Sat/Sun and Dec 24–Jan 2, restricted to a Downtown East catchment (south of Bloor St, east of Yonge St, west of the Don Valley Parkway) — bring photo ID for all household members, proof of address, and proof of recent income for a first visit. Honesty flag: the specific "free hot meals, dine-in or takeaway" claim for the general adult/youth meal program could not be independently reconfirmed on this review — the relevant YSM subpages remained unreachable to the fetch tool; the food-bank details above were independently reconfirmed. ysm.ca/get-help Who it's for: anyone, Toronto Downtown East, for the food bank (catchment-restricted); youth 16–24 for the Evergreen program. Appointment required for the food bank.

I need cheap or free health, dental, or wellness care

Mental health — this one works no matter which school you're at

Good2Talk Free, confidential, anonymous helpline for any student at an Ontario post-secondary institution — college, university, trade school, or Indigenous institution, public or private, including full-time, part-time and international students — professional counselling by phone, crisis text/chat with trained volunteers, peer-to-peer forums, and a campus/community service finder. 100+ languages via interpreter. Call 1-866-925-5454 or text GOOD2TALKON to 686868. good2talk.ca/ontario Who it's for: any Ontario post-secondary student, 24/7 — not gated on public-funding status or one campus.

Dental — supervised-student clinics, not free but well below private rates

George Brown WAVE Dental Clinic Low-cost supervised-student dental care, not free: exams $5 child / $10 mixed / $20 adult, scaling/polishing/client education $10–$20, radiographs $15–40, fillings $50–90, dentures $90–400, whitening $120 (fees in effect as of September 2025, subject to change). Comprehensive care — exams, fillings, dentures — from supervised Dental Hygiene, Dental Assisting, Denturism and Restorative Dental Hygiene students, ages 4+. Accepts CDCP, ODSP, Healthy Smiles and private insurance, which for many patients covers most or all of the cost above. No referral needed — register directly online, by email (chsclientservices@georgebrown.ca), or by phone (416-415-4547). Dental services run during the school year only; suspended during closures/holidays. georgebrown.ca — WAVE Dental Clinic Who it's for: anyone, Toronto — open to the public, not George Brown students only. Self-register, no referral required.

Toronto College of Dental Hygiene and Auxiliaries (TCDHA) student clinic One-time comprehensive dental hygiene treatment by supervised students. Confirmed fee table: child (3–12) cleaning/x-rays/sealants/fluoride free; adult (13–59) cleaning $32, x-rays/sealants/fluoride free; senior (60+) free across the board; whitening kits $95 full / $55 refill; free sports/mouth guards. No referral required — self-register via the "Become a Patient" form or by calling 416-423-3099. 28 Vanley Crescent, M3J 2B8; hours Mon–Fri 7am–8pm, Sat 7am–4pm. toronto-college-dental.org Who it's for: anyone, Toronto — open to the public. No referral required.

University of Toronto Faculty of Dentistry Patient Clinics General and specialty dental care (exams, cleanings, fillings, crowns, root canals, dentures) at reduced fees below private-clinic rates, provided by supervised dental students. Accepts the Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP) and other government dental coverage. 101 Elm St (satellite location also at 777 Bay St). Longer appointments than a private clinic (up to 3 hours, often multiple weekly visits), teaching-clinic format. patients.dentistry.utoronto.ca Who it's for: anyone, Toronto — open to the public, not U of T students only. Not free — reduced-fee, no specific discount percentage published by the provider. Phone to book (416-864-8000).

I need free or low-cost legal help

Downtown Legal Services (University of Toronto Faculty of Law clinic) Free legal help — criminal, employment, family, refugee/immigration, housing, and university- affairs law — for low-income Toronto residents and levy-paying U of T students (U of T students are exempt from the income test). Uses Legal Aid Ontario's financial-eligibility test for everyone else; automatic if you already receive OW, ODSP, or CPP-Disability. Closed for new intake several weeks each term (late Nov–Dec, April–mid-May, late Aug–mid-Sept) — call the intake line 416-978-6447 to confirm capacity before assuming it's open. downtownlegalservices.ca Who it's for: low-income Toronto residents, and any levy-paying U of T student regardless of income. Phone first.

Osgoode Hall Law School — Community Legal Aid Services Programme (CLASP) Free legal services from supervised Osgoode law-student caseworkers, across four practice areas: Administrative Law (including Tenant Rights, ODSP, CICB, and Human Rights matters), Criminal Law, Family Law, and Immigration Law — plus an ID Clinic and a Tax Dispute Clinic. No standalone Employment Law area is listed on the provider's own page. osgoode.yorku.ca/community-clinics/clasp Who it's for: low-income Toronto residents (income-tested) — not restricted to York/Osgoode students. Referral required.

TMSU (Toronto Metropolitan Students' Union) — Legal Services Free legal advice and document assistance for all full-time TMU undergraduate students — housing, employment, debt, family, criminal and corporate law, immigration, and government-agency issues — from a named lawyer via email, Zoom, or in-person consultation. Contact legal@yourtmsu.ca. yourtmsu.ca/services/legal-services Who it's for: full-time TMU undergraduate students only.

York Federation of Students (YFS) — Legal Support Free legal advice and document assistance for all full-time York University undergraduate students — family/criminal law, debt, landlord-tenant conflicts, corporate/commercial law, real estate, intellectual property, immigration and government-agency issues — from a named lawyer via email or in-person appointment. Note: the YFS office is closed Fridays during the summer, which may affect in-person appointment scheduling; email consultation (legal@yfs.ca) is unaffected. Contact legal@yfs.ca, 416-736-5324, 336 First Student Centre, Keele campus. yfs.ca/legal-support Who it's for: full-time York University undergraduate students only.

I need money — taxes, emergency grants, tuition, textbooks

TMSU (Toronto Metropolitan Students' Union) — Free Tax Clinic Free income tax return filing for TMU and TMAPS students with simple returns (employment income, pensions, benefits, scholarships, interest under $1,000 — no self-employment, business, rental, or capital-gains income), done by a CRA-trained team of student volunteers through the CRA's Community Volunteer Income Tax Program. The 2026 clinic ran Tuesday April 7 – Friday April 17, 2026, 10am–6pm, in-person at the TMSU Office (SCC, 55 Gould St, 3rd floor) — that season is now over and registration is closed as of this check. A virtual option (eligibility screener plus an external accountant) also ran for students who couldn't attend in person. Next season expected to open again around March/April 2027 — watch yourtmsu.ca/tax for the announcement. yourtmsu.ca/tax Who it's for: TMU/TMAPS students only, simple tax situations only. Seasonal — closed outside tax season.

TMSU (Toronto Metropolitan Students' Union) — Emergency Grant Program Up to $500 per semester for TMU students facing unexpected financial instability — sudden loss of income, medical/dental emergency, housing instability, urgent family travel, or other unforeseen expenses. Apply via a Google Form during each semester's open application window; one grant per semester. Not running right now: still on hold as of 2026-07-30 (no change in the two days since it was first checked) — the provider's own page states the Grants Committee is still reviewing 150+ applications received May 15–30, 2026, due to overwhelming demand. The formal Spring/Summer 2026 application window (May 15–July 15) has also now closed on its own terms, and no reopening date has been announced. Check yourtmsu.ca/services/grants before counting on this. yourtmsu.ca/services/grants Who it's for: TMU students only, application-window dependent.

Community Volunteer Income Tax Program (CVITP) — free tax clinics, any Toronto resident Free tax-return preparation by trained volunteers at community-hosted clinics, for anyone with modest income and a simple tax situation. Most clinics run during tax season (roughly February–April); summer/year-round availability is clinic-dependent, not guaranteed — check the CRA clinic locator for what's currently open. CRA's suggested income guideline: 1 person ≤$40,000; 2 people ≤$55,000; 3 ≤$60,000; 4 ≤$65,000; +$5,000 per additional person (individual host clinics may adjust locally). Not student-specific, but worth using if your campus's own tax clinic is closed or full — filing unlocks the Canada Child Benefit, GST/HST-successor credit, Canada Workers Benefit, Ontario Trillium Benefit, and more. canada.ca — CVITP Who it's for: anyone with modest/simple-tax-situation income, Canada-wide. Clinic format and season vary — check the CRA locator. Bring ID.

eCampusOntario Open Library Free, openly-licensed textbooks and course materials — a direct substitute for buying commercial textbooks where your course uses one. Built for Ontario college and university students, but the library itself is publicly browsable by anyone, anywhere, with no Ontario affiliation required. openlibrary.ecampusontario.ca Who it's for: anyone, anywhere — no account, no Ontario affiliation required to browse. Free.

Better Jobs Ontario (formerly Second Career) Skills-training grant covering tuition, books, transportation and a living allowance — up to $28,000 for programs one year or less, up to $35,000 for programs up to two years — for unemployed or underemployed Ontarians. Not aimed at current full-time students, but relevant if you're considering re-training instead of (or after) a degree. Apply through an Employment Ontario caseworker, not directly. ontario.ca/page/better-jobs-ontario Who it's for: unemployed/underemployed Ontario residents, income/program-tested. Referral required through an Employment Ontario provider.

I need a computer, software, or tech skills

RCT Outreach (Renewed Computer Technologies) Affordable (not free) renewed desktop and laptop computers for qualifying individuals, families and students, with ready-to-use software and learning modules. rcto.ca/rctech-outreach Who it's for: low-income-qualifying Ontario residents, students included — Ontario-wide via the LICO standard, not Toronto-restricted. Pickup offices in Mississauga, Toronto, Ottawa, and St. Catharines/Niagara; ship-to-home available across Ontario/Canada for a fee. Not free — subsidized pricing.

NPower Canada Free tech-training programs (Junior IT Analyst, AI & Data Analytics, Network Security Professional) with IT certifications and job-placement support — no fee, ever, per the provider's own site. Requires household income under $105,000/individual under $50,000, English at CLB 7, and being unemployed or underemployed. npowercanada.ca/programs Who it's for: Ontario residents 18+, income/program-tested, unemployed or underemployed — not a current-full-time-student program, but relevant to students who've paused or aren't enrolled. Apply directly online via NPower Canada's own application form — no referral needed. Process: online application → pre-screen/virtual interview → offer.

LinkedIn Learning & TPL eLearning Free unlimited access to thousands of LinkedIn Learning courses, plus Gale Courses, O'Reilly Learning and other eLearning platforms — usable from anywhere with a Toronto Public Library card. tpl.ca/elearning Who it's for: anyone with a TPL card, Toronto residents. Free, online.

Toronto Public Library (TPL) free media & programs A free library card unlocks books, ebooks/audiobooks, streaming, digital magazines/newspapers, borrowable wifi hotspots and instruments, free computer/printing access, and career/financial- literacy programs. Not student-specific — any Toronto resident qualifies, and it's one of the highest-value free things in the city for a student on a tight budget. tpl.ca Who it's for: anyone, Toronto. Requires ID and proof of address for a card.

Toronto Public Library Homework Help (Brainfuse) Free online tutoring for K-12 students in core subjects plus writing/French support, plus resume help and citizenship exam prep for adults — via any TPL card. tpl.ca/resource/brainfuse Who it's for: anyone with a TPL card, Toronto. Free, online.

Khan Academy Free, ad-free online courses and practice exercises from basic math through AP-level and early college subjects. I'm a student parent

This guide doesn't yet have campus-specific childcare or student-parent bursary rows confirmed — that's a real gap, not an oversight (see report). In the meantime, general-population supports that a student parent can use:

Student Nutrition Program (SNP) Free breakfast, morning meal, lunch and/or snack programs at Toronto schools and community sites, universally available to every student regardless of family income — the provider's own page frames this as deliberately universal ("all participants benefit... regardless of their socioeconomic status"). Access is set at the school/site level, so ask your child's school office what's running there. Runs during the school year (September–June) only — does NOT operate during summer break. As of this update (late July), this program is not running; it resumes in September. For summer food access for children, the City's separate CampTO Nutrition Program provides a free daily snack to children enrolled in CampTO day camps (late June–August); camp registration itself may carry a fee unless subsidized. Not for post-secondary students themselves — included here because it's the relevant "food for my kids" resource if you're a student parent with school-age children. toronto.ca — Student Nutrition Program Who it's for: all K-12 students, Toronto — no eligibility test.

I'm an Indigenous student

First Nations House — Indigenous Student Services (University of Toronto) Culturally relevant support for Indigenous students at U of T: academic advising, financial-aid/ scholarship guidance, meetings with Traditional Teachers, Indigenous Wellness Counselling, Indigenous Peer Group Mentorship, Firekeeper training, and Indigenous Student Orientation/ Graduation events. Some cultural/social events and the Resource Centre are open to the wider U of T community. Office activity continues through summer 2026, but at least one specific micro-schedule on the page (Indigenous Accessibility Advisor drop-ins) carries a stale, unrefreshed note — call or email ahead (fnh.info@utoronto.ca / 416-978-8227) to confirm summer availability before visiting in person. Borden Building North, 563 Spadina Ave, 3rd Floor. studentlife.utoronto.ca/department/first-nations-house Community-specific — this door is for Indigenous students at U of T (all three campuses); some events open more broadly per the provider's own framing. Not open to the general public.

Post-Secondary Student Support Program (PSSSP) Non-repayable federal funding for First Nations students registered under the Indian Act pursuing post-secondary education — tuition, books, travel support and living allowances, up to $53,000/yr (up to $90,000/yr in extraordinary cases). Funding is limited, not all eligible students are funded, and applications are valid one school year only. Apply through your own First Nation or First Nations-designated organization, not a direct federal portal — contact your band office. Inuit students should instead use the separate Inuit Post-Secondary Education Strategy. sac-isc.gc.ca — PSSSP Community-specific — First Nations students registered under the Indian Act, Canada-wide. Apply via your band office, not directly.

Not student-specific, but worth knowing if money is the real problem

These are general-population supports, not built for students — but disproportionately useful to anyone living on a student budget. None require you to be enrolled anywhere.

Fair Pass Transit Discount Program 36% off TTC adult single-ride PRESTO fares. The discount's continuation is subject to annual City Council funding approval — not guaranteed indefinitely. The separate TTC Monthly Pass product (including the discounted Fair Pass Monthly) is discontinued August 31, 2026 and replaced September 1, 2026 by automatic fare capping — free travel after 47 paid trips in a calendar month for anyone tapping PRESTO or debit/credit card. Income-tested (75% of LIM-AT: 1 person $22,691/yr; 4 people $45,383/yr), ages 20–64 only (other ages use TTC concession fares — check whether a standard post-secondary/student TTC pass, priced separately from Fair Pass, is a better fit if you don't qualify on income). Not student-specific — this is an income-based program, not a student discount. toronto.ca — Fair Pass Who it's for: income-qualifying Toronto residents aged 20–64. Requires proof of income.
