In Crisis Now

If tonight is the emergency — no bed, no food, thinking about ending your life, someone hurt you, or you're not sure where else to go — this page is for you. Everything on it is free. Where getting in the door takes a phone call or a reservation instead of a walk-in, that's said in bold at the start of the entry — before anything else — so you know before you travel, not after.

Don't know where to start? Call 211 (24/7, phone/text/chat, 100+ languages) and say what's happening. They will point you to the right door, including anything on this page.

If you or someone else is in immediate physical danger, call 911.

If you're thinking about suicide, or you're worried about someone who is

9-8-8 — Suicide Crisis Helpline Call or text 988. Free, 24/7, every day. Interpreter access. ASL/LSQ video relay for Deaf/Hard of Hearing callers. First Nations, Inuit and Métis callers can ask to be connected to Hope for Wellness support. Who it's for: anyone, anywhere in Canada. No eligibility test, no cost.

Distress Centres of Greater Toronto Call 416-408-HELP (416-408-4357). Free, confidential, 24/7/365 crisis and suicide-prevention line. Multilingual support (8 languages) available Mon–Fri, 10am–10pm. dcogt.com Who it's for: anyone in Toronto.

Gerstein Crisis Centre Call 416-929-5200 — free 24-hour crisis line, mobile crisis team, and short-term crisis beds for adults. gersteincentre.org Who it's for: adults 16+ in Toronto.

If you need to talk to someone right now (not 911-level, but you need support tonight)

Toronto Community Crisis Service (TCCS) Call 211 to reach this civilian, clinician-led mental-health crisis response — an alternative to sending police to a mental-health emergency. Free, 24/7, and now citywide (delivered by four area-based community partner agencies, dispatched through 211). toronto.ca — Community Crisis Service Who it's for: Toronto residents 16+. No cost, no documents. For non-emergency mental-health crisis support — for immediate danger, call 911.

ConnexOntario — 1-866-531-2600 Free 24/7 phone/text/chat connecting you to mental health, addictions, and problem-gambling treatment programs across Ontario. connexontario.ca Who it's for: anyone in Ontario.

Progress Place Warm Line and Krasman Centre Warm Line Peer-support phone/text/chat lines for isolation, loneliness, and anxiety — staffed by people with lived experience, not clinicians; neither is a crisis line. Progress Place: call 416-960-9276 or text 647-557-5882, 6pm–midnight, 7 days a week. Krasman: phone line 1-888-777-0979, confirmed 24/7 by the provider's own page (live chat separately available 10am–midnight daily — the phone line, not chat, is the 24/7 channel). As of October 1, 2025, Krasman discontinued dedicated newcomer support services (peer support generally remains available to all). progressplace.org · krasmancentre.com Who it's for: anyone in Ontario.

CAMH — Access CAMH Call 416-535-8501, option 2, Mon–Fri, 8:30am–4:30pm (closed evenings, weekends, and holidays — this is not a crisis line). Free centralized intake line to CAMH's mental-health and addictions services. Addictions services accept self-referral (if eligible); most mental-health services need a referral from a healthcare provider. camh.ca/access-camh Who it's for: anyone in Toronto, during weekday business hours. Note: mental-health streams usually need a provider referral — addictions self-referral is available, subject to eligibility. Outside these hours, or for crisis support, use 988 or Distress Centres (416-408-4357) above.

Accessible to Deaf, hard-of-hearing and non-verbal callers. canadianhumantraffickinghotline.ca Who it's for: anyone being trafficked, a survivor, or someone concerned about another person.

Otherwise, free wraparound support for trafficked persons — counselling, food security, legal referrals, and help applying for work/residence permits for trafficking survivors. fcjrefugeecentre.org/human-trafficking Who it's for: trafficking survivors, including undocumented people — status is not a barrier.

If you need a bed tonight

Start here — this is the front door to Toronto's shelter system:

Central Intake — 24/7 Shelter Access Line City-operated phone line that refers you to an available emergency shelter bed or 24-hour respite site tonight, and connects you to detox, mental-health, refugee, and legal services. toronto.ca — Central Intake Who it's for: anyone experiencing homelessness in Toronto. No documents required.

Toronto Warming Centres & Winter Respite Sites Free, walk-in emergency warming space — the City's own page confirms you do not need to contact Central Intake to access a Warming Centre; walk-ins are welcome. Not open on a fixed calendar — the City activates Warming Centres whenever the forecast reaches -5°C or a winter weather warning is issued, opening at 5pm and staying open 24 hours until conditions ease; two additional Surge Sites activate at -15°C. Check toronto.ca — Warming Centres for current activation status before travelling — if you're reading this outside a cold snap, they are very likely not active right now, whatever the calendar month. Who it's for: anyone. Walk in — no documents required. Active only during a temperature-triggered activation, not a fixed season.

Streets to Homes (S2H) — Street Outreach 24/7/365 outreach if you're sleeping outside or in an encampment: water, food, ID and income-support help, shelter referrals. The SHARC site (129 Peter St) offers free showers, laundry, phone, and hygiene supplies 24/7, plus walk-in housing counselling weekdays 9am–3pm. toronto.ca — Streets to Homes Who it's for: anyone sleeping outside, Toronto. No documents required.

The Scott Mission — Showers, Laundry & Washroom Access Free walk-in washroom, shower, and laundry access Mon–Fri 9:00–11:30am, at 502 Spadina Ave — a separate service from the Welcome Centre meals program above (same building). Also runs a Thursday Identification Clinic (9:30–11am, via the PAID project), budgeting/financial help, a seasonal income-tax clinic, and TTC tokens for verified medical appointments. scottmission.com Who it's for: anyone, Toronto. Walk in, weekday mornings only — closed afternoons and weekends.

City Storage of Belongings for People Transitioning from Encampments When you move from an encampment into shelter or housing, City staff can photograph, bag and tag your belongings and store them free of charge — up to 30 days short-term, or up to 6 months if you're actively working on a housing plan with Streets to Homes or a partner agency. To arrange pickup once you're housed, call 416-338-1876 and leave a message with a callback number; calls are returned within 48 hours, Mon–Fri. toronto.ca — Encampment Outreach & Response Who it's for: anyone moving from an encampment into shelter or housing, Toronto. Arranged through Streets to Homes outreach staff, not a walk-in service.

Covenant House Toronto 24/7 crisis shelter for youth with no safe place to stay. Drop-in centre: Mon–Sun 12:00pm–6:45pm (416-204-7037) — not 24/7 like the shelter. Health clinic: 9am–12pm & 1–4pm Mon–Fri, until 7pm Tuesdays (416-204-7995), with or without a health card. Covenant House's own anti-trafficking team line is weekdays 8am–6pm (416-593-4849 / 1-800-435-7308) — for 24/7 trafficking crisis support, use the Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline above. covenanthousetoronto.ca/get-help Who it's for: youth 16–24, Toronto. If you're a refugee claimant with nowhere to stay tonight

Calling these three is the right first move, but none of them confirms guaranteed same-night entry at any hour of night — if you're turned away or can't reach anyone, use Central Intake above, which also routes refugee claimants.

Sojourn House Call (416) 864-0515 ext. 221 or 222. Emergency shelter (76 beds) for refugee claimants, including separated youth, single adults, women and families — plus settlement and refugee-process support. The shelter itself operates 24/7, but intake works on a waitlist: front-line staff will assess your situation and add your name to the list; you'll be contacted as soon as a bed opens, not necessarily the same night. sojournhouse.org Who it's for: refugee claimants, Toronto.

Christie Refugee Welcome Centre Call (416) 588-9277. Emergency shelter (76 beds) for refugee claimant families of any background, with an onsite health clinic and a Centre for Refugee Children for unaccompanied minors. Intake hours are not published on the provider's site — phone first. christiestreetrc.com Who it's for: refugee claimant families, Toronto.

Adam House Call (416) 538-2836. Short-term shelter (average 8-week stay) for refugee claimants, with meals, legal-aid and social-assistance help. Intake hours are not published on the provider's site — phone first. adamhouse.org Who it's for: refugee claimants, Toronto.

If you or someone else has overdosed, or you use drugs and want to stay safer

If someone has overdosed right now, call 911 and use naloxone if you have it — Ontario's Good Samaritan Drug Overdose Act protects people who call for help.
