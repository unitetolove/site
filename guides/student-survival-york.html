Student Survival — York University (Keele + Glendon)

Selection rule for this page: an entry qualifies only if it's run by or exclusively for York University students — a York students'-union service (YFS or YUGSA), a York-specific school- or faculty-level emergency fund, or a York-specific campus program. Nothing here requires you to already know which office to ask, but everything here does require you to be a York student (or, for the health/dental plans, to already be paying the fee that funds one).

For everything not specific to York — general-population food banks, tax-filing clinics open to any income-qualifying individual, Good2Talk, library resources beyond laptop lending, culture/sport passes open to the public, and the honest gaps in student-parent and Indigenous- student coverage — see the cross-campus base guide, Student Survival. For same-night crisis needs (shelter, food, crisis lines), see In Crisis Now. For food-specific resources beyond a campus food bank, see Food Today & Groceries. This page doesn't repeat any of that content — it points to it once, here, and stays focused on what's actually York-specific below.

York students split two ways that both matter for this page. First, undergrad vs. grad: undergrads are represented by the York Federation of Students (YFS); graduate students by the York University Graduate Students' Association (YUGSA) — two separate unions with two separate health plans, two separate legal-aid pictures, and different emergency-money doors. Second, Keele vs. Glendon: York is a two-campus school, and several services below (laptop borrowing, campus recreation, the counselling clinic) run separately at each campus with their own hours, facility, or eligibility quirks — that split is stated on every entry where it applies. Every entry below is labelled with which student group it's for (all York students / undergrad / grad / international) and which campus(es) it actually operates at.

I need food this week

YFS Food Support Centre (York University campus food bank) Free non-perishable food and basic-need items for York University students who can no longer afford them. Access by emailing or calling to arrange support — 005 Second Student Centre, Keele campus, 416-736-2100 ext. 44861, foodcentre@yfs.ca. yfs.ca/fsc For: all York students, undergrad and grad — YUGSA's own page confirms graduate students rely on this same centre; there is no separate grad food bank. Email or phone first, not a pure walk-in. Keele-based intake; the page doesn't state a separate Glendon pickup point.

Free YFS Grocery Shuttle (York University) Free shuttle bus looping between York's Keele campus and a FreshCo grocery store (Bathurst & Steeles) on select fall/winter semester evenings, 6–9pm. Pickup points: Calumet Loop, Second Student Centre lot, Winters Bus Loop. Honesty flag, reconfirmed 2026-07-28: real and has run in prior semesters, but the current-year schedule still isn't posted — the provider's own page still says "check back later for the official dates." Confirm current dates before planning around it. yfs.ca/groceryshuttle For: York students at Keele campus. Seasonal — fall/winter semester evenings only. No Glendon equivalent found.

I need money — emergency grants, bursaries, or tax help

York students facing sudden financial hardship have five separate emergency-money doors, gated by different eligibility slices depending on who you are — undergrad, grad, international, or a specific faculty. Being turned away from one doesn't mean you're out of options.

YUGSA Student Hardship Fund (grad) Up to $300 per applicant (varies with application volume) for YUGSA members facing unforeseen financial difficulty — picket-line hardship, job loss, or urgent financial need are the provider's own listed priority cases. Regular deadlines August 1, December 1 and April 1; apply online with a 200–500 word statement of financial need to finance@yugsa.ca. For urgent situations — imminent homelessness, physical or mental injury — email info@yugsa.ca immediately rather than waiting for the next deadline. yugsa.ca/funding For: YUGSA members = registered full-time or part-time York graduate students; M.A. Schulich students explicitly excluded.

York University Emergency Bursary (undergrad — domestic only) One-time emergency bursary for actively registered undergraduate domestic students facing unexpected circumstances that threaten completing the semester. Apply by email to bursary@yorku.ca with a detailed written statement of the emergency plus supporting documentation; decisions within 3 business days. Amount isn't published as a fixed figure — the page describes it as varying by situation. students.yorku.ca — Other Funding For: undergraduate domestic students (Canadian citizen or permanent resident) only, one-time per York tenure. Run by the university (Student Financial Services), not a students' union.

FGS Emergency Bursary (grad — all faculties) The grad-side counterpart to the door above: emergency financial assistance for current registered York University graduate students, specifically for rent, food and utilities. Unlike most York bursaries, no Financial Aid, Awards and Scholarships (FAAS) profile is required — email fgseb@yorku.ca directly to request the online application. Funds post to your student account and refund by EFT. yorku.ca/gradstudies — Bursaries For: York graduate students, any faculty, currently registered. Run by the Faculty of Graduate Studies, not a students' union.

International Student Emergency Bursary (undergrad and grad — international) Up to $1,000 per student per academic year (May–April) for an unexpected, specific, urgent and temporary financial emergency — not tuition, not recurring rent/utilities, not ongoing hardship. Apply online through your studentPPY@yorku.ca account after completing the FAAS application and showing other funding sources are exhausted. Reviewed roughly twice a month across three yearly windows (Summer, Fall, Winter); decision within 10–15 business days. yorku.ca/international — Scholarships, Awards and Bursaries For: York international students (valid study permit), undergrad or grad, full-time (9.0+ credits). The domestic Emergency Bursary above (FT-15204) explicitly excludes international students — this is their equivalent door.

LA&PS Emergency Bursary (undergrad — one faculty) Up to $500 per academic year (higher for extenuating circumstances) for LA&PS undergraduate students, preference given to housing insecurity, food insecurity, wage loss, or urgent basic- living-necessity needs. One application per student across an entire York tenure. Apply by contacting the Associate Dean of Students at awdslaps@yorku.ca; decisions made monthly during fall/winter terms. Requires the FAAS application completed first. yorku.ca/laps — Emergency Funding For: LA&PS undergraduate students only (domestic, Ontario resident) — York's largest undergrad faculty, named here as a worked example of a single-faculty door; not open to students in York's other faculties. Honesty flag, per the provider's own page: "Applications for the LA&PS Emergency Bursary are now closed and will resume in the Fall of 2026." Real, annual-cycle program — not currently accepting applications as of this check.

Schulich and Osgoode also run their own faculty-specific funding, named but not individually detailed on York's general funding page — ask your Faculty's Office of the Dean if none of the five doors above fits.

I need free or low-cost health, dental, and counselling support

York students carry two different fee-funded health & dental insurance plans, split by undergrad vs. grad — plus one shared, free, on-campus counselling and health service open to everyone regardless of which plan you're on.

York University Student Counselling, Health & Well-being (SCHW) Free single-session walk-in counselling and short-term counselling, in-person and virtual, plus support groups. A separate Health Services stream offers nurse clinic consultations, vaccine programs and wellness/health-education programming. Both Keele and Glendon run their own SCHW presence — Glendon's page confirms an on-site counselling and health service distinct from Keele's, alongside a shared Virtual Well-being Hub. After hours, Glendon's page points students to Good2Talk, and to 911 or Glendon Campus Security (416-487-6808) for emergencies. students.yorku.ca/counselling For: all York students, both campuses. Honesty flag: neither provider page states general SCHW walk-in hours or a booking mechanism beyond "walk-in" and "virtual."

YFS Health & Dental Plan (undergrad) Extended health and dental insurance for York full-time and part-time undergraduate students, including Glendon and Osgoode JD/LOP students — prescription drugs (80% to $1,000), dental (75% to $600), vision care, paramedical services, contraceptives, and psychotherapy ($125/visit, up to 10 visits/year). Dependent coverage available as an add-on. Not free — included in mandatory YFS fees you already pay, unless you opt out (Full-time: $358.40/yr; Part-time: $703.04/yr; opt-out via healthplan@yfs.ca by the semester deadline — Fall 2025's Oct 17 and Winter 2026's Feb 6 deadlines have both already passed as of this check). yfs.ca/healthplan For: York undergraduate students only (Keele, Glendon and Osgoode JD/LOP). Graduate students use the separate YUGSA plan below. Fee-included, not free — opt-out available.

YUGSA Health & Dental Plan (grad) The graduate-student equivalent — prescription drugs (80% to $1,000), dental (75% to $600), vision care, professional/paramedical services (psychology, physiotherapy, acupuncture, naturopathy), and travel/accidental-death insurance. Mental-health coverage runs deeper than the undergrad plan advertises: a standard psychologist/counsellor benefit plus 5 additional sessions per year via Inkblot. Full-time grad students without a teaching contract are automatically enrolled; part-time students and dependents opt in. Not free — included in mandatory YUGSA fees unless opted out (Single: $517/yr; one dependent: $519.36/yr; two+ dependents: $807.24/yr; opt-out via Greenshield's portal by Feb 28, 2026 — already passed as of this check). yugsa.ca/health-plan For: York graduate students only, full-time (automatic) or part-time (opt-in). Undergrads use the separate YFS plan above. Fee-included, not free — opt-out available.

This is York's own campus clinic and insurance coverage — separate from Good2Talk (1-866-925-5454 / text GOOD2TALKON to 686868), the free 24/7 phone/text helpline open to any Ontario post-secondary student regardless of school, already covered in the base guide.

I need free or low-cost legal help

York Federation of Students (YFS) — Legal Support (undergrad — full-time only) Free legal advice and document assistance for all full-time York University undergraduate students — family/criminal law, debt, landlord-tenant conflicts, corporate/commercial law, real estate, intellectual property, immigration and government-agency issues — from a named lawyer via email or in-person appointment. Contact legal@yfs.ca, 336 First Student Centre, Keele campus. yfs.ca/legal-support For: full-time York University undergraduate students only.

Honest gap: no YUGSA-run legal-advice service was found. YUGSA's own services page describes general "advocacy" — helping grad students navigate university administration and resolve problems — but names no dedicated legal-advice line, lawyer, or document-review service the way YFS does for undergrads. Grad students needing legal help currently have no confirmed York-specific door; the base guide's Downtown Legal Services and Osgoode CLASP entries (open to low-income Toronto residents generally, not York-gated) are the nearest fallback — see Student Survival.

I need a laptop, software, or other tech gear

York University Libraries Laptop Borrowing (Glendon Campus) Free laptop borrowing — 5 Dell Latitude 5440 laptops, first-come first-served, no reservations — from Frost Library, Glendon campus. Loans run up to 24 hours (renewable if another device is free, otherwise a 30-minute wait before re-borrowing). Each laptop comes with a power cord and carrying case, Microsoft Office, Chrome, Antidote and PDF-XChange. First-time borrowers complete a one-time Laptop Lending Agreement Form. library.yorku.ca — Laptop Borrowing For: all registered York University students (Keele and Glendon), plus faculty/staff, in good standing (no library fines/liabilities over $25). Honest gap: the laptops themselves are physically located only at Frost Library, Glendon campus — Keele's libraries (Scott, Steacie) circulate accessories like calculators and cables, but no laptops. A Keele student is technically eligible but would need to travel to Glendon to actually borrow one.

York University Free Software & Cloud Storage (UIT) Free Microsoft 365 (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneNote, Outlook) plus OneDrive cloud storage, installable on up to 5 PCs/Macs and mobile devices. Separately, the MyApps Portal gives free remote/browser access to licensed coursework software using your Passport York login, without installing it locally, plus a MySoftware page listing further free downloads (browsers, antivirus, media players). yorku.ca/uit — Software & Apps For: all York University students, both campuses, active Passport York account. Honest gap: no Adobe Creative Cloud licence found on the provider's own software page — if York offers one, it isn't on this page. Access to Microsoft 365 ends when your account converts to Alumni status.

I need to get between Keele and Glendon

Glendon-Keele Shuttle (York University Transportation Services) Shuttle bus between York's two campuses, roughly 45 minutes each way, Monday–Friday only (excludes reading weeks/exam weeks). Summer schedule (Apr 27–Sept 8, 2026): 4 daily departures from Keele's Archives Lay-by, 5 from Glendon's York Hall. Fall schedule (from Sept 9): 7 daily departures each way, with evening service to roughly 6:25pm (Keele) / 7:25pm (Glendon). Reserve your seat through York's Mobile Ordering app — first-come, first-served, limited seating. Contact 416-736-5335 (or 416-736-5454 after 6pm). yorku.ca — Glendon-Keele Shuttle For: current York students, staff and faculty with a valid YU-card, both campuses. Cost honesty flag: the provider's own page never states a fare or says the ride is free — cost is left as unknown rather than assumed.

I want free or low-cost fitness, sports, or recreation

Both campuses run their own recreation facility, separately priced and separately booked — a membership at one doesn't cover the other.

York University Campus Recreation / Tait McKenzie Centre (Keele campus) Fitness centre, 30+ weekly group fitness/aerobics/cross-training classes, drop-in sports, intramural teams, pool access and swim lessons. Register via the RecYork portal online or in person at Tait McKenzie Client Services (416-736-5185, taitmck@yorku.ca); a PAR-Q health screening is required. A single drop-in class costs $15, fully credited toward membership if you enrol within 7 days. yorkulions.ca/recreation For: York students, Keele-campus facility. Not free — price honesty flag: two current yorkulions.ca pages disagree on cost. The recreation page lists a $15 "shoe tag" for unlimited Fitness Centre and group-fitness access; a separate, older-dated membership page states a $25 annual membership for what reads as the same access. Both reported rather than guessing which is current — call ahead to confirm before budgeting.

Glendon Athletic Club (GAC) student membership (Glendon campus) 55,000 sq ft facility on the Glendon campus: pool, gym, weight room, squash/tennis/badminton courts, sports field, locker rooms, group fitness and cycling classes, reduced pricing on paid programs (personal training, boxing, karate, swim lessons), plus free weight-room orientation, squash clinics and self-defence classes. Register in person at the GAC Membership Office with your YU-card and payment; renew each September. Hours: Mon–Thu 7am–10:30pm, Fri 7am–9pm, Sat–Sun 8am–8pm. glendon.yorku.ca/gac For: York/Glendon students, Glendon-campus facility. Not free — same price-honesty flag as the Keele entry above: the "for students" page states $20/year; a separate GAC "about" page states $25/year for the same student membership. Both current, neither resolves the $5 gap — confirm before paying.

Honest gaps — things York students will look for that the atlas doesn't have yet

A career closet / free professional-attire lending program. No York equivalent of the "career closet" programs common at other North American universities surfaced. York's Career Centre (careers.yorku.ca) publishes advice on interview attire, but no dedicated free-clothing-lending program.

No legal-advice service confirmed for graduate students. See the legal section above — YUGSA's own page describes only general advocacy, not a dedicated legal-advice door the way YFS runs one for undergrads.

Glendon's own student union (GCSU/AÉCG) has no confirmed individual student benefit distinct from what's already cited here. Its own site (yuconnect.yorku.ca/GCSU) describes governance structure, clubs support, and social/orientation programming, but no concrete food, health, legal, or emergency-funding service of its own — Glendon students' concrete resources run through GAC (athletics, cited above) and the York-wide YFS/YUGSA/FGS doors, not through GCSU directly. Not a rejection of GCSU's existence — a statement that its own page gave nothing citable beyond what's already in this guide.

A confirmed Glendon-specific food bank distinct from the Food Support Centre. A mental-health- club-run resource page (not an official York or YFS page) listed a "Food 4 Thought" on-campus food bank at the Student Centre, 4700 Keele St — this appears to be the same Food Support Centre already cited above under a different or older name, not a separate program. No Glendon-specific food-bank pickup point was found — Glendon students needing food support currently rely on the Keele-based Food Support Centre's email/phone intake.

Campus-specific childcare with a confirmed student-priority benefit. The Lee Wiggins Childcare Centre operates on York's Keele campus (Room 201, First Student Centre) and describes itself as serving "members of York University and neighbouring communities" — but its own site does not state that York students get priority admission, a discount, or any York-student-specific benefit over the general public. Because this guide only cites what's run by or exclusively for York students, it doesn't qualify as a full entry here; a real childcare facility exists on campus, but no student-specific gate was confirmed. No separate campus-specific student-parent bursary was found either.

No separate part-time-undergraduate students' union. Unlike TMU (which has TMAPS for part-time/continuing-ed students), no York equivalent surfaced in this review — YFS and YUGSA appear to be York's only two student unions, split by undergrad/grad rather than by full-time/part-time status. Part-time undergrads are covered by the YFS Health & Dental Plan above at a different rate, but YFS Legal Support is stated as full-time-only — a real gap for part-time undergrads needing legal help.
