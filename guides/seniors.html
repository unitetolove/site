Seniors

Hard selection rule for this guide: an entry belongs here only if an older adult in Toronto can use it because of their age — age-gated to, or explicitly designed for, seniors — and it is free or explicitly labelled with what it costs. A general service that merely happens to be used by older adults (a general food bank, a general legal clinic, a general recreation centre) does not belong here — it gets a pointer at most. That rule cut a real number of near-misses — general recreation-fee subsidies, a general income-tested transit discount, an electricity-credit program whose higher tier is for Indigenous/electric-heat households rather than seniors.

Age gates in this guide are carried exactly as each provider states them — 50+, 55+, 59+, 60+, 65+, and sometimes "not stated" — and they are never rounded into one number. A 57-year-old turned away by our rounding is our error; a 57-year-old who never calls because we printed "65+" when the real gate was 55+ is worse. Read the age on the specific entry you're looking at, not a general impression of what "senior" means.

Meals on Wheels is not free in Toronto, from any provider in this atlas. Every entry below states its actual price, or states plainly that the provider requires an intake call before publishing one. If you came to this guide looking for free home-delivered meals, please read the "Meals delivered to your home" entries under "Staying in your home" carefully before calling.

FT-9212, FT-9619 and FT-9716 are excluded from this guide. None of the three is a seniors-specific row (Somali settlement services, Deaf/hard-of-hearing services, and a general meal program), so none was a candidate for this guide in the first place.

Money coming in

Old Age Security (OAS) and Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) Monthly federal pension for seniors 65+ regardless of work history — up to $751.97/mo ($9,024/ yr) at 65 for the current quarter (July–Sept 2026), rising with age — plus GIS, a tax-free top-up for low-income OAS recipients, up to $1,123.17/mo single ($13,478/yr). canada.ca — Old Age Security · canada.ca — Guaranteed Income Supplement Citizens/legal residents 65+ meeting residency requirements. Often auto-enrolled — watch for a letter around your 64th birthday. GIS requires your 2025 taxes filed or payments may stop. Full detail: Guide 02 Claim Everything.**

GAINS (Guaranteed Annual Income System) Monthly non-taxable top-up for low-income Ontario seniors 65+, on top of OAS/GIS: max $92/mo single ($1,104/yr); the couple rate depends on which spouse is a pensioner — check the current rate tables rather than a single flat figure. ontario.ca — GAINS payments for seniors Citizens/PRs 65+, 10+ years Canadian residency, already on OAS and GIS, private income under $4,416 single/$8,832 couple. No separate application — automatic via annual tax filing. Full detail: Guide 02 Claim Everything.**

City of Toronto Property Tax & Water/Utility Rebate Programs Property Tax Increase Cancellation, Property Tax Increase Deferral, and Water/Solid Waste Rebate — combined household income must be $62,000 or under, plus one of: age 65+, age 60–64 with GIS/Spouse's Allowance, age 50+ with a registered pension, or receiving a disability benefit (any age). Must reapply annually with a CRA Notice of (Re)Assessment; 2026 tax-year deadline is November 2, 2026. toronto.ca — Property Tax and Utility Relief Seniors 50+/60-64/65+ as stated above, Toronto homeowners. Opt-in application, not automatic. Full detail: Guide 02 Claim Everything (also in Guide 08 Utilities and Bills).

Staying in your home

Call 1-833-515-1234, or get a doctor/nurse/self/ family-member referral. Corrected 2026-07-31: the provider's own current Contact and Making-a-Referral pages lead with 1-833-515-1234, 7 days/week 365 days/year, auto-routing to the nearest local office — this replaces 310-2222, which this guide previously led with and which is no longer what the provider's own site recommends as the number to call (found by a sibling seal lane fetching these two provider pages directly; the 310-2222 number is not retained here as a confirmed alternate, since only third-party directories, not the provider's own current page, still associate it with the Toronto Central office). Homemakers & Nurses Services Program Subsidized homemaking help — light housekeeping, laundry, shopping, meal preparation — via contracted community agencies. Call 416-392-8579 to start a 5-step process: phone intake, phone screening, wait list, in-home assessment, then subsidy approval. Wait-list length is not published. toronto.ca — Homemakers and Nurses Services Seniors 65+ with sustained/periodic impairment, or any age with a physician-confirmed disability or chronic condition, Toronto. Air Conditioner Assistance Program Free portable air conditioner for eligible low-income seniors with a health-related need, in 3+ storey apartment buildings with no existing cooling. The 2026 window has already closed: applications opened in April, roughly 1,500 units were awarded by randomized draw, and delivery ran May–June 2026 — this is not first-come-first-served and not guaranteed to everyone who qualifies. Expect the next cycle around spring 2027. toronto.ca — Air Conditioning Assistance for Low-Income Seniors Seniors 65+ with a health-related need, income under Statistics Canada's Low-Income Measure (e.g. $30,255 for 1 person), Toronto, in a 3+ storey building. Free, but not currently open for new applications.

Community Paramedicine Program (Toronto Paramedic Services) New in v0.4. Free non-emergency home visits from a certified Toronto Paramedic — a health/social assessment, vital signs, a medication review, a home hazard check, and referrals into the wider care system — plus biweekly Community Paramedic-Led Clinics at Toronto Community Housing, Toronto Seniors Housing and shelter sites, a seasonal Homebound Vaccination Program (flu/COVID, October– March), free naloxone kits and training, and weekly street outreach alongside the City's Streets-to-Homes team. Toronto ('M' postal code) for most streams. Staying well

Free Dental Care (Toronto Public Health Clinics) — including the Ontario Seniors Dental Care Program Free basic dental care (exams, x-rays, fillings, cleaning, some root canals, extractions, urgent care) for low-income seniors 65+, via the Ontario Seniors Dental Care Program (OSDCP) income thresholds: $25,480/yr net income (single) or $42,290/yr combined (couple) or less, for the program year starting August 1, 2026. You must attend a TPH clinic in person to be assessed — call first to book. toronto.ca — Free Dental Care Low-income seniors 65+ under the OSDCP thresholds above, Toronto. Call to book, then attend in person — not a walk-in door. Full and partial dentures are covered but you pay the lab cost.

OHIP-Covered Eye Exams One fully OHIP-covered major eye exam every 18 months for seniors 65+ (every 12 months with an eligible eye condition). A widely underused benefit — many seniors still pay out of pocket for an exam OHIP already covers. ontario.ca — what OHIP covers Seniors 65+, Ontario. No separate enrollment — book with any optometrist and bring your health card. Free.

Ontario Drug Benefit (ODB) program Coverage for most of the cost of 5,900+ formulary medications, auto-enrolled at 65+. Seniors pay a $100/yr deductible (prorated by birth month — as low as $8.33 if born in June) plus up to $6.11/prescription, unless enrolled in the Seniors Co-Payment Program below. ontario.ca — get coverage for prescription drugs Seniors 65+, Ontario. No separate enrollment — bring your Ontario health card to any pharmacy. Subsidized, not free.

ODB Seniors Co-Payment Program For low-income seniors already on ODB: waives the $100/yr deductible entirely and caps co-payment at $2/prescription. Income cutoff for the August 2026–July 2027 program year: $25,480/yr (no spouse) or $42,290/yr combined. ontario.ca — Seniors Co-Payment Program Low-income seniors 65+ already on ODB, under the income thresholds above, Ontario. Separate online application required — you can apply up to 3 months before your 65th birthday.

Not being alone

This section matters more than its entry count suggests. For many readers of this guide, isolation is the actual problem they came here to solve — not a side effect of one of the other six. Seniors Active Living Centres and the older-adult centre network below are the free doors built specifically to address it.

Seniors' Active Living Centre (SALC) Program — Ontario-wide network An Ontario-funded network of 300+ programs at local older-adult centres — arts and culture, book clubs, fitness classes, health/wellness services, lifelong learning, multicultural social groups and special events — in person, online and by phone. Includes "Seniors' Centre Without Walls" (oacao.org), phone-based group programming for isolated or homebound seniors who can't attend in person. ontario.ca — Active living guide for seniors The province's own hub page does not state a fee or age threshold for the network as a whole — individual centres set their own membership fee and age gate (see WoodGreen's concrete example, below). Find a centre near you via the province's own postal-code locator, linked from this page — a live interactive map, not something this guide can usefully reduce to a static list.

WoodGreen Seniors' Active Living Centre (SALC) A concrete, currently-open example of the network above: recreational activities, educational workshops, exercise/falls-prevention classes, cultural events and outings, in person at six Toronto locations (721 Broadview Ave, 1070 Queen St E, 444 Logan Ave, 266 Donlands Ave, 500 Kingston Rd lower level, 2339 Dufferin St) plus virtual and phone programming, 48 weeks/year. woodgreen.org — Seniors Active Living Centre Seniors 55+, Toronto. $20/year membership — explicitly may be waived for those who cannot afford it, and is free for WoodGreen housing tenants.   • All five phone numbers flagged missing at build time were found this seal pass and are printed in the entries above. Two are the organization's general switchboard rather than a program- dedicated line (Circle of Care Meals on Wheels: 416-635-2860; Scarborough Ride: SCHC's (416) 642-9445) — each entry says so. SPRINT Senior Care (416-481-6411), Toronto Ride (416-481-5250) and Circle of Care iRIDE Plus (1-844-474-3301) each have their own direct line, confirmed on the provider's own page.
  • Circle of Care's Adult Day Program subsidized daily rate is still not published anywhere — the program page continues to direct callers to (905) 738-1128 for the actual figure. (Circle of Care iRIDE Plus's per-ride rates, previously listed here as unpublished, were found this seal pass on the provider's own linked rate page, irideplus.com/service-rates — see the entry above.)
  • The Seniors Active Living Centre network's real reach is bigger than this guide's two entries. Scarborough Centre for Healthy Communities' own Active Living Centre, Applegrove Community Complex, and the Bob Rumball Toronto Deaf Seniors Centre were named as SALC-network sites in research but their own provider pages were not independently fetched this wave — real, findable via the province's own locator, but not yet their own atlas rows.
  • No Inuit-specific seniors program was found anywhere in the atlas — the same gap already flagged in Guide 17 Indigenous Services and Learning Traditions.
  • Regional Geriatric Program of Toronto's phone number (416-480-6026) has been reconfirmed directly on the provider's own Contact page (2026-07-31) — the earlier stale-reference-sheet caveat no longer applies.
  • TTC's Senior monthly/12-month pass prices expire August 31, 2026 (reconfirmed 2026-07-31), when TTC replaces them with fare capping — check ttc.ca directly if you're reading this after that date.
  • Age gates are genuinely inconsistent across every provider in this guide — 50+, 55+, 59+, 60+, 65+, and "not stated" all appear, sometimes for programs run by the very same organization (Circle of Care alone uses 55+, 65+, and unstated across its own programs). This guide carries each gate exactly as its provider states it rather than picking one number for convenience — every gate was spot-checked live this seal pass and none has drifted.
  • The Air Conditioner Assistance Program's "next cycle around spring 2027" remains this lane's own inference, not a page-quoted date — the City's page describes only the 2026 cycle (now fully closed) and states no 2027 timeline. Check the provider's page directly closer to that date.
