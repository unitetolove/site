International and Cross-Canadian Homelessness Comparators — Playbook

Other cities' celebrated homelessness successes, read past the headline — what actually happened and what Toronto could learn.

DRAFTThe playbookThe evidence file

What Toronto can actually do on international and cross-Canadian homelessness comparators — each move with its costs, its beneficiaries, and its receipts.

v2.0 · 2026-08-11

---

The honest bottom line

Every homelessness debate reaches for a comparator — Finland, Houston, Vienna, Rockford — and each one turns out to carry a documented complication once checked against its own primary sources, not the slogan version. Houston's HUD-mandated coordinated-entry overhaul is real, but its unsheltered count rose 15.8% in 2025 even as a downtown sidewalk-obstruction ban pushed citations from about 3 a month to over 100 a month, despite the mayor's public claim that "those homeless are not being cited" — a claim the city's own citation data directly contradicts. Finland's eleven straight years of falling homelessness is real, but its own national housing agency recorded the trend reversing in 2024. Denver's program says it moved nearly 8,000 people into housing; Denver's own City Auditor found actual spending ran $20 million higher than publicly reported and concluded the city "does not own" the data behind its own headline numbers. And Rockford, Illinois — one of only three U.S. communities to reach functional zero for both veterans (2015) and the chronically homeless (2017) [CL-856] — is living through a federal Continuum of Care funding-rule fight that its own program coordinator says could shrink its permanent-housing capacity from 308 people to 105 [NEW-2026-2]; a June 29, 2026 federal court ruling vacated that specific rule but declined to permanently block a repeat, and HUD's own June 1, 2026 fiscal-2026 NOFO already contains a similar shift away from Housing First [NEW-2026-3]. The one program in this comparator set that proved its effect rigorously, rather than just measuring a before-and-after number, is the UK's Rough Sleeping Initiative: a government-commissioned Difference-in-Differences evaluation found a 32% reduction in rough sleeping specifically attributable to the initiative — 1,321 people against a modelled counterfactual of 4,069 [CL-821] — and that same government later published a self-critical follow-up finding less than 1% of its own funding went to prevention [CL-822].

Give Toronto's own metrics the same rigor the UK gave its flagship program. (a recommendation card) Toronto's tracked shelter-system metrics — Central Intake unmatched-caller counts, sleeping-accommodation turnover — are before/after trend lines, not attributable causal estimates; they can't on their own distinguish a program's real effect from a general change in demand. The UK's Rough Sleeping Initiative is the only program in this page’s entire comparator set with a genuine, peer-review-adjacent causal evaluation, and its methodology — Difference-in-Differences, using comparator jurisdictions without the intervention as a counterfactual — is directly adoptable, not just an inspiring result. Toronto's own shelter-occupancy open dataset is already more granular than several comparator cities' (Montreal's city data portal has no equivalent at all), but even Toronto's "current" data turned out to be six to seven months old when checked directly in July 2026, the same problem found in Calgary's dataset.

Keep tracking what happens to Rockford as an early-warning signal for Toronto's own funding dependence. (a recommendation card) Rockford is the most-cited sustained municipal functional-zero success in this page’s evidence base, and it is currently a live, real-time test of whether a senior government can undo a nearly decade-long municipal win by changing funding rules underneath it [CL-856]. The specific November 2025 rule change was vacated by a Rhode Island federal court on June 29, 2026 for violating the Administrative Procedure Act, but the court refused to permanently bar HUD from trying again — and HUD's own fiscal-2026 NOFO, issued June 1, 2026, already keeps much of the same direction [NEW-2026-3]; whether Rockford's own funding specifically survived was not confirmed either way in this review [NEW-2026-1] [NEW-2026-2]. That pattern is directly relevant to Toronto's own documented dependence on provincial and federal transfers for shelter operating and capital funding.

None of this requires taking Finland's, Houston's, or Rockford's word for it — it requires reading what each of them, and the governments auditing them, actually published, including the parts that don't fit the slogan. And it comes with an honest limit the City can't engineer around: the deepest lever in most of these stories — Finland's national housing agency, HUD's own funding rules, the UK's own program design, and, closer to home, other CMSMs' data-publishing choices — sits with a government other than Toronto's own. This file does not attempt a per-dollar or per-capita comparison between any of these cities and Toronto; the programs, populations, and measurement methods differ too much for that kind of number to mean what it looks like it means.

a recommendation card — Adopt a Difference-in-Differences Evaluation Design for Toronto's Own Capacity/Outcome Metrics

Card id: a recommendation card · Issue: homelessness-international-comparators · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: carried-forward (CL-821, CL-822)

Problem

Toronto's own tracked shelter-system metrics (Central Intake unmatched-caller counts, sleeping-accommodation turnover, per shelter-system-capacity-strain) are before/after trend lines, not attributable causal estimates — they cannot on their own distinguish a program's real effect from a general change in demand. The UK's Rough Sleeping Initiative is the one program in this page’s entire comparator set with a genuine, peer-review-adjacent Difference-in-Differences evaluation, finding a 32% reduction in rough sleeping specifically attributable to the initiative — a net reduction of 1,321 people against a modelled counterfactual of 4,069 absent the program [CL-821]. This card addresses the methodological gap, not the underlying capacity question, which belongs to shelter-system-capacity-strain's own cards.

Action

Toronto Shelter and Support Services commissions a Difference-in-Differences (or comparable quasi-experimental) evaluation design for at least one of its own major interventions (e.g., the HSCIS capital pipeline or a specific Central Intake process change), using comparable Ontario or Canadian municipalities without the same intervention as a counterfactual, modelled on the UK Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government's own 2019 published methodology.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: commission the evaluation using TSSS's own existing data systems and an external academic or research-institute partner (e.g., a university public-policy program), within existing budget-office authority.
  • City demands of Province: none required for this specific action; a request for comparator-city data access (if needed from other CMSM/DSSAB areas) would be a data-sharing ask, not a funding ask.
  • City demands of Feds: none required for this specific action.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low, a single academic/research-partnership evaluation contract — comparator: the UK's own RSI evaluation was conducted by government analysts and academic partners as part of existing program-evaluation budgets, not a separate large capital or program cost [CL-821]. No Toronto-specific costed comparator was located this review; stated as low based on the nature of the deliverable (an analytical report, not a program or construction spend), consistent with the same costing discipline used in shelter-system-capacity-strain's a recommendation card.

Funding path

Existing TSSS this library's internal records budget line, or a university research-partnership arrangement (in-kind or modest contract); no new funding mechanism identified as required.

Who benefits, and how

City budget staff and Council, via a rigorous, attributable evidence base for future capacity/program decisions instead of relying on before/after trend lines alone; people experiencing homelessness, indirectly, if better evaluation leads to more effectively targeted future investment.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, negligibly, via existing TSSS this library's internal records capacity or a modest external contract; no new payer class identified.

Financial ROI

Not separately estimated — this is an analytical/methodological investment, not a program spend, so its ROI is instrumental (better-informed future spending decisions) rather than directly measurable in dollars; no comparator was identified for the specific dollar value of a program evaluation, though the UK's own RSI evaluation is cited as a methodological comparator, not a costed one [CL-821]. Confidence: low — genuinely not yet estimable in dollar terms.

Economic ROI

Not yet estimable — this is a this library's internal records action with no direct construction, employment, or induced-spending effect of its own; no comparator was sought, since the action's economic significance (if any) is entirely indirect and contingent on what future spending decisions the evaluation informs. Confidence: low.

Social ROI

Directional: a Difference-in-Differences evaluation, if it finds Toronto's own interventions produce an attributable effect comparable to or larger than the UK's measured 32% RSI reduction [CL-821], would give Toronto a defensible, exportable evidence base of its own — currently the city's own metrics support only a correlational claim, not this stronger one. Confidence: low-medium — directional, since no such evaluation has yet been conducted for any Toronto program.

Environmental ROI

Genuinely environmentally neutral — an analytical/research action with no construction, land-use, or physical-infrastructure component; no comparator is needed to support a neutral finding. Confidence: high on the neutrality of this specific action's own footprint.

Evidence

  • CL-821 · carried-forward · UK RSI Difference-in-Differences evaluation, 32% attributable reduction
  • CL-822 · carried-forward · UK systems-wide evaluation preliminary findings, under 1% prevention spending

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on the problem statement (Toronto's metrics are genuinely correlational, not causal, per direct comparison against shelter-system-capacity-strain's own metric descriptions); low confidence on cost, since no Toronto-specific evaluation-contract comparator was located in this review.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: fairness and legal review; confirmation that TSSS does not already have an internal evaluation-methodology upgrade underway that this review did not locate.

a recommendation card — Track Rockford's HUD Funding-Rule Outcome as an Early-Warning Signal for Canadian Federal Funding Design

Card id: a recommendation card · Card class: INTERNAL · Issue: homelessness-international-comparators · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: New load-bearing findings (backgrounder NEW-2026-2)

Problem

Rockford, Illinois is the single most-cited sustained municipal functional-zero success in this page’s evidence base (veteran homelessness since December 2015, chronic since January 2017) [CL-856]. A November 2025 HUD Continuum of Care rule change — redirecting federal funding from permanent to transitional housing and adding work requirements — is, per Rockford's own program coordinator, expected to shrink the city's permanently-housed count from 308 people to 105 if it takes effect as structured [NEW-2026-2]. This is a US federal policy change with no direct jurisdiction over Canadian programs, but it is a live, real-time test of whether a sustained municipal Housing-First-style success can survive a senior-government funding-design shift — directly relevant to Toronto's own documented dependence on senior-government transfers for both its refugee-shelter operating funding and its (currently zero) capital co-investment, per shelter-system-capacity-strain.

Action

This page tracks and updates its own record of Rockford's HUD application outcome as a named, dated follow-up item, and this project's Toronto-facing federal/provincial funding-architecture leaves (homelessness-federal-funding-architecture, homelessness-provincial-funding-hpp-hart) are alerted to the pattern (a senior government redesigning funding rules can undo a municipal-level sustained success) as a documented precedent worth citing if Toronto's own senior-government funding relationships show early signs of the same dynamic. Adversary-pass update (2026-07-14): the specific November/December 2025 NOFO this Problem section describes was vacated by a June 29, 2026 federal court ruling (U.S. District Court of Rhode Island) for violating the Administrative Procedure Act, but the court declined to permanently bar HUD from similar future changes, and HUD's own June 1, 2026 fiscal 2026 NOFO already contains a similar shift away from Housing First [backgrounder NEW-2026-3]. No source located in this review confirms whether Rockford's specific 308→105 impact estimate held, changed, or was mooted — that remains the open, page-specific follow-up item this card tracks, now one step further along than "decision not expected before May 2026."

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: none directly — this is a this library's internal records action, not a City of Toronto program action.
  • City demands of Province: none directly; the relevance is structural/precedential, not a direct ask.
  • City demands of Feds: none directly; Rockford's situation is US-federal, not Canadian-federal, and this card does not conflate the two systems.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible — a research-tracking action within this project's existing leaf-maintenance discipline (CURATE lane), not a program or spending action.

Funding path

None required; this is an internal research-maintenance action.

Who benefits, and how

This project's own Toronto-facing funding-architecture research, via an early, concrete precedent of what a senior-government funding-design shift can do to a previously sustained municipal success — useful context if a comparable dynamic ever appears in Toronto's own federal/provincial funding relationships (e.g., the New Deal Agreement's own uncertain post-2026 status, already flagged in shelter-system-capacity-strain).

Who bears the cost, and how

No payer class — negligible internal research-maintenance cost only.

Financial ROI

Not applicable — this is a research-tracking action with no direct financial mechanism.

Economic ROI

Not applicable — no economic effect of its own; no comparator is needed. Confidence: high on the "not applicable" finding itself, since the action has no economic mechanism.

Social ROI

Directional: keeping this page’s own Rockford case study current, rather than frozen at its 2020 case-study framing, directly serves this project's own accuracy/trust-layer discipline — a stale "success story" citation that doesn't reflect a live funding threat would itself be a disclosed-but-unaddressed gap if left unresolved. Confidence: medium — the value is to this project's own research integrity rather than to any external population directly.

Environmental ROI

Not applicable — no physical/environmental component; no comparator is needed. Confidence: high on the "not applicable" finding.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-2 · source quote (this review) · Rockford HUD CoC rule change, 308→105 permanent-housing impact estimate
  • NEW-2026-1 · source quote (this review) · Illinois statewide functional-zero trajectory, distinguishing state from Rockford-municipal
  • CL-856 · carried-forward · Rockford functional zero, veteran (2015) and chronic (2017)

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence. The problem statement and the specific 308→105 impact estimate are directly quoted from a named program coordinator in a single news source, not independently cross-verified against a second source or HUD's own public rule text in this review — flagged as a real limitation, not resolved further here. The national-level litigation outcome (June 29, 2026 ruling; fiscal 2026 NOFO reissued June 1, 2026) is independently corroborated across two sources [backgrounder NEW-2026-3], but Rockford's own specific award status was not found in either.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: Rockford's actual current CoC award status under the fiscal 2026 NOFO (not located in this review); fairness and legal review; a second independent source confirming the original 308→105 estimate before this figure is treated as more than a single named official's own projection.
