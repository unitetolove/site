RC-AC-01 — Republish a Current, Itemized Per-Operator Shelter Per-Diem Schedule — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-AC-01 · File: research/cards/homelessness-accountability-complaints.md#rc-ac-01 Issue slug: homelessness-accountability-complaints · Backgrounder: homelessness-accountability-complaints Trust class: INHERITED (backgrounder NEW-2026-AC-5) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Toronto published a detailed, shelter-by-shelter per diem rate schedule from 2010 through at least 2012; no equivalent has been published since, despite the system's budget growing roughly tenfold over the same window and every other line item in current TSSS budget documents being reported with comparable or greater precision [NEW-2026-AC-5]. A reported 2025 figure ($146/person/night) appears to exist in at least one non-headline council document, suggesting the underlying data is not necessarily gone, only no longer surfaced where the public would naturally find it. This card addresses only the publication gap, not any underlying cost-escalation argument.

Action

The City resumes publishing a current, itemized per-operator (or, at minimum, system-average) shelter per diem rate, in the same format used from 2010-2012, as a standing annual disclosure alongside the TSSS Budget Notes.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: entirely within existing municipal authority and existing TSSS budget-reporting practice — no new legislative or provincial/federal action required, since the City already published this exact data format as recently as 2012.
  • City demands of Province: none required.
  • City demands of Feds: none required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low — a reporting-format change using data the City already collects for contract administration purposes; comparator: the City's own 2010-2012 precedent, which was produced as a standard staff report at that scale of shelter system, before the roughly tenfold budget growth since documented in this leaf [NEW-2026-AC-5].

Funding path

Existing TSSS budget-office reporting capacity; no new funding mechanism required.

Who benefits, and how

Journalists, researchers, and the public, via restored ability to compare operator-level cost and value; Council itself, via better information for POS contract oversight and renewal decisions; indirectly, shelter clients, via improved public accountability pressure on operator performance.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, negligibly, via existing TSSS reporting staff time — no new payer class. POS shelter operators may experience increased public/competitive visibility into their individual per-diem rates, a real but non-fiscal cost to specific entities this card does not quantify.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder with a graded ACL-####/ENT-#### pair — the backgrounder's own Cui Bono section flags this as a plausible but unestablished lead (specific POS operators benefiting from reduced rate visibility), not yet promotable to a named row. See backgrounder "Open questions / data gaps."

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Not directly estimable — this is a disclosure action, not a spending or savings program in itself; any downstream cost-recovery effect (e.g., from improved contract-negotiation leverage) is speculative and not modeled here. Comparator source: none needed; a reporting-transparency action of this kind is not expected to carry a first-order fiscal case. Confidence: low — genuinely not the right unit for this action.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Directional: restores a specific, previously-existing accountability mechanism the City itself once operated at a smaller system scale; no claim in the ledger quantifies the social-outcome effect of restored per-diem transparency specifically. Comparator source: the City's own 2010-2012 precedent. Confidence: low-medium.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely environmentally neutral — a reporting exercise with no physical-infrastructure component. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-AC-5 · quote-context (this pass) · 2010-2012 per-diem publication precedent; current-document absence

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on the underlying gap (independently confirmed by this leaf's promoted source's own dedicated search of 2024-2026 budget documents); low confidence on whether the data still exists internally in a readily publishable format, since the $146/night figure's own source document was not independently re-located in this pass.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: independently confirming the $146/night figure's source document before citing it as evidence the data still exists; Lane R pass.

RC-AC-02 — A Dedicated Search for Operator-Specific Wrongdoing Findings (LTB, Court, Licensing Records) — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-AC-02 · File: research/cards/homelessness-accountability-complaints.md#rc-ac-02 · Card class: INTERNAL Issue slug: homelessness-accountability-complaints · Backgrounder: homelessness-accountability-complaints Trust class: INHERITED (backgrounder NEW-2026-AC-7) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

This leaf's own promoted source explicitly found no independently verified, named wrongdoing finding against any specific shelter operator (as distinct from the City itself), and flagged this as a genuine open item rather than evidence such findings don't exist, recommending a search of Landlord and Tenant Board records, Ontario civil court records, and any applicable provincial congregate-care licensing regime [NEW-2026-AC-7]. This pass did not close that gap either. This card proposes the research action itself, not a policy demand, since no finding yet exists to act on.

Action

A dedicated Lane D/Lane M research pass searches Landlord and Tenant Board records (where a shelter operator is also a registered landlord), CanLII and Ontario court dockets for civil suits naming specific shelter operators, and any provincial licensing/inspection regime applicable to congregate care settings, to determine whether this leaf's own flagged gap reflects a genuine absence of findings or an under-searched record.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: not applicable — this is a research action for this project itself, not a City policy ask.
  • City demands of Province: not applicable in this card's own action, though a substantiated finding (if one is located) could generate a future card with real jurisdictional asks.
  • City demands of Feds: not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low — staff/researcher time for a targeted records search using existing free-tier access (CanLII query-only search, per this project's own accountability-tooling notes); comparator: the Accountability Observatory's own similarly-scoped capture-backlog tasks (accountability/SEED_LANDSCAPE_2026-07-14.md §4), several of which are costed at the same low, staff-time-only scale.

Funding path

Existing research-project capacity; no new funding mechanism required.

Who benefits, and how

This project's own research integrity, via closing a flagged gap rather than leaving it open indefinitely; shelter clients and the public, if a genuine finding is located and surfaced; POS shelter operators, symmetrically, if the search confirms no substantiated finding exists — a negative result is also a legitimate, useful output.

Who bears the cost, and how

This research project's own capacity, via researcher time — no external payer.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified — this card's action is research, not a policy change, so there is no status-quo beneficiary in the L6 template's sense.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Not applicable — a research action, not a spending or savings program. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: low — not the right unit for this action.

(b) Economic ROI

Not applicable. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Not separately estimable — this card's value is instrumental (closing a research gap), contingent entirely on what, if anything, the search finds. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: low.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely environmentally neutral. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-AC-7 · quote-context (this pass) · leaf's own flagged open item on operator-specific wrongdoing

Confidence & uncertainties

High confidence that the gap is real and explicitly flagged by the leaf's own promoted source; zero confidence on what, if anything, such a search would find — stated as a genuine unknown, not a prediction.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: assignment to a Lane D/M research pass; not yet attempted.

RC-AC-03 — Publish a Combined TSSS/TESS/MLS Shelter-and-Housing-Precarity Complaint Dashboard, Modeled on the Ombudsman's Own "2025 by the Numbers" Format — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-AC-03 · File: research/cards/homelessness-accountability-complaints.md#rc-ac-03 Issue slug: homelessness-accountability-complaints · Backgrounder: homelessness-accountability-complaints Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (backgrounder NEW-2026-AC-1, NEW-2026-AC-2) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Ombudsman Toronto publishes clear, office-wide annual figures (cases handled, closure rate, fairness-problem categories, contact-channel breakdown) [NEW-2026-AC-1], and its case stories (Anthony, Petro) show individual divisions (TESS, TSSS) responding constructively once a complaint reaches the Ombudsman's office [NEW-2026-AC-2] — but no published, division-specific, shelter/housing-precarity-focused complaint dashboard comparable to the Ombudsman's own office-wide summary was located for TSSS, TESS, or MLS individually in this pass. This card addresses the absence of a division-level equivalent, not a criticism of the Ombudsman's own reporting, which this card treats as the model to extend.

Action

TSSS, TESS, and MLS each publish an annual, division-specific complaint summary (volume, category, resolution rate, communication-accessibility metrics) in the same clear format Ombudsman Toronto already uses for its own office-wide figures, cross-referenced to any Ombudsman findings involving that division in the same period.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: entirely within existing municipal authority — each division already collects this data internally for its own complaint-handling process; this asks for its publication in a standardized format, not new data collection.
  • City demands of Province: none required.
  • City demands of Feds: none required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low — a reporting-format standardization using data each division already collects; comparator: the Ombudsman's own office-wide "2025 by the Numbers" page, which this card proposes as the direct template [NEW-2026-AC-1].

Funding path

Existing divisional reporting capacity; no new funding mechanism required.

Who benefits, and how

Shelter and OW clients, via clearer visibility into how their own division handles complaints before needing to escalate to the Ombudsman; Council, via division-level accountability data comparable to what it already receives office-wide from the Ombudsman.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, negligibly, via existing divisional staff time for report compilation — no new payer class.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Not directly estimable — a reporting action, not a spending program. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: low.

(b) Economic ROI

Not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Directional: the Anthony and Petro case stories both show that even a functioning complaint-resolution outcome depended on escalation to the Ombudsman rather than the division's own process working smoothly the first time [NEW-2026-AC-2] — a published division-level dashboard is a plausible, though unquantified, mechanism for catching communication-accessibility gaps (like the one Anthony experienced) before escalation is needed. Comparator source: the two case stories themselves, cited directly. Confidence: low-medium — directional, not quantified.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely environmentally neutral. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-AC-1 · quote-context (this pass) · Ombudsman's own office-wide reporting format and figures
  • NEW-2026-AC-2 · quote-context (this pass) · Anthony and Petro case stories, division-level complaint handling

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence. The Ombudsman's own reporting format is directly sourced and real; whether TSSS/TESS/MLS already produce an internal equivalent not currently published was not independently confirmed in this pass — a real possibility this card does not rule out.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming whether an internal, unpublished divisional complaint report already exists; Lane R pass.
