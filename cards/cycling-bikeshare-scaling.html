Fund the 2025-2027 protected bikeway program to its full authorized scope — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-0001 · File: research/cards/cycling-bikeshare-scaling.md#rc-0001 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: cycling-bikeshare-scaling · Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/cycling-bikeshare-scaling.md §"The City's active bikeway capital program" Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The City's November 2025 Cycling Network Plan Q4 update sought Council authority for 20.5 centreline kilometres of new bikeways at an estimated $30.4 million, on corridors with documented, specific collision histories — including 1,910 collisions with 22 serious injuries and 6 fatalities on Kingston Road (2014-2024) and 1,315 collisions with 11 serious injuries on Trethewey Drive (10-year window) [backgrounder §"Current state — the City's active bikeway capital program"]. At least one included corridor (Kingston Road) shows a genuine, disclosed public opinion split (approximately 51% unsupportive/very unsupportive vs. 43% supportive/strongly supportive), meaning full delivery is not politically automatic even where Council has authority.

Action

Council funds and delivers the full 20.5km 2025-2027 program as authorized, including the contested Kingston Road segment, rather than allowing politically contested individual corridors to be quietly dropped from the program while the rest proceeds.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: the entire action — this is squarely within existing municipal capital-budget and Council-authority territory; every project in the November 2025 report explicitly states no motor-vehicle travel lane is being removed, which the backgrounder's own tension analysis suggests may reflect a deliberate design choice to stay outside Bill 212's removal-and-blocking trigger (flagged there as an inference, not confirmed).
  • City demands of Province: none for this specific program, precisely because it is designed not to trigger Bill 212. This is itself worth naming to residents: the City is currently building within a narrowed envelope of what the province allows.
  • City demands of Feds: none directly.

Cost

Order of magnitude: low tens of millions CAD. Named, precise comparator (the same program, not an external one): an estimated $30,400,000 to install and upgrade the bikeways, green infrastructure, and Vision Zero road-safety improvements in the November 2025 report, plus $586,000 for associated missing sidewalks and an estimated $1.0 million/year in ongoing maintenance [backgrounder, NEW, live-fetched 2026-07-14].

Funding path

Existing City of Toronto capital budget process (Transportation Services capital program), as already used for this exact authorization request. One disclosed offsetting revenue effect: the Kingston Road project's proposed removal of approximately 62 Pay-and-Display on-street parking spaces would decrease Toronto Parking Authority's annual net revenue by an estimated $23,877, plus a one-time $9,158.40 removal cost for six parking machines — a real, if small, revenue tradeoff this card does not omit.

Who benefits, and how

Cyclists and pedestrians on the named corridors, via measurably reduced collision exposure on streets with documented multi-year fatality and serious-injury histories. Bike Share Toronto users and general cyclists city-wide, via incremental network connectivity toward the City's stated (though not independently re-verified in this pass) 14%-mode-share ambition referenced in the inherited master briefing.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via the capital budget draw. Toronto Parking Authority (and indirectly the City, since TPA revenue flows back to City finances), via the disclosed ~$23,877/year Kingston Road parking-revenue reduction. Residents and businesses opposed to specific corridor changes (documented at approximately 51% unsupportive on Kingston Road specifically) bear a real, named political and access-pattern cost this card does not minimize.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder — the Cui Bono table in research/backgrounders/cycling-bikeshare-scaling.md came up empty after checking accountability/SEED_LANDSCAPE_2026-07-14.md, which contains no cycling-sector entity or finding.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Range: Net cost to the City, low tens of millions CAD for this program phase ($30.4M + $586K), partially offset by a small, disclosed parking-revenue reduction (~$24K/year) — not a revenue-positive program on its own terms. Comparator source: The program's own stated cost figures (City of Toronto, Nov 2025 Q4 report) — this is a direct-cost card, not a modelled comparator case. Confidence: High for the cost figures themselves (directly sourced, precise to the dollar in places); the program is honestly a net City expenditure, not a savings case.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: Not yet estimable — no source in this leaf's evidence base models induced local economic activity, property-value effects, or business-district impact specific to these corridors. Comparator source: None identified this pass; a future pass could look for a Toronto-specific or comparable-city study of protected-lane installation effects on adjacent business revenue (a commonly studied question elsewhere, per the inherited briefing's general claim that "protected lanes improve surrounding business," itself uncited to a specific study). Confidence: Low — genuine gap, stated plainly rather than filled with an imported multiplier.

(c) Social ROI

Range: Directional, not quantified — reduced collision/injury/fatality risk on four specific corridors with documented multi-year histories (Kingston Road, Keele Street, Kipling Avenue, Trethewey Drive), plus incremental network-connectivity benefit for existing and prospective cyclists. Comparator source: The corridors' own decade-scale collision histories serve as the baseline this program is measured against, rather than an external comparator city. Confidence: Medium — the safety case is well-documented at the corridor level (real collision data), but no source quantifies the expected collision reduction from this specific set of interventions, so the direction is well-supported while the magnitude is not.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: Modest, directionally positive — incremental mode-shift from vehicle to bicycle trips on the affected corridors reduces per-trip emissions, though no source in this leaf's evidence base models the specific volume of shifted trips for this program. Comparator source: None sixplex/cycling-specific identified this pass; see the housing-supply-affordability leaf's RC-0001 card for a worked embodied-emissions methodology example from a different building-form context, not directly transferable here. Confidence: Low for magnitude; medium for direction (mode-shift-reduces-emissions is well-established generally, just not quantified for this specific program).

Evidence

  • NEW, live-fetched 2026-07-14 · City of Toronto, "Cycling Network Plan: 2025 Cycling Infrastructure Fourth Quarter Update" (Nov 20, 2025) · cost figures, corridor collision data, Kingston Road opinion split and parking-revenue impact
  • INHERITED this leaf's inherited master briefing (cycling bikeshare) · 14%-mode-share-by-2030 target reference

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium-high confidence on feasibility and cost (both directly sourced from the authorizing Council report itself). Low confidence on Economic and Environmental ROI magnitude — both genuinely unestimated in the evidence base rather than approximated. The Kingston Road segment specifically carries real, disclosed political risk (approximately 51% unsupportive) that this card does not paper over; "fund the full program" is a position this card takes explicitly, not a neutral restatement of the backgrounder.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: an Economic ROI comparator, and confirmation of whether the no-vehicle-lane-removal pattern across this program is a deliberate Bill 212-era design choice (flagged as an open question in the backgrounder, not yet confirmed with Transportation Services directly).

Formally track and report progress against the pending Cycle Toronto v. Ontario appeal — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-0002 · File: research/cards/cycling-bikeshare-scaling.md#rc-0002 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: cycling-bikeshare-scaling · Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/cycling-bikeshare-scaling.md §"Bill 212, the Cycle Toronto v. Ontario litigation, and Bill 60" Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The 19km of existing protected lanes on Bloor, Yonge, and University survive today under a court injunction following a July 2025 trial-level Charter s.7 ruling in Cycle Toronto's favour — but the province's appeal was heard in January 2026 and, as of a June 17, 2026 status update, remained undecided [backgrounder §"Bill 212..."]. The City of Toronto itself held no official party role in the litigation, per Cycle Toronto's own account — meaning the City is not currently a direct source of public information on the case's status for residents who rely on these specific 19km of lanes.

Action

Council directs staff to publish, and update at least quarterly, a plain-language public status page tracking the Cycle Toronto v. Ontario appeal and any resulting change to the legal status of the Bloor/Yonge/University bike lanes — independent of Cycle Toronto's own advocacy-organization updates, which are the only current public source.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: the entire action — public communications and legal-status tracking of a matter directly affecting City infrastructure the City itself is not litigating is squarely an administrative/communications function within existing authority.
  • City demands of Province: none directly — this card does not ask the Province to change its legal position, only that residents have a City-sourced information channel independent of an advocacy organization's own updates.
  • City demands of Feds: none.

Cost

Order of magnitude: negligible, non-capital — a communications/staff-time cost only, on the same low order as routine Council-report publication.

Funding path

Existing City communications/Transportation Services operating budget; no new funding mechanism required.

Who benefits, and how

Residents and cyclists who use the 19km of affected lanes, via a City-sourced (rather than advocacy-organization-sourced) account of whether those lanes remain legally protected at any given time. City Council itself, via a clearer public record of a jurisdictional dispute it has already formally opposed by resolution.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via the negligible communications-staff-time cost; no other named cost-bearer.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table (empty, per the honest explanation given there).

Financial ROI

Not applicable in the traditional sense — this is a negligible-cost administrative/transparency action, not a program with a return to model. Stated as "not yet estimable, and not the relevant frame for this action" rather than forced into a range.

Economic ROI

Range: Not yet estimable — no source models an economic effect of public legal-status transparency specifically. Comparator source: None identified; a genuinely thin category for an action of this kind. Confidence: Low, and honestly a poor fit for this action type rather than a real gap in available evidence.

Social ROI

Directional only: reduces uncertainty and reliance on a single advocacy organization as the public's only information channel about the legal status of infrastructure residents use daily. No quantified figure exists or is claimed.

Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely neutral — a public-communications action does not itself change infrastructure or emissions; any environmental effect flows from the underlying litigation's outcome, not from this card's own action. Comparator source: None needed, per the template's allowance for a genuinely neutral action. Confidence: High for "the action itself is neutral."

Evidence

  • NEW, live-fetched 2026-07-14 · Cycle Toronto, "What's going on with Bill 212?" (July 2, 2025) and "Bill 212 Legal Challenge Update" (June 17, 2026) · litigation timeline, City's non-party status, pending-appeal status as of this pass

Confidence & uncertainties

High confidence on feasibility (negligible-cost, fully within existing authority) and low stakes if implemented poorly (worst case: a status page that is simply not very informative, not a program that can meaningfully fail). The underlying legal uncertainty this card responds to is real and explicitly not resolved by this card — the appeal's outcome remains genuinely unknown as of this pass.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: nothing structural; this is a low-complexity administrative action. Would benefit from confirming current City communications practice (whether any such tracking already exists informally) before treating this as a net-new ask.
