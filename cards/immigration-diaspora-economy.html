Scope a Diaspora Trade and Entrepreneurship Facilitation Function Within Toronto's Existing Economic Development Office — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-IDE-01 · File: research/cards/immigration-diaspora-economy.md#rc-ide-01 Status: DRAFT Issue slug: immigration-diaspora-economy · Backgrounder: immigration-diaspora-economy Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (problem statement anchored in independently corroborated economic literature and StatsCan finding) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The immigrant-trade link — immigration's association with increased bilateral trade via reduced information and trust costs — is a genuine, established finding in international economics literature, independently corroborated this pass [backgrounder NEW-1]. Separately, Statistics Canada research confirms immigrants in Canada are more likely than the Canadian-born population to become business owners (11.9% vs. 8.4% overall ownership/self-employment rate; about 5.2% vs. 4.0% for incorporated-business ownership specifically, 2016 Census-based data), though immigrant-owned businesses tend to be smaller on average [backgrounder NEW-2, Statistics Canada, Picot & Ostrovsky 2021, Catalogue no. 36-28-0001-2021009]. No City of Toronto program specifically facilitating diaspora trade connections or targeting diaspora entrepreneurship supports (as distinct from general small-business services) was identified in this pass [backgrounder, "Current state"] — a gap this card addresses directly, while explicitly not claiming the general small-business-size nuance identified at NEW-2 has been resolved by anything proposed here.

Action

Direct the City's Economic Development & Culture division to conduct a scoping exercise on whether a dedicated diaspora trade-facilitation and entrepreneurship-support function (distinct from, and additive to, existing general small-business supports) would be additive — including whether existing programs already reach diaspora-owned businesses effectively, and what a modest pilot connecting Toronto diaspora business networks to trade-promotion missions might look like.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: commission the scoping exercise using existing Economic Development & Culture division capacity — within existing municipal authority over local economic development.
  • City demands of Province: none required for the scoping exercise itself; a secondary note that trade missions and export-promotion supports often involve provincial economic development agencies as co-participants, named as a coordination point rather than a formal demand.
  • City demands of Feds: none required for the scoping exercise; trade and foreign policy remain federal jurisdiction, and any actual trade-mission or export-facilitation activity would need to work within, not around, federal trade frameworks — a distinction this card preserves rather than implying municipal authority over trade policy.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible to low tens of thousands of dollars CAD for a staff-conducted scoping exercise — anchored to the general order of magnitude of a single-division policy-scoping action elsewhere in this corpus, not a newly modeled figure.

Funding path

Existing City of Toronto Economic Development & Culture division operating budget for the scoping exercise — no new program or budget line proposed at this stage.

Who benefits, and how

Diaspora-owned businesses in Toronto, via a clearer future picture of whether dedicated trade-facilitation or entrepreneurship support beyond general small-business services would be valuable; City policy staff, via an evidence base for future program design rather than acting on the master briefing's conceptual framing alone.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the negligible scoping-exercise staff-time cost.

Financial ROI

Not applicable in the standard sense — a scoping exercise has no direct fiscal-return pathway; any downstream fiscal effects would depend on what the scoping exercise recommends and whether Council acts on it.

Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable for the scoping exercise itself. The master briefing's broader claim that diaspora trade facilitation could meaningfully diversify Toronto's trade base [master briefing, ⚠️ VERIFY] is cited only as motivating context, not a modeled return on this specific scoping action. Comparator source: none identified specific to a municipal diaspora-trade-facilitation scoping exercise; the StatsCan finding on immigrant business-size gaps [backgrounder NEW-2, Picot & Ostrovsky 2021, Catalogue no. 36-28-0001-2021009] is the nearest real, directly-verified data point in this leaf's evidence, though it describes the current state of immigrant entrepreneurship generally, not a modeled return on this card's specific action. Confidence: low — genuine gap; a future pass should look for a Canadian municipal diaspora-trade-facilitation program's own outcome evaluation, if one exists, before citing a figure here.

Social ROI

Not the primary dimension for this action; no source in this pass quantifies a social-wellbeing case specific to diaspora trade facilitation, as distinct from the general economic case addressed above.

Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely environmentally neutral. A scoping exercise and any resulting trade-facilitation coordination function has no material emissions, land-use, water, waste, or resilience footprint distinct from ordinary economic-development office operations. Comparator source: none sought — the action's nature makes an environmental comparator unnecessary rather than merely unfound. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-1 · quote-context, directly verified to primary source (accessed/fetched 2026-07-14) · Bratti, De Benedictis & Santoni, CEPII Working Paper 2018-01 · immigrant-trade link as established economic literature, establishing the problem's underlying economic logic (specific magnitude: 10% increase in immigrant stock associated with 1.7% increase in manufacturing exports, Italy-specific data)
  • NEW-2 · quote-context, directly verified to primary source (accessed/fetched 2026-07-14) · Statistics Canada, Picot & Ostrovsky 2021, Catalogue no. 36-28-0001-2021009 · immigrant entrepreneurship rate and business-size nuance, exact figures
  • master briefing · ⚠️ VERIFY (INHERITED) · diaspora-led development examples and base-of-the-pyramid framing, cited as motivating context only, not as this card's own load-bearing evidence

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on feasibility (a scoping exercise is a low-friction research step within existing municipal authority). Low confidence on whether a dedicated diaspora-trade function would actually prove additive over existing general small-business supports — this card does not prejudge that outcome. The StatsCan business-size nuance [backgrounder NEW-2] is a genuine complicating factor this card does not resolve: a scoping exercise should explicitly examine whether smaller average immigrant-business size reflects a market-access gap this card's proposed function could address, or a different underlying dynamic outside this card's scope.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming current reach of general City small-business supports into diaspora-owned businesses; a cost comparator; Lane R pass.

Publish an Annual Public Accounting of How Toronto's Existing Economic-Development Programs Reach Diaspora-Owned Businesses — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-IDE-02 · File: research/cards/immigration-diaspora-economy.md#rc-ide-02 Status: DRAFT Issue slug: immigration-diaspora-economy · Backgrounder: immigration-diaspora-economy Trust class: CONTEXTUAL · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

This leaf's evidence base has a specific, named data gap: no source in this pass confirms whether, or how effectively, Toronto's existing general small-business and economic-development supports currently reach diaspora-owned businesses [backgrounder, "Current state" and "Open questions"]. This matters because the directly-verified StatsCan finding that immigrant-owned businesses tend to be smaller than Canadian-born-owned businesses [backgrounder NEW-2, Picot & Ostrovsky 2021, Catalogue no. 36-28-0001-2021009] could reflect several different underlying dynamics — a genuine market-access gap existing programs don't close, a different business-model distribution, or something else entirely — and without basic reporting on program reach by ownership demographic, this question cannot currently be answered from public information.

Action

Direct the City's Economic Development & Culture division to begin publishing an annual public accounting of program participation and outcome data (grant/loan uptake, business-support program enrollment, where currently and voluntarily collected) disaggregated by immigrant/diaspora business ownership status where privacy-compliant disaggregation is possible, modeled on the level of outcome-reporting specificity this corpus's own building-retrofit leaf found the City already applies to other program types (e.g., the High-Rise Retrofit Improvement Support Program's reported 15-building/21%-reduction outcome data).

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: the reporting function itself, using existing Economic Development & Culture division data infrastructure — entirely within municipal authority, contingent on privacy-compliant data collection already existing or being feasible to add.
  • City demands of Province: none required.
  • City demands of Feds: none required, though a note that Statistics Canada's own linked-administrative-data research [backgrounder NEW-2] demonstrates this kind of disaggregation is methodologically feasible at the national level, which the City could draw on as a design reference.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible to low tens of thousands of dollars CAD for the reporting function itself, assuming underlying program-participation data is already collected in some form — this card does not assume new data-collection infrastructure is required, but flags that cost could rise materially if it is, a genuine uncertainty stated rather than hidden.

Funding path

Existing City of Toronto Economic Development & Culture division operating budget for report preparation — no new spending proposed beyond staff time, contingent on the data-availability caveat above.

Who benefits, and how

Toronto residents, City Council, and future policy researchers, via a factual answer to whether existing programs already serve diaspora-owned businesses proportionately or whether a genuine gap exists — directly informing whether RC-IDE-01's proposed dedicated function would be solving a real problem or a hypothetical one.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the negligible reporting-function staff-time cost, with the stated caveat that cost could rise if new data-collection infrastructure proves necessary.

Financial ROI

Not applicable — an information/reporting action has no direct fiscal-return pathway of its own.

Economic ROI

Range: not applicable — a reporting action's economic impact, if any, would flow entirely through whatever program changes it later informs, which is outside this card's own scope to model. Comparator source: none sought. Confidence: high (in the sense that no plausible independent economic-impact pathway exists for the reporting action itself, distinct from downstream decisions it might inform).

Social ROI

Directional case only: transparent program-reach reporting is a governance/accountability good in its own right, consistent with this leaf's own Cui Bono finding that no extraction dynamic was identified here but that basic program-reach transparency was also not confirmed to exist — closing that gap has standalone value independent of what it reveals.

Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely environmentally neutral. A reporting function has no material emissions, land-use, water, waste, or resilience footprint. Comparator source: none sought. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-2 · quote-context, directly verified to primary source (accessed/fetched 2026-07-14) · Statistics Canada, Picot & Ostrovsky 2021, Catalogue no. 36-28-0001-2021009 · business-size nuance motivating the need for disaggregated reach data
  • backgrounder "Open questions / data gaps" · genuinely uncovered gap on existing program reach into diaspora-owned businesses

Confidence & uncertainties

High confidence on feasibility if underlying data is already collected; genuinely uncertain feasibility and cost if it is not, a caveat this card states explicitly rather than assuming away. No claim is made about what such reporting would find — the City's existing programs may already reach diaspora-owned businesses proportionately, and this card does not presuppose otherwise.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming current data-collection practices in the Economic Development & Culture division; Lane R pass.
