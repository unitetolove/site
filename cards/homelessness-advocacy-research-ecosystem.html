Homelessness Advocacy and Research Ecosystem — Playbook

Who actually produces the data and advocacy behind Toronto's homelessness debate, and where research ends and campaigning begins.

DRAFTThe playbookThe evidence file

What this project can actually do on the homelessness advocacy and research ecosystem — each move with its costs, its beneficiaries, and its receipts.

v2.0 · 2026-08-11

---

The honest bottom line

Every statistic and framework this project's homelessness research uses came from somewhere. The Toronto Alliance to End Homelessness (TAEH) is a coalition of more than 100 organizations — most of the city's shelters, multi-service agencies, and supportive housing providers — co-running the City's Coordinated Access system and leading the plan behind Toronto's 18,000-supportive-homes target; as of March 16, 2026 it has a new Interim Executive Director, Sonja Nerad, who spent more than seven years running the Toronto Shelter Network before this, resolving a leadership question this project's own prior research had flagged as unclear. The Canadian Definition of Homelessness — unsheltered, emergency sheltered, provisionally accommodated, at risk — comes from the Canadian Observatory on Homelessness at York University, whose president, Dr. Stephen Gaetz, is the most-cited homelessness researcher in the country, with formal advisory roles on both a provincial (2015, Ontario) and federal (2017) government panel — relevant context, since he's been formally inside the room advising both levels of government this kind of research holds accountable, not an outside critic shouting from the sidelines. Maytree supplies the social-assistance data behind a lot of this project's income-security research; the Advocacy Centre for Tenants Ontario has a documented history of direct submissions to City Council on tenant protections; the Wellesley Institute's race-based health data underpins research on why Black and Indigenous people are overrepresented in Toronto's homeless population; the John Howard Society of Ontario's reports on the link between incarceration and homelessness are foundational to a whole strand of this project's own root-cause research. None of these are footnotes — they're the actual source of findings this project and others build on. This project found no evidence that any organization in this ecosystem profits from homelessness continuing, and is not manufacturing that claim: every organization here has funding relationships, which is true of essentially every non-profit and academic institution doing this kind of work anywhere, and funding relationships alone are not evidence of anything more than that.

Actually credit these organizations by name, every time, project-wide. (a recommendation card) This page’s own source material already says this should happen; a future pass should check whether it actually does across this project's other research.

Put leadership and contact checks on a standing refresh cycle. (a recommendation card) A one-time check goes stale. This project should plan to re-check, not assume this week's snapshot holds forever.

What this office does not settle. Several organizations' current leadership remains genuinely unclear — this project's prior research flagged Houselink & Mainstay's post-merger leadership as unconfirmed, and this review didn't independently re-check every flagged uncertainty, only TAEH's. Leadership in this sector changes often enough that any research pass will start going stale relatively quickly, and that's stated here rather than presenting a snapshot as permanent. Both moves below are internal research-practice actions, not City policy asks — this file holds zero adoptable City-facing cards; what follows required checking what these organizations say about themselves, directly, and being honest about what still isn't known.

Problem

This project's own research draws heavily on work originated by Toronto's homelessness advocacy and research sector (TAEH's data dashboard, COH/Gaetz's definitional frameworks, Wellesley Institute's equity data, and more), but individual backgrounders elsewhere in this project do not consistently name the originating organization or researcher in the same sentence as the finding — this page’s own carried-forward material names this as a stated recommended practice ("Wherever this library cites a statistic or framework originating from one of these organizations, name the organization... in the same sentence — not just in a footnote") that has not yet been checked for consistent application project-wide [From this library’s earlier research].

Action

A future CURATE-lane pass audits a sample of this project's Domain H backgrounders for whether findings originating from the organizations/researchers profiled in this page are credited by name in-line, and corrects any that only cite a formally registered claim ID without naming the originating organization where the underlying source itself is one of these named bodies.

  • City demands of Province: none.
  • City demands of Feds: none.

Order-of-magnitude: low — a documentation-audit pass across already-written backgrounders, not new research.

None required; internal CURATE-lane action.

The credited organizations/researchers themselves, via accurate public credit for originating work this project draws on — directly consistent with this project's own stated credibility argument ("everything is sourced, nothing is invented"); readers of this project's research, via clearer provenance.

a recommendation card — Keep This Leaf's Leadership/Contact Currency Checks on a Standing Refresh Cycle

Card id: a recommendation card · Card class: INTERNAL · Issue: homelessness-advocacy-research-ecosystem · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: New load-bearing findings (backgrounder NEW-2026-1)

Problem

This review found that TAEH's leadership had changed since the page’s own carried-forward material was written (a March 2026 appointment, resolving a leadership question that material had flagged as uncertain just months earlier) [NEW-2026-1]. This page’s own carried-forward material separately flags at least one other organization's (Houselink & Mainstay's) post-merger leadership as unconfirmed. Organizational leadership in this sector changes often enough that a one-time research pass will predictably go stale.

Action

This page’s own maintenance record notes that leadership/contact details for the organizations profiled here should be re-checked directly against each organization's own website on a periodic basis (e.g., each time this page is next substantively revisited), rather than treated as permanently current from this review's check.

Order-of-magnitude: negligible — a periodic direct-website-check practice, not a new research program.

Funding path

None required.

Who benefits, and how

Readers of this page’s research, via current rather than stale leadership/contact information; the profiled organizations, via not being misrepresented by an outdated leadership claim.

Who bears the cost, and how

No payer class — negligible periodic-check cost only.

Financial ROI

Not applicable.

Economic ROI

Not applicable; no comparator needed. Confidence: high on the "not applicable" finding.

Social ROI

Directional: this is a direct, low-cost application of this project's own trust-layer discipline ("anything not yet trustworthy says so on its face") to a category of fact (organizational leadership) that is unusually prone to going stale between research passes. Confidence: medium.
