Water, Wastewater & Broadband Capital Infrastructure — Playbook

Aging pipes and cables underground are easy to ignore — how far behind Toronto's replacement schedule actually runs.

DRAFTThe playbookThe evidence file

v2.0 · 2026-08-11

---

The honest bottom line

This doesn't prove Toronto's water infrastructure is in crisis — the division's own plan looks reasonably solid on its own terms. It doesn't prove the city's overall infrastructure math is unfixable, either — it's a real structural problem, but not one this document claims is unsolvable. It doesn't tell you whether killing the original 2022 municipal broadband plan was the right call, or whether reviving it would work — that debate is still open, and this review found no fresh evidence to settle it either way. And it doesn't identify anyone specifically benefiting from any of this staying unresolved; that search came up empty in the time available, reported honestly as "not yet found" rather than "confirmed nobody benefits."

a recommendation card — Publish a Standing Public Status Report on the Municipal-Fibre Feasibility Decision

Card id: a recommendation card · Issue: water-wastewater-broadband-infrastructure · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: New load-bearing findings (anchored to a documented data gap this review itself hit)

Problem

In November 2024, Toronto's Executive Committee directed staff toward assessing the feasibility of a City-owned fibre network, and the inherited briefing on this page recommended a full business case return to Council "within 18 months." This review's live search for any 2025 or 2026 update on that direction found none — the July 2025 ConnectTO program update to the same Executive Committee is silent on municipal fibre entirely, focused only on public Wi-Fi [see backgrounder]. Whether this reflects the feasibility work quietly continuing off the public record, having stalled, or having been deprioritized cannot be determined from published sources, and residents and Council members have no current, citable status to point to either way.

Action

Staff report back to Executive Committee with a specific, dated status update on the municipal-fibre feasibility question — confirming whether a feasibility study/business case is underway, complete, paused, or not commissioned — as a standalone, easily findable public status statement (not necessarily requiring a full business case, since this card addresses the transparency gap, not the underlying feasibility decision itself).

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: requesting a staff status update through Council or committee process is within existing Council authority — no external approval needed.
  • City demands of Province: none required for this specific action; not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: none required for this specific action; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible — a status report is ordinary staff-report process cost, not a new program expenditure. This card does not propose committing to the fibre network itself, only to public clarity about the decision's current status.

Funding path

Not applicable — existing Council/committee process budget covers a standard staff report.

Who benefits, and how

Council members and residents seeking to evaluate whether Toronto's broadband strategy has a live fibre component or is Wi-Fi-only going forward, via a clear, dated, citable status statement; this project's own future research passes, via a stable primary source that does not currently exist in an easily discoverable form.

Who bears the cost, and how

No meaningful direct cost-bearer; ordinary staff time.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table with a graded, sourced entry — the backgrounder's own Cui Bono section names incumbent ISPs (Bell, Rogers) as a plausible beneficiary of the fibre-decision remaining unresolved, per the inherited document's general analytical framing, but explicitly does not promote this to a graded a registered entity/registered accountability claims absent a named, sourced Cui Bono finding — flagged as a real possibility, not asserted as an established fact.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Not applicable/negligible — a status report has no meaningful financial return calculation of its own. Confidence: high — the action's own scope makes this the honest answer, not a gap.

(b) Economic ROI

Not yet estimable — the economic case for or against municipal fibre itself is large and contested (see the inherited document's own FOR/AGAINST), but this card's narrow transparency action does not itself carry an economic-ROI claim; no comparator needed at this narrow scope. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Directional only — clearer public information about a live infrastructure-policy question, not quantified. No comparator identified yet. Confidence: low-medium.

(d) Environmental ROI

Genuinely neutral — a status report has no direct environmental mechanism. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-4 · NEW · City of Toronto, "ConnectTO Program Update" (2025-07-02) — confirms the report's own silence on municipal fibre, the documented evidentiary basis for this card's Problem statement
  • Backgrounder's own "Open questions / data gaps" — the documented absence of any 2025-2026 fibre-feasibility status source

Confidence & uncertainties

High confidence the underlying gap is real (this review's own search experience, cross-checked against a directly-fetched, current, on-topic staff report that stays silent on the question, is direct evidence). This card takes no position on whether Toronto should build municipal fibre — only that the public record on the decision's current status is currently absent and fixable at near-zero cost.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: none substantively; this is a low-friction, low-cost transparency ask ready for fairness and legal review.

a recommendation card — Adopt Copenhagen-Style Explicit Grey-vs-Green Cost Comparison in the 2026 Basement Flooding Program Review

Card id: a recommendation card · Issue: water-wastewater-broadband-infrastructure · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: CONTEXTUAL (combines a NEW-confirmed current City review process with an international comparator methodology)

Problem

The City confirmed it is conducting a program-wide study in 2026 "to review and improve how projects are evaluated" under the Basement Flooding Protection Program, and separately continuing an "Enhancing Capital Construction Delivery" initiative [NEW-3]. The backgrounder's international-context research found that Copenhagen's own stormwater program made a specific, quantified, published cost comparison between conventional grey infrastructure and blue-green/surface alternatives before committing capital, finding surface solutions reduced mitigation costs by more than USD 200 million versus piping-only approaches [NEW-5]. No source located in this review confirms Toronto's 2026 program-evaluation review uses or will use an equivalent explicit, published cost-comparison methodology.

Action

As part of the already-underway 2026 program-wide evaluation review, Toronto Water adopts and publishes an explicit grey-versus-green (or hybrid) cost comparison methodology for basement-flooding and stormwater capital projects, modelled on Copenhagen's published cost-avoidance approach, so future project sequencing decisions have a citable, quantified cost basis rather than a qualitative preference for green infrastructure.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: this is a methodology addition to a review process the City has already confirmed is underway in 2026 — within existing Toronto Water authority, requiring no external approval.
  • City demands of Province: none required for this specific action; not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: none required for this specific action; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low, incremental to the already-budgeted 2026 program-wide evaluation review — this card proposes a methodology addition to work already underway and funded, not a new standalone study. No specific dollar figure is available for the marginal cost of adding a cost-comparison methodology to an existing review, and this document does not invent one.

Funding path

Existing Toronto Water 2026 program-evaluation review budget (already confirmed underway per NEW-3); no new funding mechanism proposed.

Who benefits, and how

Toronto Water capital planners and Council, via a citable, quantified decision-support tool for future basement-flooding and stormwater project sequencing; residents in flood-prone neighbourhoods, indirectly, via potentially more cost-effective project selection over time.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via the existing Toronto Water capital/operating budget already allocated to the 2026 review; no meaningful incremental cost beyond the review's existing scope.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table — the backgrounder's Cui Bono section is explicitly empty for this page’s water/wastewater capital-planning dimension specifically, with a stated reason (no ESTABLISHED/REPORTED-grade finding located in this review).

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Not yet estimable for Toronto specifically — Copenhagen's own figure (>USD 200 million avoided) is a real, named comparator (Copenhagen Cloudburst Management Plan cost-avoidance analysis [NEW-5]) but is not scoped to Toronto's specific project portfolio or currency-adjusted. Confidence: low — a foreign comparator's aggregate figure does not transfer directly to Toronto's smaller-scale, differently-structured basement-flooding program; inherited at the comparator's own confidence level, not upgraded for this narrower use.

(b) Economic ROI

Not yet estimable; no comparator identified yet specific to a methodology-adoption action (as opposed to the underlying infrastructure spending itself). Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Directional only — better-informed, more transparent capital decision-making for flood-prone neighbourhoods, not quantified. No comparator identified yet. Confidence: low.

(d) Environmental ROI

Modest positive if the methodology shifts future project selection toward more green/blue infrastructure — consistent with Copenhagen's own experience [NEW-5] — but not independently modeled for Toronto's specific project mix. Confidence: low-medium — directionally plausible given the comparator, not independently modeled for Toronto.

Evidence

  • NEW-3 · NEW (search-synthesized) · City of Toronto Basement Flooding Protection Program 2026 program-wide evaluation review, confirmed underway
  • NEW-5 · NEW (search-synthesized) · Copenhagen Cloudburst Management Plan cost-comparison methodology and figure

Confidence & uncertainties

Low-to-medium confidence overall. The 2026 review's existence is well-confirmed; whether it will or should adopt this specific methodology is this card's own recommendation, not a confirmed City intention — stated explicitly as a card-level position per L6 template discipline, not smuggled into the backgrounder's neutral prose. The Copenhagen figure's transferability to Toronto's specific program scale is genuinely uncertain and flagged as low confidence throughout rather than presented as a directly applicable number.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming with Toronto Water directly whether the 2026 program-wide evaluation review's terms of reference already include a cost-comparison methodology (this could make the card redundant or could confirm the gap), and a City-sourced cost estimate for the methodology-adoption work itself.
