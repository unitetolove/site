RC-GWB-01 — Complete and Independently Audit the 311 Transparency Dashboard's Coverage

Card id: RC-GWB-01 · File: research/cards/government-that-works-basics.md#rc-gwb-01 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: government-that-works-basics · Backgrounder: government-that-works-basics Trust class: INHERITED · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The master briefing's central transparency critique — that Toronto's open-311 data covered only ~30-35% of requests and 6 of 45 divisions — was itself flagged by the spine as unconfirmed against a primary source [backgrounder, "311: from partial transparency to a real published dashboard"]. This pass confirms the City has since launched a real, dated 311 Service Standard Dashboard (October 2025) covering a named set of service areas, but neither the spine's original gap figure nor how much of it the new dashboard closes has been independently confirmed [backgrounder, "Key tensions / tradeoffs"]. This card addresses only the coverage-verification gap — not whether 311 service itself is adequate, which is a separate, broader question this leaf's evidence does not fully resolve.

Action

The City publishes a specific, numeric statement of what share of total 311 request volume and how many of the City's 45 divisions the new Service Standard Dashboard and the underlying open-data feed actually cover, updated on a fixed schedule (e.g., annually alongside the dashboard's own data refresh), replacing the ambiguous, unconfirmed figure that has circulated in prior public discussion of Toronto's transparency performance.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: the Customer Experience Division, which already publishes the dashboard, adds a specific coverage-disclosure statement to its own existing publication — a documentation addition to a program already in place, fully within existing municipal administrative authority.
  • City demands of Province: none identified as required.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low — this is a reporting/documentation addition to an already-funded, already-operating dashboard program, not a new system build. No specific cost comparator was located this pass, but the marginal cost of adding a coverage-disclosure statement to an existing published dashboard is plausibly minimal relative to the dashboard's own existing operating cost. [Flagged: no specific costed comparator identified; this is a qualitative, not sourced, cost characterization.]

Funding path

Within the Customer Experience Division's existing operating budget for the 311 Service Standard Dashboard program, which is already funded and operating as of October 2025.

Toronto residents and civil-society researchers/journalists, via a resolved, citable, current figure for 311 transparency coverage, replacing an unconfirmed number that has circulated in public discussion since before the master briefing's own writing; the City's own credibility on transparency claims, via closing a gap the spine itself flagged as needing to be "checked or softened before public use."

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via the Customer Experience Division's existing operating budget — no new distinct cost centre identified as required.

RC-GWB-02 — Independently Evaluate the 311 Predictive-Maintenance Pilot's Own Causal Contribution

Card id: RC-GWB-02 · File: research/cards/government-that-works-basics.md#rc-gwb-02 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: government-that-works-basics · Backgrounder: government-that-works-basics Trust class: INHERITED · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Toronto is actively expanding an AI/predictive 311 pilot for pothole management, and pothole-filling volume has grown substantially (from 87,925 in Q1 2025 alone to a reported 250,000+ across all of 2025) [backgrounder, "Predictive/proactive service"]. But volume filled is an output measure, not evidence the prediction itself — as opposed to simply more crews and more budget — is doing the work; no source located this pass isolates the pilot's own causal contribution [backgrounder, "Key tensions / tradeoffs"]. This card addresses only the evaluation-design gap — not whether to continue or expand the pilot, which is a separate decision this card does not make for the City.

Action

The City commissions or conducts an evaluation specifically isolating the predictive/forecasting pilot's own marginal contribution to service outcomes (e.g., comparing prediction-flagged locations' repair timelines against a comparable non-flagged control, or a similar quasi-experimental design), distinct from simply reporting aggregate output volume, before further scaling the pilot to additional service categories beyond potholes.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: commission the evaluation through Transportation Services and/or the Customer Experience Division, both already involved in the pilot per the council directive cited in the backgrounder — within existing municipal administrative authority.
  • City demands of Province: none identified as required.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low, consistent with a program-evaluation study rather than new infrastructure — no specific comparator cost was located this pass for a 311-predictive-analytics evaluation specifically. [Flagged: no costed comparator identified.]

Funding path

Within Transportation Services' or the Customer Experience Division's existing program-evaluation or research budget, if one exists; not confirmed as a currently budgeted line item in this leaf's sources.

Who benefits, and how

Toronto residents and City budget decision-makers, via a clear answer to whether the predictive pilot itself is worth its own marginal cost as the City considers scaling it beyond potholes — directly serving the master briefing's own "genuine value for money, not cosmetic efficiency" standard [the inherited master briefing's §The strongest case AGAINST, "Genuine value for money is not service cuts in disguise"].

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via whichever division's budget funds the evaluation.

Who benefits from the status quo

No specific beneficiary entity identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono section — no beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder [backgrounder, "Cui Bono"].

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Range: not yet estimable — the evaluation itself is a small cost; its finding could support either continued investment or reallocation, but the return depends entirely on what the evaluation finds, which this card cannot pre-determine. Comparator source: none identified. Confidence: low — appropriately so, since this card's own action is designed to produce the missing evidence, not assume its conclusion.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Indirectly supported by the master briefing's own "proactive over reactive" success criterion [the inherited master briefing's §What determines success vs failure, item 4] — an evaluation that confirms the pilot works would strengthen the case for the proactive-service shift the spine already endorses; one that finds no marginal effect would be equally valuable information, since it would redirect resources rather than continue an unproven approach. Confidence: low-medium — the value of the evaluation itself is well-supported by the spine's own logic; the underlying pilot's actual effect is unknown.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: not applicable — an evaluation study has no material environmental footprint or benefit distinct from the underlying pilot it studies. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high — genuinely neutral.

Evidence

  • backgrounder, "Predictive/proactive service" · NEW (2026-07-14, VERIFY) · council agenda item 2026.EX29.4, AI/chatbot acceleration directive
  • backgrounder, "Predictive/proactive service" · NEW (2026-07-14, VERIFY) · 2025 pothole totals and year-over-year increase figure
  • the inherited master briefing's §Sources to verify · INHERITED, marked verified · Q1 2025 pothole figure (87,925)
  • the inherited master briefing's §What determines success vs failure, item 4 · INHERITED · proactive-over-reactive success criterion

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence this evaluation is a genuinely useful, low-cost action given the spine's own emphasis on "genuine," not cosmetic, value for money. Low confidence on what the evaluation would actually find, which is precisely the point — this card does not presume the pilot works or doesn't, only that the current evidence base (output volume alone) cannot answer the question either way.
