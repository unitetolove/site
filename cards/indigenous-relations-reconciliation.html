RC-IR-01 — Fund the RAP's Own Named Accountability-Structure Gap — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-IR-01 · File: research/cards/indigenous-relations-reconciliation.md#rc-ir-01 Issue slug: indigenous-relations-reconciliation · Backgrounder: indigenous-relations-reconciliation Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (backgrounder NEW-2026-IR-1) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The City's own November 2025 progress report names, in its own words, a "lack of a meaningful community advisory structure for validation, feedback, updates, or the development of required accountability measures" as one of four specific challenges facing RAP implementation [NEW-2026-IR-1]. This is a City-identified institutional gap, not a claim this card makes on Indigenous organizations' behalf — the City's report also separately states that TICAB, in the distinct homelessness-governance domain, already exercises a materially stronger model (final decision-making authority over specific policy areas, per this leaf's own cross-reference to homelessness-indigenous-overrepresentation), showing a stronger accountability structure is already precedented within Toronto's own institutions.

Action

The City funds and formally establishes a RAP-specific community advisory/accountability body with defined authority (at minimum, a formal review-and-feedback role on RAP progress reporting, modelled structurally on the binding-authority precedent TICAB already holds in the homelessness-Coordinated-Access domain, though this card does not assert TICAB's specific model should be copied wholesale without Indigenous partners' own design input) — addressing the gap the City's own report names, not a gap this card asserts independently.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: funds and formally establishes the body within existing municipal authority (the same authority already used to establish TICAB and the Aboriginal Affairs Advisory Committee).
  • City demands of Province: none identified as required — this is entirely within municipal governance authority.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands CAD annually for a formal advisory body's operating/honoraria costs — comparator: the City's own $350,000 one-time capacity-funding allocation to TASSC in the same November 2025 report [NEW-2026-IR-1, ⚠️ NONIND-ABOUT] is the closest available named anchor for the scale of capacity-funding gaps the City itself has already identified and funded once.

Funding path

Existing Social Development / Indigenous Affairs Office operating budget, at a scale comparable to the City's own recent $350,000 TASSC allocation [NEW-2026-IR-1, ⚠️ NONIND-ABOUT]; this card does not invent a new funding mechanism.

Who benefits, and how

Indigenous community partners currently engaging with the RAP without, per the City's own report, "a meaningful community advisory structure for validation, feedback, updates" [NEW-2026-IR-1] — via a formal channel for that feedback to have documented, tracked effect; City divisions, via clearer accountability benchmarks than internal self-reporting alone provides.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via existing Social Development operating budget, at a scale comparable to the City's own recent one-time capacity grants.

Financial ROI

Not separately estimated; this is a governance/accountability-structure cost, not a program with a direct fiscal-return case of its own.

Economic ROI

Range: Not yet estimable — a governance-structure change has no first-order employment or induced-spending effect distinct from existing City administrative operations. Comparator source: none identified. Confidence: low.

Social ROI

Directional: addresses a specific, City-self-identified gap in RAP accountability; whether it would satisfy Indigenous partners' own stated concerns about co-creation is not asserted by this card, since no direct IND-SELF citation locating TASSC's or TICAB's own specific proposed remedy for this gap was found in this pass — flagged as a real limitation of this card's own confidence, not smoothed over.

Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely environmentally neutral. A governance/advisory-structure change has no construction, land-use, or physical-infrastructure component. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: high on the neutrality of this specific action.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-IR-1 · quote-context (this pass) · City's own named accountability-structure gap and TASSC capacity-funding precedent
  • homelessness-indigenous-overrepresentation (cross-reference) · TICAB's documented final-decision-making authority model

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on the gap's existence (directly, plainly stated by the City itself); low-medium confidence on this specific remedy's adequacy, since this card does not have a direct IND-SELF source confirming this is the remedy Indigenous partners themselves would propose — this is stated explicitly as this card's own limitation, consistent with this leaf's voice rule that a card may propose City delivery/transparency mechanisms but must not claim to know or speak for Indigenous priorities.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: Lane R pass; direct outreach to or citation of TASSC's/TICAB's own stated position on what accountability structure they would want, which this pass did not locate and does not substitute for.

RC-IR-02 — Publish the Eight Named TRC Calls to Action Against Their Actual Text, Annually, Alongside the Sub-Action Tracker — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-IR-02 · File: research/cards/indigenous-relations-reconciliation.md#rc-ir-02 Issue slug: indigenous-relations-reconciliation · Backgrounder: indigenous-relations-reconciliation Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (backgrounder NEW-2026-IR-1, NEW-2026-IR-2) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The City's own November 2025 report tracks 141 sub-actions in granular detail but does not, in the document this pass reviewed, map that tracking directly and explicitly back to the eight specific, named TRC Calls to Action (23, 43, 57, 68, 77, 82, 88, 94) Council itself identified as Toronto's municipal priorities in 2015 [NEW-2026-IR-1, NEW-2026-IR-2]. This creates a transparency gap distinct from the sub-action tracker itself: a reader cannot currently see, in one place, which of the eight specifically-named national Calls each RAP theme or sub-action is actually advancing.

Action

The City publishes, alongside its existing sub-action progress table, a direct crosswalk mapping each of the eight named TRC Calls to Action to the specific RAP sub-actions intended to advance it and their current status — making the City's own 2015 prioritization decision and its 2022-2032 delivery plan checkable against each other in one place.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: produces the crosswalk using data the City already collects and publishes in its existing sub-action tracker, within existing Indigenous Affairs Office reporting capacity.
  • City demands of Province: none identified as required.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low — an additional reporting/mapping exercise using already-collected data, comparator: the same existing sub-action tracking table structure the City already produces and publishes biennially [NEW-2026-IR-1].

Funding path

Existing Indigenous Affairs Office / progress-reporting operating capacity; no new funding mechanism required.

Who benefits, and how

Council, the public, and Indigenous community partners seeking to assess whether Toronto's 2015 choice of eight priority Calls to Action is actually being delivered on, via a single, checkable document instead of needing to cross-reference the RAP's own text against the sub-action tracker manually.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, negligibly, via existing Indigenous Affairs Office reporting-staff time.

Financial ROI

Not applicable — a reporting/transparency action with no direct fiscal cost or saving.

Economic ROI

Range: Not yet estimable. Comparator source: none sought — this action's value is transparency-instrumental, not economic in itself. Confidence: low.

Social ROI

Directional: closes a specific, named transparency gap between the City's 2015 Call-to-Action prioritization decision and its current, more granular sub-action tracking — not independently quantified.

Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely environmentally neutral — a reporting/mapping exercise has no physical footprint. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: high on the neutrality of this specific action.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-IR-1 · quote-context (this pass) · existing sub-action tracking structure and granularity
  • NEW-2026-IR-2 · quote-context (this pass) · the eight named TRC Calls to Action, per the 2015 Council decision
  • CL-0227 · VERIFIED (ledger, IND-CO) · TRC's 94 Calls to Action, June 2, 2015

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium-high confidence. The underlying data gap (no direct eight-Calls-to-141-sub-actions crosswalk found in the report this pass reviewed) is based on a direct review of the November 2025 report's own text and appendix structure as fetched; it is possible the City's own Appendix 1 (not independently re-verified line-by-line in this pass) already contains this mapping in a form this pass's summary-level review did not fully capture — flagged as a real possibility rather than asserted as a confirmed gap.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: Lane R pass; a direct line-by-line check of Appendix 1 of the November 2025 report (141 sub-actions) to confirm whether this crosswalk already exists in a form this pass's review did not surface.
