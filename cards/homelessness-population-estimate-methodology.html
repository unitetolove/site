Card 1: A Toronto-Specific Hidden-Homelessness Census Study, Using an Existing Replicable Method

Card id: RC-HPEM-01 · File: research/cards/homelessness-population-estimate-methodology.md#card-1 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: homelessness-population-estimate-methodology · Backgrounder: homelessness-population-estimate-methodology Trust class: INHERITED (problem statement and comparator method) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Toronto's best current estimate of hidden/total homelessness (roughly 35,000–45,000, against an official SNA count of an estimated 15,418 or a more current estimated 12,196) is an extrapolation built from Ontario's own disputed, unofficial approximately 234,000 provincial estimate, not a direct Toronto measurement [backgrounder, "Ontario's disputed internal estimate..."]. The leaf's own promoted source names the specific gap this card addresses: no study has directly measured Toronto's hidden-homelessness rate against the provincial average, and a peer-reviewed, replicable methodology already exists to do so — the Chicago Coalition for the Homeless's census-microdata-based "doubled-up homelessness" model (Housing Policy Debate, 2022) [backgrounder, "International context"; INHERITED, HMK-016 Part 6].

Action

Commission a Toronto-specific hidden-homelessness estimate using the Chicago Coalition for the Homeless's doubled-up-homelessness census methodology (or a directly comparable, published, peer-reviewed method), applied to Statistics Canada census microdata for Toronto, to produce a direct, defensible estimate in place of the current extrapolation range.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: commission or partner with an academic institution (e.g., a Toronto-area university's housing/urban-studies faculty, several of which are already active in this space per the leaf's substrate) to conduct the study; provide Shelter System Flow Data and By-Name List data as inputs where methodologically appropriate and privacy-compliant.
  • City demands of Province: none required for the study itself — this is fundable and executable at the municipal/academic level. A demand bucket applies only if the City seeks provincial data-sharing cooperation (e.g., OW/ODSP "no fixed address" administrative data, already partially used in the leaf's provincial-level analysis) to strengthen the model; named as a possible, not required, ask.
  • City demands of Feds: access to Statistics Canada census microdata at the geographic and variable granularity the Chicago model requires — this may require a federal data-access agreement (e.g., through a Research Data Centre) rather than open-data access; named generically since no specific current agreement was confirmed this session.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low-to-mid six figures CAD, anchored to the general cost profile of a single-city academic census-microdata study of this type (a defined research contract with one or two academic partners over 12–18 months) — no specific dollar figure for the Chicago study itself, or for a Toronto-specific replication, was located this session; this range is anchored to the general scale of comparable single-city administrative-linkage research studies already cited elsewhere in this leaf's substrate (e.g., the ICES/Homeless Hub healthcare-cost linkage study), not to a specific published budget for either that study or this proposed one. Confidence in this cost range is accordingly low.

Funding path

No confirmed existing funding line. Plausible paths, named but not confirmed as available: a Reaching Home research/innovation stream (federal), a City research partnership budget line (as already used for the ICES/Homeless Hub study), or a university-led grant (e.g., SSHRC) with the City as a data-access and dissemination partner. This card does not assert any of these is already committed.

Who benefits, and how

Researchers, policymakers, and advocacy organizations, via a defensible, Toronto-specific figure replacing an extrapolation anchored to a disputed provincial estimate — directly closing the gap the leaf's own promoted document names as the central unresolved question in this leaf's evidence base [backgrounder, "What the evidence does and doesn't support"]. People experiencing hidden homelessness themselves, indirectly, via better-targeted prevention-spending arguments (per HMK-018's own prevention-pool framing, inherited but not re-modeled in this card) once their scale is directly measured rather than extrapolated.

Who bears the cost, and how

Whichever funder ultimately supports the study (City research budget, federal research stream, or university grant funding) — opportunity cost against other research priorities in each case; no new tax or levy is proposed by this card.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table — the backgrounder's own Cui Bono section states plainly that this leaf, being a measurement-methodology question rather than a service-delivery or procurement question, surfaced no ESTABLISHED or REPORTED beneficiary of undercounting specifically. This card accordingly carries no "who benefits from the status quo" line beyond this note, per the template's own instruction not to manufacture one.

Indirect case: a defensible Toronto-specific figure could improve the targeting of prevention spending (per HMK-018's inherited prevention-pool illustration, itself explicitly labeled "not a verified figure" in its own source document), but this card does not re-model that illustration or claim a fiscal-savings figure from it. Comparator source: none directly priced; the general comparator is the City's existing practice of commissioning administrative-linkage studies (e.g., the ICES/Homeless Hub healthcare-cost study already in this leaf's substrate), used only to anchor the low-to-mid-six-figures cost range above, not a fiscal-return figure. Card 2: Standardized Public Labeling of Which Count Is Being Cited, Across City Reporting

Card id: RC-HPEM-02 · File: research/cards/homelessness-population-estimate-methodology.md#card-2 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: homelessness-population-estimate-methodology · Backgrounder: homelessness-population-estimate-methodology Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (built from this session's direct primary-source fetches) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The City itself publishes at least four structurally different homelessness counts — the SNA (single-night, visible + service-engaged), Shelter System Flow Data (rolling three-month distinct-persons measure), nightly shelter occupancy/capacity (a bed/room snapshot), and the By-Name List (an individually tracked coordinated-access roster) — each answering a different question, on different public City pages, without a single unified page stating which figure to use for which purpose [backgrounder, "Key tensions/tradeoffs"]. The City's own Shelter System Flow Data page already states the SERVICE_USER_COUNT ≠ beds distinction explicitly in its own methodology notes (nightly occupancy of 9,010 in 2023 vs. ~22,000 unique individuals served the same year) [backgrounder, "Shelter System Flow Data..."; NEW, fetched 2026-07-14], but that clarity is not surfaced anywhere outside that one dashboard's own methodology footnotes, creating exactly the kind of conflation risk this leaf's substrate documents happening repeatedly in public discourse and prior library drafts.

Action

The City publishes a single, short, plain-language "which homelessness number should I use" explainer page — linked prominently from the SNA, Shelter System Flow Data, Daily Shelter & Overnight Service Usage, and By-Name List/Coordinated Access pages — that names each measure, states in one sentence what population and time window it covers, and states explicitly that they are not interchangeable or directly comparable without adjustment.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: publishing this explainer page and cross-linking it from existing dashboards is entirely within existing City of Toronto Housing Secretariat / communications authority — no other order of government needs to act.
  • City demands of Province: empty bucket — this action requires no provincial action.
  • City demands of Feds: empty bucket — this action requires no federal action, though the underlying PiT-count standardization this explainer would describe is itself a federal Reaching Home requirement, described but not altered by this card.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low four figures to low five figures CAD as a one-time content/communications cost, anchored to the general scale of the City's existing practice of publishing similar explainer content (e.g., the Housing Stability Service System Overview / glossary page already live on the City's site) [NEW, https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/research-reports/housing-and-homelessness-research-and-reports/housing-stability-service-system-map-and-terms/, fetched 2026-07-14] — this is a content-authoring and cross-linking exercise on data the City already collects and already partially explains piecemeal, not a new data-collection program.

Funding path

Existing Housing Secretariat / City communications operating budget — a content-publishing change, not a program requiring new funding.

Who benefits, and how

Journalists, researchers, advocacy organizations, and the general public, via a single authoritative reference point reducing the risk of citing one count as if it were another — directly mitigating the same failure mode this leaf's own promoted documents document happening historically in prior reporting and prior library drafts (e.g., the CBC "92,965 people" household/person conflation documented in the sibling social-housing-waitlist-data leaf, a structurally identical error pattern in a different dataset). City communications and 311 staff, via a single reference to point inquiries to rather than re-explaining the distinction ad hoc.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the Housing Secretariat/communications operating budget; City staff, via the one-time work of drafting and cross-linking the explainer (an opportunity cost against other communications priorities).

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table — see Card 1's identical note above; this card carries no "who benefits from the status quo" line, per the template's instruction not to manufacture one where the backgrounder found none.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

No direct fiscal-savings case is asserted — this is a transparency/communications action, not a service-delivery change. Indirect cost avoidance is plausible (fewer future correction cycles across derivative documents and press coverage, of the kind this leaf's own substrate and its sibling social-housing-waitlist-data leaf both document happening) but not quantified. Comparator source: the City's own existing Housing Stability Service System Overview/glossary page, used only as a comparator for the action's own scale and cost, not a fiscal-return figure. Confidence: low — directional only, not costed.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: Not directly modeled for this specific explainer-page action; broad open-government-data/transparency economic literature exists (e.g., the Open Data Institute's finding that open public-sector data assets generate measurable economic value, already cited in the sibling social-housing-waitlist-data leaf's Card 1) but is not sized to this narrow a scope and is not re-cited here as if it were. Comparator source: none priced specifically to this action. Confidence: low — closer to "not yet estimable" than a genuine range.

(c) Social ROI

Directly traceable to a documented, recurring failure mode across this library's own history: count/unit conflations (households vs. people in the waitlist leaf; nightly occupancy vs. distinct-persons counts in this leaf) that required dedicated correction passes after the fact. A single, cross-linked explainer is a direct, low-cost mitigation for the same failure mode recurring in future public reporting, for the same population of downstream readers this leaf's own substrate shows have already been affected. Comparator source: this leaf's own documented correction history (backgrounder, "Shelter System Flow Data" section) and the sibling waitlist leaf's parallel documented history. Confidence: medium — the underlying failure-mode pattern is well-evidenced within this library; the marginal effect of one explainer page on external reporting behaviour is not independently measured.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely environmentally neutral. A content-publishing action with no construction, land-use, or physical-infrastructure component. Comparator source: none needed to support a neutral finding. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • Backgrounder §"Shelter System Flow Data: a different, rolling measure..." · NEW, City of Toronto pages fetched 2026-07-14 · the SERVICE_USER_COUNT ≠ beds distinction, stated in the City's own words
  • Backgrounder §"Key tensions/tradeoffs" · INHERITED + NEW · four structurally different counts cited interchangeably in public discourse
  • research/cards/social-housing-waitlist-data.md, Card 1 (RC-SHWD-01) · INHERITED · a structurally identical proposed mitigation for a parallel conflation problem in the waitlist leaf, cited here as a precedent for this card's own design, not copied uncritically

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium-high confidence in the problem statement (directly evidenced by this session's own primary-source fetches showing the City already states the distinction in one dashboard's footnotes but not centrally); lower confidence in the specific cost estimate, anchored to a general comparator rather than a cited unit cost.
