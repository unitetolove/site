Card RC-YSC-01 — Fund a Toronto Youth Foyer Pilot, Co-Locating Transitional Beds With Education/Employment Pathways

Card id: RC-YSC-01 · File: research/cards/homelessness-youth-shelter-capacity.md#rc-ysc-01 Issue slug: homelessness-youth-shelter-capacity · Backgrounder: homelessness-youth-shelter-capacity Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (backgrounder NEW-YSC-2, CL-625, CL-049) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Toronto has an estimated more than 1,500 homeless youth (16-24) on any given night against up to 740 youth shelter beds — a capacity gap of at least 760 [CL-049]. Separately, the Making the Shift prevention program demonstrated that targeted, integrated intervention measurably reduces youth homelessness (a 24.6% to 12.2% reduction in youth sleeping away from home within six months), not just shelter-bed capacity alone [CL-625]. This card addresses the gap between a pure bed-count response and an integrated-pathway response, drawing on the Foyer model's design [NEW-YSC-2].

Action

The City, in partnership with an existing youth shelter operator (e.g., Covenant House Toronto or Eva's Initiatives, both already operating relevant programming [CL-196, CL-515]), pilots a Foyer-model site: transitional beds co-located with dedicated education, training, and employment support under one program design, rather than funded and delivered as separate, sequential services.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: could support site identification and zoning within existing municipal authority, similar to the HSCIS shelter-siting process documented in the sibling shelter-system-capacity-strain leaf.
  • City demands of Province: co-funding, given Ontario's own Ready, Set, Go program already funds youth-in-transition life-skills and education/employment pathways for youth aging out of care [CL-268] — a natural funding-mechanism overlap with a Foyer-model pilot's own education/employment component.
  • City demands of Feds: none directly identified as required for a first pilot at this scale.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: not independently costed in this pass. Comparator: Ontario's own $170 million, three-year Ready, Set, Go commitment [CL-268] is the nearest available order-of-magnitude anchor for a comparable youth-transition-focused provincial investment, though a single-site Foyer pilot would be a small fraction of that scale — this card does not manufacture a specific dollar figure without a dedicated costing study.

Funding path

Blended: potential redirection or expansion of existing Ready, Set, Go funding [CL-268] toward a shelter-co-located pilot (rather than solely aging-out-of-care-focused delivery), plus existing operator capital/operating budgets (e.g., Covenant House's own $60.529 million in reserves as of the FY2025 update, cited by pointer to shelter-operator-financial-profiles [CL-90681], illustrates that at least one operator has reserve capacity that could co-fund a pilot, without this card asserting Covenant House specifically should or would commit those reserves).

Who benefits, and how

Homeless youth in Toronto, via a housing model integrated with an independence pathway rather than a bed placement alone — directly responsive to the capacity-gap population [CL-049]; existing operators, via a funding model potentially less dependent on donor-cycle volatility (per this leaf's own documented funding-model contrast between Covenant House and Eva's) if provincial co-funding is secured.

Who bears the cost, and how

Provincial general revenue, via redirected or expanded Ready, Set, Go funding if that path is pursued [CL-268]; City taxpayers, minimally, via site-identification and zoning support; the specific operator's own budget, to the extent it co-invests.

Financial ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified this pass for a Toronto-specific Foyer-model cost-benefit case; the UK/Australian Foyer networks were not confirmed to have published a specific fiscal ROI study in this pass. Confidence: low — genuinely not yet estimable without a dedicated costing study.

Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified this pass — a single-site pilot would not be expected to have an independently measurable local-economic-multiplier effect. Confidence: low.

Social ROI

Directional: Making the Shift's own measured outcome (24.6% to 12.2% reduction in youth sleeping away from home within six months) [CL-625] is real, published evidence that integrated, targeted intervention works for this population — though that program's own model (family/natural-supports focused) is not identical to the Foyer model's own housing-plus-pathway design, a distinction this card notes rather than blurs. Comparator source: Making the Shift's own final reporting [CL-625]; the Foyer model's general design description [NEW-YSC-2]. Confidence: medium-low — the general principle (integrated intervention beats bed-count-alone) is evidenced by a related but not identical Canadian program; no direct Foyer-model outcome data was independently confirmed in this pass.

Environmental ROI

Range: modest, likely comparable to any single-site shelter/transitional-housing construction or retrofit — not independently modeled. Comparator source: none identified this pass specific to a Foyer-model build. Confidence: low.

Evidence

  • CL-049 · INHERITED · 1,500+ youth homeless nightly vs. 740 youth shelter beds
  • CL-625 · INHERITED · Making the Shift, measured 24.6%→12.2% outcome
  • CL-268 · INHERITED · Ready, Set, Go, $170M/3 years
  • CL-90681 · INHERITED (figure-owned by shelter-operator-financial-profiles) · Covenant House FY2025 reserves
  • NEW-YSC-2 · quote-context (this pass) · Foyer model general description

Confidence & uncertainties

Low-medium confidence. The capacity gap and the general case for integrated intervention are well-sourced; the specific Foyer-model design and its cost are not independently costed or evaluated for a Toronto context in this pass.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: a dedicated Foyer-model outcome evaluation (UK or Australian) fetched directly rather than described generally; a Toronto-specific costing study; Lane R pass.

Card RC-YSC-02 — Commission a Post-2011 Toronto Youth-Policing Contact Study

Card id: RC-YSC-02 · File: research/cards/homelessness-youth-shelter-capacity.md#rc-ysc-02 Issue slug: homelessness-youth-shelter-capacity · Backgrounder: homelessness-youth-shelter-capacity Trust class: INHERITED (CL-624, CL-618) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Two studies, 2002 and 2011, both found high rates of police contact with Toronto street youth (81.6% victimization in 2002; 78% having some police encounter in 2011, with the 2011 study framing the pattern as "social profiling" and recommending a records-amnesty program) [CL-624, CL-618]. No post-2011 Toronto-specific study was located in this pass — the most recent measured data point on this question is now over a decade old. This card addresses the data gap, not a presumed current state (which this document does not assert either way).

Action

The City, or an academic partner (e.g., a successor to the Canadian Observatory on Homelessness research programme that produced the 2002 and 2011 studies), commissions a current Toronto-specific youth-homelessness policing-contact study, replicating the 2002/2011 methodology closely enough to allow direct comparison over the two-decade span.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: could commission or co-fund the study within existing research/data-governance authority; no new legislative authority required.
  • City demands of Province: none directly required for the study itself.
  • City demands of Feds: none directly required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low — a single-city survey-based study of comparable scope to the 2011 study (240 respondents) [CL-618] is a modest research-commission cost relative to this leaf's other capital-scale figures; no specific dollar comparator was located in this pass for the 2002 or 2011 studies' own budgets.

Funding path

City research/community-safety data budget, or a partnership with an existing academic body (e.g., a successor to the Canadian Observatory on Homelessness); no new program invented.

Who benefits, and how

Homeless youth in Toronto, via updated evidence that could inform whether the 2011 study's amnesty-program recommendation or other policy responses remain relevant; policymakers and researchers, via a current data point replacing a decade-plus-old measurement.

Who bears the cost, and how

City or academic-partner research budget — a modest, one-time research-commission cost, not an ongoing program cost.

Financial ROI

Not applicable — this is a research-commissioning action with no direct fiscal cost/saving of its own beyond the study's own modest cost.

Economic ROI

Range: not applicable — a research study has no first-order economic effect. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: high on the "not applicable" finding.

Social ROI

Directional: closes a genuine, decade-plus-old data gap on a population (homeless youth) already documented as experiencing disproportionate policing contact [CL-624, CL-618] — enabling better-evidenced future policy responses, though this card cannot quantify the downstream benefit of better data.

Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely environmentally neutral — a research study has no construction or physical footprint. Comparator source: none needed. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • CL-624 · INHERITED · 2002 Toronto street youth policing/victimization survey (81.6%)
  • CL-618 · INHERITED · 2011 Toronto street youth policing follow-up study (78%, "social profiling")

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium-high confidence on the problem statement (the data gap is directly confirmed by this pass's own search); low confidence on whether an academic partner has already begun such a study without this pass locating it — flagged as a possibility this card cannot rule out.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming no more recent study already exists unpublished or in progress; Lane R pass.
