RC-EDU-01 — An Ontario-Sourced Hybrid AI-Tutoring Pilot Inside the City's Own After-School Programming — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-EDU-01 · File: research/cards/education-reimagined-k12.md#rc-edu-01 Issue slug: education-reimagined-k12 · Backgrounder: education-reimagined-k12 Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (backgrounder NEW-2026-7, NEW-2026-9) + INHERITED (Bloom's 2 Sigma, "learn with AI, test without AI") · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The inherited briefing's central proposal — AI-scaled mastery tutoring — has no path into Toronto classrooms through any lever the City actually holds, since curriculum and pedagogy are provincial and TDSB is currently under Ministry supervision rather than elected-trustee governance [NEW-2026-1, master briefing-INHERITED]. At the same time, the strongest current evidence does not support a pure AI-replacement model: a 2026 Stanford literature review of 800+ papers found AI tutoring's benefits weaken when students are tested without AI support, while a 2025 Stanford study found hybrid models — AI assisting human tutors rather than replacing them — produced a measured, independently modelled four-percentage-point topic-mastery improvement [NEW-2026-9]. This card addresses a narrow, genuinely municipal question: could the City's own existing after-school infrastructure host a small, evidence-scoped pilot of the hybrid (not replacement) model, entirely outside the school day and outside curriculum authority?

Action

The City adds a small, opt-in hybrid AI-assisted homework/skill-practice component to its existing After-School Recreation Care (ARC) or Community Leadership After-School Program (CLASP) offerings [NEW-2026-7] — staffed by the City's own existing after-school program staff (human-led, AI-assisting, never AI-replacing, per the "learn with AI, test without AI" principle the inherited briefing itself insists on [inherited from the master briefing]), at a small number of pilot sites, with outcomes tracked and independently reported rather than self-reported by any vendor.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: design and fund a small pilot within its own existing after-school program structure (ARC/CLASP), which the City already operates, staffs, and funds [NEW-2026-7] — no new provincial or school-board authority required, since this sits entirely outside the school day and outside curriculum.
  • City demands of Province: none required to run the pilot itself; a request that the Ministry of Education's own AI-in-education discussion process (the OTF/affiliates discussion paper is already underway as of March 2026 [NEW-2026-5]) include out-of-school-time/municipal after-school findings as one input, rather than confining the AI-in-education conversation solely to in-school curriculum.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low (a small pilot add-on to existing after-school programming at a handful of sites, using existing staff plus a modest AI-tool licensing cost), anchored to the general order of magnitude of the City's existing after-school program budget lines, which already fund staffing, space, and materials at scale across ARC/CLASP sites [NEW-2026-7]. This pass did not locate a specific per-site or per-participant cost figure for the City's existing after-school programs, which is stated as a gap rather than invented — a real comparator figure should be sourced before this card advances past DRAFT.

Funding path

Existing Parks, Forestry and Recreation / children's-services after-school program operating budget, as an incremental add-on to a structure the City already funds and administers [NEW-2026-7]; a small AI-tool licensing line would be new, and no specific existing budget line for that specific cost was located in this pass — stated as a gap rather than invented.

Who benefits, and how

Children enrolled in City after-school programming, via optional, human-supervised, evidence-scoped access to a hybrid AI-assisted learning tool — the model with the strongest (if still modest) independent evidence base found in this pass [NEW-2026-9], rather than an unevaluated pure-AI product; the broader Toronto/Ontario education-policy conversation, via a small, independently-reportable local data point on hybrid AI tutoring in a public, non-elite, out-of-school-time setting — something this pass did not find any existing Ontario example of.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via the after-school program operating budget; families enrolled bear no direct cost if offered on the same subsidized/reduced-fee basis as existing ARC/CLASP programming, though this card does not confirm current ARC/CLASP fee structures in this pass.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder — the backgrounder's own Cui Bono table is empty, with the explanation that no ESTABLISHED- or REPORTED-grade, Ontario/Toronto-nexused beneficiary was identified in this pass (Alpha School, the one entity with documented critical reporting, is US-based with no confirmed Ontario/Toronto nexus) [backgrounder Cui Bono section].

Confidence: Low — no comparator found for this specific mechanism.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: Not yet estimable. Comparator source: None identified. A live search this pass for economic-impact literature on municipal after-school AI-tutoring pilots found only general K-12 AI-tutoring efficacy literature (the Stanford studies cited above [NEW-2026-9]), none of it scoped to economic impact of a delivery-channel choice. Confidence: Low — genuine gap, not computed from anything real.

(c) Social ROI

Range: Directional only — plausible modest positive effect on participating children's skill mastery, anchored to the Stanford hybrid-tutoring study's own measured four-percentage-point topic-mastery improvement [NEW-2026-9], scaled down further given this card proposes a small pilot, not the full intervention the study evaluated. Comparator source: 2025 Stanford study on AI-assisted human tutoring, as reported via secondary academic commentary [NEW-2026-9] — not independently fetched from the original study in this pass. This card deliberately does not engage the inherited briefing's more radical mastery-learning/skill-tree vision [inherited from the master briefing], since that vision requires curriculum and school-day authority the City does not have — this is a narrow, evidence-scoped, out-of-school-time proposal only.

RC-EDU-02 — A Council Advocacy Motion on TDSB Governance Transparency During Provincial Supervision — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-EDU-02 · File: research/cards/education-reimagined-k12.md#rc-edu-02 Issue slug: education-reimagined-k12 · Backgrounder: education-reimagined-k12 Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (backgrounder NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-2, NEW-2026-6) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

TDSB is currently governed by a Ministry-appointed supervisor rather than elected trustees, with board meetings not taking place and trustees holding no input on policies affecting students [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-2]. This directly affects the City's own long-standing shared-use and Community Use of Schools relationship with TDSB [NEW-2026-6], since the City's usual channel for board-level coordination (working with an elected board and its trustees) is currently unavailable. This card addresses only the City's own institutional interest in maintaining continuity and transparency in its shared-space relationship with TDSB during supervision — it does not propose the City weigh in on TDSB's financial management or governance structure itself, which is outside municipal authority.

Action

City Council passes a motion directing staff to formally request that the Ministry-appointed TDSB supervisor and the Ministry of Education confirm, in writing, that existing shared-use agreements, Community Use of Schools access, and City-funded after-school programming operating on TDSB property will continue uninterrupted during the supervision period, and that the City be given a clear institutional point of contact for these operational matters given the suspension of ordinary trustee-level engagement.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: pass a Council motion and direct staff to make the request — squarely within the City's own institutional advocacy authority and its existing administrative relationship with TDSB over shared facilities [NEW-2026-6].
  • City demands of Province: written confirmation from the Ministry of Education / the Ministry-appointed supervisor that existing City-TDSB shared-use and community-programming arrangements continue during supervision, and a named institutional (not necessarily individual) point of contact for the City's ongoing facilities relationship.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible (staff time to draft and route a Council motion and a formal request letter), anchored to the general order of magnitude of any routine intergovernmental-relations motion the City already produces through its existing government-relations function; no new program or facility cost involved.

Funding path

Existing City Manager's Office / intergovernmental affairs operating budget; no new funding required, since this is a routine advocacy/correspondence action within existing staff capacity.

Who benefits, and how

Families and children participating in City-run after-school programming and Community Use of Schools access on TDSB property, via continuity assurance during a governance transition the City itself has no control over [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-6]; City staff administering these programs, via a clear point of contact replacing the now-unavailable ordinary trustee channel.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via negligible staff time within existing government-relations operations; no other payer class identified.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder — the backgrounder's own Cui Bono table is empty, with explanation (see backgrounder Cui Bono section). This card's own problem is a governance-continuity gap, not an extraction pattern with an identifiable beneficiary.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Range: Negligible cost, negligible-to-no direct fiscal offset; this is a continuity/risk-management measure, not a program with a fiscal-offset case. Comparator source: General order of magnitude of routine City intergovernmental-relations correspondence, which the City already produces at no incremental budget line in ordinary operations; no specific cited unit cost located. Confidence: Medium — the action itself (a motion plus a letter) is a well-understood, low-variance City process even without a specific cost figure.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: Not yet estimable. Comparator source: None identified; this is a governance-continuity request, not an economic intervention, and no source located in this pass models economic impact for a comparable action. Confidence: Low — genuine gap, not computed from anything real.

(c) Social ROI

Range: Directional only — plausible reduction in disruption risk to City-run after-school and community-use programming for families currently relying on it, given the documented suspension of ordinary trustee-level board engagement [NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-2]. Comparator source: None identified for a quantified version of this specific continuity-risk scenario; stated as directional based on the documented governance change itself. Confidence: Low-medium — the underlying governance disruption is well-documented; the magnitude of any actual risk to programming continuity is this card's own reasonable inference, not confirmed by any source reporting an actual disruption to shared-use programming to date.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely environmentally neutral — a Council motion and correspondence action has no physical footprint. Comparator source: None needed; stated directly per the template's allowance for a genuinely neutral action. Confidence: High — no plausible environmental effect.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-1 · quote-context (this pass) · TDSB provincial supervision, direct fetch
  • NEW-2026-2 · quote-context (this pass) · trustees sidelined, no board meetings (WebSearch-discovered)
  • NEW-2026-6 · quote-context (this pass) · City-TDSB shared-use agreements and Community Use of Schools

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence this is squarely within municipal authority (an advocacy motion plus a facilities-continuity request, not a governance intervention). Low confidence on whether any actual disruption to shared-use programming has occurred or is likely — this pass found no source reporting an actual disruption, so this card is precautionary/continuity-focused rather than responsive to a documented problem. This card deliberately does not comment on, or take a position on, the TDSB supervision decision itself, which is outside this leaf's scope and outside municipal authority.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming whether any actual disruption to City-TDSB shared-use programming has occurred (not found in this pass); Lane R pass; standard Council-motion drafting process.
