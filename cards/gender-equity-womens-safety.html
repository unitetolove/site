RC-GEWS-01 — Publish the Gender Equity Strategy's Actual Implementation Status

Card id: RC-GEWS-01 · File: research/cards/gender-equity-womens-safety.md#rc-gews-01 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: gender-equity-womens-safety · Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/gender-equity-womens-safety.md Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (anchored to this pass's discovery gap, not the inherited master briefing doc alone) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The backgrounder's own live discovery could not confirm whether Toronto's Gender Equity Strategy — whose own first-term action plan was reported as scoped to run 2023-2026 — was ever formally adopted by Council or has a published implementation status [NEW-2026-GE-4]. With that first-term window closing in 2026, the City either has an implementation record this pass's search-based discovery simply failed to locate, or a strategy whose own reported timeline is lapsing without a public account of what it delivered. This card addresses the transparency gap itself, not a guess at which of those two is true.

Action

Direct the Gender Equity Unit to publish a single, public, dated status report accounting for the Gender Equity Strategy's 2023-2026 first-term action plan — what was adopted, what was delivered, what gender-responsive-budgeting mechanism (if any) is currently operating — ahead of or alongside whatever comes next for the Strategy, so the public record matches what this leaf's own research was unable to confirm existed.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: publishing a status report on the City's own existing unit and strategy is entirely within existing municipal authority and administrative capacity — no new authority is required.
  • City demands of Province: none identified as required for this specific action; not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required for this specific action; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low, likely well under one million CAD — this is a reporting-transparency action within an existing unit's own mandate, not a new program. No specific City-stated figure for a status-report exercise was located in this pass; this range is this document's own extrapolation from the modest scale of comparable municipal reporting functions, flagged as such rather than cited to a specific source.

Funding path

Within the existing Gender Equity Unit's own operating budget; no new funding mechanism is required.

Who benefits, and how

Toronto residents and civil-society organizations (WomanACT, METRAC, and others named in the inherited backgrounder) who currently cannot verify from the public record what the Strategy has actually delivered; City Council itself, via a factual basis for deciding what comes after the current action-plan window closes.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the Gender Equity Unit's existing operating budget; opportunity cost is negligible given the action's administrative scale.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder — the backgrounder's own Cui Bono table came up empty; this card does not manufacture one. If anything, the status quo (an unclear public record) primarily serves institutional inertia rather than any identifiable financial beneficiary.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Range: not independently modeled; a transparency/reporting action of this scale has no plausible direct fiscal cost or saving beyond existing staff time. Comparator source: none identified — a live-discovery search this pass did not locate a study costing municipal equity-strategy status reporting specifically. Confidence: low — genuine gap, not computed from anything real, though the action's low scale makes this a low-stakes gap.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified yet. Confidence: low — no plausible mechanism for a broader economic effect from a status-reporting action alone.

(c) Social ROI

Range: directional only — a clear public account of what a multi-year equity strategy has delivered is a precondition for any of this leaf's other findings (the femicide-tracking gap, the CEDAW follow-up) being actionable at the municipal level; this card does not claim the reporting itself changes outcomes, only that it is a prerequisite for anyone — including this project — to assess whether the Strategy is working. Comparator source: this backgrounder's own discovery gap, cited as the motivating finding. Confidence: low-medium — the logic is sound but the actual social value depends entirely on what the (currently unknown) status report would reveal.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely environmentally neutral — this action is a reporting/transparency change with no construction, land-use, or emissions mechanism. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high — no plausible environmental mechanism attaches to a status-report requirement.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-GE-4 · NEW · CBC News, Gender Equity Strategy 2023-2026 action-plan window — motivating discovery gap

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on the mechanism (a status-report requirement is concrete, low-cost, and low-risk); low confidence on any quantified ROI, since the action's value depends on information not yet public. This card is explicit that it cannot verify whether the Gender Equity Strategy is already delivering well and simply under-reported, or genuinely stalled — the point of the card is to make that distinguishable from the outside, not to presume either answer.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: whatever the City's own (currently unlocated) implementation record actually says; Lane R review is a lower priority here than for cards making fiscal claims, given the low-risk, transparency-only nature of the action.

RC-GEWS-02 — Fund METRAC's Women's Safety Audit at Renewed, Named Scale

Card id: RC-GEWS-02 · File: research/cards/gender-equity-womens-safety.md#rc-gews-02 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: gender-equity-womens-safety · Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/gender-equity-womens-safety.md Trust class: CONTEXTUAL (combines the inherited METRAC/Vienna case with this pass's CEDAW-sourced targeting finding) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Toronto invented the Women's Safety Audit (METRAC, 1989, first applied to the TTC in 1994), a participatory safe-design method since adopted internationally, per the inherited backgrounder. Meanwhile, Canada's most recent (2024) CEDAW review specifically flagged that "refugee, asylum-seeking and migrant women and girls face intersecting forms of discrimination and disproportionate levels of gender-based violence" [NEW-2026-GE-6] — a population this leaf's own inherited document does not single out in its safety-audit discussion specifically. This card addresses funding the City's own tool at renewed scale, explicitly incorporating that current international finding into who the audits prioritize.

Action

Fund a renewed, citywide Women's Safety Audit program, administered in partnership with METRAC (the tool's own originating organization, cited by name per this leaf's VOICE CARE instruction), with an explicit outreach and translation component targeting neighbourhoods with high concentrations of refugee, asylum-seeking, and newcomer women, directly responsive to the CEDAW Committee's own 2024 finding rather than a generic "all women" design exercise.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: commissioning and funding a safety-audit program on City-controlled public space (transit stops, parks, sightlines, lighting) is within existing municipal authority; Toronto has already done this once (the 1994 TTC audit).
  • City demands of Province: the deepest GBV response levers (justice, health) remain provincial per the inherited document's own framing; this card does not ask the Province for anything specific to the audit mechanism itself, since it is a municipal design/planning tool, not a justice or health intervention.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required for this specific action; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low millions CAD, anchored to the general scale of comparable municipal public-realm safety-audit and lighting/sightline improvement programs (not a specific City-stated figure for this exact program) — this pass did not locate a current, City-stated budget figure for a renewed audit program at citywide scale, and this range is this document's own extrapolation, flagged as such rather than sourced.

Funding path

Most naturally layered onto existing Parks, Forestry and Recreation and Transportation Services capital/lighting-improvement budget lines, with the audit/outreach component funded through the Gender Equity Unit's own operating budget or a CPIP-style community-partnership grant to METRAC directly; no new funding mechanism is invented here, but no specific current City budget line was confirmed as already covering this exact program.

Who benefits, and how

Women and gender-diverse Torontonians using public space and transit generally, via the audit's design-improvement mechanism (lighting, sightlines, request-stop programs, per the inherited document's Vienna-comparator framing); refugee, asylum-seeking, and newcomer women specifically, via the outreach/translation component responsive to CEDAW's own 2024 finding [NEW-2026-GE-6].

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the relevant capital and operating budget lines; opportunity cost is the marginal capacity not directed to other public-realm safety or capital priorities.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder — the backgrounder's own Cui Bono table came up empty; this card does not manufacture one.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Range: not independently modeled; the inherited document's general "GBV is expensive when unaddressed" cost-avoidance argument applies directionally, not quantified for this specific program. Comparator source: none identified yet — a live-discovery search this pass did not locate a study quantifying fiscal offset from a municipal safety-audit program specifically, as distinct from broader public-realm lighting/safety capital spending. Confidence: low — genuine gap, not computed from anything real.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified yet. Confidence: low — flagged as a genuine gap; a future pass should look for research on public-realm safety-design economic effects (e.g., transit ridership or local-business-activity impacts of improved perceived safety) before inventing a figure.

(c) Social ROI

Range: directional only — the inherited document's own citation of the Women's Safety Audit as an internationally-adopted model, paired with Vienna's gender-mainstreaming precedent, supports a plausible mechanism (safer-feeling public space increases women's access to jobs, education, and public life, per the inherited document's own framing); this card does not claim an independently quantified effect size for a renewed Toronto program specifically. Comparator source: inherited master briefing's own METRAC/Vienna framing, cited as-is, not independently re-verified with a fresh primary-source fetch in this pass. Confidence: low-medium — a real, historically-grounded tool with plausible logic, not an independently evaluated renewed program.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: modest and likely small positive — improved lighting and public-realm design can have minor co-benefits (e.g., LED lighting upgrades over legacy fixtures) but this is incidental to the action's safety purpose, not a primary environmental case. Comparator source: none identified yet quantifying this specific incidental effect — a live-discovery search this pass found general municipal lighting-upgrade sustainability practice but nothing modeling this program's specific marginal footprint. Confidence: low — the "modest positive" framing is a plausible plain read, not independently modeled.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-GE-6 · NEW · UN CEDAW Committee meeting summary on Canada, October 2024 — migrant/refugee/undocumented women GBV concern, motivating the targeting design
  • INHERITED · this leaf's inherited master briefing (gender equity womens safety) · METRAC Women's Safety Audit origin, Vienna gender-mainstreaming comparator, cited as-is

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on the mechanism (reviving a City-invented, internationally-adopted tool is a concrete and precedented ask); low confidence on any quantified ROI dimension, since no independent outcome evaluation of a renewed citywide audit program was located in this pass. The card takes a position — that CEDAW's 2024 finding should shape the audit's targeting — that the neutral backgrounder itself does not take; this resolution is stated explicitly here per L6 template discipline, not smuggled into backgrounder prose. Per the inherited document's own honest caution, this card's action must be read alongside the community-safety and police-accountability leaves' own warning that "safety" agendas can be misused toward surveillance or over-policing — this card is scoped to design/lighting/audit methodology, not enforcement, and should not be read as endorsing an enforcement-based safety mechanism.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: a current, City-stated budget figure for a renewed audit program at citywide scale; a directly-fetched primary-source evaluation of Vienna's own program outcomes; Lane R review given the card touches both GBV-sensitive framing and newcomer/migrant-specific targeting.
