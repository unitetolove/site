Functional Zero Case Studies — Playbook

Two Canadian cities claimed to end chronic homelessness — the full, honest record of what actually happened next.

DRAFTThe playbookThe evidence file

What Toronto's homelessness debate gets right and wrong about "functional zero" — each correction with its costs, its beneficiaries, and its receipts.

v2.0 · 2026-08-11

---

The honest bottom line

Medicine Hat became the first Canadian city to hit "functional zero" chronic homelessness in June 2021; by September 2022 that had reversed to 19 people chronically homeless with roughly 150 cycling through the count [CL-175, CL-176]. The count kept moving after that: 120 in 2022, then down to 104 in 2024, then back up to 136 in the October 2025 count — and only 33 of that 136 were counted outdoors, the rest in emergency shelters, transitional housing, or in custody at the local remand centre [NEW-2026-1]. London hit functional zero for veteran homelessness in February 2021, exceeded the threshold for six months in 2022 because the system got better at finding veterans who were already homeless rather than because more became homeless, and got back under threshold by October 2022 — nothing found in this review shows what happened after. The bigger finding: Built for Zero, the US organization that invented "functional zero" as a measurable target, formally retired it in 2025-2026, citing exactly the failure mode Medicine Hat and London each lived through — a single numeric threshold that's hard to hit in large systems and that can look fake when encampments persist elsewhere in the city even after one population clears the bar [NEW-2026-2]. Of every community that ever chased the target, only three — Rockford, Illinois; Bergen County, New Jersey; and Abilene, Texas — ever hit it for both veterans and the chronically homeless.

Use Built for Zero's current framework, not the retired "functional zero" target, for anything forward-looking. (a recommendation card) Built for Zero replaced the single numeric threshold with a broader standard — progress toward homelessness being "rare, brief, nonrecurring, safe, and believable" — verified through a new process, adopted in late 2025, that forces a community to say explicitly whether a reported improvement is a real change on the ground or just better data collection catching people who were already there [NEW-2026-2]. This is a citation-discipline action internal to this project's own writing, not a City program: any Toronto-facing document, this project's own included, that still cites "functional zero" as a current target is using a standard its own author has moved past.

Cite Medicine Hat's full multi-year trend, not a single flattering or damning data point. (a recommendation card) The count moved from 19 chronically homeless at the 2022 reversal to 120 (2022), 104 (2024), and 136 (2025) [CL-176, NEW-2026-1] — a pattern that supports neither a clean "reversed and staying bad" story nor a "recovering" one. This project's own future citations state the full series and distinguish the roughly 33 people counted outdoors from the 136 total, rather than letting either number stand in for the whole picture.

Neither card is a lever Toronto's City government can pull: both name this project's own writing/citation practice as the actor, not a public body, per this file's own D1 ruling — a distinction worth stating plainly rather than dressing an internal fix up as a municipal ask. What happened in London after October 2022, and whether Medicine Hat's 2025 count keeps rising, is not settled by either card, and this file says so rather than guessing.

a recommendation card — Retire "Functional Zero" Language in Favour of Built for Zero's Current Multi-Dimensional Framework

Card id: a recommendation card · Issue: homelessness-functional-zero-case-studies · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: New load-bearing findings (backgrounder NEW-2026-2) · Card class: INTERNAL

Problem

"Functional zero" remains in common civic and advocacy usage in Canada as a target/aspiration term (including in this page’s own carried-forward material's framing), but Built for Zero — the organization that originated and defined the term — formally retired it as an active designation in 2025-2026, citing practical limits in large urban systems and confusion when visible encampments persisted alongside a technical "functional zero" designation for one population [NEW-2026-2]. Any Toronto-facing document, this project's own included, that cites "functional zero" as a still-current target risks using a standard its own author has moved past.

Action

This project's own writing — backgrounders, briefs, day-one papers, any future Toronto-facing recommendation — uses Built for Zero's current framing, progress toward homelessness being "rare, brief, nonrecurring, safe, and believable," measured via "verified reductions," rather than "functional zero," when describing a forward-looking target. "Functional zero" is used only in its historical sense, describing what Medicine Hat, London, Rockford, and others achieved under the now-retired standard.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: if Toronto ever adopts or references a Built for Zero-style target, doing so under the organization's current framework rather than the retired one is within the City's own communications/planning discretion — no external approval required.
  • City demands of Province: none — this is a terminology/methodology-currency question, not a funding or authority question.
  • City demands of Feds: none.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible — a documentation/terminology-discipline action, not a program or spending action.

Funding path

None required.

Who benefits, and how

Readers of this project's own research, and any Toronto policy document citing "functional zero," via accurate, current terminology that doesn't imply a still-active designation that has in fact been retired; this project's own credibility, via avoiding a citation that a hostile fact-checker could correctly flag as outdated.

Who bears the cost, and how

No payer class — negligible internal documentation-discipline cost only.

Financial ROI

Not applicable — no financial mechanism.

Economic ROI

Not applicable — no economic mechanism; confidence high on the not-applicable finding.

Social ROI

Directional: accurate terminology avoids a specific, documented failure mode Built for Zero itself named — "visible encampments, creating confusion about whether progress was real" [NEW-2026-2] — exactly the kind of credibility gap this project's own standing rule (survive hostile scrutiny) exists to prevent. Confidence: medium — the terminology-accuracy benefit is clear, but its practical effect on any reader's actual trust is not separately measured.

Environmental ROI

Not applicable — no physical or environmental component. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-2 · source quote (this review) · Built for Zero's formal retirement of functional zero, replacement framework

Confidence & uncertainties

High confidence on the underlying fact — Built for Zero's own current page states this plainly. This card's own recommendation (use current terminology going forward) is a low-stakes documentation-discipline action, not a policy recommendation requiring independent verification.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: fairness and legal review; a documentation-style-guide update for this project's own future writing (a mechanical follow-up, not a research task).

a recommendation card — Flag Medicine Hat's Non-Monotonic 2022-2025 Trend Precisely, Not as a Simple Reversal or Recovery

Card id: a recommendation card · Issue: homelessness-functional-zero-case-studies · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: New load-bearing findings (backgrounder NEW-2026-1) · Card class: INTERNAL

Problem

This page’s own carried-forward material stops at Medicine Hat's 2022 reversal — 19 chronically homeless, ~150 in-and-out [CL-176]. This review's live discovery found the count has since fluctuated further: 120 (2022), 104 (2024), 136 (2025) [NEW-2026-1] — a pattern that supports neither a clean "reversed and staying bad" narrative nor a "recovering" narrative. Any future citation of Medicine Hat's status, in this project or elsewhere, risks flattening this into whichever single data point supports the citing document's own argument.

Action

This project's own future citations of Medicine Hat state the full three-point trend (or whatever the current full series is at time of citation) rather than a single year's figure, and distinguish the roughly 33 people counted outdoors from the 136 total (which includes sheltered, transitional-housing, and in-custody populations) [NEW-2026-1], per this page’s own backgrounder discipline.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: not applicable — this is a citation-discipline action internal to this research project, not a Toronto municipal action, stated explicitly per template discipline for an empty bucket.
  • City demands of Province: none.
  • City demands of Feds: none.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible — internal citation-discipline practice.

Funding path

None required.

Who benefits, and how

Readers of this project's research, via an accurate, non-flattened picture of Medicine Hat's actual trajectory; Medicine Hat's own local stakeholders, indirectly, via not having their city's record used as a simplified prop for an argument the actual data doesn't cleanly support in either direction.

Who bears the cost, and how

No payer class — negligible internal cost only.

Financial ROI

Not applicable.

Economic ROI

Not applicable; confidence high on the not-applicable finding.

Social ROI

Directional: precise, non-flattened citation of a real city's real record is a direct application of this project's own trust rule ("anything not yet trustworthy says so on its face") — Medicine Hat's own multi-directional trend is exactly the kind of finding that should say so rather than be smoothed into a cleaner story. Confidence: medium.

Environmental ROI

Not applicable. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-1 · source quote (this review) · Medicine Hat 2025 PiT count, full three-year series and category breakdown
  • CL-175, CL-176 · carried-forward · Medicine Hat's original 2021 achievement and 2022 reversal

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium-high confidence: the three-point count series (120/104/136) is directly quoted from a single news source citing the Medicine Hat Community Housing Society's own report, not independently cross-verified against the MHCHS report itself in this review (flagged in the backgrounder's own open questions).

Status
