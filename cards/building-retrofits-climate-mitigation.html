Scale Tower Renewal (TATR/Hi-RIS) to Replace the Now-Closed Federal Retrofit Financing Gap — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-BRC-01 · File: research/cards/building-retrofits-climate-mitigation.md#rc-brc-01 Status: DRAFT Issue slug: building-retrofits-climate-mitigation · Backgrounder: building-retrofits-climate-mitigation Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (financing-gap problem statement from this pass's live-discovery findings; program mechanics from ledger VERIFY claims) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Three of the four federal/CMHC retrofit-financing programs in this leaf's evidence are confirmed closed or closing to new applicants as of mid-2026: the Canada Greener Homes Grant's intake closed in early 2024, with only a document-submission tail running to December 31, 2025 [backgrounder CL-80335, NEW-2]; CMHC's Canada Greener Affordable Housing Program's application portal is closed, having fully committed its budget [backgrounder CL-120006]; and the Oil to Heat Pump Affordability Program's application deadline (July 31, 2026) falls within weeks of this card's writing [backgrounder CL-80334]. Meanwhile, the City's own existing-building retrofit programs remain small in absolute scale relative to Toronto's total building stock — the High-Rise Retrofit Improvement Support Program had supported only 15 buildings as of a 2022 assessment, and the Home Energy Loan Program only 187 projects over 2014-2019 [backgrounder CL-80339]. This card addresses the resulting financing gap: as the federal layer winds down, the City's own TATR/Hi-RIS financing tools — which already solve the split-incentive and tenant-displacement problems other financing mechanisms don't [backgrounder CL-80340] — are positioned to absorb more of the load, but are not sized to do so at their current $11.7M/$1.7M scale [backgrounder CL-80340].

Action

Direct the City's climate/environment and housing divisions to prepare a funding-envelope expansion proposal for TATR and Hi-RIS, explicitly framed around the confirmed closure of the Canada Greener Homes Grant and CMHC's Canada Greener Affordable Housing Program, with a target of at least doubling the program's current $11.7M financing / $1.7M grant envelope, while preserving its existing tenant-protection conditions (no rent increases or renovictions tied to funded retrofit work) as a non-negotiable design feature of any expanded version.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: propose and, subject to Council approval, fund the TATR/Hi-RIS expansion using existing program administration infrastructure — this is a scale-up of an already-operating municipal program, not a new authority question.
  • City demands of Province: none required for the expansion itself; the City does not need provincial permission to expand its own retrofit financing programs. A secondary ask — that the Province consider a matching or complementary Ontario-specific retrofit financing tool, given the federal gap — is included as an advocacy ask, not a precondition.
  • City demands of Feds: that Ottawa announce a successor to the now-closed Canada Greener Homes Grant, given how central federal co-financing was to the retrofit-financing landscape this leaf's evidence describes [backgrounder, "Key tensions"] — stated as a genuine ask, not a report that one is already planned.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low tens of millions CAD to double TATR/Hi-RIS's current envelope — anchored directly to TATR's own primary-sourced $11.7M financing + $1.7M grant baseline [backgrounder CL-80340], which is the single most directly comparable, already-cited figure in this leaf's evidence for a program of this exact type and City. A precise doubled figure is not asserted beyond this order-of-magnitude range, since program administration costs, uptake rates, and per-building cost variation were not modeled in this pass.

Funding path

A blend of existing City capital budget allocation (the mechanism TATR's original $11.7M financing envelope itself likely drew on, though this pass did not confirm the specific budget line) plus continued Federation of Canadian Municipalities Green Municipal Fund grant matching, the same funding structure TATR's original $1.7M grant portion already used [backgrounder CL-80340]; the Green Municipal Fund's own GHG Impact Retrofit program (up to $10M per project, one project per municipality) [backgrounder CL-80336] is a distinct, larger-scale FCM channel the City has not confirmed using, and is named here as a funding path worth actively pursuing rather than a confirmed source.

Who benefits, and how

Tenants and owners of pre-1990 rental apartment buildings in low-income or tax-exempt-property areas, via continued and expanded access to no-upfront-cost retrofit financing with binding protection against retrofit-triggered rent increases or renovictions [backgrounder CL-80340] — the same population TATR already serves, at greater scale.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the capital budget allocation for the expanded envelope; federal taxpayers, via continued Green Municipal Fund grant matching (a pre-existing cost-sharing structure, not a new federal ask beyond what FCM funding already implies).

Financial ROI

Range: not independently modeled in this pass; the master briefing's general framing that retrofits are "not a sunk expense but an investment that pays back in bills, health, durability, and jobs" is cited only as directional context [master briefing, ⚠️ VERIFY], not a City-specific fiscal-return estimate. Comparator source: TATR's own existing $11.7M financing + $1.7M grant structure [backgrounder CL-80340] is the nearest real comparator for what an expanded program of the same design would cost per dollar of financing extended, though no source in this pass quantifies the City's own fiscal return (e.g., avoided future retrofit-driven emergency repair costs, or property-tax-base effects) from the existing TATR program. Confidence: low — a genuine gap; no City-specific fiscal-return study for TATR specifically was identified in this pass.

Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable specifically for a TATR/Hi-RIS expansion. The master briefing's general claim that retrofits are "labour-intensive and non-offshorable," implying local job creation [master briefing, ⚠️ VERIFY], is directional only. Comparator source: none identified yet specific to Toronto's own tower-retrofit program at this scale; a future pass should look for a Canadian or Ontario retrofit-sector employment-impact study (e.g., an Efficiency Canada or QUEST Canada workforce estimate) rather than relying on the master briefing's general framing. Confidence: low.

Social ROI

Directional case only: the 21% average emissions reduction achieved by the 15 buildings already retrofitted under the High-Rise Retrofit Improvement Support Program [backgrounder CL-80339] is a real, cited outcome figure for a comparable (though not identical) City retrofit program, suggesting a genuine social/environmental co-benefit case (lower energy bills, improved comfort, summer cooling per the master briefing's framing [master briefing, ⚠️ VERIFY]) — but this figure describes emissions reduction specifically, not a quantified social-wellbeing outcome, and no study quantifying tenant health, comfort, or affordability outcomes specifically from TATR (as opposed to the different, earlier High-Rise Retrofit Improvement Support Program) was identified in this pass.

Environmental ROI

Range: order-of-magnitude comparable to the Deep Retrofit Challenge's own projected ~1,750 tCO2e/year reduction across a much smaller cohort of 10-16 buildings [backgrounder CL-80333] — an expanded TATR program targeting Toronto's much larger pre-1990 rental apartment stock would plausibly exceed this on a per-dollar-financed basis, but this is a reasoned inference from the Deep Retrofit Challenge's own figures, not a figure independently modeled or sourced for TATR specifically. Comparator source: Deep Retrofit Challenge's projected emissions reduction [backgrounder CL-80333] and the High-Rise Retrofit Improvement Support Program's realized 21% average emissions reduction across 15 buildings [backgrounder CL-80339] — both real, cited City program outcomes, used here as the nearest comparators rather than an independently modeled TATR-specific projection. Confidence: medium — directional confidence is reasonable given two independently cited City program precedents, but no source directly models TATR's own emissions-reduction output at an expanded scale.

Evidence

  • CL-80340 · VERIFY · TATR mechanics, financing structure, and tenant protections
  • CL-80334, CL-80335, CL-120006 · VERIFY · federal/CMHC program closures establishing the financing-gap problem
  • CL-80336 · VERIFY · Green Municipal Fund GHG Impact Retrofit program as an alternative funding path
  • CL-80339 · VERIFY · High-Rise Retrofit Improvement Support Program's realized emissions-reduction outcome, used as an Environmental ROI comparator
  • NEW-2 · quote-context (Natural Resources Canada, accessed 2026-07-14) · confirms Greener Homes Grant intake closure

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on feasibility (TATR/Hi-RIS is an already-operating, already-successful-by-its-own-design program; expanding it is a scale question, not a new-authority question). Low confidence on precise cost and ROI figures, since no source in this pass modeled an expanded-scale version of the program specifically — all cost and ROI figures here are anchored to the existing program's own baseline scale, not independently projected. Whether the City's capital budget has room for a doubled TATR/Hi-RIS envelope without displacing other priorities was not examined in this pass.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: a City capital-budget capacity check; confirmation of whether any federal successor to Greener Homes has been announced since this card was drafted; Lane R pass.

Council Resolution: Clarify the Toronto Green Standard's Post-Bill-98 Status and Verify Better Buildings Partnership's Current Scope — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-BRC-02 · File: research/cards/building-retrofits-climate-mitigation.md#rc-brc-02 Status: DRAFT Issue slug: building-retrofits-climate-mitigation · Backgrounder: building-retrofits-climate-mitigation Trust class: CONTEXTUAL, updated with a NEW-LOAD-BEARING finding on adversarial re-check (backgrounder NEW-3) · Date: 2026-07-14 (card text updated same date after adversarial audit)

Problem

⚠️ FIX (this pass's re-verification): this card's original problem statement — framed around confirming a Toronto Green Standard Version 5 timeline — is superseded by a more urgent and more consequential finding. Ontario's Bill 98 (Building Homes and Improving Transportation Infrastructure Act, 2026; Royal Assent June 2, 2026) eliminated mandatory "sustainable design" references from provincial planning law, which the City's own Toronto Green Standard program page and independent reporting both describe as effectively ending the TGS's enforceable mandatory tier — over the City's own chief planner's on-record objection that the change "would limit Toronto's ability to advance local climate objectives" [backgrounder NEW-3]. This is not a version-numbering question (TGS Version 4's number is unchanged [backgrounder CL-80331, NEW-1]) but a question of whether the TGS has any remaining mandatory legal force at all, and if so, in what form. Separately, and still unresolved: the Better Buildings Partnership — named specifically in this leaf's own research brief as a program to verify — was not independently confirmed against a primary source in this pass despite being a real, catalogued City program [backgrounder, "Open questions / data gaps"]. Both gaps matter because a 2022 peer-reviewed third-party assessment found Toronto not on track to meet its 2030 emissions target and concluded transformational action across all sectors was needed [backgrounder CL-80338] — a finding that makes confirming whether the City's own standards and partnership programs retain any real force, and are still active and current, a live accountability question, not a bookkeeping detail.

Action

A Toronto City Council information request (or, if the information is not already held by staff, a formal staff direction) requiring the City's Environment & Climate division and City Solicitor to report publicly and urgently: (1) the precise legal effect of Bill 98 on the Toronto Green Standard's mandatory Tier 1 floor — whether any part of it remains enforceable, through what mechanism (e.g., the Development Charge Refund Program's voluntary Tier 2-4 incentives, which per this leaf's evidence over 230 projects have already used [backgrounder, WebSearch finding on Bill 98's development-charge-refund context]), and what if anything the City intends to do to preserve climate-performance requirements within its remaining authority; and (2) a current-status report on the Better Buildings Partnership's funding envelope, number of active participating buildings, and measured outcomes to date, comparable in specificity to the outcome data already publicly reported for the High-Rise Retrofit Improvement Support Program and Home Energy Loan Program [backgrounder CL-80339].

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: commission and receive the staff report on both items; the Better Buildings Partnership question remains entirely within municipal authority.
  • City demands of Province: clarify Bill 98's precise effect on municipal sustainable-design authority in writing, and consider carving out narrow room for municipalities to set climate-performance requirements within provincial planning law — this is no longer a City-only information question, since the City's own chief planner is on record that the Province's action limits the City's authority in this area [backgrounder NEW-3].
  • City demands of Feds: none.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible direct cost — a staff information report is prepared using existing Environment & Climate division capacity, the same comparator basis used elsewhere in this corpus for bare information/reporting requests (e.g., newcomer-success-credential-recognition.md RC-NCR-02's Council-resolution cost framing), not a new program or budget line.

Funding path

Existing City of Toronto Environment & Climate division operating budget for report preparation — no new spending proposed.

Who benefits, and how

Toronto residents, City Council, and future card/backgrounder authors on this leaf, via a confirmed, current picture of whether the City's two named standard-setting/financing programs (TGS's remaining legal force post-Bill-98, Better Buildings Partnership's actual scope) are on track — directly closing the two largest named gaps in this leaf's evidence base.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the negligible staff-time cost of the report; no other payer identified.

Financial ROI

Not applicable in the standard sense — this is an information/reporting action, not a spending program. No fiscal return is claimed or modeled.

Economic ROI

Range: not applicable — an information-reporting action has no direct economic-impact pathway distinct from its (negligible) direct cost. Comparator source: none sought — the action's nature (a reporting request) makes an economic comparator unnecessary rather than merely unfound. Confidence: high — no plausible economic-impact pathway for this action type beyond its own negligible cost.

Social ROI

Directional case only: closing an information gap about whether the City's own escalating-standard and partnership-financing tools are on track is a precondition for any future, better-informed retrofit-acceleration decision — consistent with the third-party assessment's finding that the City is currently off-track for 2030 without knowing precisely why [backgrounder CL-80338] — but this is a governance/accountability benefit, not a directly measurable social-wellbeing outcome, and no source quantifies it.

Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely environmentally neutral in itself — an information report has no direct emissions, land-use, water, or waste footprint. Its value is entirely upstream of whatever future action the report's findings enable. Comparator source: none sought — the action's nature (an information/reporting request) makes an environmental comparator unnecessary rather than merely unfound. Confidence: high — no plausible direct environmental pathway for this action type.

Evidence

  • CL-80331 · VERIFY · TGS Version 4 effective date and scope
  • NEW-1 · quote-context (City of Toronto TGS page, accessed 2026-07-14) · confirms TGS v4 still current version number
  • NEW-3 · quote-context (adversarial-audit addition; City of Toronto TGS page banner notice + Corporate Knights, both accessed 2026-07-14) · Bill 98's effective end of TGS mandatory status, City chief planner's on-record objection
  • CL-80338 · VERIFY · 2022 third-party assessment finding Toronto off-track for 2030 target
  • CL-80339 · VERIFY · comparator showing the level of program-outcome detail the City has previously reported for other retrofit programs

Confidence & uncertainties

High confidence on feasibility (an information request to City staff about the City's own programs is a routine, low-friction Council action). Medium confidence that Bill 98 has materially changed the TGS's mandatory status (corroborated by the City's own program page and one independent journalism source, but this card's own drafting did not independently fetch Bill 98's primary statutory text — see backgrounder "Open questions"). Low confidence on what the Better Buildings Partnership report will actually find — this card does not assume or predict the program is behind schedule or underfunded, only that the current public evidence base (including this leaf's own backgrounder) cannot currently confirm its status, which is itself the gap this card closes.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming Bill 98's precise operative text and legal effect on TGS Tier 1 directly (not yet independently fetched in this pass); confirming whether Better Buildings Partnership information already exists in a City staff report not yet identified in this pass; Lane R pass.
