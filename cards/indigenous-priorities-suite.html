RC-IPS-01 — Toronto Reconciliation Action Plan: Publish an Action-by-Action Completion Tracker

Card id: RC-IPS-01 · File: research/cards/indigenous-priorities-suite.md#rc-ips-01 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: indigenous-priorities-suite · Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/indigenous-priorities-suite.md Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (anchored to this pass's live-discovery comparator finding, not the inherited master briefing doc alone) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Toronto's Reconciliation Action Plan (2022-2032) commits to 28 actions across five themes, developed over three years with direct First Nations, Inuit, and Métis community, Elder, and youth participation [CL-0221, CL-0222, both VERIFIED]. The backgrounder's live discovery in this pass found no City-published, action-by-action delivery tracker for Toronto's own 28 actions comparable to what exists for a smaller Ontario comparator: the City of Ottawa's 2018 Reconciliation Action Plan (14 actions, four areas), where an independently VERIFIED claim already states that "as of 2025, all actions... were marked as completed or embedded into City practices" [CL-0224, CL-0225]. This is a transparency-mechanism gap, not a claim that Toronto's Plan has failed to deliver — no source located in this pass either confirms or denies Toronto's own delivery status [backgrounder, "Key tensions / tradeoffs"].

Action

The City publishes a public, action-by-action status accounting for all 28 Reconciliation Action Plan actions (a status ledger comparable in kind to Ottawa's own public completion accounting for its 14 actions [CL-0225]), updated on a defined, recurring schedule, through the existing Indigenous Affairs Office's reporting function [CL-0223, VERIFIED]. This card proposes the transparency mechanism only — it does not propose, characterize, or take a position on what any of the 28 actions' content should be, per DESIGN.md §7's standing rule that this lane may recommend City transparency/delivery mechanisms only, never speak for Indigenous priorities.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: publishing a status tracker for a City-authored, City-published plan is entirely within existing municipal authority and the Indigenous Affairs Office's existing reporting mandate [CL-0223, VERIFIED] — no provincial or federal authorization is required.
  • City demands of Province: none identified for this specific action; not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified for this specific action; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low, likely well under one million CAD annually — this is a reporting/publication mechanism (a public status page or annual report addendum), not a program-delivery cost, anchored to the fact that Ottawa's own comparable 14-action tracker already exists as a precedent within the same tier of Ontario municipal government [CL-0225] at what this card assumes, without a specific cited City of Ottawa administrative-cost figure, to be existing staff-reporting capacity rather than a major new expenditure. No specific City of Toronto cost figure for this exact mechanism was located in this pass — flagged as an estimate, not a sourced figure.

Funding path

Existing Indigenous Affairs Office operating budget and existing annual reporting cycle [CL-0223, VERIFIED]; this is a reporting-format change, not a new program requiring new appropriation.

Who benefits, and how

Toronto residents broadly, and specifically the First Nations, Inuit, and Métis community members, Elders, Knowledge Carriers, and youth who participated in the Plan's three-year co-development process [CL-0222, VERIFIED], via a public accountability mechanism that lets the Plan's own stated commitments be checked against delivery — a transparency benefit, not a claim about what outcome the tracker would show.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the existing Indigenous Affairs Office operating budget; the marginal cost is staff time to compile and publish an existing internal status accounting in public form, not a new data-collection burden, since the Plan's 28 actions are already tracked internally by definition of being implemented actions.

Financial ROI

Range: not independently modeled — no source located in this pass quantifies the administrative cost or savings of a public status-tracker mechanism specifically. Comparator source: Ottawa's own comparable 14-action public completion tracker exists and is independently VERIFIED as operating [CL-0225], establishing feasibility at a smaller (14-action) scale within the same province, though no cost figure for operating it was located. Confidence: low — feasibility is well-evidenced by the Ottawa comparator; cost is not.

Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified — a live-discovery search this pass did not locate any study modeling the economic impact of a municipal reconciliation-plan transparency mechanism specifically. Confidence: low — genuine gap, not computed from anything real.

Social ROI

Directional only: a public, checkable completion tracker is the specific, nameable mechanism this leaf's own "Key tensions" section identifies as the concrete difference between Toronto's and Ottawa's current accountability posture [backgrounder, "Key tensions / tradeoffs"] — a transparency mechanism, not a claim about whether the Plan's substantive actions themselves are the right ones, which this card does not evaluate. Comparator source: Ottawa's own operating tracker [CL-0225]. Confidence: low-medium — the mechanism's existence-and-feasibility is well-evidenced; its effect on public trust or Indigenous-community confidence in the Plan is not measured by any source in this pass.

Environmental ROI

Range: none — this action is a reporting/publication mechanism with no construction, land-use, or resource component. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high — no plausible environmental mechanism attaches to a public status-report publication.

Evidence

  • CL-0221 · VERIFIED · Toronto Reconciliation Action Plan structure (2022-2032, 28 actions, five themes)
  • CL-0222 · VERIFIED · Toronto Reconciliation Action Plan's three-year co-development process
  • CL-0223 · VERIFIED · Indigenous Affairs Office's existing reporting/support function
  • CL-0224 · VERIFIED · City of Ottawa Reconciliation Action Plan structure (2018, 14 actions, four areas)
  • CL-0225 · VERIFIED · City of Ottawa Reconciliation Action Plan completion status (2025) — the comparator this card's Action is modeled on

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on feasibility (a directly comparable municipal precedent exists and is independently verified operating); low confidence on cost, since no City of Toronto or City of Ottawa administrative-cost figure for the tracker mechanism itself was located in this pass. This card does not take a position on whether Toronto's 28 actions are on track — only that the transparency mechanism to let that question be answered publicly does not yet appear to exist for Toronto specifically, per the backgrounder's own live-discovery gap.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: a City of Toronto-sourced figure for the administrative cost of the proposed tracker, and confirmation (not located in this pass) of whether an internal, non-public version of this tracking already exists that could be republished rather than newly built.

RC-IPS-02 — Log a Standing Action Item for Direct TICAB/TASSC Review of Indigenous-Related Corpus Content

Card id: RC-IPS-02 · File: research/cards/indigenous-priorities-suite.md#rc-ips-02 · Status: DRAFT · Card class: INTERNAL Issue slug: indigenous-priorities-suite · Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/indigenous-priorities-suite.md Trust class: CONTEXTUAL (a process/governance action about this project's own practice, not a City-facing recommendation) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Per research/indigenous/DESIGN.md §7 (the honest-limitation statement, carried into this document as required): this lane is curated by a non-Indigenous-led project, and no Indigenous person has reviewed this design document, the seed atlas, or any content this lane has produced as of this writing. DESIGN.md §7 names TICAB and TASSC as the two bodies with clearest standing to review the sibling homelessness-indigenous-overrepresentation document's framing specifically, and states that any future move toward direct outreach "should be logged as a standing action item, not left as a one-time suggestion inside a single document's prose." This card exists to satisfy exactly that instruction for this leaf.

Action

Log, in this project's own standing tracking mechanism (not a City-facing ask), a persistent action item: seek direct review of this leaf's backgrounder and any derived cards/briefs/day-one materials by TICAB and/or TASSC (or another Indigenous-governed body with standing on urban Indigenous priorities in Toronto specifically), before any public claim is made that this leaf's content represents Indigenous perspectives rather than a non-Indigenous-led pointer toward Indigenous-stated sources. This is an internal project-governance action, not a recommendation to the City of Toronto, and is included here because DESIGN.md §7 requires it to be logged as standing rather than left implicit.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: not applicable — this action is internal to this research project, not a municipal-government ask.
  • City demands of Province: not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: not a monetary cost in the sense this template otherwise anchors — the cost is the operator/project's own outreach time and, if review is granted, whatever honorarium or compensation practice is appropriate for the reviewing body's time (a specific figure is not estimated here, since no outreach has occurred yet).

Funding path

Not applicable in the City-budget sense; if honorarium compensation for reviewer time becomes appropriate, that would draw on this project's own operating resources, not a City or government funding line.

Who benefits, and how

This project's own readers and any downstream user of this leaf's content, via a future review that would let this corpus honestly upgrade its own framing (from "non-Indigenous-authored pointer" to "reviewed by named Indigenous-governed bodies") if and when that review occurs — not a benefit to Indigenous people or organizations themselves, who are not the addressee of this internal action item.

Who bears the cost, and how

This project itself (operator/executor time), not any government or taxpayer entity.

Financial ROI

Not applicable — this is a project-governance action, not a fiscal one.

Economic ROI

Not applicable.

Social ROI

Directional: closing the gap DESIGN.md §7 names as a standing, unresolved limitation is itself the outcome sought — not quantifiable, but the specific, correct next step per this project's own binding design document. Comparator source: DESIGN.md §7 itself, which names this exact action as the standing requirement. Confidence: high confidence that this is the correct next step per the project's own binding rules; no confidence claim is made about whether or when review would actually occur, which depends on the named bodies' own capacity and willingness.

Environmental ROI

Not applicable — no environmental mechanism attaches to a review-outreach action item.

Evidence

  • INHERITED · research/indigenous/DESIGN.md §7 · the honest-limitation statement and its explicit instruction to log future outreach as a standing action item
  • INHERITED · research/leaves/homelessness-indigenous-overrepresentation/ · the sibling document naming TICAB and TASSC as the two bodies with clearest standing to review Indigenous-related framing

Confidence & uncertainties

This card is process-governance, not a substantive policy recommendation, and should not be read as claiming TICAB or TASSC have agreed to, or would agree to, any review — no outreach has occurred as of this document's date. Its only claim is that DESIGN.md §7 requires this action item to exist in standing, logged form, and this card is that log entry rendered in this leaf's card format.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: this project's own orchestration layer actually initiating outreach to TICAB and/or TASSC; until that occurs, this card remains a logged intention, not a completed or in-progress review.
