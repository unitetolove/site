a recommendation card — Commission a Current TPL Value/ROI Refresh, Anchored to the 2025-2026 Hours-Expansion Outcome Data

Card id: a recommendation card · Status: DRAFT · Backgrounder: our research file for that page Provenance: New load-bearing findings (anchored to directly-fetched 2026 TPL outcome data and a confirmed gap) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

Toronto's most-cited library value-for-money figure — $5.63 in value per $1 invested, ~$1 billion in total economic impact — comes from a 2013 Martin Prosperity Institute study that the backgrounder's inherited master briefing itself already flags as "due for a refresh." This review's own search confirms no superseding study has been produced in the years since. Meanwhile, TPL's own 2025-2026 data shows demonstrably rising usage (a 50% Sunday-visit increase and 54% Sunday-borrowing increase from a single quarter's hours expansion, a 44% visit increase from the 2024 expansion) [see backgrounder]. The library's usage case is getting stronger while its economic-value case is now over a decade stale — a growing mismatch this card addresses directly.

Action

Commission an updated Toronto library ROI/economic-value study, using the same or a comparable methodology to the 2013 Martin Prosperity Institute study, explicitly incorporating the 2025-2026 hours-expansion outcome data as a natural experiment (a measurable before/after usage shift tied to a specific, dated policy change) rather than starting from a generic usage baseline.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: commissioning a study is within existing TPL Board / City budget authority — a research-procurement decision, not requiring new legislative power.
  • City demands of Province: none identified as required; not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low, anchored to typical municipal economic-impact study costs (generally low-to-mid tens of thousands of dollars for a study of this type and scale, based on general knowledge of comparable municipal social-infrastructure ROI studies; no specific Toronto-current comparator cost figure was located in this review, flagged as an estimate rather than a sourced figure) — small relative to TPL's own multi-hundred-million-dollar operating budget (per the sibling local-services-libraries-animal-control page’s own scope).

Funding path

Existing TPL operating or research budget line, or a partnership with a local academic institution (as the original 2013 study was itself an academic partnership with the Martin Prosperity Institute, then housed at the University of Toronto) — a precedented mechanism, not a new one.

Who benefits, and how

City Council and budget decision-makers, via a current, defensible value-for-money figure to weigh against competing budget priorities, replacing a decade-plus-old figure with a current one; TPL itself, via a stronger evidentiary basis for defending its own budget in the boom-bust funding cycle the master briefing names as the sector's central risk; the public, via transparent, current information about what their library investment actually returns.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, negligibly, via the existing TPL operating/research budget; opportunity cost is the marginal research budget not directed elsewhere.

Financial ROI

Not applicable in the conventional sense — this card is itself a measurement/research action, not a spending program with its own direct financial return. Indirectly protective: a current ROI figure could strengthen the case for continued or expanded library investment in future budget cycles, though this card does not assert a specific dollar outcome from commissioning the study itself.

Economic ROI

Range: not applicable to the study-commissioning action itself; the output of the study (a refreshed dollar-per-dollar ROI figure) is what this card is designed to produce, not a pre-existing figure this card can cite. Comparator source: the 2013 study's own $5.63-per-$1 figure, cited as the historical baseline the new study would update, not as a current comparator. Confidence: not applicable to the action itself — the action's entire purpose is producing the missing figure.

Social ROI

Directional: a current, credible ROI figure plausibly strengthens the public and political case for library investment as core social infrastructure, per the master briefing's own "making the value visible" framing, though this is not independently quantified by any source in this review. Confidence: low-medium — directional logic only.

Environmental ROI

Range: not applicable — no environmental mechanism attaches to commissioning a research study. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high — no plausible environmental mechanism.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-1 · NEW · TPL, "City Council approves 2026 budget to expand library hours and services" (Feb 11, 2026) — hours-expansion outcome data
  • NEW-2026-2 · NEW (confirmed absence) · No superseding ROI study located in this review
  • carried-forward · this page’s carried-forward master briefing (libraries civic commons) · 2013 ROI figure and its own flagged refresh need, cited as-is

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium-high confidence on the problem statement (the 2013 figure's staleness is directly confirmed by both the master briefing's own text and this review's independent search); the card's own proposed action is standard, low-risk municipal research practice with clear precedent (the original study itself). The main uncertainty is what a refreshed figure would actually show — this card does not assume the new figure would necessarily be as favourable as the 2013 one, and states that explicitly rather than assuming a predetermined favourable outcome.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming whether TPL or the City has any current, unpublished, or in-progress ROI refresh effort already underway, which this review's search did not surface but cannot rule out.

a recommendation card — Formalize TPL's "Shared Community Spaces" Priority as an Explicit Civic-Commons Coordination Point with Parks, Forestry and Recreation

Card id: a recommendation card · Status: DRAFT · Backgrounder: our research file for that page Provenance: CONTEXTUAL (combines a search-confirmed TPL priority with the master briefing's own "invest in the whole commons" recommendation) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The master briefing's own bottom-line synthesis calls for treating "libraries, recreation, parks, and public space together as the shared civic-commons backbone" of the city, but names this review's own discovery confirms only a partial, TPL-internal version of this exists today: TPL's own "shared community spaces" strategic priority, aimed at creating "more welcoming, safe, inclusive and sustainable multi-use environments that build resilience and belonging in every neighbourhood" [search-confirmed this review, not independently fetched in full text — see backgrounder]. This review's own search did not locate a single, formally-branded, cross-divisional "civic commons" strategy bundling TPL with Parks, Forestry and Recreation under one coordinated policy umbrella, consistent with the backgrounder's own honest finding that no clearly-named comparator exists for this either internationally or, seemingly, within Toronto's own current governance structure.

Action

Formalize a standing coordination mechanism between TPL's "shared community spaces" priority and Parks, Forestry and Recreation's own free-recreation-centre access work (the same division whose 2025 early-registration pilot lifted participation from 24% to 67%, per the connection-community-belonging page’s own independently-sourced finding, cross-referenced here rather than re-derived) — a joint planning table or shared reporting mechanism, not a merger of the two divisions.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: entirely within existing City divisional coordination authority — TPL and Parks, Forestry and Recreation are both City bodies (TPL via its arm's-length Board); establishing a joint coordination mechanism requires no new legislative authority.
  • City demands of Province: none identified; not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible — a coordination/governance mechanism using existing staff capacity in both divisions, not a new program or capital cost.

Funding path

Absorbed within existing TPL and Parks, Forestry and Recreation administrative capacity; no new funding mechanism required.

Who benefits, and how

Residents in amenity-gap neighbourhoods, via better-coordinated (rather than independently-planned) library and recreation-centre access improvements, avoiding a scenario where one division's amenity-gap targeting misses a neighbourhood the other division's own data would have flagged; both divisions themselves, via shared learning (e.g., TPL's own hours-expansion outcome data could inform Parks' own access-reform planning, and vice versa).

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, negligibly, via existing administrative capacity in both divisions.

Financial ROI

Not applicable in a quantified sense — this is a coordination mechanism, not a spending program with its own direct financial return.

Economic ROI

Range: not applicable. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high (no plausible economic mechanism, positive or negative, attaches to inter-divisional coordination itself).

Social ROI

Directional: better-coordinated civic-commons planning plausibly reduces the risk of amenity gaps being addressed inconsistently across the library and recreation systems, consistent with the master briefing's own "invest in the whole commons" recommendation, though this is not independently quantified. Confidence: low — directional logic only, no source quantifies the specific benefit of formal inter-divisional coordination versus the status quo.

Environmental ROI

Range: not applicable. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high — no plausible environmental mechanism.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-3 · NEW (cross-referenced) · connection-community-belonging.md's own NEW-2026-3 finding, Parks/Forestry/Recreation 24%→67% early-registration result
  • NEW-2026-5 · NEW (search-confirmed) · TPL's own "shared community spaces" strategic priority
  • carried-forward · this page’s carried-forward master briefing (libraries civic commons) · "invest in the whole commons" recommendation, cited as-is

Confidence & uncertainties

Low-medium confidence — this card's underlying logic (coordination is plausibly beneficial) is intuitive but not independently evidenced by any source located in this review; no study found measures whether Toronto's library and recreation systems currently plan amenity-gap responses in a coordinated or siloed way, so this card cannot state with confidence how large a problem the status quo actually represents. This card resolves toward proposing the coordination mechanism on the strength of the master briefing's own recommendation rather than new independent evidence, and states that explicitly.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming whether an inter-divisional coordination mechanism of this kind already exists in some form that this review's search did not surface.
