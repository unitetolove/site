Immigration, Mutual Learning, and Integration — Playbook

Integration doesn't have to mean one-way assimilation — what a genuinely two-way model with newcomers looks like.

DRAFTThe playbookThe evidence file

What Toronto can actually do on two-way intercultural integration — each move with its costs, its beneficiaries, and its receipts.

v2.0 · 2026-08-11

---

The honest bottom line

Most of the time, when a city talks about "integrating" newcomers, the question underneath is one-directional: how do they adapt to us? There's a real, internationally used alternative: the Council of Europe's Intercultural Cities Programme, running since 2008, now well over 160 member cities and regions, with a structured self-assessment tool cities can use to check their own progress rather than just declaring victory. Toronto, on the evidence gathered here, hasn't formally engaged it — even though six Quebec municipalities, including Montreal, already have, coordinated through a network called RÉMIRI since 2021. Toronto already brands itself around diversity and runs real newcomer programming (a Newcomer Office, credential-mentoring partnerships), but that programming is oriented toward helping newcomers adapt here, not toward structuring exchange in both directions — different things, and conflating them would be dishonest.

Assess Toronto's fit for the Council of Europe's Intercultural Cities Index. (a recommendation card) Before recommending Toronto join anything, the honest first step is a scoping question: what would participation actually involve, and what can Toronto learn from how Montreal and the other Quebec member cities (Sherbrooke, Drummondville, Gatineau, Repentigny, Rimouski) approached it? Non-European membership is already confirmed open — Canada, Australia, Japan, Mexico, and others already participate — so that part is no longer a live uncertainty; this card is a small, low-cost piece of homework, not a commitment.

Pilot small, deliberately two-way exchange programming through the existing Newcomer Office. (a recommendation card) Nothing about running a handful of two-way exchange events — not a festival, not an orientation session, but something actually designed for exchange in both directions — requires new infrastructure. It requires deciding to try it, using rooms and staff time the City already has.

This is one of the more honest gaps in this research: the underlying case for "mutual learning" — that newcomers bring community and family traditions that could help a lonelier, more isolated society — is a real, coherent argument, but a conceptual one, not a proven one. The inherited briefing behind this file says so itself, calling it "largely conceptual and values-based," with far fewer hard numbers than almost every other topic in this library. Neither card claims a Toronto pilot would produce measurable results before it's tried, and neither knows whether Toronto has quietly looked at this before and decided not to pursue it, for reasons this research didn't uncover.

a recommendation card — Assess Toronto's Fit for the Council of Europe's Intercultural Cities Index — Recommendation Card

Card id: a recommendation card · Issue: immigration-mutual-learning-integration · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: New load-bearing findings (problem and mechanism from this review's independently confirmed finding)

Problem

The Council of Europe's Intercultural Cities Programme is a real, independently confirmed, 160+-city-and-region international network offering a structured self-assessment tool (the Intercultural Cities Index) for benchmarking a city's own intercultural-policy maturity [backgrounder NEW-1]. No evidence was found in this review that Toronto has ever formally engaged this specific framework, despite Toronto's own long-standing diversity-positive civic identity [backgrounder NEW-2, partial] — though six Quebec municipalities, including Montreal, are already confirmed current members [backgrounder NEW-3], showing this is a live option other Canadian cities have already exercised, not a purely hypothetical one. This represents a real, if modest, gap: a mature, internationally-used assessment tool exists for exactly the kind of "are we actually doing two-way integration, or just saying we value diversity" question this page’s inherited framing raises conceptually but cannot itself answer empirically [master briefing, explicitly conceptual/values-based].

Action

Direct the City's Social Development, Finance and Administration division to conduct a scoping assessment of Toronto's fit for, and potential value of, formal engagement with the Council of Europe's Intercultural Cities Index or Programme, before making any recommendation on formal membership — drawing on the directly relevant domestic precedent that six Quebec municipalities (including Montreal) are already confirmed current members via the RÉMIRI-coordinated Quebec network [backgrounder NEW-3], rather than treating non-European eligibility as an open question.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: commission the scoping assessment using existing staff capacity — entirely within municipal authority, since this is a this library's internal records step, not a binding commitment.
  • City demands of Province: none required.
  • City demands of Feds: none required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible to low tens of thousands of dollars CAD for a staff-conducted scoping assessment — anchored to the general order of magnitude of a policy-this library's internal records exercise elsewhere in this corpus (comparable to other single-division research-scoping actions), not a newly modeled figure, since no specific Intercultural Cities Index membership-fee or assessment-cost figure was identified in this review.

Funding path

Existing City of Toronto Social Development, Finance and Administration operating budget for the scoping assessment — no new program or budget line proposed at this stage.

Who benefits, and how

Toronto residents broadly, via a more rigorously benchmarked understanding of whether the City's existing diversity and settlement programming actually achieves the "two-way exchange" this page’s framing describes, rather than relying on aspiration or civic branding alone; City policy staff, via access to a structured, internationally-tested assessment methodology rather than needing to invent one.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the negligible scoping-assessment staff-time cost.

Financial ROI

Not applicable in the standard sense — a scoping assessment has no direct fiscal-return pathway; any downstream fiscal effects would depend entirely on what the assessment recommends and whether Council acts on it, which is outside this card's own scope.

Economic ROI

Not yet estimable — no source quantifies an economic-impact case for a municipal Intercultural Cities Index assessment specifically, and no comparator was identified. Confidence: low — a genuine gap.

Social ROI

Directional case only: the master briefing's own framing that newcomer traditions of community and mutual aid address documented atomization in the receiving society [master briefing, conceptual/values-based] is suggestive but not independently quantified; a structured assessment tool like the Intercultural Cities Index would, if adopted, provide a more rigorous basis for measuring this kind of claim in the future than currently exists — but this card does not claim the assessment itself produces a social-wellbeing outcome, only that it is a precondition for measuring one credibly.

Environmental ROI

Genuinely environmentally neutral — a policy scoping assessment has no material emissions, land-use, water, waste, or resilience footprint. No comparator sought; the action's nature makes one unnecessary rather than merely unfound. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-1 · source quote (Council of Europe, accessed 2026-07-14) · Intercultural Cities Programme and Index as a real, existing assessment framework
  • NEW-3 · source quote (Council of Europe, accessed 2026-07-14) · six Quebec municipalities as confirmed current Programme members, resolving Programme non-European eligibility
  • master briefing · conceptual/values-based (carried-forward) · two-way integration framing as the conceptual motivation, cited as context only, not as this card's own load-bearing evidence

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on feasibility (a scoping assessment is a low-friction, low-commitment research step). Low confidence on whether formal Intercultural Cities Programme membership would actually be recommended or valuable for Toronto specifically — this card does not prejudge that outcome, only proposes finding out. Programme membership eligibility for non-European and North American cities is now confirmed (Canada, via Quebec's RÉMIRI network, is already a participant, alongside Australia, Japan, Israel, Mexico, Morocco, and South Korea) — this is no longer an open question for the scoping assessment to resolve, though the specific accession process and any Toronto-specific fit questions remain for the assessment to determine.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: a cost comparator; fairness and legal review. (Programme membership eligibility for non-European/North American cities, previously listed as a blocker, was confirmed during this review's Verify check — see backgrounder NEW-3.)

a recommendation card — Formalize Intercultural Exchange Programming Within the City's Existing Newcomer Office / Local Immigration Partnership Structure — Recommendation Card

Card id: a recommendation card · Issue: immigration-mutual-learning-integration · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: CONTEXTUAL

Problem

Toronto's Newcomer Office and Local Immigration Partnership convening structure already exists and runs at least one direct program (Profession to Profession mentoring), per this page’s sibling backgrounder [cross-referenced from newcomer-success-credential-recognition.md NEW-3, not independently re-fetched in this review]. But no evidence was found in this review of any City program specifically branded or designed around the "mutual learning" / two-way intercultural exchange framing this page’s inherited briefing describes conceptually [master briefing, explicitly conceptual/values-based] — existing City newcomer programming, on the evidence available, is oriented toward newcomer-side service delivery (credential mentoring, settlement support) rather than the bidirectional exchange the master briefing's framing envisions. This card addresses that specific, narrow gap: using existing convening infrastructure differently, not building new infrastructure.

Action

Direct the City's existing Newcomer Office / Local Immigration Partnership convening structure to pilot a small number of formally organized intercultural exchange events (shared community programming explicitly designed for two-way exchange, distinct from newcomer-only orientation programming or general multicultural festival programming) at existing community hubs or libraries, drawing on the Council of Europe Intercultural Cities framework's own program-design guidance where available [backgrounder NEW-1] as a starting design reference rather than inventing a format from scratch.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: the pilot programming itself, using existing Newcomer Office convening capacity and existing community hub/library space — entirely within municipal authority.
  • City demands of Province: none required.
  • City demands of Feds: none required.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low tens of thousands of dollars CAD for a small pilot (a handful of events using existing venue space and existing staff convening capacity) — anchored to the general order of magnitude of a modest community-programming pilot, not a newly modeled figure, since no specific comparable Toronto program's per-event cost was identified in this review.

Funding path

Existing City of Toronto Newcomer Office / community-hub programming operating budget — no new program infrastructure or dedicated budget line proposed at pilot scale.

Who benefits, and how

Newcomers and long-settled Toronto residents participating in pilot programming, via structured opportunities for the kind of two-way exchange the master briefing's own framing describes conceptually [master briefing] — though this card is explicit that no study quantifies this specific intervention's effect, and the pilot's own primary value is generating a first, real, measurable Toronto data point rather than delivering a pre-proven outcome.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the existing Newcomer Office / community-hub programming budget allocation for the pilot.

Financial ROI

Not modeled in this review — no source quantifies a City-specific fiscal return from intercultural exchange programming.

Economic ROI

Not yet estimable — no comparator was identified specific to this program type. Confidence: low.

Social ROI

Directional case only, and explicitly modest: the master briefing's conceptual framing that intercultural exchange addresses atomization and builds social cohesion [master briefing, conceptual/values-based] is the motivating theory, not a quantified finding — this card proposes a pilot specifically because no Toronto-specific outcome data exists yet to cite, and treats generating that data as part of the pilot's own value.

Environmental ROI

Genuinely environmentally neutral — community programming using existing venue space has no material emissions, land-use, water, waste, or resilience footprint beyond ordinary venue operation, which this card does not attribute to itself as a new footprint. No comparator sought. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-1 · source quote (Council of Europe, accessed 2026-07-14) · Intercultural Cities framework as a program-design reference
  • master briefing · conceptual/values-based (carried-forward) · the two-way exchange motivating theory, cited as context only
  • Cross-referenced (not independently re-fetched this review) · newcomer-success-credential-recognition.md NEW-3 · existing Newcomer Office / Local Immigration Partnership convening structure this card would use

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence on feasibility (uses existing convening infrastructure and venue space rather than requiring new authority or capacity). Low confidence on impact, since this page’s evidence is explicitly conceptual rather than empirical — this card is honest that a pilot's primary value is generating Toronto-specific outcome data for the first time, not delivering a pre-validated result. Whether the City's existing Newcomer Office has capacity to add pilot programming without displacing existing work was not confirmed in this review.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirming Newcomer Office current program capacity; independently re-fetching the newcomer-success-credential-recognition.md cross-reference rather than relying on it secondhand; fairness and legal review.
