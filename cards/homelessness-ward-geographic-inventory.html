Ward-Geographic Shelter Inventory — Playbook

Where Toronto's shelters actually sit, which wards have none at all, and how new sites get fought over and approved.

DRAFTThe playbookThe evidence file

What Toronto's shelter geography actually looks like — each finding with its scope, its gaps, and its receipts.

v2.0 · 2026-08-11

---

The honest bottom line

Toronto has 59 base emergency shelter sites, and the distribution across the city's 25 wards is exactly as uneven as it looks: seven wards have none at all (Don Valley West, Etobicoke Centre, Humber River-Black Creek, Scarborough North, Scarborough-Agincourt, York Centre, and York South-Weston), one ward — Toronto Centre — has 14, including the single largest site in the system, the 580-bed Seaton House Main location, and three downtown wards together hold just over half the entire system's base capacity. That's a direct export from the City's own shelter locator, not a model or an estimate. It doesn't by itself prove the distribution is wrong, though — a ward with zero base shelters might still carry meaningful temporary or hotel capacity the City counts separately, and this project doesn't yet have that combined picture.

Note on this file (FIX-2, W1b cards audit, 2026-08-06): both cards below name this project's own tracking process as the actor, not a City program — both are labelled Card class: INTERNAL per a recorded judgment ruling, and this file holds zero cards proposing action for Council to adopt. What follows is a research-integrity exercise, not a policy ask.

Actually join the base-shelter map with the broader occupancy map. (a recommendation card) This project already has both datasets it needs to determine whether any of the seven zero-base-shelter wards have meaningful temporary capacity once hotel and warming-centre programs are counted — a straightforward data exercise that would turn "seven wards have zero shelter capacity" from a real-but-partial finding into a complete one, not a new research undertaking.

Keep watching what happens to 66 Third Street. (a recommendation card) A planned 50-bed seniors' shelter in Etobicoke is the subject of an active $1-million lawsuit from the New Toronto Initiative, which argues the process was unfair and that the roughly 9,200-square-foot lot falls about 40% short of the City's own stated minimum shelter-site size; the plaintiff group's own website puts the twenty planned HSCIS shelters at roughly $422,000 per bed on average, more than $675,000 per bed at this specific reduced site, against a City modular supportive-housing program the same group says delivered permanent units at roughly $309,000 each — the plaintiff's own framing, not independently verified here. A second organized group, the Etobicoke Lakeshore Shelter Support Coalition, formed to counter that opposition and, by its own account, helped get all six of that round's shelter sites through a 22-3 Council vote despite the pushback. Both groups are real and active; the litigation is still unresolved, and this page tracks the outcome as the one live test case available for how the City's confidentiality-first siting approach plays out when it's actually contested.

This doesn't resolve whether 66 Third Street should proceed — that's for the court and Council, not this project — and it doesn't independently verify either advocacy group's own cost figures against a City source; both are named as open, bounded next steps rather than skipped by accident.

a recommendation card — Join the Base-Shelter and Broader-Occupancy Datasets by Ward to Close This Leaf's Own Named Gap — Recommendation Card

Card id: a recommendation card · Card class: INTERNAL · Issue: homelessness-ward-geographic-inventory · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: carried-forward (CL-771)

Problem

This page’s authoritative inventory establishes that 7 of 25 Toronto wards have zero base emergency shelter sites [CL-771], but this figure does not account for temporary hotel or warming-centre capacity tracked separately in shelter-system-capacity-strain's own broader occupancy dataset. The page’s own carried-forward material names this exact combined analysis as a specific, bounded, not-yet-done next step. Without it, "7 wards have zero shelter capacity" could overstate or understate the real geographic-equity picture depending on where temporary capacity happens to sit.

Action

This project (CURATE lane) performs a one-time ward-level join of the 59-site base inventory against shelter-system-capacity-strain's broader occupancy dataset, to determine whether any of the seven zero-base-shelter wards have meaningful temporary capacity, and publishes the combined finding as an update to this backgrounder.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: not applicable — this is an internal research action, not a City of Toronto program action. (Stated explicitly per template guardrail rather than left blank.)
  • City demands of Province: none.
  • City demands of Feds: none.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low — a data-join exercise using two datasets this project already has committed (this library's prior data compilation (Current Base Shelter Locations Wards) and whatever occupancy-by-address data shelter-system-capacity-strain already cites), not a new data-collection effort.

Funding path

None required; internal research-maintenance action within existing CURATE-lane discipline.

Who benefits, and how

Readers of this project's ward-equity findings, via an accurate combined picture rather than a base-shelter-only one; any future ward-councillor-facing accountability use of this data, via a more complete and defensible figure.

a recommendation card — Track the 66 Third Street Litigation Outcome as a Precedent for Future HSCIS Site Disputes — Recommendation Card

Card id: a recommendation card · Card class: INTERNAL · Issue: homelessness-ward-geographic-inventory · Backgrounder: our research file for that page · Trust: New load-bearing findings (backgrounder NEW-2026-1, NEW-2026-2)

Problem

The 66 Third Street litigation is this page’s own concrete test case for the site-selection-transparency tension, and this review found it is more actively contested — with both organized opposition and organized support — than the page’s own carried-forward material alone showed. The litigation's outcome (still pending as of this review) will likely set a practical precedent for how future HSCIS site disputes unfold, given 7 more unfunded sites remain to be selected [CL-163].

Action

This page tracks and updates its own record of the 66 Third Street litigation's outcome as a named, dated follow-up item, and flags the precedent for shelter-system-capacity-strain's own a recommendation card card (which proposes funding the remaining 7 HSCIS sites) as directly relevant context — new sites funded under that card would face the same site-selection-transparency tension this page documents.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: not applicable — research-tracking action only.
  • City demands of Province: none directly.
  • City demands of Feds: none directly.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: negligible — research-tracking action within CURATE-lane discipline.

Funding path

None required.

Who benefits, and how

This project's own HSCIS-related research (this page and shelter-system-capacity-strain), via an up-to-date precedent for how site-selection disputes actually resolve; any future ward affected by one of the 7 remaining unfunded HSCIS sites, indirectly, via this project's own research reflecting the real, current state of the one test case available.

Who bears the cost, and how

No payer class — negligible internal research-maintenance cost only.
