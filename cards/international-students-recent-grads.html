RC-INTL-01 — Fund a Dedicated International-Student Housing & Exploitation Information Hub — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-INTL-01 · File: research/cards/international-students-recent-grads.md#rc-intl-01 Status: DRAFT Issue slug: international-students-recent-grads · Backgrounder: international-students-recent-grads Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (built entirely on this run's quote-context findings) · Date: 2026-07-13

Problem

Toronto international students face documented, targeted housing exploitation distinct from the general tenant-protection problem — ⚠️ per one student-newspaper investigation's two named-but-anonymized individual accounts, not a systematic prevalence survey; see Confidence & uncertainties below: landlords asking about immigration status during unit viewings, threatening students with "immigration issues," and property managers renting out illegally subdivided "flex rooms" specifically marketed to international students, with tenants reporting being told "if you don't like it, you can leave" when raising safety concerns [backgrounder §"Housing precarity and landlord exploitation, Toronto-specific," NEW-6]. A 2024 Statistics Canada study cited in the same reporting found international students more likely than domestic peers to experience unsuitable housing, attributing this partly to unfamiliarity with Canada's housing system and language barriers [NEW-6]. No City of Toronto program specifically targeted at this population's housing exploitation was identified in this pass — the closest existing tool, RentSafeTO enforcement (recently expanded via a March 2026 mayoral motion), addresses building-standard compliance generally, not international-student-targeted exploitation or students' own unfamiliarity with their rights [NEW-6]. This card addresses the information/reporting gap specifically, not the broader rental-market regulation question already covered by the rental-market-tenant-protections leaf.

Action

Fund and publicize a single, plain-language, multilingual information hub (web page plus in-person/drop-in presence at existing settlement-sector partner locations already on the Newcomer Leadership Table) covering: tenant rights under the Residential Tenancies Act in the specific context international students face (no security-of-status leverage a landlord can lawfully use), how to report suspected exploitation or illegal rooming conversions to RentSafeTO and the provincial Rental Housing Enforcement Unit, and a clearly signposted path to existing eviction-prevention supports (Rent Bank, EPIC). This is an information/referral and light coordination function, not a new direct-service caseworker program.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: hosting and publicizing the hub itself, using the existing Toronto Newcomer Office/Local Immigration Partnership coordination structure [backgrounder NEW-8] as the delivery mechanism; directing RentSafeTO officers (already being expanded per the March 2026 motion) to treat immigration-status-based intimidation as an aggravating enforcement factor where reported.
  • City demands of Province: none required for the hub itself; the underlying enforcement tools it points to (Rental Housing Enforcement Unit, Landlord and Tenant Board) are already provincial and already exist — this card does not ask the Province to create anything new, only signposts what exists.
  • City demands of Feds: none — this card is deliberately scoped to information/referral within existing municipal coordination authority, precisely because the leaf's own backgrounder finds the structural housing-precarity levers are not municipal [backgrounder §"Key tensions/tradeoffs"].

Cost

No genuine dollar comparator was located for this estimate, despite a targeted search this pass: neither the City's Newcomer Orientation Week nor Welcome and Information for Newcomers program [backgrounder NEW-8] has a publicly quantified budget line identified in any source checked (the City's own 2026 Budget Notes for Social Development and a 2025 Council backgrounder on IRCC funding cuts both discuss the Toronto Newcomer Office's funding structure — including that it is receiving an "approximately 18-20% per year" IRCC funding cut for 2025-2028 — without ever stating a specific dollar figure for the office's total budget or for either named program individually). "Low hundreds of thousands of dollars CAD annually" is therefore an unanchored floor-level guess by this document, based only on the general scale of comparable small municipal information/coordination functions, not on any City-stated or comparator dollar figure — stated plainly as a guess rather than dressed in comparator language. A future pass should request the Toronto Newcomer Office's actual line-item budget directly (e.g., via the Social Development, Finance & Administration division's budget notes or a direct information request) before this card's cost estimate is used for anything beyond illustrative order-of-magnitude purposes.

Funding path

City general revenue via the existing Toronto Newcomer Office's operating budget, alongside the Local Immigration Partnership structure, which the City's own page states is federally (IRCC) funded for its coordination-table function [backgrounder NEW-8] — meaning a share of this card's cost may already be fundable through the existing LIP funding stream rather than requiring new City money, though this is not confirmed in this pass and should be checked against the actual LIP funding agreement before costing this card further.

Who benefits, and how

International students facing housing exploitation, via a single, known, plain-language place to learn their rights and report abuse rather than the current fragmented reliance on individual institutions' own international offices [backgrounder, WebSearch summary on TMU's advisor office]; the City itself, via better-documented evidence of the scale of the problem this card's own backgrounder found only anecdotal (named, verified, but individual) accounts of in this pass.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the operating-budget allocation; no cost imposed on landlords or students themselves under this card (distinct from a licensing-fee-funded model).

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder. The backgrounder's own Cui Bono table (research/backgrounders/international-students-recent-grads.md) is empty — not for lack of a genuine on-topic finding (the Auditor General of Ontario's 2021 finding on Ontario public colleges' financial dependency on international tuition, naming Centennial College and Seneca College, is recorded there as a checked, on-topic ESTABLISHED-grade lead), but because the Accountability Observatory's ledger infrastructure (accountability/ledger/entities.csv, accountability/ledger/claims.csv) does not yet exist to mint the ENT-####/ACL-#### pair this line would require. That finding, in any case, concerns institutional tuition-revenue dependency rather than the housing-exploitation landlords/property managers this specific card's Action addresses — a future pass should re-check once ledger infrastructure exists, since the two are related but not identical "who benefits" questions.

Financial ROI

Not estimated in this pass — no cited source quantifies avoided downstream costs (e.g., reduced shelter-system or food-bank pressure from earlier intervention in a housing-exploitation situation) specifically attributable to an information hub. Flagged as a gap rather than estimated without a source.

Economic ROI

Range: Not yet estimable at the intervention level — no source located in this pass models the economic effect of an information/referral hub specifically. The broader stakes are real (Global Affairs Canada's own 2022 economic-impact study found international students contributed $37.3 billion in spending and $30.9 billion to Canadian GDP nationally, with Ontario alone accounting for $16.9 billion of that GDP contribution — Global Affairs Canada, "Economic impact of international education in Canada — 2022 update," https://www.international.gc.ca/education/report-rapport/impact-2022/index.aspx?lang=eng), but that figure describes the international-student population's overall economic footprint, not the marginal economic return of retaining students who would otherwise leave due to unresolved housing exploitation, or of an information hub's effect on that retention — using it as this card's own ROI figure would misattribute a population-wide statistic to a narrow intervention.

Comparator source: Global Affairs Canada's 2022 economic-impact report (above) is named as context for the scale of what is at stake, explicitly not as a comparator for this specific intervention's return; no information-hub-specific economic study was found despite a targeted search.

Confidence: Low — the stakes are large and well-documented, but no source connects this specific intervention to a quantified economic outcome.

Social ROI

Directional case only: every documented account in this leaf's backgrounder of a student who did not know their rights or where to report exploitation [NEW-6] is, on its face, a case an accessible information hub could plausibly have shortened or prevented — but no quantified outcome study of a comparable existing hub elsewhere was identified or cited in this pass, so this remains a directional claim, not a modeled one.

Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely environmentally neutral — a web page plus drop-in presence at existing settlement-sector partner locations involves no construction, land-use change, or new physical infrastructure of its own. Comparator source: None needed; stated directly per the template's allowance for a genuinely neutral action. Confidence: High — the action is an information/referral service riding on existing physical locations, with no incremental physical footprint.

Evidence

  • NEW-6 · quote-context (The Eyeopener, 2026-04-07, accessed 2026-07-13) · Toronto international-student housing exploitation accounts; TMU residence capacity; RentSafeTO expansion
  • NEW-8 · quote-context (City of Toronto, Toronto Newcomer Strategy page, accessed 2026-07-13) · existing Newcomer Office/LIP coordination structure and its federal funding source

Confidence & uncertainties

Low-to-medium confidence. The problem statement rests on a small number of named-but-anonymized individual accounts from one student newspaper investigation, not a systematic survey of exploitation prevalence — a real evidence-thinness this card does not paper over (see the inline hedge in Problem, above). The cost estimate is an unanchored floor-level guess, not a quantified City budget figure or a genuine dollar comparator — no such comparator was found despite a targeted search, per Cost above. Whether the LIP funding stream could actually cover this card's cost, or whether it would require new City money, is unconfirmed.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: pulling an actual Toronto Newcomer Office budget line to replace the named-but-unquantified comparator; confirming LIP funding-agreement scope; a systematic (not anecdotal) prevalence estimate of international-student housing exploitation in Toronto specifically; Lane R pass.

RC-INTL-02 — Publish a Toronto-Specific Recent-Graduate Retention Information Package — Recommendation Card

Card id: RC-INTL-02 · File: research/cards/international-students-recent-grads.md#rc-intl-02 Status: DRAFT Issue slug: international-students-recent-grads · Backgrounder: international-students-recent-grads Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (built entirely on this run's quote-context findings) · Date: 2026-07-13

Problem

The federal PGWP eligibility list changed three times within eight months in 2025–2026 (a June 25, 2025 narrowing, a July 4, 2025 partial reversal, and a freeze confirmed as of January 15, 2026) [backgrounder §"PGWP eligibility," NEW-3], and Ontario's international student enrolment fell 36% in 2025-26 alone on top of a 6% decline the year before [backgrounder NEW-2] — a volatile, fast-moving federal/provincial pathway that individual recent graduates are left to track largely on their own, with no Toronto-specific retention data even identified in this pass to show whether the City's own graduates are staying, leaving for elsewhere in Canada, or leaving the country entirely after PGWP expiry (flagged as a genuine data gap in the backgrounder, not resolved here). This card addresses the information-asymmetry problem — graduates navigating a fast-changing federal pathway with no single, current, Toronto-oriented reference point — not the underlying federal policy itself, which the City does not control.

Action

Publish and keep current (quarterly review minimum, given the pathway's demonstrated volatility) a single City-hosted reference page aimed specifically at recent international graduates of Toronto-area institutions, consolidating: current PGWP field-of-study eligibility status in plain language, links to the Express Entry/Canadian Experience Class/Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program pathways as they currently stand, and a direct pointer to Toronto-based settlement and employment-support services (TRIEC, ACCES Employment, and similar Newcomer Leadership Table members already listed on the City's own Newcomer Strategy page) [backgrounder NEW-8] that serve exactly this population already.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: publishing and maintaining the reference page itself; using its existing Newcomer Leadership Table relationships [NEW-8] to keep the settlement/employment-service links current — an entirely within-authority coordination/communications action.
  • City demands of Province: none for this specific action; the OINP program itself is provincial and already exists — this card only points to it, does not ask for provincial policy change.
  • City demands of Feds: none for this specific action, though the backgrounder's own "Key tensions" section notes the underlying pathway volatility (PGWP list changes, cap levels) is entirely federal and outside this card's or the City's control — stated here rather than glossed over.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low tens of thousands of dollars CAD annually — a materially smaller undertaking than RC-INTL-01's coordination-hub-plus-drop-in-presence model, since it is page maintenance only. As with RC-INTL-01's cost estimate above, no City-stated dollar comparator was located for a program of this kind despite a targeted search this pass (the City's Newcomer Office budget documents checked do not break out communications/web-maintenance costs at this granularity); this figure is this document's own unanchored floor-level guess, not a cited comparator figure, and is stated as such rather than implied to rest on a named program's actual budget.

Funding path

City general revenue via existing communications/newcomer-office budget lines; no new program infrastructure required beyond page maintenance and the existing Newcomer Leadership Table relationships already in place [NEW-8].

Who benefits, and how

Recent international graduates of Toronto institutions navigating PGWP-to-permanent-residency pathways, via one current, trustworthy reference point instead of tracking IRCC notices and multiple pathway programs independently; existing Toronto settlement/employment-service organizations (TRIEC, ACCES Employment, and similar), via a City-endorsed referral channel to a population they already serve.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the communications/newcomer-office budget allocation; no cost imposed on graduates themselves.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder. The backgrounder's own Cui Bono table (research/backgrounders/international-students-recent-grads.md) is empty — the ledger infrastructure required to cite a real ENT-####/ACL-#### pair (accountability/ledger/entities.csv, accountability/ledger/claims.csv) does not yet exist. The one genuine, on-topic lead the backgrounder does record — the Auditor General of Ontario's 2021 finding on Ontario public colleges' dependency on international tuition revenue — concerns institutional finances rather than the PGWP pathway volatility and information-asymmetry problem this specific card's Action addresses, so it would not be the natural fit for this card even once ledger infrastructure exists.

Financial ROI

Not estimated in this pass — no source in this backgrounder quantifies a fiscal return (e.g., retained-graduate tax contribution, employer cost savings) attributable to a reference-page intervention specifically. The backgrounder itself flags Toronto-specific recent-graduate retention as a genuinely uncovered data gap [backgrounder §"Open questions / data gaps"] — this card cannot responsibly claim a modeled ROI against data that does not yet exist.

Economic ROI

Range: Not yet estimable — no source in this pass models the marginal economic return of a reference-page intervention on graduate retention specifically. As with RC-INTL-01 above, the population-level stakes are real and documented (Global Affairs Canada's 2022 study found international education contributed $30.9 billion to Canadian GDP, with Ontario alone at $16.9 billion — https://www.international.gc.ca/education/report-rapport/impact-2022/index.aspx?lang=eng — and each retained graduate represents foregone Canadian labour-market and tax contribution if they leave post-PGWP), but converting that population-wide figure into a per-graduate retained-value estimate, or estimating how many additional graduates a reference page would retain, is not attempted here — the backgrounder itself already flags Toronto-specific retention data as a genuine, unfilled gap.

Comparator source: Global Affairs Canada 2022 economic-impact report (above), named as stakes-context only, not as a comparator for this intervention's own return; no reference-page-specific or information-intervention-specific retention study was found.

Confidence: Low — real stakes, no source connecting this specific low-cost intervention to a quantified retention or economic outcome.

Social ROI

Directional only: reducing information-asymmetry for a population navigating a pathway that changed three times in eight months [NEW-3] is plausible on its face as reducing stress and wasted effort, but no claim or study quantifying this specific outcome was found or cited in this pass.

Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely environmentally neutral — a web-page maintenance function involves no construction, land-use change, or new physical infrastructure. Comparator source: None needed; stated directly per the template's allowance for a genuinely neutral action. Confidence: High — the action is page maintenance only, with no physical footprint.

Evidence

  • NEW-2 · quote-context (Global News, 2026-05-05, citing Statistics Canada) · Ontario enrolment decline scale, establishing the shrinking-cohort context this card responds to
  • NEW-3 · quote-context (IRCC official notice, 2025-06-25/2026-01-15) · PGWP field-of-study list volatility across 2025–2026
  • NEW-8 · quote-context (City of Toronto, Toronto Newcomer Strategy page) · existing Newcomer Leadership Table members and coordination structure this card would route through

Confidence & uncertainties

Low confidence on impact (no retention outcome data exists to measure against, a gap this card inherits directly from the backgrounder rather than resolving); medium confidence on feasibility (the action itself is small in scope and uses only existing City relationships and authority). This card should be read as a genuinely modest, low-cost first step precisely because the evidence base for anything larger does not yet exist — proposing more than this on the current evidence would overstate what is known.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: sourcing actual Toronto-specific graduate retention data (the single biggest gap this card and its backgrounder share); a City communications-budget comparator figure; Lane R pass.
