RC-CSWB-01 — Formalize Provincial Cost-Sharing for CSWB Plan Implementation, Not Just Adjacent Grant Programs

Card id: RC-CSWB-01 · File: research/cards/community-safety-wellbeing-plans.md#rc-cswb-01 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: community-safety-wellbeing-plans · Backgrounder: community-safety-wellbeing-plans Trust class: NEW · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019 legally requires every Ontario municipality to prepare, adopt, and periodically review a CSWB plan, but the City of Toronto's own report states plainly that "Ontario municipalities do not receive any direct investments from the province to implement" that requirement — while a specific $6.8 million federal funding stream supporting SafeTO's Phase 1/2 transition expires by March 2026 with no confirmed replacement [backgrounder, "Legislative basis: a provincial mandate with a province-wide funding gap"; "Funding scale and its stated fragility"]. This card addresses only the core-implementation funding gap — not the separate, already-funded provincial grant programs (Safer and Vital Communities Grant, Mobile Crisis Response Team Enhancement Grant), which this card's own backgrounder documents as real but narrower in scope than full plan-implementation funding.

Action

The City formally requests the Province establish a dedicated, ongoing CSWB plan-implementation transfer (distinct from the existing narrower, program-specific grants), sized to the legislative obligation the Province itself created, joining the advocacy the City's own report notes is already underway by the Association of Municipalities of Ontario and the Ontario Municipal Social Services Association [backgrounder, "Legislative basis: a provincial mandate with a province-wide funding gap"].

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: continue and formalize existing advocacy (the City's own report already documents joining AMO/OMSSA's call); ensure SafeTO's own budget requests to Council transparently distinguish core-implementation costs from grant-funded program costs, so the funding gap this card targets is legible in the City's own budget documents.
  • City demands of Province: establish a dedicated CSWB plan-implementation transfer program — this is squarely a provincial funding-and-legislative-design decision, since the Province created the mandate under the CSPA without an accompanying implementation-funding mechanism [backgrounder, "Legislative basis: a provincial mandate with a province-wide funding gap"].
  • City demands of Feds: continued funding for the Crime Prevention Grant and Building Safer Communities Fund specifically — the City's own report's Recommendation 4 already makes this request directly, which this card treats as an existing, City-endorsed ask rather than a new one [backgrounder claim-index, City of Toronto SafeTO report Recommendations section].

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: not separately costed as a province-wide figure in this leaf's sources; Toronto's own cumulative SafeTO investment since 2021 is reported at $60.6 million gross, with annual figures separately cited in the $43-49 million range (not fully reconciled to the cumulative figure) — a single Ontario municipality's scale gives a rough sense of the per-city order of magnitude, but no source in this leaf estimates a province-wide implementation-funding total across all CSWB-obligated municipalities [backgrounder, "Funding scale and its stated fragility"]. Comparator source: Toronto's own reported spending is the only concrete anchor available; no comparator province or jurisdiction's CSWB-equivalent funding formula was identified in this pass. Confidence: low — the ask is directionally clear (a funding gap the City itself has already identified and is already advocating against) but no source in this leaf provides a costed provincial-transfer-program design to anchor a specific dollar figure.

Funding path

Provincial general revenue, via a new dedicated transfer program — this is the core of the ask itself, not a pre-existing mechanism being requested to continue. The federal Crime Prevention Grant/Building Safer Communities Fund renewal (Recommendation 4 of the City's own report) is a separate, narrower funding path already formally requested by the City.

Who benefits, and how

All Ontario municipalities carrying the CSWB planning mandate, via reduced reliance on general municipal tax revenue and federal grants with defined expiry dates to fund a provincially-legislated obligation; Toronto residents specifically benefit from reduced funding-cliff risk to programs like the Toronto Community Crisis Service and Community Crisis Response Program, both flagged in the backgrounder as currently funded through a mix of City resources and expiring federal grants [backgrounder, "Funding scale and its stated fragility"].

Who bears the cost, and how

Ontario provincial taxpayers, via general revenue funding a new transfer program; in the absence of this card's ask, the cost continues to fall on municipal property taxpayers city-by-city (the current status quo, per the City's own "own resources" framing) and on federal taxpayers via time-limited grant renewals that are not guaranteed.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder — the backgrounder's own Cui Bono table came up empty for this leaf, since CSWB planning is delivered through direct municipal social-development divisions and community-partner grants rather than a privatized or single-vendor-contracted model [backgrounder, "Cui Bono"].

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Not separately modeled in this leaf's sources as a fiscal-return figure for the province; the ask is a cost-shifting/formalization request (moving an existing City-borne cost partly onto provincial books) rather than a program with its own projected savings. Comparator source: none identified — a live-discovery search this pass for a costed CSWB-implementation-funding-formula proposal (from AMO, OMSSA, or a comparable advocacy body) did not surface one with a specific dollar ask. Confidence: low — genuine gap; the City's own report confirms the advocacy exists but this leaf's sources do not include AMO/OMSSA's own costed proposal, if one exists.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified — this is a funding-mechanism/governance request, not a spending program with a direct local-economic-impact study attached. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Directionally supported by the backgrounder's own documentation of concrete Phase 1 outcomes (25,000+ mental health crisis calls responded to, 78% resolved without police involvement, 1,844 critical incidents responded to at an 88% 48-hour response rate) that are themselves dependent on continued funding stability — the mechanism (funding certainty → program continuity → sustained outcomes) is directly supported by the backgrounder's own account of programs currently facing a funding cliff [backgrounder, "SafeTO: Toronto's plan, Phase 1 outcomes"; "Funding scale and its stated fragility"]. Confidence: medium — the underlying programs' outcomes are well-documented in the City's own self-report; the causal link from "more secure provincial funding" to "better outcomes" specifically is a reasonable inference from funding-continuity logic rather than a separately modeled or cited finding.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: not applicable / non-factor. Comparator source: not applicable — a funding-mechanism request has no meaningful direct environmental footprint of its own. Confidence: low (stated as a non-factor, not a computed low-confidence estimate).

Evidence

  • City of Toronto, "2025 Update on SafeTO Implementation" (2025-04-29) · NEW · no-direct-provincial-investment finding, federal grant expiry dates, Phase 1 outcome figures, AMO/OMSSA advocacy reference
  • Ontario Ministry of the Solicitor General, provincial grant program materials · NEW · named existing (narrower) provincial grant streams

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence that a real, documented funding gap exists and that the City's own report already supports the direction of this ask (it names the same advocacy coalition this card would join). Lower confidence on the specific dollar figure a "dedicated CSWB implementation transfer" should carry, since no source in this leaf provides a costed proposal at that scale. The distinction between "no direct implementation funding" and "real but narrower program-specific grants exist" is preserved deliberately in this card rather than collapsed into a simpler but less accurate "totally unfunded mandate" framing [backgrounder, "Key tensions / tradeoffs"].

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: a costed provincial-transfer-program proposal (from AMO/OMSSA or a comparable source) to anchor a specific dollar ask, Lane R pass.

RC-CSWB-02 — Adopt a Sustained, Health-System-Linked Outcome Metric Set for SafeTO, Modeled on Scotland's Violence Reduction Unit

Card id: RC-CSWB-02 · File: research/cards/community-safety-wellbeing-plans.md#rc-cswb-02 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: community-safety-wellbeing-plans · Backgrounder: community-safety-wellbeing-plans Trust class: NEW · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

SafeTO's own Phase 1 progress report claims over 90% of its 26 actions complete or in progress in the same document that discloses a 34% rise in gun violence in 2024, and no independent (Auditor General-level or academic) evaluation of SafeTO specifically was located in this leaf's sources — unlike Toronto Paramedic Services, which received independent Auditor General scrutiny [backgrounder, "A documented setback alongside the reported progress"; "Open questions / data gaps"]. The UK's own multi-decade Community Safety Partnership experience shows this is a common failure mode internationally, not a Toronto-specific oversight: an independent evaluation found partnerships had "no common agreement as to how to measure 'effectiveness'" and over-relied on recorded crime data alone [backgrounder, "International context"]. This card addresses only the measurement/evaluation-design gap — not a claim that SafeTO's programs are ineffective, which this leaf's sources do not establish either way.

Action

Adopt a small, fixed set of health-system-linked outcome metrics (analogous to Scotland's VRU tracking weapon-carrying rates and hospital admissions for sharp-object assaults) tracked consistently over SafeTO's full 10-year horizon, reported alongside — not instead of — the existing action-completion and program-output metrics, and commission an independent (non-Social-Development-authored) evaluation at the Phase 2 report-back point already scheduled for 2027 [backgrounder, "SafeTO: Toronto's plan, Phase 1 outcomes"; "International context"].

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: define and adopt the metric set, direct Toronto Public Health and the SafeTO Collaborative Analytics and Learning Environment (already an existing, functioning data-sharing infrastructure per the backgrounder) to track it, and commission an independent evaluator for the 2027 report-back already scheduled — all within existing municipal authority and existing infrastructure.
  • City demands of Province: access to relevant provincial health-system data (hospital admissions data specifically, mirroring the Scottish model's own hospital-admissions metric) where not already available through existing data-sharing agreements — the backgrounder notes the SafeTO Collaborative Analytics and Learning Environment has already built partnerships with the University of Toronto and Statistics Canada, suggesting this kind of data partnership is a demonstrated, not novel, City capability, but hospital-system data specifically may require a distinct provincial data-sharing agreement not yet confirmed in this leaf's sources.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as directly relevant to this specific action.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: modest relative to SafeTO's existing $43-49 million annual investment — an independent evaluation and an expanded metrics-tracking function are typically a small fraction of a program's total budget, though no specific comparator figure for evaluation cost as a percentage of program budget was identified in this leaf's sources. Comparator source: none identified with a specific dollar figure — the qualitative comparator (Scotland's own sustained, multi-decade tracking practice) supports the design, not the cost estimate. [Flagged: no direct cost figure for this specific action was found in this leaf's sources.] Confidence: low — the design rationale is well-supported, but no source anchors a specific evaluation-cost figure.

Funding path

Within SafeTO's existing operating budget (Social Development's own analytics function already exists per the backgrounder) plus a modest incremental allocation for external evaluator contracting — no new dedicated funding mechanism is proposed beyond what SafeTO's own 2027 report-back commitment already implies should happen.

Who benefits, and how

All Toronto residents, via a SafeTO program whose actual effectiveness becomes independently checkable rather than resting solely on the implementing division's own self-report; residents in the six geographic areas the backgrounder identifies as bearing 46% of 2024 firearm incidents specifically benefit if sustained, disaggregated metrics reveal whether place-based interventions are working in those specific areas, which the current action-completion metrics do not directly measure [backgrounder, "A documented setback alongside the reported progress"].

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via SafeTO's existing operating budget plus a modest incremental evaluation cost; no new tax or fee proposed.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder — same empty Cui Bono finding as RC-CSWB-01 [backgrounder, "Cui Bono"].

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Not quantified in this leaf's sources; better-targeted, evidence-checked interventions plausibly avoid wasted spending on ineffective program elements, but no source models this as a specific dollar figure. Comparator source: none identified. Confidence: low — genuine gap, not computed.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified — an evaluation/measurement action does not have a direct economic-impact study attached in this leaf's sources. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

The strongest case in this leaf's evidence: Scotland's VRU model, tracked consistently over 11 years, produced independently confirmed, substantial outcome improvements (69% fall in weapon-carrying, 63% fall in hospital admissions, 50% reduction in sharp-object-assault deaths) [backgrounder, "International context"]. This card's own action does not claim SafeTO will replicate those exact figures — only that adopting a comparably sustained, health-linked metric discipline is the transferable design element that could eventually allow a comparably confident outcome claim for Toronto, which is not currently possible given the evaluation gap this card addresses. Confidence: medium — the international precedent's outcome evidence is strong and multi-source-corroborated; the inferential leap to "adopting similar metrics will produce similar results in Toronto" is directional, not guaranteed, and this card does not overstate it as a prediction.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: not applicable / non-factor. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: low (stated as a non-factor).

Evidence

  • City of Toronto, "2025 Update on SafeTO Implementation" · NEW · 90%+ action completion, 2024 gun-violence increase, SafeTO Collaborative Analytics and Learning Environment existing capability
  • search-summary re: Scottish Violence Reduction Unit outcomes · NEW · 11-year outcome dataset ⚠️ VERIFY
  • search-summary re: UK Community Safety Partnership evaluation gaps · NEW · evaluation-discipline failure mode ⚠️ VERIFY

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence that the measurement-design gap this card identifies is real (no independent SafeTO evaluation was located, and the UK comparator literature shows this is a common failure mode for this model generally). Lower confidence on the specific cost of implementing this card's Action, and explicitly no claim that SafeTO's current programs are underperforming — the 2024 gun-violence increase is stated in the backgrounder as an open interpretive question, not resolved by this card in either direction [backgrounder, "What the evidence does and doesn't support"].

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: confirmation of what hospital-admissions or comparable health-system data-sharing arrangement would actually be available to the City, a specific evaluation-cost estimate, Lane R pass.
