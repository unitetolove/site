a recommendation card — Audit and Reform the City's Own Hiring Practice for Credential-Recognition and "Canadian Experience" Barriers — Recommendation Card

Card id: a recommendation card · Status: DRAFT · Backgrounder: newcomer-success-credential-recognition Provenance: New load-bearing findings (problem statement from this review's source quote findings; macro framing from carried-forward master briefing, flagged) · Date: 2026-07-13 (narrowed 2026-08-06, FIX-3: the Social Procurement Program ask formerly bundled into this card now lives at a recommendation card)

Problem

The City of Toronto's own "Newcomer Job Seekers" page shows the City's direct role in credential recognition is narrow: credential assessment runs through World Education Services, funded by the Province, and fairness oversight runs through the Province's own Office of the Fairness Commissioner — both provincial, not municipal [backgrounder NEW-3]. The City's one direct program, Profession to Profession mentoring, is a four-to-six-month mentorship match, not a hiring-practice reform, and is bounded to internationally-trained professionals who have lived in Canada under three years [backgrounder NEW-3]. No City of Toronto policy specifically conditioning its own job postings on recognizing international credentials or dropping "Canadian experience" requirements was identified in this review — a gap directly against the inherited master briefing's own recommendation that "the City recognizes international credentials and drops 'Canadian experience' requirements in its own hiring" as a leading-by-example step [master briefing, ⚠️ still being checked as macro framing only; the gap itself is a NEW finding, not an inherited one]. This card addresses that specific, bounded gap — City-as-employer practice — not provincial licensing reform, which the City cannot set.

Action

Direct the City's HR/Employment Equity function to audit current City of Toronto job postings for blanket "Canadian experience" requirements and remove them where the underlying skill, not the jurisdiction of prior experience, is what the role actually needs, formalizing a standard for treating assessed-equivalent international credentials and international work experience as satisfying posting requirements.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: the HR job-posting audit and reform is entirely within existing municipal authority over the City's own hiring.
  • City demands of Province: none required for this card's own action; the City does not need provincial permission to change its own hiring practices.
  • City demands of Feds: none — this card is scoped entirely to municipal-authority action, precisely because the backgrounder's own findings show credential assessment and licensing sit with the Province and, for internationally trained applicants specifically, with a still-developing federal Foreign Credential Recognition track [backgrounder NEW-1] that this card does not attempt to reach.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low hundreds of thousands of dollars CAD for the initial audit-and-policy-reform phase (comparable in scale to a coordination/policy-review exercise, not a new caseload program) — anchored to the same comparator type used for a prior deepen page’s information-hub card (international-students-recent-grads a recommendation card), since no specific City HR-audit budget line was pulled and quantified in this review. Ongoing cost (updated posting templates) is expected to be marginal once the initial audit is complete, but this is a reasoned estimate, not a cited figure.

Funding path

Existing City of Toronto HR/Employment Equity operating budget for the audit and policy-drafting work — no new program infrastructure or new budget line is proposed.

Who benefits, and how

Internationally trained Toronto residents applying directly to City of Toronto jobs, via removal of blanket "Canadian experience" screens that the backgrounder's inherited framing identifies as a discriminatory Catch-22 [master briefing, ⚠️ still being checked].

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers city-wide, via the HR/Employment Equity operating-budget allocation for the audit phase.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder. The backgrounder's Cui Bono table (our research file for that page) came up empty: no this library's internal records register/ register exists yet in this repo, and the live-discovery search run for that section found no ESTABLISHED or REPORTED finding naming a specific entity that profits financially from credential-recognition failure or "Canadian experience" gatekeeping persisting — the two candidate angles searched (credential-assessment-service fees; employer "Canadian experience" gatekeeping) each surfaced either unaggregated consumer-complaint signal or a named discrimination finding without an identified financial beneficiary, neither of which met the provenance bar for a table row.
