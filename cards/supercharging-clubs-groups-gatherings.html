Card RC-SCG-01 — Expand and Promote the Local Leadership Grant as a Low-Barrier "First Group" On-Ramp

Card id: RC-SCG-01 · File: research/cards/supercharging-clubs-groups-gatherings.md#rc-scg-01 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: supercharging-clubs-groups-gatherings · Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/supercharging-clubs-groups-gatherings.md Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (anchored to a directly-identified existing City program with a specific, low, three-person eligibility bar) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The backgrounder's own current-evidence finding is that reducing specific, named frictions (not vague "friction" generally) measurably grows group formation and participation [NEW-2]. Toronto already operates a specific, low-barrier grant — the Local Leadership Grant — requiring only "a resident-led group with at least three members who do not reside in the same household" [NEW-4] — but this document did not locate evidence in this pass that this specific, low-barrier program is well-known or heavily promoted relative to the City's larger, more established-organization-oriented CPIP channel.

Action

The City increases public awareness of and funding for the Local Leadership Grant specifically as the entry point for brand-new, three-person-minimum resident groups — distinct from and explicitly signposted as a lower-barrier alternative to CPIP, which the sibling connection-community-belonging leaf's own RC-CCB-02 card already proposes expanding for bridging-specific programming at established-organization scale. This card does not propose a new program; it proposes better use and visibility of an existing one specifically sized for first-time, informal organizers.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: the Local Leadership Grant is an existing City program; expanding its funding envelope and promotional visibility is within existing municipal grant-making authority.
  • City demands of Province: none required for this specific action; not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: none required for this specific action; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: not independently costed in this pass — no source located gives the Local Leadership Grant's current total budget or per-grant award size. This document does not invent a figure; flagged as a genuine gap for a future revision to close with a direct fetch of the program's own budget documentation.

Funding path

Existing City Community Funding program budget; this card proposes reallocating promotional emphasis and potentially incremental funding within the existing program structure, not a new funding mechanism.

Who benefits, and how

First-time, informal group organizers (the specific population the backgrounder's "organizer is the scarce engine" finding identifies as the highest-leverage intervention point) [inherited framing], via a funding on-ramp sized appropriately to their actual scale (three residents, not an established nonprofit); residents in the earliest stages of civic-association formation who might be deterred by CPIP's larger, more established-organization-oriented application process.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, via the existing Community Funding operating budget; opportunity cost is the marginal budget capacity and promotional attention not directed to CPIP or other existing programs.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table — explicitly empty, per the backgrounder's own stated reason.

(a) Financial ROI

Range: not yet estimable — the Local Leadership Grant's own budget scale was not independently confirmed in this pass. Comparator source: none identified yet. Confidence: low — genuine gap.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable. Comparator source: none identified yet specific to a small-grant, first-time-organizer program. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Range: directional, anchored to the backgrounder's mechanism-level evidence that reducing specific, named frictions (not vague general friction) measurably grows participation — Meetup's own platform data showed a 20% RSVP increase from discoverability improvements and 9-10% gains from search/join friction reduction [NEW-2]. This card applies the same "reduce a specific, named friction" logic to a funding-visibility gap rather than a platform-design gap, but the underlying mechanism-matters-more-than-generic-effort principle is the same. Comparator source: Meetup's 2025 progress report [NEW-2], acknowledged as a commercial platform's own data, not a neutral third-party study, per the backgrounder's own flagged caveat. Confidence: low-medium — the mechanism analogy (specific friction reduction works) is well-evidenced in the platform context; whether it transfers to a grant-visibility context specifically is not independently tested.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: genuinely neutral — a grant-visibility and funding-emphasis action has no direct environmental mechanism. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-2 · NEW · Meetup Blog, "The 2025 Meetup progress report" — mechanism-level friction-reduction evidence, cited as an analogy for this card's own logic, not as direct evidence for the Local Leadership Grant itself
  • NEW-4 · NEW (search-synthesized) · City of Toronto Community Funding program materials — Local Leadership Grant eligibility rule (3-resident minimum)

Confidence & uncertainties

Low-to-medium confidence overall. The existence and low-barrier eligibility rule of the Local Leadership Grant is well-confirmed; whether it is currently under-promoted relative to CPIP is this document's own inference from the absence of prominent search results about it, not a confirmed City finding — flagged as an inference, not a sourced fact. The social-ROI analogy to Meetup's platform data is explicitly a mechanism-level analogy, not direct evidence for this specific program.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: the Local Leadership Grant's actual current budget/award-size figures, and confirmation (through a City-side awareness survey or application-volume data) that under-promotion, rather than some other barrier, is the actual constraint on this program's uptake.

Card RC-SCG-02 — Adopt a "Name the Specific Friction" Design Discipline for Any Future Toronto Civic "Front Door" Platform

Card id: RC-SCG-02 · File: research/cards/supercharging-clubs-groups-gatherings.md#rc-scg-02 · Status: DRAFT Issue slug: supercharging-clubs-groups-gatherings · Backgrounder: research/backgrounders/supercharging-clubs-groups-gatherings.md Trust class: NEW-LOAD-BEARING (anchored to Meetup's own 2025 operational diagnostic method) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The inherited master briefing proposes Toronto build or partner on a civic digital "front door" for community discovery, ideally as a non-extractive civic/cooperative commons. This pass's research found a specific, current, concrete methodological lesson from the dominant commercial platform in this space: Meetup's own 2025 diagnostic work explicitly separated "friction" into named, distinct sub-mechanisms — communication-channel overlap, notification fatigue, and undiagnosed bugs — rather than treating it as one undifferentiated problem, and measured distinct, attributable improvements from addressing each separately (a 20% RSVP increase from discovery-page redesign; 9-10% gains from search-specific improvements) [NEW-2]. No design specification for a hypothetical Toronto civic platform (this remains, per the inherited document, an unbuilt, unfunded concept) currently reflects this specific diagnostic discipline, because no such platform exists yet to specify.

Action

Any future Toronto civic "front door" platform scoping or feasibility work (which the inherited document proposes but which does not yet exist as a funded project) should explicitly adopt a "decompose friction into named mechanisms, measure each intervention's effect separately" design discipline from the outset, rather than a generic "reduce friction" design brief — citing Meetup's own demonstrated method as the concrete precedent for how to do this, while explicitly building the resulting platform as a civic/cooperative commons (per the inherited document's own extraction-risk warning) rather than replicating Meetup's commercial ownership structure.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: if and when the City scopes a civic platform feasibility study (not yet committed, per the inherited document's own "open question" status), adopting this design discipline is a specification decision within existing municipal digital-strategy authority.
  • City demands of Province: none required for this specific action; not applicable.
  • City demands of Feds: none required for this specific action; not applicable.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: not applicable as a standalone cost — this card proposes a design-methodology addition to a not-yet-funded, not-yet-scoped hypothetical project, not a new expenditure of its own. Any cost belongs to the platform-scoping decision itself, which remains unresolved per the inherited document's own open-questions section.

Funding path

Not applicable at this card's scope — contingent entirely on whether and when the City decides to fund civic-platform scoping work at all, a decision this card does not make.

Who benefits, and how

Future group organizers on any eventual Toronto civic platform, via a more effective, evidence-informed design than a generic "make it easier to find groups" brief would produce on its own; City digital-strategy staff, via a concrete, named precedent method to specify in any future request for proposals or in-house design brief.

Who bears the cost, and how

Not applicable at this card's narrow scope — no direct cost-bearer, since this is a design-methodology recommendation contingent on a larger, unfunded decision.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table — explicitly empty. Note also: since no civic platform currently exists, there is no current "status quo" extraction to end in the same sense other cards address — this card is prospective design guidance, not a remedy for a currently operating harm.

ROI (four dimensions) — schema v2

(a) Financial ROI

Range: not applicable — contingent on an unfunded future decision this card does not make. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high — the honest answer given the card's own scope.

(b) Economic ROI

Range: not yet estimable, same contingency. Comparator source: not applicable at this scope. Confidence: low.

(c) Social ROI

Range: directional only — a better-designed future platform (if built) would plausibly serve organizers more effectively than a generically-designed one, per Meetup's own demonstrated mechanism-specific results [NEW-2], but this is inference about a hypothetical future project, not a measured outcome. Comparator source: Meetup's 2025 progress report [NEW-2], with the same commercial-platform caveat as RC-SCG-01. Confidence: low — genuinely speculative given the contingent, unfunded nature of the underlying project.

(d) Environmental ROI

Range: not applicable — a design-methodology recommendation for an unbuilt digital platform has no environmental mechanism. Comparator source: not applicable. Confidence: high.

Evidence

  • NEW-2 · NEW · Meetup Blog, "The 2025 Meetup progress report" — the specific diagnostic method (named friction sub-mechanisms, separately measured interventions) this card recommends Toronto adopt if and when it scopes a civic platform

Confidence & uncertainties

This card is explicitly contingent and prospective — it does not argue Toronto should build a civic platform (that remains the inherited document's own open question, not resolved by this leaf), only that if such a project is ever scoped, it should borrow a specific, demonstrated design discipline from the current market leader's own recent operational experience, while avoiding that same leader's commercial/extractive ownership structure per the inherited document's own warning. Confidence in the underlying method's value is medium-high (Meetup's own data is concrete and current); confidence that Toronto will act on this at all is not something this card can estimate.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: the prior, larger decision (does Toronto fund civic-platform scoping work at all) that this card does not resolve and is not positioned to resolve; this card is a specification note for if/when that larger decision proceeds, not a standalone action ready for independent budget allocation.
