Indigenous Self-Determination in Homelessness Response — Recommendation Cards

DRAFT Date: 2026-07-14 ·

Status: DRAFT · Version: v1.0 · Date: 2026-07-14 · Backgrounder: our research file for that page Written per this library's standard page structure and this library's Indigenous-sources provenance standard's voice rule, which binds this document specifically: these cards may recommend City-side process, transparency, or funding-mechanism changes, but do not, anywhere, purport to state what Indigenous people or organizations need, want, or should decide — every place an Indigenous position is invoked, it is a direct quotation or paraphrase of an Indigenous-authored source, cited as such, never this card's own synthesis of what Indigenous communities want. a recommendation card — Publish Annual Delivery Reporting Against the ALFDC 20%/25% Targets — Recommendation Card

Card id: a recommendation card · · Backgrounder: homelessness-indigenous-overrepresentation Provenance: earlier research plus new load-bearing findings (CL-367 + backgrounder NEW-2026-IND-2) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The City has a Council-mandated minimum 20% funding set-aside for Indigenous-led organizations, administered through ALFDC as the Indigenous Community Entity [CL-367], and a stated 25% housing-match target that this page’s own backgrounder flags as ⚠️ still being checked — not confirmed in any source independently re-checked in this review. Separately, the City's own co-produced Sixth Annual Gathering report records that "both TSSS and TICAB agreed that improvements are needed in the administration process to ensure greater accessibility for Indigenous partners" for the existing 20% funding stream [NEW-2026-IND-2, TICAB's own assessment, quoted]. This card addresses a narrow, bounded transparency gap: whether the City publishes clear, annual, public reporting against its own stated targets, in a form TICAB and ALFDC have already said would help make the administration process more accessible.

Action

The City publishes a standing annual public report — timed to the Meeting in the Middle gathering cycle — stating the dollar amount and percentage of Reaching Home/HPP/City Homelessness Investment Fund grant funding that reached Indigenous-led organizations against the 20% floor, the percentage of Coordinated Access housing matches that went to Indigenous people against the 25% target, and a plain-language accounting of administrative-process changes made in response to TICAB/ALFDC's documented feedback. This card does not propose changing the targets themselves.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: publish the report annually using data TSSS and the Housing Secretariat already collect for their own budget and program-tracking purposes (per the pattern already visible in the 2026 Budget Notes' own service-level metrics for the general shelter system) — no new authority required, an extension of existing public reporting practice to a target the City itself already set.
  • City demands of Province: none identified as required for this specific transparency action.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required for this specific transparency action.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low, an annual reporting exercise using data systems TSSS/the Housing Secretariat already operate for other public budget reporting (comparator: the general shelter system's own annual budget-notes reporting cycle, already produced at no separately identified marginal cost per shelter-system-capacity-strain's backgrounder) — not a program or capital cost.

Funding path

Existing TSSS/Housing Secretariat operating and reporting capacity; no new funding mechanism required for the reporting itself.

Who benefits, and how

TICAB and ALFDC, via a standing public accountability mechanism for a commitment they did not design but that governs funding flowing through their own administrative role — consistent with TICAB's own documented request for improved "administration process" accessibility [NEW-2026-IND-2, quoted]. The broader public and this corpus's own future research passes, via a citable, dated figure replacing the current ⚠️ still being checked status of the 25% target.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, negligibly, via existing TSSS/Housing Secretariat reporting-staff time; no new payer class identified.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table — the backgrounder's own search found no established or credibly reported entity with a financial stake in continued non-transparency on these targets specifically.

Financial ROI

Range: negligible direct cost, effectively an extension of existing reporting infrastructure. Comparator source: the general TSSS annual Budget Notes reporting cycle already produces comparable service-level metric tables (per shelter-system-capacity-strain's NEW-2026-5 through NEW-2026-24 findings) at no separately identified marginal cost — this card asks for the same kind of table applied to the Indigenous-specific targets. Confidence: medium — the comparator (existing TSSS reporting practice) is real and directly observed in this corpus's own sister leaf, though this card's own reporting requirement was not independently costed by any source.

Economic ROI

Range: Not estimable and not claimed. A transparency/reporting action has no meaningful direct local-spending or employment effect of its own. Comparator source: none sought — the action's value is accountability-based, not economic, and stretching an economic case onto it would manufacture a connection the action doesn't actually have. Confidence: low — genuinely not applicable to this kind of action, stated plainly rather than forced.

Social ROI

Directional, and grounded in what TICAB has itself said it needs: TICAB's own documented statement that "improvements are needed in the administration process to ensure greater accessibility for Indigenous partners" [NEW-2026-IND-2, quoted] is the direct evidentiary basis for this card's Social ROI claim — a standing public reporting mechanism is a plausible, though not proven, contributor to the administrative accessibility TICAB itself named as a gap. This card does not claim to know what "accessibility" improvements TICAB actually wants beyond publishing the mechanism TICAB and ALFDC could use to hold the City to its own stated commitment. Comparator source: TICAB's own quoted statement (NEW-2026-IND-2) is the comparator/evidence base itself, not a third-party study. Confidence: low-medium — directional, tied to a real quoted concern, but this card does not have direct evidence that annual public reporting specifically (as opposed to some other administrative fix) is what TICAB would judge as the right remedy.

Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely environmentally neutral. A reporting/transparency action has no construction, land-use, or physical-infrastructure component. Comparator source: none needed to support a neutral finding. Confidence: high on the neutrality of this specific action's own footprint.

Evidence

  • CL-367 · carried-forward · Council-mandated 20% Indigenous funding set-aside via ALFDC MOU
  • NEW-2026-IND-2 · source quote (this review) · TICAB/TSSS on administration-process accessibility gap; 20% commitment reaffirmed
  • Backgrounder's own ⚠️ still being checked flag · the 25% match-target figure's sourcing status

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence. The problem (a real funding floor and target, with TICAB's own documented concern about administrative accessibility) is well-sourced. This card's specific mechanism (annual public reporting) is this card's own proposed remedy, not something TICAB is quoted as having asked for by name — stated explicitly as this card's own inference from a related but distinct TICAB concern, consistent with the guardrail that a card may take a position but must not present its own synthesis as an Indigenous position. The 25% figure's own sourcing remains unresolved and this card does not resolve it.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: fairness and legal review; direct outreach to TICAB/ALFDC (per the backgrounder's own honest-limitation statement) before this card's specific proposed mechanism is presented as responsive to their stated concern, rather than this project's own guess at what would help.

a recommendation card — A Named, Council-Tracked Follow-Through Report on the 4-Shelter Indigenous EOI Pipeline — Recommendation Card

Card id: a recommendation card · · Backgrounder: homelessness-indigenous-overrepresentation Provenance: New load-bearing findings (backgrounder NEW-2026-IND-2) · Date: 2026-07-14

Problem

The City's Sixth Annual Gathering report documents a new Indigenous Expression of Interest (EOI) process, under the Homelessness Services Capital Infrastructure Strategy, that "emerged from recommendations by Indigenous providers to ensure more autonomy for Indigenous providers" and targets four new Indigenous shelters including "the first Indigenous women's shelter which had been identified as a critical need by Indigenous housing and service providers" [NEW-2026-IND-2]. This is a genuine self-determination-consistent process — Indigenous providers recommended it, and the City adopted it. But the backgrounder's own "Open questions / data gaps" section flags that no source located in this review confirms how many of the 4 sites have progressed from "process" to "site acquired," unlike the general (non-Indigenous-specific) HSCIS pipeline, which shelter-system-capacity-strain's backgrounder already tracks site-by-site with a published, dated table. This card addresses only that tracking-visibility gap.

Action

The City extends the same site-by-site, dated public tracking table it already publishes for the general HSCIS pipeline (per shelter-system-capacity-strain's NEW-2026-14 finding) to the 4-shelter Indigenous EOI pipeline specifically, including the first Indigenous women's shelter, so that progress is as publicly visible and Council-trackable as the general pipeline already is.

Jurisdiction split

  • City does: extend an existing public-tracking practice (the general HSCIS site table) to a sub-pipeline it already administers — no new authority or process required, since the underlying EOI process and site-tracking infrastructure already exist for the general pipeline.
  • City demands of Province: none identified as required for this specific tracking action.
  • City demands of Feds: none identified as required for this specific tracking action.

Cost

Order-of-magnitude: low, an extension of an existing public-facing tracking table (comparator: the City's own "Developing Shelter Sites" page, already maintained and dated, per shelter-system-capacity-strain's NEW-2026-12/NEW-2026-14 findings) — not a program or capital cost.

Funding path

Existing City web/communications and TSSS capital-planning operating capacity; no new funding mechanism required for the tracking table itself. The underlying 4-shelter construction funding path is not addressed by this card, which is scoped to tracking/visibility only.

Who benefits, and how

TICAB and the Indigenous provider community, via public visibility into a process they themselves recommended, allowing them to hold the City accountable to its own stated commitment in the same way this corpus's own research was able to track the general HSCIS pipeline's progress; the broader public and future research passes, via a citable, dated figure replacing the current gap this backgrounder flags.

Who bears the cost, and how

City taxpayers, negligibly, via existing web/communications and capital-planning staff time already producing the general-pipeline table; no new payer class identified.

Who benefits from the status quo

No beneficiary identified in the backing backgrounder's Cui Bono table.

Financial ROI

Range: negligible direct cost, an extension of an existing public tracking page. Comparator source: the City's own already-published "Developing Shelter Sites" table for the general 20-site HSCIS pipeline is the direct comparator and proof of concept — this card asks for the same practice applied to a named sub-set of 4 sites. Confidence: medium — the comparator is directly observed and already funded/operating; this card's own marginal cost was not separately quantified but is plausibly small given the comparator's existing scope.

Economic ROI

Range: Not estimable and not claimed. A tracking/transparency action has no direct local-spending or employment effect of its own. Comparator source: none sought. Confidence: low — not applicable to this kind of action.

Social ROI

Directional: visibility into a self-determination-consistent process Indigenous providers themselves recommended [NEW-2026-IND-2] plausibly supports the kind of accountability TICAB has separately named as a general administrative-process concern (see a recommendation card) — this card does not claim TICAB has specifically asked for this tracking table by name, only that it responds to a documented pattern of TICAB requesting more accessible administrative processes. Comparator source: NEW-2026-IND-2 (TICAB's administrative-accessibility concern, same evidentiary basis as a recommendation card). Confidence: low-medium — directional, not a claim this project can verify TICAB would specifically prioritize.

Environmental ROI

Range: Genuinely environmentally neutral. A public tracking table has no construction, land-use, or physical-infrastructure component; it reports on construction happening elsewhere but does not itself have a footprint. Comparator source: none needed to support a neutral finding. Confidence: high on the neutrality of this specific action's own footprint.

Evidence

  • NEW-2026-IND-2 · source quote (this review) · 4-shelter Indigenous EOI process, Indigenous-provider-recommended, first Indigenous women's shelter
  • Backgrounder's own "Open questions / data gaps" · site-by-site progress tracking gap, named explicitly

Confidence & uncertainties

Medium confidence. The underlying process (Indigenous-provider-recommended EOI, City-adopted) is well-sourced from a single primary document (the Sixth Annual Gathering report); this card's own tracking-table proposal is not itself something TICAB is quoted as requesting by name, and is this card's own inference by analogy to the general HSCIS pipeline's existing practice — stated explicitly rather than presented as an Indigenous ask. Whether the City already maintains this tracking internally without publishing it was not ruled out by this review.

Status

DRAFT — blocked on: fairness and legal review; confirming the City does not already publish this tracking in a form this review simply did not locate; direct outreach to TICAB (per the backgrounder's honest-limitation statement) before treating this card's proposed mechanism as responsive to their stated concerns rather than this project's own inference.
