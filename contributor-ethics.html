Contributor ethics

Required reading before you submit anything. These are our standing contributor-ethics rules, published here in full. Every external contribution is held to them, and reading this page is the gate before any submission is accepted.

Adapted, in our own words, from the principles underlying investigative-ethics practice (the Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations; general citizen-investigation safety and ethics guidance in the tradition of GIJN's practice library) — not copied text, our own statement of the same underlying principles: safety, legality, verification, and dignity. This is required reading before any external contributor's submission is accepted into the pipeline.

Why this page exists

Anyone can find a document, a photo, a public record, or a lead worth investigating. What separates a contribution this project can use from one it can't isn't cleverness — it's whether it was gathered safely, legally, verifiably, and without unnecessary harm to the people involved. This page sets that bar before you submit anything, not after.

The four principles

1. Safety — yours first, then everyone else's

Nothing you find is worth your physical safety, your legal exposure, or your digital security.

Never trespass, break into an account, impersonate someone, or access anything you don't have a legitimate right to see. If a document, database, or system requires bypassing a login, a paywall, or a lock to reach — stop. That's not research, and it's not something this project will use even if you get it.

If following a lead would put you in a physically risky situation (confronting someone in person, entering a restricted space, engaging with anyone who might react with hostility), don't. Report what you know and let the project decide whether professional follow-up is warranted.

Protect your own identity if you have any reason to want to: use a pseudonym when submitting if you prefer, and know that this project's own identity-firewall discipline means we won't pressure you to reveal who you are.

If your research touches anyone who could be put at risk by your inquiry becoming visible to them — a whistleblower, a vulnerable person, someone in an unequal power relationship with the subject of your research — think about their safety before yours, not after.

2. Legality — know the line, and stay well back from it

Only collect what's genuinely public or what you have clear legal right to access. Public social media posts, published records, government filings, court documents that are a matter of public record — yes. Anything obtained by hacking, social engineering, deception to gain access, or violating a platform's terms of service in a way that could expose you or this project to legal liability — no.

If you're not sure whether something is legally accessible, treat it as off-limits and ask before you act, not after you've already gathered it.

Screenshots, downloads, and captures of public material are fine and encouraged — that's exactly the kind of contribution this project can use, and our own archiving pipeline (hash, timestamp, redundant capture) exists to make sure it survives and stays checkable.

3. Verification — a claim without a source is not a contribution

Every fact you submit needs a source: a URL, a document, a screenshot with context, something another person could independently check. "I heard" or "everyone knows" doesn't clear this bar, no matter how confident you are.

If you're not certain something is true, say so when you submit it. Mark your own uncertainty honestly — this project would rather receive "I think this is true but haven't confirmed X" than a confident claim that turns out to be wrong. Overclaiming to seem more useful actively hurts the project; it does not help it.

Cross-check where you can. If a claim seems too clean, too convenient, or too perfectly timed to support a conclusion you already wanted to reach, that's exactly when to slow down and verify harder, not less.

Understand that everything you submit goes through this project's own independent review before it's trusted or published — your submission is a lead and a source, not a finished verdict, and it will be checked.

4. Dignity — the people in your research are people

Investigate power, not vulnerability. This project's focus is institutions, officials, and entities exercising public power — not private individuals' personal lives, medical histories, family matters, or anything unrelated to their public conduct.

If a document or record incidentally contains private information about someone not relevant to the investigation (a bystander's personal details, an unrelated person's contact information), don't include it in your submission, and don't spread it further.

Consider proportionality: does what you're gathering actually serve public accountability, or is it satisfying curiosity at someone's expense? If you can't answer that clearly, don't submit it.

Never use anything you find to harass, intimidate, or publicly shame anyone outside of what the verified facts themselves support — the project's job is to publish checkable claims, not to punish people through exposure.

Before you submit

  • I gathered this legally and safely, without trespassing, hacking, or impersonation.
  • I can point to a specific, checkable source for every factual claim I'm submitting.
  • I've marked anything I'm not fully certain about as uncertain, not as fact.
  • I've excluded private information about people not relevant to the public-accountability question at hand.
  • I understand my submission will be independently reviewed and may be corrected, held, or declined — that's not a rejection of me, it's the same standard every claim in this project is held to.

If something feels wrong

If a lead makes you uncomfortable, feels legally risky, or seems like it could put someone in danger — including you — stop and flag it rather than push through. A contribution held back for review is never a failure. A contribution that causes harm because a warning sign was ignored is the only real failure mode this page exists to prevent.

Questions or a lead to flag first: unknownsoldier@unitetolove.ca.
